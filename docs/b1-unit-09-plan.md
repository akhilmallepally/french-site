## 54. b1-food-and-cooking: Food and cooking

- **Type:** vocabulary · **Order:** 54
- **Prerequisites:**
  - a2-quantities: recipes are full of quantities. It implies a1-imperative, which the syllabus also listed.
- **Teaches:** recipes, cooking verbs, utensils and describing taste; recipe style (infinitive or *vous* imperative).
- **Not yet taught:** *faire cuire*, *faire bouillir*, *faire fondre* look like *faire* + infinitive (b2-faire-causatif). Taught as fixed cooking phrases, listed in review-needed.md.
- **Vocab (23):**
  - *une recette*: a recipe
  - *un ingrédient*: an ingredient
  - *couper*: to cut
  - *éplucher*: to peel
  - *mélanger*: to mix
  - *ajouter*: to add
  - *faire cuire*: to cook (fixed phrase)
  - *faire bouillir*: to boil (fixed phrase)
  - *faire fondre*: to melt (fixed phrase)
  - *cuire au four*: to bake
  - *le four*: the oven
  - *une poêle*: a frying pan
  - *une casserole*: a saucepan
  - *un saladier*: a mixing bowl
  - *une cuillère à soupe*: a tablespoon
  - *le poivre*: pepper
  - *l'huile*: oil
  - *sucré / sucrée*: sweet
  - *salé / salée*: salty, savoury
  - *épicé / épicée*: spicy
  - *amer / amère*: bitter
  - *goûter*: to taste (the verb; a1-culture-french-meals teaches *le goûter*, the afternoon snack)
  - *la farine*: flour
- **Dialogue:** Two friends make crêpes together, following a recipe.
- **Production tasks:** translate "Mix the flour and the eggs."; write out a short recipe.
- **Needs review:** "French recipes use the infinitive or the *vous* imperative." "*Faire cuire* is the usual way to say 'to cook' in a recipe."

## 55. b1-culture-regional-cuisine: Regional cuisine

- **Type:** culture · **Order:** 55
- **Prerequisites:**
  - b1-food-and-cooking: cooking vocabulary.
- **Teaches:** regions through their dishes: Alsace, Provence, Brittany and Lyon; cheese and wine culture; quality labels; the French gastronomic meal.
- **Not yet taught:** none.
- **Vocab (15):**
  - *une spécialité*: a speciality
  - *régional / régionale*: regional
  - *la choucroute*: sauerkraut with meats (Alsace)
  - *la ratatouille*: ratatouille (Provence)
  - *la bouillabaisse*: fish soup (Marseille)
  - *une galette de sarrasin*: a buckwheat pancake (Brittany)
  - *le cidre*: cider
  - *un bouchon*: a traditional Lyon restaurant (new meaning: lesson 7 teaches *un bouchon* as a traffic jam)
  - *un vignoble*: a vineyard
  - *le terroir*: the local land and its produce
  - *l'AOP*: protected designation of origin
  - *un produit local*: a local product
  - *la gastronomie*: gastronomy, fine food
  - *typique*: typical
  - *une dégustation*: a tasting
- **Dialogue:** A tourist in Lyon asks a waiter what a *bouchon* serves.
- **Production tasks:** translate "Brittany is famous for its crêpes."; write out three sentences about a regional dish.
- **Needs review (facts):** the dish for each region; "*Les bouchons* are traditional Lyon restaurants"; "France has hundreds of cheeses" (give no exact number); the AOP label; "The gastronomic meal of the French was added to UNESCO's intangible heritage list in 2010."

## 56. b1-making-suggestions: Making suggestions

- **Type:** communication · **Order:** 56
- **Prerequisites:**
  - b1-si-imparfait-conditionnel: *Si on allait… ?* uses *si* + imparfait, and *on pourrait* the conditionnel. It implies a2-invitations, which the syllabus also listed.
- **Teaches:** *Si on allait… ?*, *Et si on… ?*, *Ça te dirait de… ?* (building on *Ça te dit de… ?* from A2), *On pourrait…*, *Pourquoi pas… ?*; accepting and turning down (*Ça marche !*, *Je préférerais…*, *Ça ne me dit rien*).
- **Not yet taught:** none.
- **Vocab (13):**
  - *Si on allait… ?*: How about going…?
  - *Et si on… ?*: What if we…?
  - *Ça te dirait de… ?*: Would you fancy…?
  - *On pourrait…*: We could…
  - *Pourquoi pas ?*: Why not?
  - *Ça ne me dit rien.*: I don't fancy it.
  - *J'ai une idée !*: I've got an idea!
  - *Ça marche !*: Deal! OK!
  - *C'est parti !*: Let's go!
  - *Je suis partant / partante.*: I'm up for it.
  - *un pique-nique*: a picnic
  - *une balade*: a walk, a stroll
  - *un plan*: a plan (informal)
- **Dialogue (8 to 10 lines):** Friends plan a Saturday, making and turning down suggestions.
- **Longer text:** A group-chat thread organizing a dinner at a regional restaurant (reuses lesson 55).
- **Production tasks:** translate "How about going to the cinema?"; write out three suggestions with different structures.
- **Needs review:** "*Si on allait…* is a suggestion, not a condition." "*Ça ne me dit rien* can also mean 'it doesn't ring a bell'."

## 57. b1-formal-email: Writing a formal email

- **Type:** communication · **Order:** 57
- **Prerequisites (changed from the syllabus):**
  - b1-making-a-complaint: the written complaint is the most recent formal text. It implies b1-cover-letter, which the syllabus also listed.
- **Teaches:** register (*vous*, no abbreviations); structure (subject line, greeting, purpose, request, closing); openings (*Madame, Monsieur,*; *Bonjour Madame Dupont,*); requests (*Je vous serais reconnaissant(e) de…*, *Pourriez-vous…*); follow-ups (*Je me permets de revenir vers vous*, *Suite à notre conversation*); closings (*Cordialement*, *Bien cordialement*); attachments (*Vous trouverez ci-joint*).
- **Not yet taught:** *Veuillez trouver ci-joint* (imperative of *vouloir*): fixed phrase, listed in review-needed.md. *En vous remerciant par avance* uses the gérondif taught in lesson 38.
- **Vocab (15):**
  - *l'objet*: the subject line
  - *une formule de politesse*: a polite closing
  - *Bien cordialement*: Kind regards
  - *Je vous serais reconnaissant / reconnaissante de…*: I would be grateful if you could…
  - *suite à*: following
  - *revenir vers quelqu'un*: to get back to someone
  - *par avance*: in advance
  - *En vous remerciant par avance,*: Thanking you in advance,
  - *Vous trouverez ci-joint…*: Please find attached…
  - *Veuillez trouver ci-joint…*: Please find attached… (fixed phrase)
  - *une demande*: a request
  - *transférer*: to forward
  - *en copie*: copied in (cc)
  - *une relance*: a reminder, a follow-up
  - *une conversation*: a conversation
- **Dialogue:** A colleague asks another how formal an email to a new client should be.
- **Longer text:** A formal email asking for information about a language course, and a polite follow-up a week later.
- **Production tasks:** write out a formal email from prompts; translate "I would be grateful if you could send me the price."
- **Needs review:** "*Revenir vers vous* is very common in business emails, though some people criticize it." "*Bonjour* + name is acceptable in most formal emails." "*Cordialement* is the neutral closing; *Je vous prie d'agréer…* is for letters."

## 58. b1-culture-quebec: French in Québec

- **Type:** culture · **Order:** 58
- **Prerequisites:**
  - a2-culture-francophone-world: it introduced Québec and three Québec words.
- **Teaches:** history (New France, the founding of Québec City, British rule, the Charter of the French Language); vocabulary differences; *tu* used more freely; common expressions; the Fête nationale on 24 June.
- **Not yet taught:** none.
- **Vocab (15):**
  - *la Nouvelle-France*: New France
  - *un Québécois / une Québécoise*: a person from Québec
  - *un char*: a car (Québec)
  - *un chum*: a boyfriend, a mate (Québec)
  - *une blonde*: a girlfriend (Québec)
  - *un courriel*: an email
  - *le dîner*: lunch (in Québec)
  - *Bienvenue !*: You're welcome! (in Québec)
  - *C'est le fun.*: It's fun.
  - *avoir de la misère à*: to have trouble …ing
  - *frette*: freezing cold
  - *pantoute*: not at all
  - *une tuque*: a woolly hat
  - *la poutine*: chips with cheese curds and gravy
  - *la Charte de la langue française*: the Charter of the French Language
- **Dialogue:** A French visitor in Montréal is puzzled by a Québécois friend's words.
- **Production tasks:** translate "In Québec, people call lunch *le dîner*."; write out three differences between French in France and in Québec.
- **Needs review (facts and usage):** "Champlain founded Québec City in 1608." "New France passed to Britain in 1763." "The Charter of the French Language (Bill 101) dates from 1977." "The Fête nationale du Québec is on 24 June." "*Bienvenue* is used for 'you're welcome'." The meanings of *char*, *chum*, *blonde*, *frette* and *pantoute*.

## 59. b1-review-final: B1 final review

- **Type:** review · **Order:** 59
- **Prerequisites:** the eight unit reviews (b1-review-opinions, b1-review-hypotheses, b1-review-stories, b1-review-subjunctive, b1-review-work, b1-review-media, b1-review-society, b1-review-home-money-problems) and the four Unit 9 lessons, which have no unit review (b1-culture-regional-cuisine, b1-making-suggestions, b1-formal-email, b1-culture-quebec). Twelve in all: final reviews are exempt from the six-prerequisite limit.
- **Teaches:** a can-do checklist for the whole level; one sentence from each unit; the French instruction words (callout required by the B1 conventions).
- **Vocab (14):**
  - *un bilan*: an assessment, a stock-take
  - *à l'aise*: comfortable, at ease
  - *couramment*: fluently
  - *la compréhension*: understanding
  - *l'expression écrite*: writing
  - *l'expression orale*: speaking
  - *s'exprimer*: to express yourself
  - *une consigne*: an instruction (in an exercise)
  - *Complétez.*: Complete.
  - *Choisissez.*: Choose.
  - *Mettez dans l'ordre.*: Put in order.
  - *Traduisez.*: Translate.
  - *Reliez.*: Match.
  - *Corrigez.*: Correct.
- **Model text:** Eight sentences, one per unit, then a final writing task about yourself that uses something from each unit.
- **Production tasks:** the writing task; two write-out paragraphs; one translation per main structure (conditionnel, subjunctive, plus-que-parfait).
- **Needs review:** the instruction words list: confirm these are the forms French textbooks use.
