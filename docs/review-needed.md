# Review needed

Claims in written lessons that a fluent speaker or teacher should confirm. Each entry gives the lesson id and the exact claim as it appears (or is implied) in the lesson. Remove an entry once it's checked.

## a1-greetings

- **"There is no exact time to change, but most people switch somewhere around six or seven o'clock, or when it starts to get dark."** Confirm the time range for switching from *bonjour* to *bonsoir*.
- **"French has no separate greeting for the afternoon: *bonjour* covers the whole day."** *Bon après-midi* exists, but as a goodbye. Confirm the wording is fair.
- **"*Au revoir* … comes from *revoir*, 'to see again'."** Confirm.
- **"In *bonjour* … the *n* isn't pronounced on its own: it changes the sound of the vowel before it. The last letters of *plaît* and *salut* are silent."** Simplified for beginners; confirm.

## a1-alphabet-and-accents

- **"When a line is bad, French speakers often spell with names: *B comme Bernard, D comme Daniel*."** Confirm this is still common practice and that these example names are typical.
- **"Many circumflexes stand where an s was written in older French … forêt (forest), hôpital (hospital), fête (feast)."** Confident about the examples. Confirm the wording "very often" isn't overstated.
- **"In French that's normally the last syllable of a word or short phrase"** (pronunciation hints: where the stress falls). Simplified for beginners; confirm it's acceptable as stated.

- **"*Mon nom est…* isn't wrong, but it sounds stiff, like reading from a form."** Confirm the register judgement.
- **"In formal French paperwork, people often give the surname first and sometimes write it in capitals, for example *MARTIN Sophie*."**

## a1-numbers-0-20

## b1-subjonctif-formation

- **"The subjunctive isn't a tense. It has a present form … and it covers both present and future."** Confirm.
- **"The rule still works: take *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils* from the *ils* form, and *nous* and *vous* from the *nous* form of the present."** Confirm for the two-stem verbs listed (*prendre*, *venir*, *boire*, *acheter*, *appeler*).
- **"For most *-er* verbs, *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils* sound exactly like the present."** Confirm.

## b1-subjonctif-irregular

- **"*Être*, *avoir*, *faire*, *pouvoir* and *savoir* are exceptions to the rule … that *nous* and *vous* look like the imparfait: *que nous soyons*, *que nous ayons*, *que nous fassions*, *que nous puissions*, *que nous sachions*."** Confirm.
- **"There's no *i* after the *y* in *soyons*, *soyez*, *ayons* and *ayez*."** Confirm.
- **"*Sois*, *soyons*, *soyez*, *aie* and *ayez* are also the imperative forms of *être* and *avoir*."** Confirm (and that *ayons* is too).
- **"Most of the *avoir* forms sound like *ai*: *que j'aie*, *qu'il ait* and *qu'ils aient* all sound the same."** Confirm.
- **"*Falloir* has one too, *qu'il faille*, but you'll rarely need it."** Confirm.
- **"*Aller* and *vouloir* have two stems … *Faire*, *pouvoir* and *savoir* keep one stem all the way through."** Confirm.

## b1-subjonctif-necessity

- **"Each of these expressions also works with *de* + infinitive, for a general statement. With *il faut* and *il vaut mieux*, there's no *de*."** Confirm, including *il suffit de partir*.
- **"To say 'you don't have to', use *ce n'est pas nécessaire*: *Ce n'est pas nécessaire de venir*."** Confirm it's natural.
- **"*Il vaut mieux que* is the present of *il vaudrait mieux*."** Confirm the framing.

## b1-subjonctif-wishes-emotions

- **"After *avoir peur que* and *craindre que*, written French often adds a small *ne* that doesn't make the sentence negative: *J'ai peur qu'il ne soit en retard*."** Confirm.
- **"*Souhaiter que* is more formal, and it's common in wishes for other people: *Je souhaite que tout se passe bien*."** Confirm.
- **"*Regretter que* … sounds more formal [than *je suis désolé que*], and it's common in letters and emails."** Confirm.
- **"*Maman* and *papa* are what children and adults call their own parents."** Confirm.
- **"*Mes parents préfèrent que nous prenions le train*"** translated as "prefer that we take". Confirm.
