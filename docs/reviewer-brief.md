# Reviewer brief: Parlons

Parlons is a free, text-only website that teaches French to English speakers, from A1 (beginner) to C1. Each line below quotes a claim made in a lesson.
Please tick each claim that's correct and mark it **OK**, or write the correction underneath it.

The claims are grouped by lesson in course order, with the riskiest claim first in each lesson. "Natural?" means: does this French sentence sound like something a native speaker would say?

---

## 1. B1: grammar and usage

### Expressing opinions (b1-expressing-opinions)
- [ ] "For a negative opinion, keep the opinion verb positive and put the negative after *que*: *Je trouve que ce n'est pas très original*." Natural?
- [ ] "*Je pense que oui* (I think so) and *Je crois que non* (I don't think so)." Both natural?
- [ ] "*Je crois que* can sound a little less certain, close to 'I believe'."
- [ ] "*Je trouve que* suggests an opinion based on your own experience."
- [ ] "*Je pense que* is the neutral choice."
- [ ] "*Pour moi* … can introduce an opinion too: *Pour moi, c'est le meilleur restaurant de la ville*."
- [ ] "*À vrai dire* (to tell the truth) often comes before an opinion the other person may not expect."

### Agreeing and disagreeing (b1-agreeing-disagreeing)
- [ ] "*Moi si* contradicts a negative statement: *Je n'aime pas le café. Moi si !*" Is it usually written *Moi, si*?
- [ ] "*D'accord* by itself accepts a plan … To agree with an opinion, you need the full verb: *Je suis d'accord*."
- [ ] "*Tu as tort*: direct, and it can sound rude." / "*Je ne suis pas sûr*: polite doubt."
- [ ] "*Ce n'est pas faux*" (that's a fair point) works as a polite concession.
- [ ] "*Je suis du même avis*" is natural in conversation, not only in writing.
- [ ] "*Je suis en partie d'accord.*" Natural word order?
- [ ] "*Pour moi, le dimanche matin, d'accord, mais pas l'après-midi.*" Natural?

### ce qui, ce que (b1-ce-qui-ce-que)
- [ ] "In the passé composé, the participle after *ce que* doesn't agree: *ce que j'ai vu*, *ce que nous avons fait*."
- [ ] "*Ce* is masculine and singular, so the verb after *ce qui* is in the *il* form."
- [ ] "*Qu'est-ce qui* becomes *ce qui*: *Je regarde ce qui se passe*."
- [ ] "*Ce qui m'énerve, ce sont les voitures le matin.*" Is *ce sont* right before a plural, rather than *c'est*?
- [ ] "*Plaire* works the opposite way round from 'like', just like *manquer* in *tu me manques*."

### The relative pronoun dont (b1-relative-dont)
- [ ] "When the noun is the object of the verb, it moves after the verb: *C'est la femme dont je connais les enfants*."
- [ ] "Don't add a possessive: it's *dont le père*, not *dont son père*."
- [ ] "*Dont* never changes. It's the same for masculine and feminine, singular and plural, people and things."
- [ ] "*Se servir* works like *partir*: *je me sers*, *nous nous servons*."
- [ ] "*C'est une raison dont on parle peu.*" / "*Voici le rêve dont je te parle depuis des années.*" Natural?

### Relationships and feelings (b1-relationships)
- [ ] "*Marier* without *se* means to marry other people, as a mayor or a priest does."
- [ ] "*Une dispute* is an argument between people who know each other, often about something small. *Un conflit* is bigger and lasts longer."
- [ ] "*Le mariage* means both the marriage and the wedding day."
- [ ] "*L'amour* is masculine (*un grand amour*)." Is it fine to leave out the literary feminine plural?
- [ ] "*Mon frère est célibataire, et ça lui va très bien !*" Natural?

### Review: Opinions and feelings (b1-review-opinions)
- [ ] "In writing, *tu as tort* or *vous avez tort* sounds much harsher than in conversation."
- [ ] "*C'est mieux d'habiter en ville ou à la campagne ?*" Natural?
- [ ] "*À mon avis, deux ou trois amis proches, c'est mieux que beaucoup d'amis.*" / "*Il faut du temps pour une vraie amitié.*" Natural?

### Si + present, future (b1-si-present-futur)
- [ ] "*Si* becomes *s'* before *il* and *ils*, and only there. Before *elle*, *on* or any other word, it stays *si*."
- [ ] "For a habit or something that's always true, both halves are in the present. *Si* then means something close to 'whenever'."
- [ ] "When *si* comes second, you usually don't need a comma."
- [ ] "*Rater* is the everyday word for missing a train, a bus or a plane."
- [ ] "An *imprévu* is literally something 'not foreseen'."

### The conditionnel présent (b1-conditionnel-present)
- [ ] "*Pouvoir* has a double *r* in the conditionnel, like *mourir* and *voir* … *Devoir*, *vouloir* and *savoir* don't double."
- [ ] "In careful speech, *-ai* sounds like the *é* of *été*, and *-ais* like the *è* of *mère*. Many speakers don't make the difference."
- [ ] "News reports often use the conditionnel for something they've heard but can't confirm: *Selon les journaux, le ministre serait malade*."
- [ ] Politeness scale: *je veux*, then *je voudrais*, then *j'aimerais bien* ("softer still, … when you're asking for a favour").
- [ ] "*Il paraît que* and *apparemment* say the same thing, but with an ordinary verb."
- [ ] Conditionnel stems: *pouvoir* → *pourr-*, *devoir* → *devr-*, *savoir* → *saur-*, *venir* → *viendr-*, *voir* → *verr-*, *falloir* → *faudr-*.

### Si + imparfait, conditionnel (b1-si-imparfait-conditionnel)
- [ ] "*Si j'aurais le temps* … You may hear it in casual speech, but it's considered a mistake."
- [ ] "Someone who has bought a ticket and feels lucky might say *si je gagne*. Someone who never plays says *si je gagnais*."
- [ ] "*Si j'étais vous*" as the *vous* form of *si j'étais toi*. Natural?

### Giving advice (b1-giving-advice)
- [ ] "Dropping the *ne* is common in casual speech with a conjugated verb, but keep it in front of an infinitive."
- [ ] "*Il vaudrait mieux ne pas boire de café le soir.*" Is *ne pas* before the infinitive correct?
- [ ] "*Un conseil* is one piece of advice, and you can count it: *deux conseils*."
- [ ] "*Il faut* can sound bossy unless the situation really is urgent."

### Dreams and ambitions (b1-dreams-and-ambitions)
- [ ] "French doesn't use [a special verb form after 'hope']: *J'espère que tu viendras*."
- [ ] "*Compter* is followed straight by an infinitive, with no preposition."
- [ ] "*Un projet* is an everyday plan as well as a big one."
- [ ] "*Un but*: the final *t* is often pronounced, but some speakers say *bew*."

### Review: Hypotheses and wishes (b1-review-hypotheses)
- [ ] "*À votre place, je pèserais* is advice, with the conditionnel. *Si vous partez, vous aurez* is a real possibility."
- [ ] "*J'hésite entre deux choix.*" Natural?

### The plus-que-parfait (b1-plus-que-parfait)
- [ ] "*Se rendre compte* … its participle doesn't agree, because the real object is *compte*: *elle s'est rendu compte*. *S'apercevoir* agrees normally: *elle s'est aperçue*."
- [ ] "*Quand je suis arrivé, le train est parti*: the train leaves as you arrive" (compared with *était parti*).
- [ ] "English often uses the simple past where French needs the plus-que-parfait: *Je me suis rendu compte que j'avais oublié mes clés*."
- [ ] "*Nous avions déjà visité Lyon auparavant, alors nous sommes allés à la plage.*" Does *auparavant* sound natural?

### Time connectors (b1-time-connectors)
- [ ] "About the future, *dès que* and *aussitôt que* take the futur, where English uses the present."
- [ ] "In casual speech you'll hear *quand j'arrive, je t'appelle* … but don't mix a present after *quand* with a futur in the other half."
- [ ] "*Lorsque* … is more formal, and you'll see it more in writing than hear it in conversation."
- [ ] "*Aussitôt qu'il aura le temps, il viendra.*" Natural?

### Telling an anecdote (b1-telling-an-anecdote)
- [ ] "*J'allais* + infinitive … says you were about to do something, often when something interrupted you."
- [ ] "*Venir de* in the imparfait … says something had just happened."
- [ ] "*Figure-toi que* is informal … With *vous* or with several friends, it's *figurez-vous que*."
- [ ] "*La honte !* is informal: keep it for friends."
- [ ] "*Au final* is common in speech, but some people consider it incorrect." Is it acceptable to teach?

### Cinema and books (b1-cinema-and-books)
- [ ] "*Autrice* is the usual feminine of *auteur* today, though you'll also see *auteure*."
- [ ] "*Un polar* is an informal word for a crime film or novel."
- [ ] "*Le dernier film* here means 'the latest film'."

### Reviewing a film or book (b1-reviewing-a-film-or-book)
- [ ] "French reviews, like English ones, tell the story in the present."
- [ ] "*Un film raté* is a failure, not a film you missed."
- [ ] "*Ça vaut le coup* is informal but very common in reviews."
- [ ] "*À lire absolument* works like *à voir absolument*."

### French cinema (b1-culture-french-cinema)
- [ ] "*Les César*, with no s."

### Review: Telling stories (b1-review-stories)
- [ ] "*Au final* … means 'in the end, all things considered' … *finalement* can mean either 'finally, at last' or 'in the end'."
- [ ] "*Dès le début, tout est allé mal.*" Natural?

### The subjunctive: regular forms (b1-subjonctif-formation)
- [ ] "The subjunctive isn't a tense. It has a present form … and it covers both present and future."
- [ ] "Take *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils* from the *ils* form, and *nous* and *vous* from the *nous* form of the present." Does this hold for *prendre*, *venir*, *boire*, *acheter* and *appeler*?
- [ ] "For most *-er* verbs, *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils* sound exactly like the present."

### Irregular subjunctives (b1-subjonctif-irregular)
- [ ] "*Aller* and *vouloir* have two stems … *Faire*, *pouvoir* and *savoir* keep one stem all the way through."
- [ ] "*Sois*, *soyons*, *soyez*, *aie* and *ayez* are also the imperative forms of *être* and *avoir*." Is *ayons* one too?
- [ ] "*Que j'aie*, *qu'il ait* and *qu'ils aient* all sound the same" (like *ai*).
- [ ] "*Falloir* has one too, *qu'il faille*, but you'll rarely need it."
- [ ] "*Être*, *avoir*, *faire*, *pouvoir* and *savoir* are exceptions to the rule … that *nous* and *vous* look like the imparfait": *que nous soyons*, *ayons*, *fassions*, *puissions*, *sachions*.

### Subjunctive after il faut que (b1-subjonctif-necessity)
- [ ] "Each of these expressions also works with *de* + infinitive, for a general statement. With *il faut* and *il vaut mieux*, there's no *de*." (*il suffit de partir*)
- [ ] "To say 'you don't have to', use *ce n'est pas nécessaire*: *Ce n'est pas nécessaire de venir*." Natural?
- [ ] "*Il vaut mieux que* is the present of *il vaudrait mieux*."

### Subjunctive after wishes and emotions (b1-subjonctif-wishes-emotions)
- [ ] "After *avoir peur que* and *craindre que*, written French often adds a small *ne* that doesn't make the sentence negative: *J'ai peur qu'il ne soit en retard*."
- [ ] "*Souhaiter que* is more formal, and it's common in wishes for other people: *Je souhaite que tout se passe bien*."
- [ ] "*Regretter que* … sounds more formal [than *je suis désolé que*], and it's common in letters and emails."
- [ ] "*Mes parents préfèrent que nous prenions le train*" translated as "My parents prefer that we take the train."
- [ ] "*Maman* and *papa* are what children and adults call their own parents."

### Subjunctive or infinitive? (b1-subjunctive-vs-infinitive)
- [ ] "French never uses *que* when the two verbs share a subject" (with *vouloir*, *préférer*, *aimer*, *être content*, *avoir peur*, *regretter*).
- [ ] "*Oser* … Followed straight by an infinitive."
- [ ] "*Elle m'a proposé de partir ensemble*" means "she suggested we leave together".

### Doubt and certainty (b1-expressing-doubt-certainty)
- [ ] "*Douter* … takes the subjunctive even in the affirmative."
- [ ] "The subjunctive after a negative opinion is also a polite way to disagree."
- [ ] "*Sûrement* is weaker than its English look-alike: it often means 'probably'."
- [ ] "In fast speech, French speakers often drop the *ne*: *Je pense pas qu'il vienne*."

### Review: The subjunctive (b1-review-subjunctive)
- [ ] "*Elle pense qu'on va dîner*: what Léa thinks is true for her, so the indicative."

### Careers and job hunting (b1-careers-and-job-hunting)
- [ ] "*Postuler* is followed by *à*: *postuler à un poste*. You'll also hear *postuler pour*." Are both acceptable?
- [ ] "Job ads drop articles and verbs: *CDD de six mois, expérience demandée*."

### CV and cover letter (b1-cover-letter)
- [ ] "To say 'actually' in the sense of 'in fact', French uses *en fait*."
- [ ] "Never *Cher Monsieur* in a first letter."
- [ ] "In an email, *Cordialement* is usually enough, but in a letter, the full formula is still expected."

### The job interview (b1-job-interview)
- [ ] "*Ça fait… que* is the most common in speech … *Il y a… que* is a little more formal."
- [ ] "*Je reste disponible si vous avez des questions.*" (follow-up email) Natural?

### Double object pronouns (b1-double-object-pronouns)
- [ ] Pronoun order: *me/te/se/nous/vous*, then *le/la/les*, then *lui/leur*, then *y*, then *en*.
- [ ] "In negative commands, the normal order comes back: *Ne me le donne pas !*"
- [ ] "*Me*, *te*, *nous* and *vous* can't go with *lui* or *leur*. When both people are pronouns, French puts one after *à*: *Il me présente à elle*."
- [ ] "*Je la lui ai envoyée*": the participle agrees with *la*.

### Possessive pronouns (b1-possessive-pronouns)
- [ ] "The pronouns *nôtre* and *vôtre* have a circumflex, and the *o* is closed, as in *eau*. The adjectives *notre* and *votre* have no accent, and the *o* is more open."
- [ ] "*C'est à moi* … just says who owns it. *C'est le mien* is used when you're comparing, or choosing between several similar things."

### Demonstrative pronouns (b1-demonstrative-pronouns)
- [ ] "*Celui*, *celle*, *ceux* and *celles* never stand alone at the end of a sentence."
- [ ] "In careful French, *-ci* is the nearer one and *-là* the further one. In everyday speech, people often use *celui-là* for both, and point."

### Work life in France (b1-culture-work-life)
- [ ] "It's *faire grève*, with no article."
- [ ] "*Un jour de congé* (a day off) is the main exception [to plural *congés*]."

### Review: Work and careers (b1-review-work)
- [ ] "*Un libraire* sells books, and *un bibliothécaire* works in a library."

### Media and news (b1-media-and-news)
- [ ] "French headlines … often drop the verb, especially *être*": *Le musée fermé dimanche*, *Grève des trains lundi*, *Nouveau film pour Dany Boon*. Natural headlines?
- [ ] "*Les infos* and *les informations* are plural … *une info*, *une information* (a piece of news). And *une nouvelle* is a piece of personal news."
- [ ] "*Le journal* means a newspaper, but also the news bulletin on TV or radio: *le journal de 20 heures*."
- [ ] "*Une interview* is for the media. For a job, use *un entretien*."

### Technology and the internet (b1-technology-and-internet)
- [ ] "*Internet* usually has no article: *sur internet*."
- [ ] "*Un portable* on its own is a mobile phone … *un mobile* is also used for a mobile phone, especially in writing."
- [ ] "*Publier* is the general verb for posting. You'll also hear *poster*."
- [ ] "*Une appli* … you'll hear it far more often in speech" (than *une application*).
- [ ] *Paramètres*, *Enregistrer*, *Supprimer* and *Mettre à jour* are the usual French interface labels.

### Reported speech (present) (b1-reported-speech-present)
- [ ] "In a reported question, the word order goes back to normal: subject, then verb … never *si est-ce que tu viens*."
- [ ] "*Qu'est-ce que* and *qu'est-ce qui* become *ce que* and *ce qui*."
- [ ] "*Que* is never left out."
- [ ] "When [the reporting verb is] in the past, the tenses in the second half often change."

### The gérondif (b1-gerondif)
- [ ] "The gérondif always refers to the subject of the main verb. *J'ai vu Paul en sortant du cinéma* means *I* was leaving the cinema."
- [ ] "Three verbs are irregular: *étant*, *ayant*, *sachant*."
- [ ] "*En partant tôt* means *si tu pars tôt*."
- [ ] "You can also put it first, followed by a comma … The meaning stays the same."
- [ ] "*Tout en* stresses that the two things happen together … *En attendant* is a fixed phrase meaning 'in the meantime'."

### Indefinite words (b1-indefinite-pronouns)
- [ ] "*Aucun* stays singular, because it means 'not one'. When it's the subject, *ne* still goes before the verb: *Aucun commentaire n'est gentil*."
- [ ] "*Quelque chose de* + adjective … The adjective stays masculine. *Rien de* and *personne de* work the same way."
- [ ] "*Plusieurs* and *quelques* go straight before the noun, with no article."
- [ ] "*Tout le monde* … takes a singular verb."
- [ ] "*Quelques* suggests a small number … *plusieurs* suggests 'more than two', and often more than you expected."
- [ ] "*Chacun son goût !* (Each to their own!)" Is this form right? (*À chacun son goût* and *Chacun ses goûts* also exist.)

### Lequel, laquelle (b1-interrogative-lequel)
- [ ] *Auquel, à laquelle, auxquels, auxquelles; duquel, de laquelle, desquels, desquelles.*
- [ ] "In speech, people often avoid them: instead of *Auquel ?*, you'll often hear *À quel appareil ?*, or simply *Lequel ?*"
- [ ] "*Lequel de ces téléphones est le moins cher ?*" Natural?
- [ ] "All four plurals sound the same" (*lesquels*, *lesquelles*).
- [ ] "You can also answer with *le* or *la* and an adjective: *Laquelle tu prends ? La noire.*"

### Review: Media and technology (b1-review-media)
- [ ] "The verb after *que* stays in the passé composé, because the reporting verb is in the present."
- [ ] "*Contrôler* is for official checks, like tickets on a train or passports at a border. *Je contrôle l'article* sounds as if you're the police."
- [ ] "*Un fait divers*: a news item (crime, accidents, odd stories)."

### The environment (b1-environment)
- [ ] "Before an adjective or after a verb, there's nothing more to add. Before a noun, add *de*, with no article": *de plus en plus chaud*, *de plus en plus de plastique*.
- [ ] "*De moins en moins de* covers both 'less and less' and 'fewer and fewer'."
- [ ] "*Protéger* changes its accent like *préférer*: *je protège*, but *nous protégeons*."
- [ ] "As a noun, *le bio* means organic food in general … As an adjective, it never changes: *des pommes bio*."
- [ ] "*Les énergies renouvelables* is usually plural."

### Cause and consequence (b1-cause-and-consequence)
- [ ] "*Car* … always comes in the middle of a sentence, after the main idea, and it can never start a sentence or answer a question on its own."
- [ ] "*Puisque* gives a reason that the other person already knows, or can see."
- [ ] "*Comme* (as, since) goes at the start of the sentence, before the main idea."
- [ ] "*À cause de* is for a cause with a bad result. *Grâce à* is for a cause with a good result."
- [ ] "*En raison de* is neutral and formal … rarely in conversation."
- [ ] "*Alors* … is more spoken. *Donc* works in speech and writing … *c'est pour ça que* is [*c'est pourquoi*'s] everyday spoken form."
- [ ] "*Avec plaisir, mais un café froid, alors !*" Does *un café froid* work for "an iced coffee"?

### The passive voice: introduction (b1-passive-voice-intro)
- [ ] "Only the second participle agrees: *elles ont été sauvées*. *Été* never changes."
- [ ] "French uses the passive less than English, especially in conversation. When there's no *par*, French often prefers *on* with an active verb."
- [ ] "Very often, there's no *par* at all, because the agent is obvious or unknown."
- [ ] "*Incendie dans les Alpes : une forêt a été détruite dimanche.*" Natural as a news headline?

### Presenting a simple argument (b1-presenting-an-argument)
- [ ] "*Il est vrai que* … states a fact, so it takes the indicative."
- [ ] "*Premièrement* and *deuxièmement* are more formal [than *d'abord, ensuite*] … Don't mix the two series."
- [ ] "*D'une part… d'autre part* is a little more formal than *d'un côté… de l'autre*."
- [ ] "*Interdire* is conjugated like *dire*, except for *vous interdisez*."
- [ ] "*Faut-il interdire les voitures dans le centre-ville ?*" Natural as a debate question?

### The French Republic (b1-culture-french-republic)
- [ ] "*Maire* is masculine for a man, and you'll hear *la maire* for a woman."
- [ ] "People often call it *le drapeau tricolore*, or simply *le tricolore*."

### Review: Society and environment (b1-review-society)
- [ ] "*Sensibiliser quelqu'un à quelque chose*: to make someone aware of something" (*sensibiliser les élèves au recyclage*).
- [ ] "*Il coûte cher à la planète.*" Natural?

### Housing and renting (b1-housing-and-renting)
- [ ] "*Louer* works in both directions: the owner *loue* the flat to you, and you *louez* it from the owner."
- [ ] "*Une rente* … is a regular income you receive", so the rent is always *le loyer*.
- [ ] "*Un T2* or *un F2* is a flat with two main rooms … without counting the kitchen and the bathroom."
- [ ] "*Les charges* cover shared costs, such as water or cleaning the building." Ad codes: *CC* (*charges comprises*), *HC* (*hors charges*).

### Money and banking (b1-money-and-banking)
- [ ] "*Emprunter* … you borrow *à* someone: *J'emprunte de l'argent à ma sœur*."
- [ ] "You'll often hear *la carte bleue*, or *la CB*, for any bank card, whatever its colour."
- [ ] "*Le code* is short for *le code secret*, and that's the word cashiers use."
- [ ] "*Être à découvert* means there's less than nothing in your account."

### ne…que and ni…ni (b1-ne-que-ni-ni)
- [ ] "*Ne… que* … the article doesn't change to *de* as it does after *pas*: *Je n'ai que des pièces*."
- [ ] "With *un*, *une*, *des* and the partitive, the article disappears after *ni*. With *le*, *la* and *les* … it stays."
- [ ] "*Ni Paul ni Marie ne viennent ce soir.*" Is the plural verb right?
- [ ] "*Je n'ai pas que dix euros* … means 'I haven't only got ten euros'."
- [ ] Both *Je n'ai ni temps ni argent* and *Je n'ai ni le temps ni l'argent* are accepted.
- [ ] "*À peine* … isn't a negative at all, so it doesn't need *ne*. It usually goes after the verb."
- [ ] "In everyday speech, people often drop *ne*: *J'ai que dix euros*."
- [ ] *Une pièce* (a coin) and *un billet* (a banknote) are taught as new meanings of "room" and "ticket".

### Writing a formal email (b1-formal-email)
- [ ] "*Cordialement* and *Bien cordialement* are the usual closings for emails. *Je vous prie d'agréer…* … belongs to letters, and it sounds too heavy in most emails."
- [ ] "If you know the name, *Bonjour Madame Martin,* is fine in most emails, and *Madame,* or *Monsieur,* alone is more formal."
- [ ] "French doesn't translate the 'please' [of 'Please find attached'] with *s'il vous plaît*": *Vous trouverez ci-joint…* or *Veuillez trouver ci-joint…*
- [ ] "*Revenir vers vous* is everywhere in business emails, though some people find it a little heavy."
- [ ] "*Suite à* … works like a preposition, and a noun follows it directly: *suite à votre demande*."

### Making a complaint (b1-making-a-complaint)
- [ ] "*Une plainte* is a complaint in general, and *porter plainte* means making an official complaint to the police. To a shop or a company, the word is *une réclamation*."
- [ ] "Without the pronoun, *plaindre* means to feel sorry for someone: *je te plains*."
- [ ] "*Ce n'est pas normal* is the standard French way to say that something isn't acceptable. It's firm without being rude."
- [ ] "*Exiger* is strong, so keep it for the second complaint. *Dans les plus brefs délais* is formal written French."

### Travel problems (b1-travel-problems)
- [ ] "If the airline lost your suitcase, don't say *J'ai perdu ma valise* … Say *Ma valise n'est pas arrivée* or *Ma valise est perdue*."
- [ ] "*Rater* is the everyday verb for missing a train, a plane or a connection."
- [ ] "A flight *sans escale* is a direct one."
- [ ] "*Est-ce que j'ai droit à une indemnisation ?*" Natural?

### Review: Home, money and problems (b1-review-home-money-problems)
- [ ] "*Attacher* means to tie or fasten something … For an email, use *joindre*."
- [ ] *Un dédommagement* (compensation for a loss or trouble) and *une indemnisation* are close enough to use interchangeably at this level.

### Food and cooking (b1-food-and-cooking)
- [ ] "For cooking a particular ingredient or dish, French uses *faire cuire* … not *cuisinez les pâtes*."
- [ ] "Most [recipes] use the *vous* imperative … Many recipe books and websites use the infinitive instead."
- [ ] "*Salé* means salty, but also savoury, as opposed to sweet: *une crêpe salée*."
- [ ] "The *oê* in *poêle* sounds like *wa*: it rhymes with *étoile*."
- [ ] "In recipes, you'll usually see *faire cuire au four*, or simply *enfourner*."

### Regional cuisine (b1-culture-regional-cuisine)

## 3. A2 and A1: grammar

### Common irregular verbs (a2-irregular-verbs-present)
- [ ] "The *tu* form almost always sounds like *je*, *il* usually sounds like them too, and *vous* normally follows *nous*."

### Irregular past participles (a2-past-participles-irregular)
- [ ] "Verbs built on another verb usually share its participle."

### Numbers over 100, ordinals and years (a2-numbers-years-ordinals)
- [ ] "Numbers are written with a space or a point between thousands, never a comma."
- [ ] "For the 1900s, *mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-cinq* is the more usual form, and *dix-neuf cent quatre-vingt-cinq* also exists." / "1900 itself is *mille neuf cents*."
- [ ] "Ordinals go before the noun." Safe to state without exceptions (*François Ier*, *chapitre deux*)?
- [ ] "*Second* … exists and you'll see it, but *deuxième* is always a safe choice."

### Time expressions for the past (a2-time-expressions-past)
- [ ] "*Déjà* can also mean 'ever'" in a question.
- [ ] *Pas encore* "takes the place of *pas*."
- [ ] "These words usually go at the very beginning or the very end of a sentence."

### ne…jamais, rien, personne, plus (a2-negative-expressions)
- [ ] "*Je n'ai pas rien vu* is a double negative with a different meaning (I did see something), so it's not the way to say 'I saw nothing'."
- [ ] "*Ils ne sont plus venus*" means "They didn't come any more."

### Talking about your weekend (a2-talking-about-your-weekend)
- [ ] "When *on* means 'we' and the verb takes *être*, the participle usually agrees as it would with *nous*" (*on est allés*).

### Reflexive verbs in the passé composé (a2-reflexive-passe-compose)
- [ ] "As a rule of thumb, the reflexive pronoun is the direct object, so the participle agrees with the subject": *elle s'est levée*, *nous nous sommes dépêchés*.
- [ ] No agreement when a direct object follows: *elle s'est cassé le bras*, *il s'est lavé les mains*, *elle s'est brossé les dents*.
- [ ] Reciprocal: *ils se sont vus*, *elles se sont rencontrées* agree; *ils se sont téléphoné* doesn't.
- [ ] "*Au genou* has *à* in it, so it isn't a direct object": *elle s'est blessée au genou*.
- [ ] "With *on* meaning 'we', the participle usually agrees as it would with *nous* … *on s'est levés*."

### Il faut and giving simple advice (a2-il-faut-and-advice)
- [ ] "It's a fixed form: the *il* doesn't refer to anyone, so you can't use it with *je* or *tu*."
- [ ] "Use *il faut* for something that's true in general … Use *devoir* when you're talking directly about one person or a group."
- [ ] After *il faut* + noun, "use *du*, *de la* or *un* as you'd expect."

### Adverbs (a2-adverbs-ment)
- [ ] "In the passé composé … short everyday adverbs go between the auxiliary and the participle. Most -ment adverbs go after the participle."
- [ ] "*Lent* is an exception …: it's *lentement*."
- [ ] "[-emment and -amment] sound the same, with the vowel of *a*."
- [ ] "*Très* … doesn't go before a verb."

### The imparfait (a2-imparfait-formation)
- [ ] "Four of the six forms sound the same: *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils* … Only *nous* and *vous* (*-ions*, *-iez*) sound different."

### Passé composé or imparfait? (a2-passe-compose-vs-imparfait)
- [ ] "*Toujours* (always) isn't a reliable signal on its own: *J'ai toujours habité ici* … is common French."
- [ ] The imparfait is for "actions still going on at that moment."
- [ ] "The imparfait tells you how things were, and the passé composé tells you what happened next."

### Telling a simple story (a2-telling-a-story)
- [ ] "*Ce jour-là* often introduces the scene, in the imparfait, and *un jour* often introduces the first event, in the passé composé."
- [ ] "The connector usually goes first in the sentence, and it's often followed by a comma." (*Puis* usually isn't.)

### Review: Memories and childhood (a2-review-memories)
- [ ] Rule of thumb: "try putting 'and then' in front [to choose the passé composé] … A feeling or a state can still be imparfait even between two events: *j'avais peur*."

### Jobs and the workplace (a2-jobs-and-workplace)
- [ ] Feminine job titles *professeure*, *ingénieure* and *écrivaine*, with *une professeur*, *une ingénieur* and *une écrivain* "also seen."
- [ ] "*Médecin* usually stays the same for women: *une médecin*, and you'll also hear *un médecin* used of a woman."
- [ ] "Often -eur becomes -euse. Often -teur becomes -trice."

### C'est or il est? (a2-cest-vs-il-est)
- [ ] "*Il est médecin* and *C'est un médecin* mean the same thing."
- [ ] [*C'est un/une* is needed] "even if there's an adjective too": *C'est une bonne dentiste*.

### Savoir and connaître (a2-savoir-connaitre)
- [ ] "[*Connaître* is] always followed directly by a noun or a name."
- [ ] "*Je peux nager.* (I'm able to swim, or I'm allowed to swim)", in contrast with *je sais nager*.

### depuis, pendant, il y a (a2-depuis-pendant-il-y-a)
- [ ] "With a positive verb, *depuis* takes the present: *J'habite ici depuis 2019*, and *J'ai habité ici depuis 2019* is wrong."
- [ ] "[*Il y a*, meaning ago] usually goes at the start or the end of the sentence."

### Recent past and actions in progress (a2-venir-de-en-train-de)
- [ ] "*En train de* adds emphasis."
- [ ] "*À l'instant* fits naturally with *venir de*, and *en ce moment* fits naturally with the present or *en train de*."

### Stressed pronouns (a2-stressed-pronouns)
- [ ] "*C'est eux* is the normal way to say 'It's them'."
- [ ] Both *Paul et moi, nous allons à Lyon* and *Paul et moi allons à Lyon* are correct.

### Direct object pronouns (a2-direct-object-pronouns)
- [ ] "The noun being replaced has a definite word in front of it: *le*, *la*, *les*, *mon*, *ton* or *ce*, for instance."
- [ ] "Before a noun, it's an article. Before a verb, it's a pronoun."

### Indirect object pronouns (a2-indirect-object-pronouns)
- [ ] "*Dire à* works this way too, with a simple object": *Je lui dis bonjour*.

### The pronoun y (a2-pronoun-y)
- [ ] "*Tu joues au tennis ? Oui, j'y joue.*" Natural?

### Expressions of quantity (a2-quantities)
- [ ] "*Un peu de* goes with things you can't count, such as milk or time. It isn't used with things you can count, such as apples."

### The pronoun en (a2-pronoun-en)
- [ ] "The number stays, at the end of the sentence": *J'en ai trois*.
- [ ] *Tu en veux ?*, *Est-ce que tu en veux ?* and *En veux-tu ?* are all correct.

### Pronouns in the passé composé (a2-pronouns-in-passe-compose)
- [ ] The participle agrees with a direct object before the verb: *Je l'ai vue*, *Je les ai vues*, *Il nous a vus*, *Je l'ai prise*, *Je l'ai faite*.
- [ ] No agreement with *lui*, *leur*, *y* or *en*: *Elle lui a parlé*, *J'y ai mangé*, *J'en ai pris trois*.
- [ ] "Agreement is silent for many participles … It's audible for others, where a consonant comes back: *pris* and *prise*, *fait* and *faite*."
- [ ] "*Me*, *te*, *nous* and *vous* can also be indirect objects … Then there's no agreement, even for a woman: *Elle m'a téléphoné*."

### Review: Pronouns in action (a2-review-pronouns)
- [ ] "*Elle l'a prise au marché*" (*la salade*): the participle agrees.

### Travel and transport (a2-travel-and-transport)
- [ ] "*En* for something enclosed … *À* for something you ride on, or on foot": *en bus*, *à moto*, and *à vélo* rather than *en vélo*.
- [ ] "*Le train a du retard*" (with *du*).

### Prepositions: countries, cities and more (a2-prepositions-with-places)
- [ ] "A country ending in -e is usually feminine," with *le Mexique*, *le Cambodge*, *le Mozambique* and *le Zimbabwe* as exceptions.
- [ ] "*À la maison* means 'at home', and *dans la maison* puts more stress on being inside the house."

### The futur simple (a2-futur-simple)
- [ ] "The near future [is] common for plans and things coming soon. The futur simple is common for things further ahead, for promises, for predictions, and in formal writing."
- [ ] "-ai and -ez sound alike."
- [ ] "The endings are [the present of] *avoir*."

### Comparatives (a2-comparatives)
- [ ] "Before a consonant, the s of *plus* is usually silent: *plus grand*. Before a vowel, it links: *plus intéressant*. In *plus de* meaning 'more', the s is usually pronounced. In *ne… plus de*, it's silent."

### Superlatives (a2-superlatives)
- [ ] "[*Le meilleur*] usually goes before its noun." Is *le restaurant le meilleur de Paris* acceptable too?
- [ ] "BAGS adjectives … can stay before it, or they can go after it with the article repeated. Both are correct": *la plus grande ville*, *la ville la plus grande*.

### Home and household chores (a2-home-and-chores)
- [ ] "*Je vais les sortir*" (pronoun before the infinitive). Natural?

### Relative pronouns qui, que, où (a2-relative-qui-que-ou)
- [ ] The participle after *que* agrees with a preceding feminine or plural noun (*écrite*, *prises*, *faite*, *vue*).
- [ ] "Use [*où*] when the clause says what happens in that place or at that time … If the place is the direct object of the next verb, it's still *que*." (*La maison que j'habite* is also grammatical.)

### Review: Comparing and describing (a2-review-comparing)
- [ ] "*La cuisine que j'ai rangée hier*": the participle agrees with *la cuisine*.

### Emotions and feelings (a2-emotions-and-feelings)
- [ ] "*Fier* and *fière* sound the same."
- [ ] "*Fâchée*, *déçue*, *énervée* and *stressée* … sound the same as their masculine forms."

### The imperative with pronouns (a2-imperative-with-pronouns)
- [ ] "Before *y* and *en*, that -s comes back": *Vas-y*, *Manges-en*, *Penses-y*.
- [ ] *Le*, *la* or *les* comes first in a command with two pronouns: *Donne-le-moi*, *Dis-le-moi*.

### Verbs + à / de + infinitive (a2-verbs-with-a-and-de)
- [ ] "*Continuer de* also exists, with the same meaning [as *continuer à*]."
- [ ] "*J'essaie / j'essaye* … Both spellings are correct," for *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils*.

- [ ] "*Tu me manques* works the opposite way round from English. In French, the person you miss is the subject."
