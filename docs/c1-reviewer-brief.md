# Reviewer brief: Parlons, level C1

## What Parlons is

Parlons is a free, text-only website that teaches French to English speakers, from beginner (A1) to advanced (C1). Each lesson explains one point in English, gives French examples with translations, often has a dialogue or a longer original text, and ends with exercises that the site marks automatically. C1 is the advanced level: 57 lessons in 8 units, ending with practice for the skills of the DALF C1.

## The C1 rules, in brief

- Rules and nuance are explained in English; every French example, dialogue line and longer text has an English translation.
- Grammar and vocabulary lessons end with a short summary in plain French, *En résumé*.
- Exercise instructions are in French (*Complétez*, *Reformulez*, *Corrigez*…); feedback is in English.
- Lessons use only words and grammar taught earlier in the course. Anything that isn't standard French is labelled: (literary), (formal), (familiar), (slang) or (spoken).
- The passé simple is produced only in narrative writing; the imperfect subjunctive is for recognition, and is never the only accepted answer.
- Quotations come only from authors who died before 1900, are under 15 words, and are translated by us. Later authors are paraphrased. Longer literary texts, letters, articles and the poem are original, and labelled as such.
- Facts are neutral, with no statistics. Contested history (May 68, the Enlightenment's limits), religion and laïcité, and regional and francophone identity present positions, not verdicts. The DALF practice lessons say nothing about the exam's format, timing or scoring.

## How to review

For each item, and for anything else you notice, check:

1. **Accuracy:** is the French correct (grammar, mood, tense, agreement, spelling)?
2. **Naturalness:** would a native speaker say or write this sentence, in this register?
3. **Translations:** does the English say the same thing as the French?
4. **Register labels:** are "literary", "formal", "familiar", "slang" and "spoken" right?
5. **Answers:** would the site wrongly reject a valid answer, or accept one that changes the meaning?
6. **Facts and culture:** are dates, works, laws and institutions right, and is the tone neutral?
7. **Quotations and verse:** is each quotation word for word, with the right author and work? Does the original poem scan as the lesson says?

**How to reply:** one line per item, `lesson id, item number: OK` or `change to "…"` or `remove`. For example: `c1-passe-simple 2: OK`.

## Where the lessons are

- `docs/review/c1-unit-1.txt` to `c1-unit-8.txt`: every lesson of each unit, in course order, each starting with `===== content/lessons/c1/<file>.mdx =====`. In each lesson, the vocab and exercises come first (`fr` is the French, `answers` the accepted answers), then the lesson text.
- `docs/c1-review-items.md`: every item to check, grouped by unit and by type, numbered within each group (309 in all, duplicates removed). The 22 items marked *(possibly resolved)* quote wording that a later fix changed: glance at the lesson, then answer "OK" if the new wording is fine.
- `docs/c1-wrapup.md`: the editor's cross-lesson audit (links, repeats, quotations, every date, recurring invented names). You don't need to review it, but its quotation and date lists are a quick way to check facts.

## Priorities

1. The **Language** items in Units 1 to 3: the passé simple and passé antérieur, the imperfect subjunctive, stylistic inversion, the ne explétif and literary negation, participle clauses, formal relatives and *quand bien même*. A wrong rule here would mislead every learner.
2. The **Sensitive** items (14): May 68, laïcité and the 1905 law, the limits of the Enlightenment, regional languages and the French-speaking world. Check that each sentence is neutral and accurate.
3. **Quotations and the poem** (Units 1 and 6): fourteen short quotations from authors on the safe list, and the original poem *Lecture du soir* in c1-culture-french-poetry, with its scansion and the elided e at the caesura in lines 7 and 8.
4. The **Facts** in Units 6 and 8: laws and their dates (1905, 1958, 2004, the GDPR), institutions, and the Enlightenment and May 68 chronologies.
5. The **Culture and register** items in Units 7 and 8: proverbs, idioms, regional words, spoken discourse markers and euphemisms.

Thank you: even a partial review, one unit at a time, is very useful.
