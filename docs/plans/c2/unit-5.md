# C2 Unit 5 plan: Writing at length and translating

Status: plan only. Rules: the draft "C2 conventions" in docs/plans/c2/overview.md. **This unit depends on the writing-task decision** (overview, open question 3): its main products are long texts that typed keys can't grade.

## 25. c2-contraction-de-texte: The contraction and the résumé to a word count (communication · harder)

- **Goal:** reduce a text to a given proportion, keeping its logic and its enunciation, in your own words.
- **Recaps:** summarizing an article (b2-summarizing-an-article); the synthesis (c1-synthesis); nominalisation and ellipsis (b2-nominalisation, c2-ellipsis-and-nominal-style).
- **New:** the rules of the exercise (keep the order and the author's voice, no quotation, no personal comment, reformulate), counting words the French way (*c'est-à-dire* and elisions), the margin of tolerance as a rule of thumb, how to compress (nominalise, merge, drop examples).
- **Key vocab (new):** *une contraction*, *le nombre de mots*, *une marge de tolérance*, *condenser*, *l'énonciation*, *respecter le système d'énonciation*.
- **Longer text:** an original 450-word article and a model contraction to about a quarter, with the word count shown.
- **Exercises:** multiple-choice (which sentence keeps the logic?), reorder (the skeleton), reformulation (compress a sentence), error-correction (a personal comment slipped in), a writing task with a word target.
- **Risks:** the counting rules differ between institutions: teach one convention and say so; no claim about any exam's counting rules.

## 26. c2-long-essay: The long essay (communication · harder)

- **Goal:** write a full essay of several pages: introduction, three-part development, transitions and conclusion.
- **Recaps:** the dissertation, its plans and transitions (c1-dissertation); concession and hypothesis (c1-concession-and-hypothesis); the essay introduction (c1-review-ideas).
- **New:** building a paragraph (idea, argument, example, mini-conclusion), managing length across parts, citing ideas without quotations, avoiding padding, the final rereading in three passes (recap of the C1 tip, not repeated as a tip).
- **Key vocab (new):** *un paragraphe argumentatif*, *une sous-partie*, *le remplissage*, *une transition* (recap), *une idée directrice*.
- **Longer text:** an original full essay plan and one fully written part (about 400 words).
- **Exercises:** reorder (paragraph steps), multiple-choice (the best transition), error-correction (padding), reformulation, a writing task (one part).
- **Risks:** the model's length must stay within the C2 longer-text limit: write one part in full and the rest as a detailed plan.

## 27. c2-version: Translating into English (communication · harder)

- **Goal:** translate a French text into natural English, keeping meaning, register and tone.
- **Recaps:** false friends (b2-false-friends, c2-advanced-false-friends); register labels (c1-register-shifting).
- **New:** the method (read, analyse, draft, revise as an English text); traps: tense equivalence (passé composé, imparfait, passé simple), *on*, the historical present, idioms, register, sentence length.
- **Key vocab (new):** *une version*, *un calque*, *un faux sens*, *un contresens* (recap: c1-exam-written-tasks), *un non-sens*, *une omission*, *une surtraduction*.
- **Longer text:** an original French passage (about 300 words) with an annotated English translation.
- **Exercises:** multiple-choice (best English rendering), error-correction (in the English), translate (short sentences only), reorder, a writing task (translate a paragraph, then compare with the model).
- **Risks:** English answers can't be keyed fairly beyond short sentences: long translation is a writing task. Keep the English checker behaviour in mind (lib/check-answer.ts normalizes French accents; English keys need their own variants).

## 28. c2-theme: Translating into French (communication · harder)

- **Goal:** translate an English text into correct, idiomatic French.
- **Recaps:** the whole course; especially precise verbs (c1-precise-verbs), collocations (c1-collocations), anglicisms (c2-anglicisms-and-official-terms).
- **New:** the method; structures English uses and French avoids (the passive, noun stacks, *-ing* forms); choosing tense and register; checking with a dictionary.
- **Key vocab (new):** *un thème*, *un équivalent*, *une tournure idiomatique*, *étoffer*, *un chassé-croisé* (translation technique).
- **Longer text:** an original English passage, a literal (bad) French version and a good one, side by side.
- **Exercises:** translate (sentence level, with every taught variant listed), error-correction (a calque), multiple-choice, reorder, a writing task (a paragraph).
- **Risks:** sentence-level keys need many variants: list every valid answer built from taught material or pin the cue (C1 rule).

## 29. c2-revising-your-style: Revising your own writing (communication · easier)

- **Goal:** edit a text for repetition, heaviness, register breaks and rhythm.
- **Recaps:** the checklist for rereading (c1-exam-written-tasks); register shifting (c1-register-shifting); the stylistic tools of Unit 4.
- **New:** *les lourdeurs* (stacked relatives, noun chains), *les répétitions* and their remedies, *les ruptures de registre*, the *que* and *qui* chain, *il y a* overuse, weak verbs (*faire, mettre, dire*) and their precise replacements (recap: c1-precise-verbs).
- **Key vocab (new):** *une lourdeur*, *alléger une phrase*, *une rupture de registre*, *une phrase bancale*, *fluidifier*.
- **Longer text:** an original heavy paragraph and its revised version, with each change explained.
- **Exercises:** reformulation (lighten a sentence), error-correction, multiple-choice, reorder, a writing task.
- **Risks:** many revisions are matters of taste: keys accept every taught variant, and the explanation says when another choice is also good.

## 30. c2-review-writing: Review: Writing at length (review · easier)

- **New words (3 to 8):** *rédiger au propre*, *un premier jet*, *peaufiner*, *la mise en page*.
- **Model:** one invented text taken through four stages: notes, first draft, revised draft, contraction.
- **Exercises:** one per lesson, plus a writing task.
