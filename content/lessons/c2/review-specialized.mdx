## A few new words

<VocabTable group="new" />

These four words name what the unit has been doing. *Un lexique spécialisé* is the vocabulary of a field: law, medicine, science or administration. When that vocabulary shuts out the reader, people call it *un jargon*, often with a critical tone. To *vulgariser* a text is to explain it to non-specialists, and the activity is *la vulgarisation*.

The trap is the verb. *Vulgariser* has a rare literary sense, to make something vulgar, but for knowledge it means to make it accessible. In that sense, it's a neutral, even positive word, used for science writers, museums and doctors who explain things well.

<Example fr="Chaque métier a son lexique spécialisé, que les autres prennent parfois pour un jargon." en="Every profession has its specialized vocabulary, which others sometimes take for jargon." />

<Example fr="La vulgarisation demande de bien connaître un sujet, pas de le rendre trop simple." en="Popularization requires knowing a subject well, not making it too simple." />

<Example fr="Ce médecin sait vulgariser : ses patients comprennent toujours leur traitement." en="This doctor knows how to explain things simply: the patients always understand their treatment." />

## Four registers, one method

The four lessons of the unit studied four kinds of specialized text. They differ in their words, but they share a method.

- **Who speaks**: a contract speaks of *les parties* and *ledit bailleur*; a leaflet speaks to every patient at once; an article says *nous* for its authors; an administration says *nous* for an institution.
- **The voice**: each register keeps people in the background, with the passive (*peut être résilié*, *sera traitée*), the infinitive (*ne pas dépasser*) or the nominal style (*diagnostic : otite aiguë*, *après examen*).
- **The small words**: *sous réserve de*, *sous peine de*, *à défaut de*, *en cas de*, *le cas échéant*, *à compter de*. They carry the conditions and the deadlines, so they're the first thing to look for.
- **The strength of a claim**: a judgment decides, a leaflet instructs, an article suggests. A plain version keeps that strength: it doesn't turn *est corrélé à* into a cause, or *peut* into *doit*.

## How to vulgarize

Every lesson of the unit ended with the same rules of thumb, applied to its own texts. Put together, they make one checklist:

- **Replace each term by a description**, or by its everyday equivalent: *une gastralgie* becomes *une douleur d'estomac*; *il vous appartient de* becomes *c'est à vous de*.
- **Keep every fact, every condition and every limit**: the notice, the contre-indication, the *ne permettent pas d'établir*.
- **Turn notes into sentences**: the nominal style of a medical note or an administrative decision becomes full sentences with a subject and a verb.
- **Add nothing**: no advice, no reassurance, no opinion, no figure.
- **Choose one reader**, and the register that suits them: *tu* for a friend, *vous* for a patient or a client.

## The situation

The three texts below tell one invented situation. Every fact appears in all three.

- **The letter**: on 10 April, the town hall of Valbrune asked Inès Ferrand for two supporting documents for her application for aid, within a month.
- **The illness**: after receiving it, she had acute appendicitis and an appendectomy.
- **The consequence**: her doctor prescribed rest, so she can't gather the documents in time.
- **The rule**: the aid's rules allow the deadline to be extended if a medical certificate is presented.
- **The request**: she asks for an extension, and sends a medical certificate stating that her health requires the rest.

The town hall, the aid, its rules and the medical facts are all invented. The texts give no medical or legal advice.

## The model: the legal register

The first text is written in the style of a formal legal request.

*Par courrier du 10 avril, la mairie de Valbrune a demandé à Mme Inès Ferrand, dans le cadre de sa demande d'aide, de lui faire parvenir deux pièces justificatives dans un délai d'un mois à compter de la date dudit courrier. Postérieurement à sa réception, l'intéressée a présenté une appendicite aiguë ayant nécessité une appendicectomie. Le repos prescrit par son médecin la met dans l'impossibilité de réunir lesdites pièces dans le délai imparti.*

*L'article 4 du règlement de l'aide dispose que ce délai peut être prolongé sur présentation d'un certificat médical. En conséquence, l'intéressée sollicite la prolongation du délai et joint à la présente un certificat médical. Ledit certificat atteste que l'état de santé de l'intéressée justifie le repos prescrit.*

(By letter of 10 April, the town hall of Valbrune asked Mrs Inès Ferrand, as part of her application for aid, to send it two supporting documents within a month from the date of the said letter. After receiving it, the person concerned had acute appendicitis requiring an appendectomy. The rest prescribed by her doctor makes it impossible for her to gather the said documents within the time allowed. Article 4 of the aid's rules provides that this deadline may be extended on presentation of a medical certificate. Consequently, the person concerned requests an extension of the deadline and encloses a medical certificate herewith. The said certificate states that the state of health of the person concerned justifies the prescribed rest.)

## The model: the medical register

The second text is a consultation note, in the nominal style of medical French.

*Compte rendu de consultation (document inventé). Patiente : Mme Inès Ferrand. Motif : demande de certificat médical. Contexte : courrier de la mairie du 10 avril, concernant sa demande d'aide, demandant deux pièces justificatives dans un délai d'un mois.*

*Antécédents récents : appendicite aiguë, survenue après la réception de ce courrier, traitée par appendicectomie. Repos prescrit ; patiente dans l'impossibilité de se déplacer pour réunir les pièces dans le délai. Le règlement de l'aide prévoyant une prolongation du délai sur certificat médical, certificat remis ce jour à la patiente pour sa demande de prolongation. Le certificat atteste que l'état de santé de la patiente justifie le repos prescrit.*

(Consultation summary (invented document). Patient: Mrs Inès Ferrand. Reason: request for a medical certificate. Context: a letter from the town hall dated 10 April, about her application for aid, asking for two supporting documents within a month. Recent history: acute appendicitis, which occurred after receipt of this letter, treated by appendectomy. Rest prescribed; patient unable to go out to gather the documents within the deadline. As the aid's rules provide for an extension of the deadline on a medical certificate, certificate handed to the patient today for her request for an extension. The certificate states that the patient's state of health justifies the prescribed rest.)

## The model: the plain register

The third text is a message from Inès to her friend Hugo. They use *tu*.

*Tu te souviens du courrier de la mairie du 10 avril, pour ma demande d'aide ? Ils me demandaient deux documents, et j'avais un mois pour les envoyer. Juste après l'avoir reçu, j'ai eu une appendicite aiguë, et on m'a fait une appendicectomie, c'est-à-dire qu'on m'a retiré l'appendice.*

*Mon médecin m'a dit de me reposer, donc je ne peux pas sortir chercher les documents à temps. Heureusement, le règlement de l'aide dit qu'on peut avoir plus de temps si on présente un certificat médical. Mon médecin m'en a donné un, qui explique que mon état de santé m'oblige à me reposer, et je l'envoie à la mairie pour demander plus de temps.*

(Do you remember the town hall's letter of 10 April, for my application for aid? They were asking me for two documents, and I had a month to send them. Just after I got it, I had acute appendicitis, and I had an appendectomy, which means they removed my appendix. My doctor told me to rest, so I can't go out and get the documents in time. Luckily, the aid's rules say you can get more time if you present a medical certificate. My doctor gave me one, which explains that my state of health means I have to rest, and I'm sending it to the town hall to ask for more time.)

## The same facts in three registers

| Fact | Legal | Medical | Plain |
|---|---|---|---|
| The letter | *par courrier du 10 avril… dans un délai d'un mois à compter de la date dudit courrier* | *Contexte : courrier de la mairie du 10 avril… dans un délai d'un mois* | *le courrier de la mairie du 10 avril… j'avais un mois* |
| The illness | *postérieurement à sa réception… une appendicite aiguë ayant nécessité une appendicectomie* | *appendicite aiguë, survenue après la réception de ce courrier, traitée par appendicectomie* | *juste après l'avoir reçu, j'ai eu une appendicite aiguë… on m'a retiré l'appendice* |
| The consequence | *le repos prescrit… la met dans l'impossibilité de réunir lesdites pièces* | *Repos prescrit ; patiente dans l'impossibilité de se déplacer* | *mon médecin m'a dit de me reposer, donc je ne peux pas sortir* |
| The rule | *l'article 4 du règlement… dispose que ce délai peut être prolongé* | *le règlement de l'aide prévoyant une prolongation* | *le règlement de l'aide dit qu'on peut avoir plus de temps* |
| The request | *sollicite la prolongation du délai et joint… un certificat médical. Ledit certificat atteste que…* | *certificat remis ce jour… Le certificat atteste que…* | *mon médecin m'en a donné un, qui explique que… je l'envoie à la mairie* |

Each register keeps the condition of the rule: *sur présentation d'un certificat médical*, *sur certificat médical*, *si on présente un certificat médical*. The plain version also explains the one term it keeps, *une appendicectomie*, with *c'est-à-dire*. Only the legal register names the article of the rules; the other two refer to the rules without a number, which leaves out a detail but adds nothing.

## Your turn

The writing task in the exercises gives you a short consultation summary in medical French. Vulgarize it for a friend: replace every term, keep every fact and the condition for coming back, write full sentences, and add nothing. Then check your text against the five-line checklist from "How to vulgarize".

<Callout type="warning" title="Vulgariser is the usual word for knowledge">
  English speakers often avoid *vulgariser* because it looks like *vulgar*, and use *populariser* in its place. For specialized knowledge explained to the public, the usual word is **vulgariser**: **ce médecin sait vulgariser**. *Populariser* means to make something popular, which is another idea.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Check a plain version fact by fact">
  When you've rewritten a specialized text, make a small table like the one above: one row per fact, one column per version. An empty cell shows a lost fact at once, and a cell with more than the original shows something you've added.
</Callout>
