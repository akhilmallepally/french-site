## Pastiche, parody, plagiarism

<VocabTable group="kinds" />

Three words are often confused. Their real test is what the writer does with the model, and whether they say so.

- **Un pastiche** imitates a style: its words, its syntax, its rhythm. It's written *à la manière de* (in the style of) an author or a genre, and says so. It can be a game or a way to *rendre hommage à* (pay tribute to) a writer.
- **Une parodie** imitates in order to mock: it *parodie* (parodies) its model by exaggerating its features until they become funny.
- **Le plagiat** (plagiarism) is something else: it copies another's text and presents it as one's own, without saying so. To *plagier* is dishonest, whether the copy is long or short.

A pastiche or a parody that names its model is never plagiarism, however close it comes.

Two famous books show how far the game can go. In *Pastiches et mélanges* (1919), Proust included pastiches of other writers. In *Exercices de style* (1947), Queneau told one tiny incident again and again, each time in a different style: *un exercice de style* (a stylistic exercise) taken to its limit. This lesson describes both books without quoting them.

<Example fr="Un pastiche imite le style d'un auteur ; une parodie s'en moque ; le plagiat copie sans le dire." en="A pastiche imitates an author's style; a parody mocks it; plagiarism copies without saying so." />

## Finding the markers of a style

<VocabTable group="markers" />

To imitate a style, first list its *marques de style* (stylistic markers): what makes a text recognizable before you know who wrote it. Look at four things:

- **Vocabulary**: the words a style likes (*il appartient à*, *a-t-on appris*).
- **Syntax**: long or short sentences, impersonal turns, inversions.
- **Rhythm**: the length of the paragraphs, *la concision* (brevity), repetitions.
- **Tense and voice**: the passé simple of a tale, the passé composé and the conditionnel de prudence of a wire, the passive of a notice.

Each style also has its *tics d'écriture* (habits, mannerisms): a formula that comes back, a way of opening or closing. A pastiche reproduces them with a light hand. A parody *force le trait* (exaggerates them) until the reader laughs.

## The fable à la manière de La Fontaine

La Fontaine, who died in 1695, is on the safe list, so his fables can be quoted. His *Le Lièvre et la Tortue* opens with its moral, before the story:

<Example fr="« Rien ne sert de courir ; il faut partir à point » (La Fontaine, Fables, « Le Lièvre et la Tortue »)" en="&quot;There's no point running; you have to set off at the right time.&quot; (our translation)" />

A prose pastiche of a fable keeps a few of his *traits de style* (features of style): animals that act like people, written with a capital letter (*le Chat*); a short story with a sudden turn; a moment of dialogue; the passé simple for the events; and a moral in one line, here at the end. The pastiche in this lesson is in prose, so it makes no claim about verse or metre.

## The dépêche and the notice

The other two pastiches imitate genres rather than an author. The dépêche gives the fact first, then the place and the day, and *cite* (quotes) its source in an inverted incise (*a-t-on appris auprès de la mairie*); it uses the conditionnel de prudence for what it hasn't checked, and ends with a formula such as *aucun blessé n'est à déplorer*. The notice speaks impersonally (*il est porté à la connaissance de*), uses the passive, and ends with a reminder of everyone's duties.

## Imitating honestly

<VocabTable group="ethics" />

A pastiche is honest when it *signale* (points out) what it imitates. As a rule of thumb:

- **Label it**: *à la manière de…*, *pastiche écrit pour…*
- **Don't copy sentences**: imitate the markers, write your own sentences. A copied line must be quoted, in quotation marks, with its source.
- ***Citez vos sources*** (name your sources) whenever you borrow a fact or a phrase.
- **Keep the facts** when you tell the same event in several styles: only the style changes.

<Example fr="Une imitation honnête signale son modèle et cite ses sources." en="An honest imitation points out its model and names its sources." />

## The model: one event, three styles

The texts below tell the same invented event, an afternoon at the primary school of Valbrune. The first three are original pastiches, written for the lesson; the fourth is a short parody of the third.

*À la manière d'une fable de La Fontaine (en prose). Un Chat, monté sur le toit de l'école, ne savait plus descendre. Tout l'après-midi, les Enfants criaient, les Parents criaient, et le Maire criait plus fort que tous. « Descends ! » lui criait-on. « Pourquoi descendre, pensa le Chat, puisque tout le monde crie en bas ? » Et, effrayé par tant de bruit, il montait toujours plus haut. Survint une vieille Dame, qui ne dit rien. Elle posa une assiette de lait au pied du mur, et s'assit. Le Chat la regarda longtemps, puis il descendit. Rien ne sert de crier ; il faut savoir se taire.*

*À la manière d'une dépêche. VALBRUNE, 14 mai (agence inventée). Un chat est resté bloqué, mardi après-midi, sur le toit de l'école primaire de Valbrune, a-t-on appris auprès de la mairie. Selon des témoins, de nombreux parents et le maire se trouvaient devant le bâtiment. L'animal serait redescendu en fin d'après-midi, après qu'une habitante a déposé une assiette de lait au pied du mur. L'incident a duré tout l'après-midi, selon les mêmes témoins. Aucun blessé n'est à déplorer.*

*À la manière d'un avis administratif. Mairie de Valbrune. Avis aux parents d'élèves. Objet : incident du mardi 14 mai. Il est porté à la connaissance des familles qu'un chat s'est trouvé bloqué sur le toit de l'école primaire au cours de l'après-midi du mardi 14 mai. L'animal a regagné le sol en fin d'après-midi, après qu'une habitante a déposé de la nourriture au pied du bâtiment. Il est rappelé qu'il appartient à chacun de ne pas se rassembler devant l'établissement en cas d'incident. Les services de la mairie remercient les familles de leur compréhension.*

*À la manière d'une parodie de l'avis. Il est porté à la connaissance des familles qu'il est rappelé qu'un chat s'est trouvé, au cours de l'après-midi, en situation de présence non autorisée sur le toit de l'établissement. Il est rappelé, en outre, qu'il est rappelé qu'il appartient à chacun de ne pas se rassembler, ni de crier, ni de penser trop fort.*

(In the manner of a fable by La Fontaine (in prose). A Cat, who had climbed onto the school roof, no longer knew how to get down. All afternoon, the Children shouted, the Parents shouted, and the Mayor shouted louder than all of them. "Come down!" they shouted at him. "Why come down," thought the Cat, "when everyone is shouting down there?" And, frightened by so much noise, he kept climbing higher. Along came an old Lady, who said nothing. She put a plate of milk at the foot of the wall, and sat down. The Cat looked at her for a long time, then came down. There's no point shouting; you have to know how to keep quiet. / In the manner of a wire. VALBRUNE, 14 May (invented agency). A cat was stuck on Tuesday afternoon on the roof of the Valbrune primary school, the town hall said. According to witnesses, many parents and the mayor were in front of the building. The animal reportedly came back down late in the afternoon, after a local woman put a plate of milk at the foot of the wall. The incident lasted all afternoon, according to the same witnesses. No one was injured. / In the manner of an administrative notice. Valbrune town hall. Notice to parents. Subject: incident of Tuesday 14 May. Families are hereby informed that a cat was stuck on the roof of the primary school during the afternoon of Tuesday 14 May. The animal got back to the ground late in the afternoon, after a local woman put food at the foot of the building. Everyone is reminded that they must not gather in front of the school in case of an incident. The town hall's services thank families for their understanding. / In the manner of a parody of the notice. Families are hereby informed that it is recalled that a cat found itself, during the afternoon, in a situation of unauthorized presence on the roof of the school. It is recalled, moreover, that it is recalled that everyone must not gather, nor shout, nor think too loudly.)

## How the pastiches work

- **The same facts** in all three: a cat stuck on the school roof, in the afternoon, a crowd with the mayor, a woman who puts down food (a plate of milk), the cat coming down. The fable leaves out the date and the notice says *de la nourriture* rather than *du lait*, but none adds a fact.
- **The fable**: capitals for the characters, a moment of direct speech (*« Descends ! »*, then the Cat's thought), the passé simple for the events (*survint*, *posa*, *descendit*), a repetition (*criaient… criaient… criait*), a sudden turn and a moral that echoes La Fontaine's line without copying it.
- **The dépêche**: the fact and the place first, the source in an inverted incise, *selon des témoins*, the conditionnel de prudence (*serait redescendu*) and the closing formula.
- **The notice**: *objet*, an impersonal opening, the passive (*s'est trouvé bloqué*), no named person, and a reminder of duties (*il appartient à chacun de*).
- **The parody** forces the trait of the notice: *il est rappelé que* doubled, a heavy noun phrase (*en situation de présence non autorisée*), and a final list that turns absurd (*ni de penser trop fort*). It adds no fact; it only makes the style laugh at itself.
- **The labels**: each text says what it imitates, so none of them could pass for the original.

<Callout type="warning" title="A pastiche isn't a copy">
  When they write a pastiche, learners sometimes reuse whole lines of the model with one word changed: *Rien ne sert de courir ; il faut partir à point* becomes *Rien ne sert de courir ; il faut partir tôt*. That's a near copy, not an imitation. Write your own sentence with the same markers, **Rien ne sert de crier ; il faut savoir se taire**, and quote the original only in quotation marks, with its source.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Write one fact in three styles">
  Take one ordinary event from your week and write it three times, in three short paragraphs: as a tale, as a news wire and as an official notice. Then underline, in each one, the two markers that make it recognizable.
</Callout>
