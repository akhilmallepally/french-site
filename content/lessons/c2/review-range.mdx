## A few new words

<VocabTable group="new" />

These words name what the unit has been training: range. *La palette* of a writer is the set of registers and styles they can use, and a wide palette lets them *varier* (vary) their writing for each reader. *La justesse* is the quality of the right word or the right tone: not the rarest word, but the one that fits exactly. To *trouver le ton juste* (find the right tone) is the aim of every text in this unit. You know *l'aisance* (ease) from <LessonLink id="b2-review-final" text="the final B2 review" />: range is what makes it possible at C2.

## One event, five ways

The texts below tell one invented event: the two benches decided by the town council, in the column of the lesson on register breaks, have been installed on place du Marché. The same facts appear in each:

- **The event**: this Monday, two benches were installed on place du Marché.
- **The decision**: the town council had decided it, after a four-hour meeting.
- **What's still to come**: the rest of the project will be examined at another meeting.

Each text keeps these three facts and changes only its register or its style. The familiar text is labelled as such; the fable and the dépêche are original pastiches, written for the lesson.

## Range across the level

Range isn't only a matter of this unit. Each unit of C2 added to the palette: Unit 1 the fine points of form, Unit 2 the exact word, Unit 3 the registers of law, medicine, science and administration, Unit 4 the reading of what a text implies, Unit 5 the long forms of writing, Unit 6 the voices of mediation and negotiation, and Unit 7 the history and the literatures of the language. This unit asks you to choose among all of them, for one reader at a time.

A wide palette doesn't mean using every register in one text. It means knowing which one the situation calls for, and being able to write it without effort.

## Checking a text for its register

When a text is written, check it in three passes:

- **The words**: is there a word from another register, and is it signalled if it's meant?
- **The sentences**: do their length and structure suit the register (short and direct for a message, complete and connected for an official text)?
- **The facts**: if the text is a new version of another one, does it keep every fact and add none?

## Choosing the register

As a rule of thumb, choose by the reader and the situation, then keep the choice for the whole text:

- **Familiar**, for a friend, in a message: short sentences, *on* for *nous*, familiar words such as *galérer*.
- **Everyday**, for the general public: complete sentences, the passive where the agent doesn't matter, no image that needs explaining.
- **Formal**, for an official text: impersonal turns (*c'est avec… que*), nouns rather than verbs, no familiar word unless it's quoted and signalled.

A style adds its own markers on top of a register: the capitals and the moral of a fable, the inverted incise and the closing formula of a dépêche.

## The model: one event in three registers and two styles

*Registre familier (message à une amie). Tu sais quoi ? Ils ont enfin mis les deux bancs sur la place du Marché, ce lundi. Quatre heures de réunion au conseil municipal pour ça, on a bien galéré ! Et pour le reste du projet, il faudra encore une autre réunion. Bon, en attendant, on pourra au moins s'asseoir ! Tu viendras les essayer avec moi ?*

*Registre courant (site d'information locale). Deux bancs ont été installés ce lundi sur la place du Marché. Le conseil municipal en avait décidé ainsi, au terme d'une séance de quatre heures. Le reste du projet d'aménagement de la place sera examiné lors d'une prochaine séance. Les habitants peuvent dès maintenant profiter des bancs.*

*Registre soutenu (communiqué de la mairie). C'est avec satisfaction que la municipalité annonce l'installation, ce lundi, de deux bancs sur la place du Marché, conformément à la décision du conseil municipal prise au terme d'une séance de quatre heures. Les autres parties du projet feront l'objet d'un examen lors d'une prochaine séance. La municipalité invite les habitants à profiter de ces nouveaux équipements.*

*À la manière d'une fable (en prose). Le Conseil, ayant parlé quatre heures, accoucha de deux Bancs. On les posa ce lundi sur la place du Marché. Les Passants, qui n'en demandaient pas tant, s'y assirent, et attendirent la suite, qui devait venir à une autre séance. Qui parle longtemps n'agit pas toujours beaucoup ; mais un banc, même petit, vaut mieux qu'un long discours.*

*À la manière d'une dépêche. VALBRUNE, lundi (agence inventée). Deux bancs ont été installés lundi sur la place du Marché, a-t-on appris auprès de la mairie. Le conseil municipal en avait décidé ainsi après une séance de quatre heures. Le reste du projet doit être examiné, selon la même source, lors d'une prochaine séance, dont la date n'a pas été précisée.*

(Familiar register (a message to a friend). Guess what? They've finally put the two benches on place du Marché, this Monday. Four hours of council meeting for that, we really had a hard time of it! And for the rest of the project, there'll have to be yet another meeting. Well, in the meantime, at least we'll be able to sit down! Will you come and try them with me? / Everyday register (a local news site). Two benches were installed this Monday on place du Marché. The town council had decided so, at the end of a four-hour meeting. The rest of the plan for redeveloping the square will be examined at a later meeting. Residents can already make use of the benches. / Formal register (a town hall press release). It is with satisfaction that the town authorities announce the installation, this Monday, of two benches on place du Marché, in accordance with the town council's decision taken at the end of a four-hour meeting. The other aspects of the plan will be examined at a later meeting. The town authorities invite residents to make use of this new equipment. / In the manner of a fable (in prose). The Council, having spoken for four hours, gave birth to two Benches. They were set down this Monday on place du Marché. The Passers-by, who hadn't asked for so much, sat on them, and waited for what came next, which was to come at another meeting. Those who talk for long don't always do much; but a bench, even a small one, is worth more than a long speech. / In the manner of a wire. VALBRUNE, Monday (invented agency). Two benches were installed on Monday on place du Marché, the town hall said. The town council had decided so after a four-hour meeting. The rest of the plan is to be examined, according to the same source, at a later meeting, whose date has not been specified.)

## How the five texts work

- **The same three facts** in each: the two benches installed this Monday on place du Marché, the council's decision after four hours, and the rest of the plan still to be examined.
- **The familiar message**: *tu sais quoi ?*, *ils ont mis*, *on a bien galéré*, an exclamation.
- **The everyday text**: the passive (*ont été installés*), *au terme de*, no image.
- **The formal release**: *c'est avec satisfaction que*, nouns (*l'installation*, *un examen*), *conformément à*, *faire l'objet de*.
- **The fable**: capitals, the passé simple (*accoucha*, *posa*, *s'assirent*), a moral that plays on a well-known contrast between talking and acting.
- **The dépêche**: the fact first, *a-t-on appris auprès de*, and a final detail on what isn't known.

The dépêche's last detail (*dont la date n'a pas été précisée*), the familiar *on a bien galéré* and the closing question, and the release's invitation to residents add comments, not facts: none of the five texts adds an event.

<Callout type="warning" title="A register is a choice for the whole text">
  When they write in a register they don't use often, learners slip back halfway through: *C'est avec satisfaction que la municipalité annonce l'installation des bancs, et franchement, c'est top.* Choose the register at the start and keep it: **C'est avec satisfaction que la municipalité annonce l'installation de deux bancs.** If you want a break, signal it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Write the facts first, then the registers">
  Before you write a text in several registers or styles, list its facts in three or four short lines, in neutral French. Then write each version from that list, not from the previous version. You'll change the words without losing or adding a fact.
</Callout>
