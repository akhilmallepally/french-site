By the end of this lesson you'll be able to recognize the main senses of nine common words with several meanings, and to let the context tell you which one is meant.

## What you already know

You've met several words with more than one sense already. You know *une pièce* as a room, from <LessonLink id="a2-home-and-chores" text="the lesson on the home" />, as a coin, from <LessonLink id="b1-ne-que-ni-ni" text="the lesson on ne… que and ni… ni" />, and as a play (*la pièce de théâtre*), from <LessonLink id="c1-dislocation-and-fronting" text="the lesson on dislocation" />. You know *une note* as a mark, from <LessonLink id="a2-culture-french-school-system" text="the lesson on the school system" />. You know *le cadre* and *la portée* from <LessonLink id="c1-abstract-nouns" text="the lesson on abstract nouns" />, and *relever un défi* and *tenir compte de* from <LessonLink id="c1-collocations" text="the lesson on collocations" />. This lesson adds the senses a C2 reader meets in formal French.

## What polysemy is

<VocabTable group="terms" />

The term *la polysémie* (polysemy) is the fact that one word has several related senses. *Un mot polysémique* (a polysemous word) isn't two words that happen to look alike, like the paronyms of <LessonLink id="c2-paronyms" text="the last lesson" />. It's one word whose meaning has spread: from a part of a house to a part of a machine, from a mark on paper to a bill.

Each sense is an *acception*, as you saw in the last lesson. Dictionaries usually start with *le sens propre* (the literal sense) and then give *le sens figuré* (the figurative sense): *un coup* is first a blow, and then a quick action (*un coup d'œil*), an attempt (*du premier coup*) or some help (*un coup de main*).

<Example fr="Au sens propre, un coup fait mal ; au sens figuré, un coup de main fait du bien." en="In the literal sense, a blow hurts; in the figurative sense, a hand (some help) does you good." />

## Let the context choose

A polysemous word rarely leaves you in doubt, because its partners choose the sense. Three clues do most of the work.

- **The words around it.** The phrase *payer la note* is a bill; *une bonne note* is a mark; *une note de musique* is a sound.
- **The construction.** The verb in *relever une erreur* (with a direct object) means to point it out; *relever de* (with *de*) means to fall under.
- **The situation.** In a form, *une pièce* is a document; in a flat, it's a room; in a garage, it's a part.

When you read, check the partner before you choose the sense. When a sentence still allows two senses, rewrite it. *La note est trop élevée* could be about a mark or a bill; *la note de l'hôtel est trop élevée* leaves no doubt.

<Example fr="La note de l'hôtel est plus élevée que prévu." en="The hotel bill is higher than expected." />

When you write, give the reader a partner that makes the sense clear.

## Pièce and note

<VocabTable group="piece" />

In administrative French, *une pièce* is a document, and *une pièce justificative* is one that proves what you say: a bill, a certificate, a payslip. In a garage or a workshop, *une pièce détachée* is a spare part.

<Example fr="Votre demande n'est pas complète : il manque une pièce justificative." en="Your application is incomplete: a supporting document is missing." />

<Example fr="Le garage attend une pièce détachée pour réparer la voiture." en="The garage is waiting for a spare part to repair the car." />

At a hotel or in a restaurant, *la note* is the bill. In music, *une note* is a sound. The bill is the sense learners miss most often, because English uses "note" for neither.

<Example fr="Avant de partir, n'oubliez pas de payer la note." en="Before you leave, don't forget to pay the bill." />

## Coup

<VocabTable group="coup" />

The term *un coup* starts as a blow, and most of its other senses keep the idea of something quick or sudden. *Jeter un coup d'œil* is to have a quick look; *réussir du premier coup* is to succeed at the first attempt; *donner un coup de main* is to give someone a hand. You already know *ça vaut le coup* (it's worth it), from B1.

<Example fr="Elle a trouvé la solution du premier coup." en="She found the solution at the first attempt." />

The phrase *un coup de main* is informal: in a formal letter, write *une aide*.

## Matière and valeur

<VocabTable group="matiere" />

The term *la matière* is first what something is made of, and then a subject at school (*les mathématiques sont sa matière préférée*). *En matière de* introduces a field, in formal writing: *en matière de logement*, *en matière de santé*.

The term *la valeur* is what something is worth (*la valeur d'une maison*). In the plural, *les valeurs* are also the principles a person or a group believes in. *Mettre en valeur* means to show something at its best.

<Example fr="En matière de logement, la loi a beaucoup changé." en="As regards housing, the law has changed a lot. (formal)" />

<Example fr="Cette entreprise met en valeur le travail de ses employés." en="This company showcases its employees' work." />

## Titre

<VocabTable group="titre" />

You know *le titre* of a book or an article. *À juste titre* means "rightly, with good reason", and it usually follows the verb or closes the sentence. *Un titre de transport* is the formal word for a ticket or a pass, the one you see on signs in stations.

<Example fr="Les habitants se plaignent, à juste titre, du bruit." en="The residents complain, rightly, about the noise." />

## Relever, soutenir and se tenir

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

The word *relever* has many senses. With a direct object, *relever une erreur* is to notice it and point it out. With *de*, *relever de* means "to fall under, to be a matter for": *cette question relève du ministère*.

The word *soutenir* means to support a person or a project. It's the verb English speakers need when they think of "to support": *supporter*, as you saw in <LessonLink id="b2-false-friends" text="the lesson on false friends" />, means to put up with. At a university, *soutenir une thèse* is to defend a thesis in front of a jury.

The phrase *se tenir* is the formal verb for a meeting or an event that takes place: *la réunion va se tenir à Lyon*. The future works like *tenir*.

<Example fr="Plusieurs associations ont soutenu le projet de nouvelle bibliothèque." en="Several associations have supported the plan for a new library." />

<Example fr="La conférence va se tenir en mars, à la mairie." en="The conference is going to be held in March, at the town hall. (formal)" />

## The whole list

- The noun *une pièce*: a room, a coin, a play; *une pièce justificative* (a document), *une pièce détachée* (a spare part).
- The noun *la note*: a mark; *payer la note* (the bill); *une note de musique*.
- The noun *un coup*: a blow; *un coup d'œil*, *du premier coup*, *un coup de main* (informal).
- The nouns *la matière* (a subject, a material); *en matière de* (formal). *La valeur*, *les valeurs*, *mettre en valeur*.
- The phrases *à juste titre* (rightly); *un titre de transport* (a ticket, formal).
- The verbs *relever une erreur* (to point out), *relever de* (to fall under); *soutenir* (to support), *soutenir une thèse*; *se tenir* (to take place, formal).

## En français

Sam, an exchange student, is filling in a form for his university. His flatmate Chloé helps him. They use *tu*.

- *Chloé, la secrétaire veut une pièce de plus pour mon dossier. Je ne comprends pas : j'ai déjà une chambre !* (Chloé, the secretary wants one more pièce for my file. I don't understand: I already have a room!)
- *Ici, une pièce, c'est un document : une pièce justificative, comme une facture.* (Here, a pièce is a document: a supporting document, like a bill.)
- *Ah ! Elle a aussi relevé une erreur dans ma demande. Elle l'a corrigée ?* (Oh! She also relevé a mistake in my application. Did she correct it?)
- *Non, relever une erreur, c'est la remarquer et la signaler. C'est à toi de la corriger.* (No, relever a mistake is to notice it and point it out. It's up to you to correct it.)
- *D'accord. Elle m'a dit aussi que ma demande relevait du service des étudiants étrangers.* (OK. She also told me that my application relevait du international students' office.)
- *Ça veut dire que c'est ce service qui doit s'en occuper.* (That means it's that office that has to deal with it.)
- *Et hier, au restaurant, le serveur m'a apporté la note. J'ai pensé qu'il me donnait une note pour le repas !* (And yesterday, at the restaurant, the waiter brought me the note. I thought he was giving me a mark for the meal!)
- *Presque : la note, c'est ce que tu dois payer.* (Almost: the note is what you have to pay.)
- *Je comprends mieux. Tu peux me donner un coup de main pour le formulaire ?* (That's clearer. Can you give me a hand with the form?)
- *Bien sûr. Je jette un coup d'œil à tes papiers ce soir.* (Of course. I'll have a quick look at your papers this evening.)
- *Un coup d'œil, un coup de main… Personne ne va me frapper, j'espère ?* (A coup d'œil, a coup de main… Nobody's going to hit me, I hope?)
- *Mais non ! Au sens figuré, un coup, c'est souvent quelque chose de rapide.* (Of course not! In the figurative sense, a coup is often something quick.)
- *Avec la polysémie, je dois regarder chaque mot avec ses voisins.* (With polysemy, I have to look at each word with its neighbours.)
- *Exactement, et en matière de vocabulaire, c'est la meilleure méthode.* (Exactly, and as regards vocabulary, it's the best method.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Soutenir un projet, not supporter un projet">
  English speakers often write *je supporte ce projet* for "I support this project". In French, *supporter* means to put up with, so the sentence sounds as if you're only tolerating it. Use *soutenir*: **je soutiens ce projet**, **plusieurs associations soutiennent la candidature**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Give each word a sense map">
  For each polysemous word you meet, keep one page with the word in the middle and one branch per sense, each with a short sentence: *une pièce* → room, coin, play, document, part. When you meet a new sense, add a branch rather than a new page. After a few weeks, you see how the senses connect, and new ones are easier to guess.
</Callout>

## En résumé

La polysémie est le fait qu'un seul mot a plusieurs sens. Un mot polysémique a souvent un sens propre et un ou plusieurs sens figurés. Le contexte choisit le sens : les mots qui l'entourent, la construction et la situation. Dans un dossier, une pièce justificative est un document qui prouve ce qu'on dit, et dans un garage, une pièce détachée est une partie d'une machine. Au restaurant, on paie la note, mais en musique, on joue une note de musique. Un coup d'œil est un regard rapide, du premier coup veut dire dès le premier essai, et un coup de main est une aide familière. En matière de santé veut dire quand il s'agit de santé. Les valeurs sont aussi des principes, et mettre en valeur veut dire montrer sous son meilleur jour. À juste titre veut dire avec raison. On relève une erreur, une question relève d'un service, on soutient un projet ou une thèse, et une réunion se tient quelque part.
