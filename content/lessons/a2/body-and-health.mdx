You've already met *les yeux* and *les cheveux* in <LessonLink id="a1-describing-people" text="the lesson on describing people" />. *Le dos* and *le bras* end in a silent consonant, and they look the same in the plural: *les bras*. *Le genou* takes an -x: *les genoux*.

*Le ventre* is the general word for your stomach or belly. *L'estomac* is the organ itself.

## Saying what hurts

French doesn't say "my back hurts". It says "I have pain at the back", using **avoir mal à**. *Avoir* comes from <LessonLink id="a1-avoir-present" text="the lesson on avoir" />, and *à* joins the article exactly as you learned in <LessonLink id="a1-contractions-a-de" text="the lesson on à and de with articles" />.

| Article | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| le | au | *J'ai mal au dos.* (My back hurts.) |
| la | à la | *J'ai mal à la tête.* (My head hurts.) |
| l' | à l' | *J'ai mal à l'estomac.* (My stomach hurts.) |
| les | aux | *J'ai mal aux dents.* (My teeth hurt.) |

Only *avoir* changes with the subject.

<Example fr="Elle a mal à la gorge." en="She has a sore throat." />
<Example fr="Tu as mal au ventre ?" en="Does your stomach hurt?" />
<Example fr="Nous avons mal aux jambes." en="Our legs hurt." />
<Example fr="Il a mal à l'oreille." en="His ear hurts." />

## Being ill

Here are the words for everyday illnesses.

<VocabTable group="illness" />

Notice which verb goes with which word. You **have** a cold, the flu or a temperature, but you **are** ill.

- *J'ai un rhume.* (I have a cold.)
- *J'ai la grippe.* (I have the flu.)
- *J'ai de la fièvre.* (I've got a temperature.) Learn this one as a whole phrase.
- *Je suis malade.* (I'm ill.)
- *Je suis enrhumé.* (I have a cold.) A woman writes *enrhumée*.

<Example fr="Mon frère tousse et il a de la fièvre." en="My brother is coughing and he has a temperature." />
<Example fr="J'ai un mal de tête aujourd'hui." en="I have a headache today." />

Saying *J'ai un mal de tête* is correct, but *J'ai mal à la tête* is the more common way of saying it.

## En français

- *Ça va ?* (Are you OK?)
- *Non, je suis malade. J'ai mal à la tête et à la gorge.* (No, I'm ill. My head and throat hurt.)
- *Tu as de la fièvre ?* (Do you have a temperature?)
- *Oui, et je tousse. J'ai la grippe.* (Yes, and I'm coughing. I have the flu.)

<Callout type="warning" title="No 'my' with mal à">
  English says "my head" and "my teeth", so it's natural to write *J'ai mal à ma tête*. French uses the plain article, because *j'ai* already shows whose head it is: **J'ai mal à la tête**, **J'ai mal aux dents**. It's the same idea as *Je me brosse les dents* in the routine you've already learned.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Mal au cœur isn't your heart">
  *J'ai mal au cœur* doesn't mean your heart hurts. It means you feel sick, as if you might throw up. If you actually have chest pain, say *J'ai mal à la poitrine* (My chest hurts), so that nobody misunderstands you.
</Callout>
