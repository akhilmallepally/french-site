By the end of this lesson you'll be able to use all the pronouns in this unit in the past, and make the participle agree when it needs to.

## Before the auxiliary

In the passé composé, every pronoun goes directly **before the auxiliary**, the form of *avoir* or *être*. That goes for all the pronouns you've met, including <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the pronoun en" /> and <LessonLink id="a2-indirect-object-pronouns" text="the indirect object pronouns" />.

<Example fr="Je l'ai vu." en="I saw him." />
<Example fr="Elle lui a parlé." en="She spoke to him." />
<Example fr="Nous y sommes allés." en="We went there." />
<Example fr="J'en ai mangé." en="I ate some." />
<Example fr="Il m'a appelé." en="He called me. (said by a man)" />

*Ne…pas* wraps around the pronoun and the auxiliary. The participle comes after *pas*.

<Example fr="Je ne l'ai pas vu." en="I didn't see him." />

Questions use intonation, as usual: *Tu l'as vu ?* (Did you see him?), *Elle t'a téléphoné ?* (Did she phone you?).

## The agreement rule

You've learned that with *avoir*, the participle doesn't change. <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-avoir" text="The lesson on the passé composé with avoir" /> promised one exception, and here it is.

**With *avoir*, the participle agrees with a direct object that comes before the verb.** That means *le*, *la*, *l'*, *les*, *me*, *te*, *nous* and *vous*, when they're direct objects.

Add **-e** for a feminine object, **-s** for a plural one, and **-es** for a feminine plural one.

| Who or what is seen | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Paul (masculine singular) | *Je l'ai vu.* (I saw him.) |
| Marie (feminine singular) | *Je l'ai vue.* (I saw her.) |
| Paul et Marc (masculine plural) | *Je les ai vus.* (I saw them.) |
| Anne et Léa (feminine plural) | *Je les ai vues.* (I saw them.) |

<Example fr="Il m'a vue." en="He saw me. (said by a woman)" />
<Example fr="Il nous a vus." en="He saw us. (a group of men, or a mixed group)" />

## Where there's no agreement

The participle doesn't agree with the other pronouns.

- **Lui and leur** are indirect, so nothing changes: *Je lui ai parlé* (I spoke to him).
- **Y** causes no agreement: *J'y ai mangé* (I ate there).
- **En** causes no agreement: *J'en ai mangé* (I ate some), *J'en ai pris trois* (I took three).

With verbs that take *être*, the rule is the one you know from <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-etre" text="the lesson on the passé composé with être" />: the participle agrees with the subject. So *Marie y est allée* agrees with Marie. Reflexive verbs follow <LessonLink id="a2-reflexive-passe-compose" text="the lesson on reflexive verbs in the passé composé" />, and nothing changes there.

## Can you hear it?

With most participles, agreement only shows in writing. *Vu* and *vue* sound the same.

<VocabTable group="silent" />

With participles ending in a consonant, you can hear the feminine, because the consonant gets pronounced.

<VocabTable group="heard" />

<Example fr="La lettre ? Je l'ai écrite." en="The letter? I wrote it." />
<Example fr="La clé ? Je l'ai prise." en="The key? I took it." />
<Example fr="La cuisine ? Je l'ai faite." en="The cooking? I did it." />

## En français

- *Tu as vu Marie ?* (Did you see Marie?)
- *Oui, je l'ai vue samedi.* (Yes, I saw her on Saturday.)
- *Tu lui as parlé ?* (Did you speak to her?)
- *Oui, je lui ai parlé.* (Yes, I spoke to her.)
- *Et les clés ?* (And the keys?)
- *Je les ai prises.* (I took them.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Agree with the object, and only if it comes first">
  The classic mistake is to agree with the subject: *Elle a mangée la pomme* is wrong, because with *avoir*, the subject doesn't count. And when the direct object comes after the verb, there's no agreement either: **J'ai vu Marie**, with no -e. Agreement only happens when the object comes before the verb: **Je l'ai vue**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Mostly a writing issue">
  Agreement is silent for many participles: *vu* and *vue* sound the same, and so do *mangé* and *mangée*. It's audible for others, where a consonant comes back: *pris* and *prise*, *fait* and *faite*. So check every participle when you write, and when you speak, pay attention to the ones you can hear.
</Callout>
