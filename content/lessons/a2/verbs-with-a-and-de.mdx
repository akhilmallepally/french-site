By the end of this lesson you'll know which verbs need *à* or *de* before an infinitive, and which don't need anything.

## Three groups of verbs

When one verb follows another, the second one is an infinitive. Between the two, French has three possibilities: *à*, *de* or nothing. It depends only on the first verb.

## Verbs with à

<VocabTable group="a" />

<Example fr="Je commence à comprendre." en="I'm starting to understand." />
<Example fr="Anne apprend à nager." en="Anne is learning to swim." />
<Example fr="Il continue à pleuvoir." en="It's still raining." />

*Continuer de* also exists, with the same meaning, but stick to *continuer à* for now.

With *aider*, the person comes first, and *à* follows: *J'aide Paul à ranger* (I'm helping Paul to tidy). With a pronoun, it goes before *aider*: *Je l'aide à ranger* (I'm helping him to tidy), as in <LessonLink id="a2-direct-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on direct object pronouns" />.

## Verbs with de

<VocabTable group="de" />

<Example fr="J'essaie de dormir." en="I'm trying to sleep." />
<Example fr="Il a décidé de partir." en="He decided to leave." />
<Example fr="Arrête de manger le chocolat !" en="Stop eating the chocolate!" />
<Example fr="Elle a fini de travailler." en="She's finished working." />
<Example fr="J'ai oublié de fermer la porte." en="I forgot to close the door." />

You've already seen *avoir envie de* and *avoir besoin de*, in <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the lesson on en" />. They take *de* too: *J'ai envie de sortir* (I feel like going out).

## Verbs with nothing

Many of the most common verbs go straight to the infinitive. Don't add anything here.

- *aimer*, *adorer* and *détester*, from <LessonLink id="a1-likes-and-dislikes" text="the lesson on likes and dislikes" />
- *préférer*, from <LessonLink id="a2-comparatives" text="the lesson on comparatives" />
- *vouloir*, *pouvoir* and *devoir*, from <LessonLink id="a1-modal-verbs" text="the lesson on modal verbs" />
- *savoir*, from <LessonLink id="a2-savoir-connaitre" text="the lesson on savoir and connaître" />
- *aller*, from <LessonLink id="a1-near-future" text="the lesson on the near future" />
- *il faut*, from <LessonLink id="a2-il-faut-and-advice" text="the lesson on il faut and simple advice" />

<Example fr="J'adore danser." en="I love dancing." />
<Example fr="Tu peux venir samedi ?" en="Can you come on Saturday?" />

## Apprendre with a noun

*Apprendre* changes with what follows it. Before a noun, there's no preposition. Before an infinitive, you need *à*.

- *J'apprends le français.* (I'm learning French.)
- *J'apprends à nager.* (I'm learning to swim.)

## Spelling and the past

Two spellings are worth a look.

<VocabTable group="spelling" />

*Essayer* has a choice of spelling in *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils*. *Commencer* takes a cedilla before *o*, for the same reason it does before *a*, which you saw in <LessonLink id="a2-imparfait-formation" text="the lesson on the imparfait" />.

In the passé composé, the auxiliary and the participle come first, and the little word and the infinitive follow them.

<Example fr="J'ai commencé à travailler." en="I started working." />

## The whole list

Here are the three groups in one place. It's a good list to come back to.

- **À:** commencer à, apprendre à, réussir à, aider quelqu'un à, continuer à
- **De:** essayer de, décider de, arrêter de, finir de, oublier de, refuser de, avoir envie de, avoir besoin de
- **Nothing:** aimer, adorer, détester, préférer, vouloir, pouvoir, devoir, savoir, aller, il faut

There's no rule that tells you which group a verb belongs to. So learn each verb and its word as one block: not *essayer*, but *essayer de*.

## En français

- *Tu as commencé à apprendre la guitare ?* (Have you started learning the guitar?)
- *Oui, mais j'ai oublié de la prendre aujourd'hui !* (Yes, but I forgot to bring it today!)
- *Tu continues à travailler ce soir ?* (Are you carrying on working this evening?)
- *Non, j'ai décidé d'arrêter. Je veux dormir.* (No, I've decided to stop. I want to sleep.)

<Callout type="warning" title="English won't help you here">
  English "to" and "-ing" tell you nothing about the French. "I'm trying **to** sleep" is **J'essaie de dormir**, and "I want **to** sleep" is **Je veux dormir**, with no little word at all. Don't translate word by word. And never add a preposition after *aimer*, *vouloir* or *pouvoir*: it's **je veux partir**, and not *je veux de partir*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep two columns">
  Save one page in your notebook, with **à** at the top of one column and **de** at the top of the other. Every time you meet one of these verbs in a real sentence, copy the whole sentence into the right column. After a while, you'll start to remember the sentences, and the little word comes along with them.
</Callout>
