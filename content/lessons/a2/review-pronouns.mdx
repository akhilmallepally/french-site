## Which pronoun?

Before you replace a noun, ask what's in front of it.

| In front of the noun | Pronoun |
|---|---|
| a preposition such as *avec*, *pour*, *sans* | stressed: *moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles* |
| nothing, or *le / la / les / mon / ce* | direct: *le, la, l', les* |
| *à* + a person | indirect: *lui, leur* |
| *à* + a place or a thing | *y* |
| *du, de la, des, de*, a number, or a quantity | *en* |

Then ask where it goes. In the present, it's before the verb. With *aller*, *pouvoir* or *vouloir*, it's before the infinitive. In the passé composé, it's before the auxiliary.

Finally, in the passé composé with *avoir*, ask whether the pronoun is a direct object that comes before the verb. If it is, the participle agrees.

## A model rewrite

Here's a short text with a lot of repeated nouns.

*Paul regarde le match. Paul aime le match. Paul téléphone à Marc. Paul parle à Marc. Hier, Paul a vu Marc au café.* (Paul is watching the match. Paul likes the match. Paul phones Marc. Paul speaks to Marc. Yesterday, Paul saw Marc at the café.)

Here's the same text, line by line, with pronouns.

<Example fr="Paul regarde le match. Il l'aime." en="Paul is watching the match. He likes it." />
<Example fr="Il téléphone à Marc. Il lui parle." en="He phones Marc. He speaks to him." />
<Example fr="Hier, il l'a vu au café." en="Yesterday, he saw him at the café." />

The first *Paul* and the first *le match* stay, because the reader needs to know who and what you mean.

## Your turn

Take the paragraph below, and rewrite it on paper or in a notes app. Keep the first mention of each noun, and replace the others.

*Léa téléphone à Anne. Léa parle à Anne de son travail. Léa va à Lyon. Léa va à Lyon le lundi. Léa a pris les billets. Léa a pris les billets hier. Léa a mangé du pain, et Léa a mangé du fromage.* (Léa phones Anne. Léa talks to Anne about her work. Léa goes to Lyon. Léa goes to Lyon on Mondays. Léa took the tickets. Léa took the tickets yesterday. Léa ate some bread, and Léa ate some cheese.)

## Check your work

Go through your rewrite once for each question below.

- Is every pronoun before the conjugated verb, the infinitive, or the auxiliary?
- Does *ne…pas* wrap around the pronoun and the verb?
- With *avoir*, does the participle agree only with a direct object that comes before it?
- Did you use *lui* or *leur* for people after *à*, and *y* for places and things?

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't agree with everything">
  Once you've learned about agreement, it's easy to apply it everywhere. In the passé composé with *avoir*, agreement happens only with a direct object before the verb. **Je l'ai vue** agrees. **Je lui ai parlé** doesn't, and neither does **J'en ai mangé**. Before you add an ending, ask which kind of pronoun you're looking at.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep the noun the first time">
  It's possible to overdo pronouns. If you replace every noun, nobody can tell who or what you mean. When you write, keep the noun the first time it appears, use a pronoun the next time, and bring the noun back whenever there could be any doubt about what the pronoun refers to.
</Callout>
