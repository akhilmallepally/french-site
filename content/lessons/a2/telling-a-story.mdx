By the end of this lesson you'll be able to tell a short story in French, setting the scene and then moving through what happened.

## Three parts

A simple story has three parts, and you can use the tenses from <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-vs-imparfait" text="the lesson on choosing between the two past tenses" /> to build them.

1. **The scene**: when and where, the weather, who was there. Use the imparfait.
2. **The events**: what happened, one after the other. Use the passé composé.
3. **The ending**: how it finished. Use the passé composé again.

## Connecting words

These words show the order of events. You already know *un jour* (one day) and *soudain* (suddenly) from <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-vs-imparfait" text="the lesson on choosing between the two past tenses" />.

<VocabTable group="connectors" />

*D'abord*, *ensuite*, *puis* and *enfin* mark the steps in order. *Ensuite* and *puis* are close in meaning, so you can use both to avoid repeating yourself. *Soudain* and *tout à coup* announce a surprise.

*Un jour* and *ce jour-là* work well near the start. *Ce jour-là* often introduces the scene, in the imparfait, and *un jour* often introduces the first event, in the passé composé.

<Example fr="Ce jour-là, il neigeait." en="That day, it was snowing." />
<Example fr="Un jour, Karim a trouvé un chat." en="One day, Karim found a cat." />
<Example fr="D'abord, j'ai pris un café." en="First, I had a coffee." />
<Example fr="Puis nous avons regardé un film." en="Then we watched a film." />
<Example fr="Tout à coup, Marie a crié." en="All of a sudden, Marie shouted." />
<Example fr="Enfin, le bus est arrivé." en="At last, the bus arrived." />

The connector usually goes first in the sentence, and it's often followed by a comma. *Perdre* (to lose, participle *perdu*) comes from <LessonLink id="a1-ir-re-verbs-present" text="the lesson on -ir and -re verbs" />. Here's one more verb you'll need for the stories.

## A model story

Read this story, and notice how it moves from the scene to the events.

| French | English |
|---|---|
| Ce jour-là, il faisait chaud. | That day, it was hot. |
| Nous étions à la plage. | We were at the beach. |
| D'abord, nous avons mangé. | First, we ate. |
| Ensuite, nous avons joué au ballon. | Next, we played ball. |
| Soudain, j'ai perdu mon téléphone ! | Suddenly, I lost my phone! |
| Puis Léa a trouvé le téléphone. | Then Léa found the phone. |
| Enfin, nous sommes rentrés. | Finally, we went home. |

The first two sentences set the scene, so they're in the imparfait. Everything after that is a separate event, so it's in the passé composé.

## Your second story

Now you try. Here are the jumbled pieces of a different story, which you'll find in the practice below: someone has a cold morning, and they lose their keys. Put the sentences in order, starting with the scene, and let the connectors guide you.

Then write your own, about a day when something went wrong. Aim for six or seven sentences, and try to use at least four connectors.

## En français

- *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait samedi ?* (What did you do on Saturday?)
- *D'abord, je suis allée au marché.* (First, I went to the market.)
- *Il faisait beau ?* (Was the weather nice?)
- *Oui. Ensuite, j'ai vu Paul. Puis, tout à coup, il a neigé !* (Yes. Next, I saw Paul. Then, all of a sudden, it snowed!)
- *Et enfin ?* (And finally?)
- *Enfin, nous sommes rentrés.* (Finally, we went home.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Connectors go with events">
  When you list things that happened, don't slip into the imparfait because it's a story. *D'abord, je mangeais. Ensuite, j'allais au parc* describes habits, not a series of events. Use the passé composé for the steps: **D'abord, j'ai mangé. Ensuite, je suis allé au parc.** Keep the imparfait for the scene around them.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Write the connectors first">
  Before you write a story, put the connectors down the left side of the page: *Ce jour-là*, *D'abord*, *Ensuite*, *Soudain*, *Enfin*. Then fill in a sentence next to each one. You'll have the shape of the story before you start, and each line only has to do one job.
</Callout>
