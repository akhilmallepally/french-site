By the end of this lesson you'll be able to give commands with pronouns, and tell people what not to do.

## After the verb, with a hyphen

You know how to give commands from <LessonLink id="a1-imperative" text="the lesson on the imperative" />, and you know the pronouns from <LessonLink id="a2-direct-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on direct object pronouns" /> and <LessonLink id="a2-indirect-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on indirect object pronouns" />. In a positive command, the pronoun comes **after** the verb, joined by a hyphen.

<Example fr="Regarde-le !" en="Look at him!" />
<Example fr="Écoute-la." en="Listen to her." />
<Example fr="Prends-les." en="Take them." />
<Example fr="Parle-lui." en="Speak to him." />
<Example fr="Réponds-leur." en="Answer them." />
<Example fr="Aide-nous." en="Help us." />

**Me** changes after the verb: it becomes **moi**. So you get *Regarde-moi* (Look at me) and *Écris-moi* (Write to me).

## Y and en

*Y* and *en* from <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-y" text="the lesson on y" /> and <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the lesson on en" /> go after the verb too. There's one surprise. You know that -er verbs and *aller* lose the -s of the *tu* form in a command. Before *y* and *en*, that **-s comes back**, so that the two words can link together.

<VocabTable group="y-en" />

So *Va à la gare* becomes *Vas-y*, and *Mange un peu de gâteau* becomes *Manges-en un peu*. Verbs that keep their -s anyway, like *prendre*, don't change: *Prends-en deux*.

## The negative goes back to normal

In a negative command, everything returns to the usual order. The pronoun goes **before** the verb, and it keeps its normal form, so there's no hyphen and no *moi*.

<Example fr="Ne le fais pas." en="Don't do it." />
<Example fr="Ne lui parle pas." en="Don't speak to her." />
<Example fr="N'y va pas." en="Don't go there." />
<Example fr="N'en prends pas." en="Don't take any." />
<Example fr="Ne m'appelle pas." en="Don't call me." />

## Reflexive commands

Reflexive verbs, from <LessonLink id="a1-daily-routine-reflexive" text="the lesson on daily routines" />, follow the same pattern. In the positive, *te* becomes **toi** after the verb, and the *vous* and *nous* forms use *vous* and *nous*. The *tu* form of an -er verb still has no -s.

<VocabTable group="reflexive" />

In the negative, the pronoun goes back in front, and it's *te* again: *Ne te lève pas*, *Ne t'inquiète pas*. *Ne t'inquiète pas* uses *inquiet* from <LessonLink id="a2-emotions-and-feelings" text="the lesson on emotions and feelings" />, and it's worth learning as a block.

## Two pronouns in a command

French often puts two pronouns in a positive command. At this level, learn a few of these as fixed phrases. The rule is simple: **le, la or les comes first**.

<VocabTable group="two" />

You'll meet all the other combinations at B1.

## At a glance

| | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | *Regarde-le* (Look at him) | *Ne le regarde pas* (Don't look at him) |
| Indirect | *Parle-lui* (Speak to her) | *Ne lui parle pas* (Don't speak to her) |
| Me | *Regarde-moi* (Look at me) | *Ne me regarde pas* (Don't look at me) |
| Y | *Vas-y* (Go there) | *N'y va pas* (Don't go there) |
| En | *Prends-en* (Take some) | *N'en prends pas* (Don't take any) |
| Reflexive | *Lève-toi* (Get up) | *Ne te lève pas* (Don't get up) |

## Putting it to use

You'll hear these most often when someone is giving directions or advice, as in <LessonLink id="a2-il-faut-and-advice" text="the lesson on il faut and simple advice" />, or when food is being offered round.

- *Le pain ? Prends-le, il est là.* (The bread? Take it, it's there.)
- *Tu as mal à la tête ? Repose-toi.* (You have a headache? Have a rest.)

## En français

- *La gare, c'est loin ?* (Is the station far?)
- *Non, vas-y à pied. Prends la première rue à gauche.* (No, go on foot. Take the first street on the left.)
- *Et le billet, je le prends ?* (And the ticket, shall I take it?)
- *Oui, prends-le, mais ne l'oublie pas !* (Yes, take it, but don't forget it!)
- *D'accord. À ce soir !* (OK. See you tonight!)
- *Dépêche-toi, le train part à dix heures !* (Hurry up, the train leaves at ten o'clock!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Three things to check">
  **Only the affirmative changes.** Hyphens and the pronoun after the verb belong to positive commands: **Regarde-le**, but **Ne le regarde pas**. **Use moi and toi after the verb,** never *me* or *te*: **Lève-toi**, **Écris-moi**. **Put the -s back before y and en:** **Vas-y**, **Manges-en**, but **Va à la gare** and **Mange ton pain**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Boss your things around">
  When you tidy up, give orders about the objects in your hand: *La tasse ? Lave-la. Les livres ? Range-les. Le stylo ? Ne l'oublie pas.* Each command makes you think about the gender of the noun and where the pronoun goes, and it turns a boring job into quick practice.
</Callout>
