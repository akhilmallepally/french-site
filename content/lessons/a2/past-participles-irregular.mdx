By the end of this lesson you'll be able to use the past participles of thirteen very common verbs, so you can say what you did, saw, read, ate and drank.

## Same structure, different participles

You already know how the passé composé works from <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-avoir" text="the lesson on the passé composé with avoir" />: the present of *avoir*, then a participle. Nothing changes in that structure. The only difference is that these verbs have participles you have to learn. Many of them come from <LessonLink id="a2-irregular-verbs-present" text="the lesson on common irregular verbs" />.

Luckily, they fall into a few groups by their ending.

## Participles ending in -u

<VocabTable group="u" />

<Example fr="Tu as vu Julie au parc ?" en="Did you see Julie in the park?" />
<Example fr="Nous avons bu du jus d'orange." en="We drank some orange juice." />
<Example fr="Elle a lu le journal." en="She read the newspaper." />

The last three are the modal verbs. After *pu*, *voulu* and *dû*, an infinitive follows, just as it does in the present.

<Example fr="Il a dû rentrer." en="He had to go home." />
<Example fr="Elles ont voulu danser." en="They wanted to dance." />
<Example fr="Je n'ai pas pu finir." en="I wasn't able to finish." />

Only the masculine singular *dû* has a circumflex, which separates it from *du* (*de* + *le*). "Had to" is its main meaning for now, but *dû* can also mean "must have": *Il a dû partir* can mean "He had to leave" or "He must have left".

## Participles ending in -s or -t

The final letter of these participles is silent, so you'll have to learn the spelling by sight.

<VocabTable group="is-it" />

<Example fr="J'ai pris le bus à Nantes." en="I took the bus in Nantes." />
<Example fr="Tu as mis du beurre ?" en="Did you put any butter in?" />
<Example fr="Ils ont dit bonsoir." en="They said good evening." />
<Example fr="Vous avez écrit à Paul ?" en="Did you write to Paul?" />

*Faire* gives you the question you'll use most in this tense: *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait ?* (What did you do?).

<Example fr="Nous avons fait les courses." en="We did the food shopping." />

## Avoir and être

The two most important verbs of all have the most surprising participles.

<VocabTable group="big-two" />

**Eu** is a single short vowel, the same one as in *tu*. **Été** looks like the word for summer, but it has nothing to do with it here.

Both form their passé composé with *avoir*, including *être*.

<Example fr="J'ai eu froid." en="I was cold." />
<Example fr="Nous avons été contents." en="We were happy." />

## Questions and negatives

These participles work exactly like the regular ones you've already practised. In a question, the participle stays after *avoir*: *Tu as lu le journal ?* (Did you read the newspaper?), *Est-ce que vous avez bu du café ?* (Did you drink any coffee?) In a negative, *ne…pas* wraps around *avoir*, and the participle comes after *pas*: *Je n'ai pas fait les courses* (I didn't do the shopping), *Il n'a pas vu le film* (He didn't see the film).

The only new thing to remember is that a modal participle comes before the infinitive, so you can have three verbs in a row: *Je n'ai pas voulu lire* (I didn't want to read). Only *avoir* changes with the subject.

Not every irregular verb from the earlier lesson is here. *Venir*, *partir* and *sortir* use *être*, so they're in the next lesson.

## En français

- *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait samedi ?* (What did you do on Saturday?)
- *J'ai pris le train et j'ai vu Paul.* (I took the train and saw Paul.)
- *Vous avez mangé au restaurant ?* (Did you eat at a restaurant?)
- *Non, nous avons fait la cuisine, et j'ai bu du thé.* (No, we cooked, and I drank some tea.)
- *Et dimanche ?* (And Sunday?)
- *J'ai lu, et j'ai écrit à Marc. Je n'ai pas pu sortir : j'ai dû travailler.* (I read and wrote to Marc. I couldn't go out: I had to work.)

<Callout type="warning" title="J'ai été, never je suis été">
  It's natural to think that *être* would form its past with itself. It doesn't. The passé composé of *être* uses **avoir**: **j'ai été**, **elle a été**. *Je suis été* is a very common mistake. And *été* doesn't change its ending: **Elles ont été contentes** (They were happy), where only the adjective agrees.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Learn one, get the family">
  Verbs built on another verb usually share its participle. Once you know *pris*, you get *appris* (learned) and *compris* (understood) for free. So *J'ai compris* means "I understood", and *J'ai appris le français* means "I learned French". When you meet a long verb, look for a short verb inside it.
</Callout>
