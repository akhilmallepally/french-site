By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say where you went, when you arrived and when you got home, and make the past participle agree with the subject.

## Être as the auxiliary

You've learned to form the past with *avoir* in <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-avoir" text="the lesson on the passé composé with avoir" />. A smaller group of verbs takes **être** instead. Use the present of *être*, from <LessonLink id="a1-etre-present" text="the lesson on être" />, and then the participle.

<VocabTable group="forms" />

These verbs are mostly about **moving from one place to another**, or about **a change of state**. *Rester* (to stay) also takes *être*.

## Verbs of movement

<VocabTable group="motion" />

<Example fr="Je suis arrivé à la gare à huit heures." en="I arrived at the station at eight o'clock." />
<Example fr="Elle est sortie avec Karim." en="She went out with Karim." />
<Example fr="Nous sommes montés au parc." en="We went up to the park." />

Not every verb of movement belongs in this group. *Voyager*, for example, uses *avoir*: *J'ai voyagé*. So you do need to learn the list.

## Other verbs that take être

*Rester* (to stay) also takes *être*, and so do these verbs of change.

<VocabTable group="change" />

<Example fr="Tu es resté à Toulouse ?" en="Did you stay in Toulouse?" />
<Example fr="Léa est née en mai." en="Léa was born in May." />
<Example fr="Le chat est tombé !" en="The cat fell!" />

## Agreement

Here's the big difference from *avoir*. With *être*, the participle behaves like an adjective, and it **agrees with the subject**, as you saw in <LessonLink id="a1-adjective-agreement" text="the lesson on adjective agreement" />. Add **-e** for a feminine subject and **-s** for a plural one, or **-es** for feminine plural.

| Subject | Participle |
|---|---|
| Paul | *Paul est parti.* (Paul left.) |
| Marie | *Marie est partie.* (Marie left.) |
| Paul et Marc | *Paul et Marc sont partis.* (Paul and Marc left.) |
| Anne et Marie | *Anne et Marie sont parties.* (Anne and Marie left.) |
| Paul et Marie | *Paul et Marie sont partis.* (Paul and Marie left.) |

A mixed group takes the masculine plural, as other adjectives do. With *je* and *tu*, the ending depends on who's speaking or being spoken to: a woman writes *je suis partie*.

*Vous* depends on who it refers to: *Vous êtes allé, monsieur ?* (Did you go, sir?), *Vous êtes allée, madame ?* (Did you go, madam?)

For most of these verbs, the agreement is only visible in writing, because *parti* and *partie* sound the same. *Mort* and *morte* are an exception: in *morte* you can hear the t.

With *avoir*, none of this applies. Compare *Elle a mangé* (She ate) and *Elle est allée* (She went).

## The negative

As with *avoir*, *ne…pas* goes around the auxiliary, and the participle follows.

<Example fr="Nous ne sommes pas restés." en="We didn't stay." />
<Example fr="Elles ne sont pas venues." en="They didn't come." />
<Example fr="Je ne suis pas entrée." en="I didn't go in. (said by a woman)" />

## When these verbs take avoir

*Passer*, *sortir*, *monter*, *descendre* and *rentrer* take **avoir** when they have a direct object, that is, when they're doing something to a thing. There's no agreement then, as with any other *avoir* verb. You can also say *J'ai monté les valises* (I took the suitcases up).

<Example fr="J'ai passé un bon week-end." en="I had a good weekend." />
<Example fr="J'ai sorti la voiture." en="I took the car out." />

## En français

- *Tu es allée à Lyon samedi ?* (Did you go to Lyon on Saturday?)
- *Oui, je suis partie à neuf heures, et je suis arrivée à onze heures.* (Yes, I left at nine and arrived at eleven.)
- *Tu es restée dimanche ?* (Did you stay on Sunday?)
- *Non, je suis rentrée samedi soir.* (No, I came home on Saturday evening.)
- *Et Marc ?* (And Marc?)
- *Il n'est pas venu. Il est resté à Paris.* (He didn't come. He stayed in Paris.)

<Callout type="warning" title="J'ai allé isn't French">
  Most verbs use *avoir*, so it's natural to reach for it: *J'ai allé*, *J'ai parti*. For the verbs in this lesson, the auxiliary is **être**: **Je suis allé**, **Je suis parti**. Then look at the subject and adjust the ending: **Sophie est allée**, **Mes parents sont partis**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Picture a house">
  Most of these verbs can fit into a picture of a house and a life. Someone is born there (*naître*), arrives (*arriver*), goes in (*entrer*), goes up (*monter*) and comes down (*descendre*), stays (*rester*), goes out (*sortir*), falls on the stairs (*tomber*), comes back (*revenir*) and, at the end, dies (*mourir*). Walk through the house in your head, and you'll have the list.
</Callout>
