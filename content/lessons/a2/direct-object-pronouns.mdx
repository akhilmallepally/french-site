By the end of this lesson you'll be able to avoid repeating a noun, by replacing it with a small word that goes in front of the verb.

## What a direct object is

In *Je regarde le film*, *le film* is the **direct object**: it receives the action, with no little word like *à* in between. To avoid saying it again, French replaces it with a pronoun.

<VocabTable group="third" />

The choice depends on the gender and number of the noun you're replacing.

- *Je regarde **le film**.* → *Je **le** regarde.* (I'm watching it.)
- *Je mange **la pomme**.* → *Je **la** mange.* (I'm eating it.)
- *Je cherche **les clés**.* → *Je **les** cherche.* (I'm looking for them.)

Before a vowel or silent *h*, *le* and *la* shorten to **l'**: *Tu l'aimes ?* (Do you like it?).

The noun being replaced has a definite word in front of it: *le*, *la*, *les*, *mon*, *ton* or *ce*, for instance. These articles come from <LessonLink id="a1-definite-articles" text="the lesson on le, la and les" />.

<Example fr="Le pull ? Je le prends." en="The jumper? I'll take it." />
<Example fr="Tu aimes ce restaurant ? Oui, je l'aime." en="Do you like this restaurant? Yes, I like it." />

## Me, te, nous, vous

There are four more, for people.

<VocabTable group="others" />

*Me* and *te* shorten to **m'** and **t'** before a vowel or silent *h*. Verbs you'll use include *appeler* (to call), as well as verbs you already know.

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

<Example fr="Elle nous voit." en="She sees us." />
<Example fr="Je t'attends." en="I'm waiting for you." />
<Example fr="Il m'appelle." en="He's calling me." />

## Where the pronoun goes

The pronoun goes **directly before the conjugated verb**.

<Example fr="Je les connais." en="I know them." />

**In a negative**, *ne* goes before the pronoun, and *pas* after the verb, as you learned in <LessonLink id="a1-negation-ne-pas" text="the lesson on negation" />. The pronoun stays attached to the verb.

<Example fr="Je ne le vois pas." en="I don't see him." />

**After aller, pouvoir and vouloir**, the pronoun goes before the **infinitive**, because that's the verb it belongs to. You know *aller* + infinitive from <LessonLink id="a1-near-future" text="the lesson on the near future" />, and *pouvoir* and *vouloir* from <LessonLink id="a1-modal-verbs" text="the lesson on modal verbs" />.

<Example fr="Je vais le faire." en="I'm going to do it." />
<Example fr="Je ne veux pas la voir." en="I don't want to see her." />
<Example fr="Tu peux m'aider ?" en="Can you help me?" />

**In a question**, use intonation or *est-ce que*, and leave the pronoun where it is.

<Example fr="Est-ce que tu les achètes ?" en="Are you buying them?" />

## Verbs with no preposition

Some verbs need a word like "for" or "to" in English, but not in French. *Attendre* (to wait for), *chercher* (to look for), *écouter* (to listen to) and *regarder* (to look at) all take a direct object: *Je l'attends*, *Je l'écoute*.

## En français

- *Tu connais Paul ?* (Do you know Paul?)
- *Oui, je le connais bien.* (Yes, I know him well.)
- *Tu le vois samedi ?* (Are you seeing him on Saturday?)
- *Non, je ne le vois pas samedi. Tu veux l'appeler ?* (No, I'm not seeing him on Saturday. Do you want to call him?)
- *Oui, je vais l'appeler.* (Yes, I'm going to call him.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Three things to watch">
  **Article or pronoun?** *Le*, *la* and *les* are also articles. Before a noun, it's an article. Before a verb, it's a pronoun. **Position.** English says "I eat it", but French says **Je le mange**, never *Je mange le*. **Which nouns?** A noun with *un*, *une*, *des* or *du* can't be replaced by *le*, *la* or *les*. That comes later, in <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the lesson on the pronoun en" />.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Look back for the noun">
  When you're reading and you see *le*, *la* or *les* in front of a verb, pause and look back. Somewhere in the sentences before, there'll be the noun it stands for. Finding it takes a second, and it's the fastest way of getting used to pronouns: you learn to follow them before you need to produce them.
</Callout>
