By the end of this lesson you'll be able to write a friendly email, with your news, your plans and a question for the other person.

## Opening and closing

You've already written short messages in <LessonLink id="a1-writing-a-short-message" text="the lesson on writing a short message" />, so you know *Salut*, *Coucou* and *Cher* or *Chère* plus a name. You also know *À bientôt* and *Grosses bises*. Here are some more ways to finish.

<VocabTable group="closings" />

*Écris-moi vite* is the imperative with a pronoun, from <LessonLink id="a2-imperative-with-pronouns" text="the lesson on the imperative with pronouns" />. *Cordialement*, which you met in the same lesson as *Grosses bises*, is a different kind of closing. It's for someone you call *vous*, such as a teacher. Choose it when you aren't close to the person, and you'll learn more about formal emails in <LessonLink id="b1-formal-email" text="the lesson on writing a formal email" />.

## Getting started

These phrases help you begin, and keep things friendly.

<VocabTable group="starting" />

*Tu me manques* works the opposite way round from English. In French, the person you miss is the subject: literally, "you are lacking to me".

*Dis bonjour à Paul de ma part* (Say hello to Paul from me) is a handy way to finish. And if you think of something after you've finished, add it at the very end with *P.-S.*

## What to put in the middle

A good email has three parts after the opening.

**1. Your news.** Use the passé composé and the imparfait, as you did in <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-vs-imparfait" text="the lesson on the passé composé and the imparfait" /> and <LessonLink id="a2-telling-a-story" text="the lesson on telling a simple story" />. Set the scene, then say what happened.

<Example fr="Il faisait très chaud, alors nous sommes allés à la plage." en="It was very hot, so we went to the beach." />

**2. Your plans.** Use *aller* + infinitive or <LessonLink id="a2-futur-simple" text="the futur simple" />.

<Example fr="Samedi, je vais voir mes parents." en="On Saturday, I'm going to see my parents." />
<Example fr="En août, nous partirons en Italie." en="In August, we'll go to Italy." />

**3. A question back.** A friendly email always gives the other person something to answer.

<Example fr="Et toi, qu'est-ce que tu fais cet été ?" en="And you, what are you doing this summer?" />

Keep *tu* the whole way through. In an email to a friend, *ton*, *ta*, *tes*, *te* and *toi* all go together, so if a *vous* or a *votre* has crept in, change it.

## A model email

*Chère Sophie,*

*Merci pour ton message. Comment vas-tu ? J'espère que tu vas bien.*

*Je t'écris pour te donner des nouvelles. La semaine dernière, nous étions à Toulouse. Il faisait beau, et nous avons visité la ville. Un soir, nous avons mangé dans un très bon restaurant.*

*En juillet, je vais venir à Paris. Tu seras là ? Dis bonjour à Marc de ma part.*

*Tu me manques ! Écris-moi vite.*

*Je t'embrasse,*

*Julien*

*P.-S. J'ai trouvé ton livre chez moi !*

(Dear Sophie, Thanks for your message. How are you? I hope you're well. I'm writing to give you some news. Last week, we were in Toulouse. The weather was good, and we visited the town. One evening, we ate in a very good restaurant. In July, I'm going to come to Paris. Will you be there? Say hello to Marc from me. I miss you! Write back soon. Love, Julien. P.S. I found your book at my place!)

## A second email to put in order

The paragraphs of this email have been mixed up. Decide on the order before you read the answer below.

- **A.** *Le mois prochain, j'irai à Bordeaux. Tu veux venir avec moi ?* (Next month, I'll go to Bordeaux. Do you want to come with me?)
- **B.** *Salut Thomas ! Merci pour ton message.* (Hi Thomas! Thanks for your message.)
- **C.** *À très vite ! Bises, Inès* (Talk soon! Love, Inès)
- **D.** *Hier, je suis allée au marché. Il y avait beaucoup de monde.* (Yesterday, I went to the market. There were a lot of people.)

The order is B, D, A, C: the greeting, the news, the plan with a question, then the closing.

## Your turn

Write an email of six to eight sentences to a friend. Include an opening, your news with both past tenses, one plan, one question and a closing.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't mix tu and vous">
  It's easy to copy a phrase from somewhere else and end up with both. If you open with **Salut** and **Merci pour ton message**, then keep *tu* and *ton* all the way through, and close with **Bises** or **À bientôt**. Don't use *Cordialement* or *Je vous embrasse* with a friend. Don't use *Bises* with a teacher, either.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Reply to one thing they said">
  Before you share your own news, pick out one detail from your friend's last message and ask about it: *Et ton examen, c'était bien ?* It takes one line, and it shows you actually read what they wrote. It also means you always have a way to start.
</Callout>
