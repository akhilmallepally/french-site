By the end of this lesson you'll be able to form the imparfait of any verb you know, and use it to describe how things were and what you used to do.

## A second past tense

You've learned to tell what happened with <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-avoir" text="the passé composé" />. The **imparfait** does a different job: it describes how things were, and what used to happen again and again.

Forming it is easy. Take the *nous* form of the present, remove *-ons*, and add these endings: **-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient**.

<VocabTable group="parler" />

Other verb families work exactly the same way, as *nous finissons* and *nous attendons* show.

<VocabTable group="finir" />

<VocabTable group="attendre" />

The irregular verbs from <LessonLink id="a2-irregular-verbs-present" text="the lesson on common irregular verbs" /> follow the same rule, because you start from *nous*: *nous prenons* gives *je prenais*, *nous buvons* gives *je buvais*, *nous lisons* gives *je lisais*, *nous faisons* gives *je faisais*, and *nous avons* gives *j'avais*.

## Être, the one exception

*Nous sommes* has no *-ons* to remove, so <LessonLink id="a1-etre-present" text="être" /> has a stem of its own: **ét-**.

<VocabTable group="etre" />

<Example fr="J'étais fatigué." en="I was tired. (said by a man)" />

## How it sounds

Four of the six forms sound the same: *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils*, spelled *-ais*, *-ais*, *-ait* and *-aient*. So *je parlais*, *tu parlais*, *il parlait* and *ils parlaient* are identical to the ear. Only *nous* and *vous* (*-ions*, *-iez*) sound different.

## Three spelling changes

Three small groups need care. Each change comes from a simple logic.

<VocabTable group="spelling" />

- **Manger** and other verbs in *-ger* keep an **e** before *a*, to keep the g soft: *je mangeais*. Before *i*, the g is already soft, so there's no e: *nous mangions*.
- **Commencer** and other verbs in *-cer* take a **ç** before *a*, to keep the s sound: *je commençais*. Before *i*, a plain c already sounds like s: *nous commencions*.
- **Étudier** and other verbs in *-ier* have a stem ending in *i*, and the ending starts with *i*, so you get two in a row: *nous étudiions*, *vous étudiiez*.

## What it's for

At this stage, the imparfait has two main uses.

**Habits**: what used to happen regularly. Words like *tous les jours*, *souvent*, *le samedi* and *d'habitude* go naturally with it. *Souvent* is from <LessonLink id="a2-adverbs-ment" text="the lesson on adverbs" />.

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="Tous les jours, je prenais le bus." en="Every day, I took the bus." />
<Example fr="Le samedi, nous allions au marché." en="On Saturdays, we used to go to the market." />
<Example fr="D'habitude, il finissait à cinq heures." en="He usually finished at five o'clock." />

**Descriptions**: age, weather, feelings and appearance, with the words from <LessonLink id="a1-describing-people" text="the lesson on describing people" /> and <LessonLink id="a1-weather-seasons" text="the lesson on weather and seasons" />.

<Example fr="J'avais douze ans." en="I was twelve." />
<Example fr="Il pleuvait, et il faisait froid." en="It was raining, and it was cold." />
<Example fr="Elle avait les cheveux longs et elle était timide." en="She had long hair and she was shy." />

Two fixed forms come up all the time: **il y avait** (there was, there were) and **c'était** (it was). *Il pleuvait* and *il y avait* are learned as forms, since there's no *nous* form to start from.

<Example fr="Il y avait un marché, et c'était super." en="There was a market, and it was great." />

## En français

- *Tu habitais à Lille ?* (Did you live in Lille?)
- *Oui, j'avais dix ans. Il pleuvait souvent !* (Yes, I was ten. It often rained!)
- *Et le samedi ?* (And on Saturdays?)
- *Nous allions au cinéma. C'était super.* (We used to go to the cinema. It was great.)

<Callout type="warning" title="One letter from the present">
  With *nous* and *vous*, the imparfait is only one letter away from the present. **Nous parlons** is the present, and **nous parlions** is the imparfait. **Vous parlez** is the present, and **vous parliez** is the imparfait. When you read or write these, check for the *i*: it's what makes the sentence past.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Know nous, know the tense">
  Apart from *être*, you don't need to learn new stems. If you can say the *nous* form of a verb in the present, you already have the imparfait. So when you meet a new verb, practise its *nous* form first, and you get a whole past tense along with it.
</Callout>
