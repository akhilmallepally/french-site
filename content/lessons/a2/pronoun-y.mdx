By the end of this lesson you'll be able to replace a place or a thing after *à* with one tiny word, and you'll understand one of the most common phrases in French: *On y va !*

## What y replaces

**Y** replaces *à* (or *au*, *à la*, *à l'*, *aux*, as in <LessonLink id="a1-contractions-a-de" text="the lesson on à and de with articles" />) together with a place or a thing that's already been mentioned.

<VocabTable group="core" />

**A place:**

<Example fr="Tu vas à Lille ? — Oui, j'y vais." en="Are you going to Lille? — Yes, I'm going there." />
<Example fr="Nous y habitons." en="We live there." />
<Example fr="Tu y es ?" en="Are you there?" />
<Example fr="Elle y reste." en="She's staying there." />

**A thing, after a verb with à:**

<Example fr="La lettre ? J'y réponds." en="The letter? I'm answering it." />
<Example fr="Tu joues au tennis ? Oui, j'y joue." en="Do you play tennis? Yes, I play it." />
<Example fr="Mon travail ? J'y pense." en="My work? I'm thinking about it." />

## Where it goes

*Y* follows the same rules as the pronouns in <LessonLink id="a2-direct-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on direct object pronouns" />. It goes **before the verb**, and it causes *je* and *ne* to shorten.

<Example fr="Je n'y vais pas." en="I'm not going there." />

After *aller*, *pouvoir* and *vouloir*, it goes before the **infinitive**.

<Example fr="Je vais y aller." en="I'm going to go there." />
<Example fr="Je veux y habiter." en="I want to live there." />

## On y va

**On y va !** is everywhere in French. It's a fixed phrase meaning "Let's go!", and you can use it whenever a group is ready to leave.

## Person or thing?

Here's how to choose, using what you learned in <LessonLink id="a2-indirect-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on indirect object pronouns" />.

| After à | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a person | *lui* / *leur* | *Je réponds à Paul.* → *Je **lui** réponds.* (I'm answering him.) |
| a place or a thing | *y* | *Je réponds à la lettre.* → *J'**y** réponds.* (I'm answering it.) |

There's one small complication. With *penser à* and a person, French uses *à* with a stressed pronoun, from <LessonLink id="a2-stressed-pronouns" text="the lesson on stressed pronouns" />: *Je pense à lui* (I'm thinking about him).

## The y you already know

You've been using this word since A1, in *il y a*. It's the same little *y*, as part of a fixed phrase, and you don't need to think about it separately.

## En français

- *Tu vas au marché ?* (Are you going to the market?)
- *Oui, j'y vais samedi. Et toi ?* (Yes, I'm going there on Saturday. And you?)
- *Non, je n'y vais pas. Je vais à la piscine.* (No, I'm not going there. I'm going to the pool.)
- *Tu y vas à vélo ?* (Are you going there by bike?)
- *Oui. Tu veux y aller avec moi ?* (Yes. Do you want to go there with me?)
- *Oui ! On y va !* (Yes! Let's go!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Y isn't for people">
  **Y never replaces a person.** *Je réponds à Paul* becomes **Je lui réponds**, and never *J'y réponds*. It also doesn't replace a place introduced by *de*: for that, you'll need *en*, which comes later, in <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the lesson on the pronoun en" />. Check what comes after *à* before you reach for *y*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="J'y vais means I'm off">
  *J'y vais* isn't only about going to a particular place. French speakers use it all the time to say "I'm off" or "I'm heading out", as they get up to leave. At the end of a coffee with a friend, try *Bon, j'y vais !* It's short, it's natural, and it signals politely that you need to go.
</Callout>
