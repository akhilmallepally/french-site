## A model plan

Here's Inès, planning a trip and writing to a friend.

<Example fr="J'adore voyager à l'étranger." en="I love travelling abroad." />
<Example fr="L'été prochain, je ferai un voyage au Québec." en="Next summer, I'll take a trip to Québec." />
<Example fr="Je prendrai l'avion et je serai à Montréal en juillet." en="I'll take the plane and I'll be in Montréal in July." />
<Example fr="Voici mon itinéraire : Montréal, Trois-Rivières, Québec." en="Here's my route: Montréal, Trois-Rivières, Québec." />
<Example fr="Je voudrais réserver une chambre du 3 au 10 juillet." en="I'd like to book a room from 3 to 10 July." />
<Example fr="Mon séjour sera très court, mais il y aura beaucoup de choses à voir." en="My stay will be very short, but there will be a lot of things to see." />
<Example fr="Tu es libre en juillet ? Tu veux venir avec moi ?" en="Are you free in July? Do you want to come with me?" />

And a reply:

<Example fr="Avec plaisir ! Je viens du Canada, et je connais bien le Québec." en="With pleasure! I come from Canada, and I know Québec well." />

## Putting the plan in order

A travel plan is easiest to follow if it goes step by step: where, how, when, where you'll stay, and who's coming. Notice how the model does that. It names the place first, with the right preposition. Then it gives the transport and the date, followed by the booking. Only at the end does it turn to the friend, with a simple invitation in the present. The futur simple carries the plan, and the present handles the invitation.

## Your turn

Write a short plan on paper or in a notes app. It can be real or invented. Aim for eight sentences or so, and include:

1. where you'll go, with the right preposition for a country and a city
2. how you'll get there, with *en* or *à*
3. two verbs in the futur simple, including one with an irregular stem
4. how long you'll stay, and a booking request with *je voudrais*
5. an invitation to a friend, with *Ça te dit de…* or *Tu veux venir…*

Then write the friend's reply, accepting or politely declining.

## Check your work

Go through your plan once for each question below.

- Did you use *à* for cities, *en* for feminine countries, *au* for masculine ones and *aux* for plural ones?
- Is your transport right: *en voiture*, but *à pied*?
- Does each futur simple have the right stem and the sound of an *r* before the ending?
- Is your refusal polite, with thanks and a reason?

<Callout type="warning" title="Check the country first">
  When you're planning a trip, the preposition depends entirely on the place. It's **au Canada**, **en Italie**, **aux États-Unis**, and **à Lyon**. Before you write one, ask whether it's a city or a country, and if it's a country, whether it's masculine, feminine or plural. It only takes a second, and it saves one of the most common mistakes.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Trace it on a map">
  Before you write about a trip, sketch the route on a map, or just a list of stops. Each stop tells you what you need: a city needs *à*, a country needs its gender, and the distance between them suggests whether you'll go *en train* or *en avion*. With the map in front of you, the sentences almost write themselves.
</Callout>
