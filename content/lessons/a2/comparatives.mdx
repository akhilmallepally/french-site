By the end of this lesson you'll be able to compare people, things and actions, and use the two French words for "better".

## With adjectives and adverbs

Put **plus**, **moins** or **aussi** before an adjective or an adverb, and **que** after it.

<VocabTable group="adjectives" />

<Example fr="Paul est plus grand que Marie." en="Paul is taller than Marie." />
<Example fr="Ce quartier est moins calme que le centre." en="This neighbourhood is less quiet than the centre." />
<Example fr="Elle parle aussi vite que lui." en="She speaks as fast as he does." />

The adjective agrees with the **subject**, as you learned in <LessonLink id="a1-adjective-agreement" text="the lesson on adjective agreement" />, and not with the thing being compared: *Marie est plus grande que Paul.*

After **que**, you can have a name, a noun or a stressed pronoun, from <LessonLink id="a2-stressed-pronouns" text="the lesson on stressed pronouns" />: *que Paul*, *que le centre*, *que moi*. For now, don't follow it with a whole clause. *Que* shortens to *qu'* before a vowel: *qu'Anne*, *qu'en hiver*.

Adverbs work the same way as adjectives, as you'll remember from <LessonLink id="a2-adverbs-ment" text="the lesson on adverbs" />: *Elle parle plus lentement que moi* (She speaks more slowly than I do).

## How plus sounds

Before a consonant, the s of *plus* is usually silent: *plus grand*. Before a vowel, it links to the next word: *plus intéressant*. In *plus de* meaning "more", the s is usually pronounced: *plus de touristes*. In *ne…plus de* (no more), it's silent: *Je n'ai plus de lait*.

## With nouns and verbs

With a **noun**, add **de**: *plus de*, *moins de*, *autant de*.

<VocabTable group="nouns" />

<Example fr="Il y a plus de touristes en été qu'en hiver." en="There are more tourists in summer than in winter." />
<Example fr="J'ai autant de livres que toi." en="I have as many books as you." />
<Example fr="Nous avons moins de temps que Paul." en="We have less time than Paul." />

With a **verb**, put *plus*, *moins* or *autant* straight after it, then **que**.

<Example fr="Elle voyage plus que moi." en="She travels more than I do." />
<Example fr="Tu dors moins que Karim." en="You sleep less than Karim." />

## Better: meilleur and mieux

English has only one word, but French has two.

<VocabTable group="better" />

**Meilleur** is the comparative of *bon*. It's an adjective, so it agrees with its noun. You can't say *plus bon*. *Moins bon* and *aussi bon* are fine, though.

<Example fr="Ce pain est meilleur que l'autre." en="This bread is better than the other one." />
<Example fr="Ces pommes sont meilleures." en="These apples are better." />
<Example fr="Ce vin est moins bon." en="This wine isn't as good." />

**Mieux** is the comparative of *bien*. It's an adverb, so it goes with a verb and it never changes.

<Example fr="Léa chante mieux que Paul." en="Léa sings better than Paul." />
<Example fr="Elles travaillent mieux que nous." en="They work better than we do." />

*Ça va mieux* (I'm feeling better) is a fixed phrase, and it's useful with what you learned in <LessonLink id="a2-review-health" text="the review of health and the body" />.

## With a number

With a number, French uses **plus de** or **moins de**, and not *que*: *plus de dix personnes* (more than ten people), *moins de vingt euros* (less than twenty euros). This *plus* isn't the same as *ne…plus* in <LessonLink id="a2-negative-expressions" text="the lesson on other negative expressions" />, which means "no longer".

## Saying which one you prefer

After a comparison, you often want to say which one you like more. For that, use *préférer*.

<VocabTable group="prefer" />

The *é* before the ending becomes *è* with *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils*, but not with *nous* and *vous*.

<Example fr="Je préfère Dijon." en="I prefer Dijon." />

## En français

- *Lyon est plus grand que Dijon ?* (Is Lyon bigger than Dijon?)
- *Oui, et il y a plus de restaurants.* (Yes, and there are more restaurants.)
- *Tu préfères Lyon ?* (Do you prefer Lyon?)
- *Non. Dijon est moins bruyant, et les gens sont aussi sympas.* (No. Dijon is less noisy, and the people are just as nice.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Three common slips">
  **Aussi or autant?** *Aussi* goes with adjectives and adverbs: **aussi grand**. *Autant* goes with nouns and verbs: **autant de livres**, **autant que lui**. **"Than" is que**, and never *de* or *comme*: **plus grand que Paul**. **Plus bon doesn't exist.** Say **meilleur**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Moins bon is the tactful choice">
  French gives you a very polite way to criticize something. Instead of saying that a dish or a film is bad, you can say it's *moins bon* than something else: *C'est moins bon que l'autre.* You're making a comparison, not a complaint, and the person who cooked it won't mind nearly as much.
</Callout>
