By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say how much or how many of something you want, and shop for food by the kilo, the bottle or the slice.

## How much?

These four expressions give a general amount.

<VocabTable group="amounts" />

For *trop* and *assez* as adverbs on their own, see <LessonLink id="a2-adverbs-ment" text="the lesson on adverbs" />. To ask how much or how many, use *combien de*, from <LessonLink id="a1-question-words" text="the lesson on question words" />: *Tu veux combien de pommes ?* (How many apples do you want?).

<Example fr="J'ai beaucoup de livres." en="I have a lot of books." />
<Example fr="Il y a un peu de lait." en="There's a little milk." />
<Example fr="Nous avons assez de temps." en="We have enough time." />
<Example fr="Tu manges trop de chocolat." en="You eat too much chocolate." />

*Un peu de* goes with things you can't count, such as milk or time. It isn't used with things you can count, such as apples.

## Containers and measures

These are the words you'll use in shops and at the table.

<VocabTable group="containers" />

<Example fr="Je voudrais un kilo de tomates." en="I'd like a kilo of tomatoes." />
<Example fr="Une tasse de café, s'il vous plaît." en="A cup of coffee, please." />
<Example fr="Tu veux une tranche de pain ?" en="Do you want a slice of bread?" />

## The rule: de, with no article

After a quantity, use **de**, or **d'** before a vowel or silent *h*. There's no *le*, *la* or *les*, and no *du* or *de la*.

| Without a quantity | With a quantity |
|---|---|
| *du pain* (some bread) | *un morceau de pain* (a piece of bread) |
| *de l'eau* (some water) | *un verre d'eau* (a glass of water) |
| *des pommes* (some apples) | *un kilo de pommes* (a kilo of apples) |

The noun can be plural: *un kilo de pommes*. The measure itself takes -s if there's more than one: *deux kilos de pommes*, *trois bouteilles d'eau*.

It's the same idea as *pas de* after a negative verb, which you met in <LessonLink id="a1-partitive-articles" text="the lesson on du, de la and des" />. The article drops, and only *de* is left.

## Beaucoup and un peu without de

*Beaucoup* and *un peu* can also go straight after a verb, as adverbs, and then there's no *de*.

<VocabTable group="adverb" />

<Example fr="J'aime beaucoup le fromage." en="I like cheese very much." />
<Example fr="Il dort un peu." en="He's sleeping a little." />

*Très* goes before an adjective or an adverb, but *beaucoup* goes with a verb. See <LessonLink id="a2-adverbs-ment" text="the lesson on adverbs" />.

## En français

Here's a short exchange at a market stall.

- *Bonjour. Vous désirez ?* (Hello. What would you like?)
- *Bonjour. Je voudrais un kilo de pommes et une boîte de tomates.* (Hello. I'd like a kilo of apples and a can of tomatoes.)
- *Et avec ça ?* (Anything else?)
- *Un morceau de fromage et une bouteille d'eau, s'il vous plaît.* (A piece of cheese and a bottle of water, please.)
- *C'est tout ?* (Is that all?)
- *Oui, merci. J'ai assez de courses !* (Yes, thank you. I've got enough shopping!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Beaucoup des gâteaux is wrong">
  After a quantity, the article disappears. It's **beaucoup de gâteaux**, not *beaucoup des gâteaux*. It's **un verre de vin**, not *un verre du vin*. Whenever you've said a word of quantity, what follows is plain *de*, or *d'* before a vowel: **une bouteille d'eau**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Une livre at the market">
  At a French market, you may hear *une livre*, which means half a kilo. It's handy to know because it's so often the amount you actually want: *une livre de tomates*. Don't confuse it with *un livre*, which is a book. The gender is the only difference.
</Callout>
