By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say "never", "nothing", "nobody" and "no longer" in French, and put them in the right place in the passé composé.

## More words that replace pas

You know *ne…pas* from <LessonLink id="a1-negation-ne-pas" text="the lesson on negation" />. Four other words can take the place of *pas*, and *ne* stays in front of the verb.

<VocabTable group="negatives" />

In the present, they all work just like *pas*.

<Example fr="Je ne bois jamais de café." en="I never drink coffee." />
<Example fr="Il ne dit rien." en="He doesn't say anything." />
<Example fr="Nous ne voyons personne ici." en="We don't see anybody here." />
<Example fr="Elle n'habite plus à Metz." en="She doesn't live in Metz any more." />

As with *pas*, *un*, *une*, *des*, *du* and *de la* change to *de* after *jamais* and *plus*: *Je ne mange plus de viande* (I don't eat meat any more).

The s of *plus* is silent in this negative.

## Positive counterparts

Each of these words has a positive partner, and you'll often hear them in a question and its answer.

<VocabTable group="pairs" />

- *Tu vois quelqu'un ?* — *Non, je ne vois personne.* (Do you see someone? — No, I don't see anybody.)
- *Tu veux quelque chose ?* — *Non, je ne veux rien.* (Do you want something? — No, I don't want anything.)
- *Il est toujours en retard ?* — *Non, il n'est jamais en retard.* (Is he always late? — No, he's never late.)
- *Tu habites encore à Lyon ?* — *Non, je n'habite plus à Lyon.* (Do you still live in Lyon? — No, I don't live in Lyon any more.)

## In the passé composé

As in <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-etre" text="the lesson on the passé composé with être" />, the *ne* goes before the auxiliary. **Jamais**, **plus** and **rien** then go between the auxiliary and the participle, just where *pas* does. These work with both *avoir* and *être*.

Some of the sentences here use *vu* and *fait*, from <LessonLink id="a2-past-participles-irregular" text="the lesson on irregular past participles" />.

<Example fr="Je n'ai jamais voyagé." en="I've never travelled." />
<Example fr="Ils ne sont plus venus." en="They didn't come any more." />
<Example fr="Tu n'as rien fait !" en="You didn't do anything!" />

**Personne** is different. It goes **after** the participle.

<Example fr="Elle n'a invité personne." en="She didn't invite anybody." />
<Example fr="Je n'ai rencontré personne." en="I didn't meet anybody." />

## Starting a sentence

*Personne* and *rien* can also be the subject of a sentence. They come first, and *ne* follows them, but there's no *pas*.

<Example fr="Personne ne parle." en="Nobody's speaking." />
<Example fr="Rien n'a changé." en="Nothing has changed." />

Don't add *pas* to any of these negatives. *Je n'ai pas rien vu* is a double negative with a different meaning (I did see something), so it's not the way to say "I saw nothing".

## En français

- *Tu as fait quelque chose samedi ?* (Did you do something on Saturday?)
- *Non, je n'ai rien fait.* (No, I didn't do anything.)
- *Tu as vu quelqu'un ?* (Did you see someone?)
- *Non, je n'ai vu personne.* (No, I didn't see anybody.)
- *Tu vas encore à la piscine ?* (Do you still go to the pool?)
- *Non, je ne vais plus à la piscine.* (No, I don't go to the pool any more.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Rien before, personne after">
  These two look as if they should go in the same place, but they don't. **Rien** goes before the participle: **Je n'ai rien vu**. **Personne** goes after it: **Je n'ai vu personne**. So it's never *Je n'ai vu rien*, and never *Je n'ai personne vu*. In the present, there's only one verb, so both simply follow it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="One word is a whole answer">
  *Rien*, *personne* and *jamais* can stand alone as answers, and then there's no *ne*. *Qu'est-ce que tu fais ? — Rien.* (What are you doing? — Nothing.) *Qui est là ? — Personne.* (Who's there? — Nobody.) *Tu fumes ? — Jamais !* (Do you smoke? — Never!) When you're stuck for a full sentence, one word will do.
</Callout>
