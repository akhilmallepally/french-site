## A model story

Here's a childhood story in three parts: the habits, a particular day, and the end. Look at each verb and ask why it's in that tense.

**The habits and scene (imparfait)**

<Example fr="Quand j'étais petit, nous habitions près d'une forêt." en="When I was little, we lived near a forest. (said by a man)" />
<Example fr="Tous les jours, je jouais dehors avec mon chien, Filou." en="Every day, I played outside with my dog, Filou." />

**A particular day (passé composé)**

<Example fr="Un jour, Filou est parti." en="One day, Filou ran away." />
<Example fr="D'abord, j'ai cherché Filou." en="First, I looked for Filou." />
<Example fr="Ensuite, je suis allé à la forêt, mais j'avais peur." en="Next, I went to the forest, but I was afraid." />
<Example fr="Soudain, j'ai entendu Filou !" en="Suddenly, I heard Filou!" />

**The end (passé composé)**

<Example fr="Enfin, nous sommes rentrés à la maison." en="Finally, we went home." />

Notice *j'avais peur* in the middle of the events. It's a feeling at that moment, so it stays in the imparfait, even between two events.

## Why each verb has its tense

Look at how the story moves. The first two sentences don't tell you what happened. They paint a picture of life at the time, so they stay in the imparfait. *Un jour* is the signal that the story is starting, and from there each sentence moves things along, so you need the passé composé. *Soudain* brings in the surprise, and *enfin* closes the story. If you took out the connectors, the tenses would still tell you which sentences are background and which are events.

## Your turn

Write your own childhood story on paper or in a notes app. It can be true or invented. Aim for eight to ten sentences, in three parts.

1. **Set the scene** with *Quand j'étais petit* or *petite*. Say where you lived, who you lived with, and what you used to do.
2. **Tell one event.** Start with *Un jour*, and use at least three connectors, such as *d'abord*, *ensuite* and *soudain*.
3. **End it** with *enfin*, and say how you felt.

## Check your work

Go through your story once for each question below.

- Is each description and habit in the imparfait?
- Is each separate event in the passé composé?
- Does each passé composé have the right auxiliary?
- With *être*, does the participle agree with the subject?
- Did you remember the spelling changes in *mangeais*, *commençais* and *étudiions*?

<Callout type="warning" title="Keep the agreement in a mixed story">
  When two tenses share a paragraph, it's easy to forget the small things. In **Marie est arrivée**, *arrivée* agrees because the auxiliary is *être*. In **Marie a mangé**, nothing agrees. In **Marie était contente**, the adjective agrees with her. Check each one separately, even when you're concentrating on the choice of tense.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Try 'and then' in front">
  If you can't decide between the two tenses, try putting "and then" in front of the English sentence. "And then the dog ran off" makes sense, so it's an event, and you need the passé composé. "And then it was raining" sounds strange, because that's the background, so you need the imparfait.
</Callout>
