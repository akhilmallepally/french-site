By the end of this lesson you'll be able to describe your home room by room, say where things are, and talk about who does which job around the house.

## Where you live

You probably live in *une maison* or in one of these.

<VocabTable group="housing" />

Floors use the ordinals from <LessonLink id="a2-numbers-years-ordinals" text="the lesson on numbers over 100, ordinals and years" />: *au rez-de-chaussée*, *au premier étage*, *au deuxième étage*. *Le premier étage* is one floor above the ground floor: the British first floor, and the American second floor.

<Example fr="J'habite dans un immeuble, au troisième étage." en="I live in a block of flats, on the third floor." />

## Rooms

<VocabTable group="rooms" />

You already know *la salle de bains* from <LessonLink id="a2-booking-a-hotel" text="the lesson on booking a hotel" />, *le bureau* from <LessonLink id="a1-plural-nouns" text="the lesson on plural nouns" />, and *le jardin* from <LessonLink id="a2-childhood-memories" text="the lesson on childhood memories" />. In France, *les toilettes* are usually a separate room, not part of the bathroom.

When someone describes a flat as having *trois pièces*, they usually don't count the kitchen or the bathroom.

<Example fr="Il y a trois pièces et un balcon." en="There are three rooms and a balcony." />
<Example fr="La cuisine est à côté du salon." en="The kitchen is next to the living room." />

*À côté de*, *sur*, *sous* and the others are from <LessonLink id="a1-prepositions-of-place" text="the lesson on prepositions of place" />, and *dans* from <LessonLink id="a2-prepositions-with-places" text="the lesson on prepositions" />.

## Furniture

You know *la chaise* from <LessonLink id="a1-indefinite-articles" text="the lesson on indefinite articles" />, and *la table* and *le lit* have come up too. Here are some more words.

<VocabTable group="furniture" />

Before a vowel, *le* and *la* both become *l'*, so *l'armoire* and *l'étagère* hide their gender. That's why they're listed with *une*: learn them as *une armoire* and *une étagère*, and you'll always have the gender you need.

<Example fr="Le canapé est devant la fenêtre." en="The sofa is in front of the window." />
<Example fr="Les livres sont sur l'étagère." en="The books are on the shelf." />
<Example fr="Il y a une lampe à côté du fauteuil." en="There's a lamp next to the armchair." />

## Jobs around the house

You know *faire la vaisselle*, *faire le ménage*, *faire les courses* and *faire la cuisine* from <LessonLink id="a1-faire-and-aller" text="the lesson on faire and aller" />. Here are a few more.

<VocabTable group="chores" />

*Il faut* from <LessonLink id="a2-il-faut-and-advice" text="the lesson on il faut and simple advice" /> is ideal for talking about chores, since an infinitive follows it.

<Example fr="Il faut passer l'aspirateur dans le salon." en="The living room needs vacuuming." />
<Example fr="Je range la chambre le dimanche." en="I tidy the bedroom on Sundays." />
<Example fr="Mon frère sort les poubelles le mardi." en="My brother takes out the bins on Tuesdays." />

## Who does what?

Household jobs are a good everyday subject, and you already have what you need to talk about them. Say who does the job, then add how often: *le samedi*, *souvent*, *tous les jours*. To ask about someone else's routine, try *Qui fait la vaisselle chez toi ?* (Who does the dishes at your place?).

## En français

- *Qui fait le ménage aujourd'hui ?* (Who's doing the housework today?)
- *Moi, je passe l'aspirateur. Tu peux faire la vaisselle ?* (I'll vacuum. Can you do the dishes?)
- *D'accord. Et les poubelles ?* (OK. And the bins?)
- *Il faut sortir les poubelles ce soir.* (The bins need to go out this evening.)
- *Je vais les sortir.* (I'm going to take them out.)

<Callout type="warning" title="La cuisine does two jobs">
  **La cuisine** means both "the kitchen" and "the cooking". So *faire la cuisine* means "to cook", and *Je suis dans la cuisine* means "I'm in the kitchen". The context tells you which you're looking at. And remember that *une chambre* is a bedroom. For a room in general, say *une pièce*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Label your home">
  Write these words on sticky notes, with their articles, and put them on the real things: *le frigo*, *la lampe*, *l'armoire*. You'll see each word many times a day without any effort, and it fixes the gender along with the word. When you can recall a label without looking, take it down.
</Callout>
