By the end of this lesson you'll be able to replace *à* + a person with a small word in front of the verb, and tell the difference between *le* and *lui*.

## Verbs with à

Some verbs are followed by *à* and a person. That person is an **indirect object**. To replace it, use one of these pronouns.

<VocabTable group="forms" />

The other four are the same as the set you've met in <LessonLink id="a2-direct-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on direct object pronouns" />: **me**, **te**, **nous**, **vous**.

- **Lui** means "to him" or "to her". It doesn't show gender.
- **Leur** means "to them". It's for men or women, and it never has an -s.

Here are the most common verbs that take *à* + a person.

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

You also know *parler à*, *écrire à* and *répondre à*.

<Example fr="Je parle à Karim." en="I'm speaking to Karim." />
<Example fr="Je lui parle." en="I'm speaking to him." />
<Example fr="Elle leur écrit." en="She's writing to them." />
<Example fr="Tu nous réponds ?" en="Are you answering us?" />
<Example fr="Il me donne un livre." en="He's giving me a book." />
<Example fr="Je leur offre un cadeau." en="I'm giving them a present." />

*Dire à* works this way too, with a simple object: *Je lui dis bonjour* (I say hello to him).

## Where it goes

The rules are exactly the same as for direct object pronouns. The pronoun goes before the conjugated verb, *ne* wraps around pronoun and verb, and after *aller*, *pouvoir* and *vouloir*, the pronoun goes before the infinitive.

<Example fr="Je ne lui réponds pas." en="I'm not answering her." />
<Example fr="Je vais lui téléphoner." en="I'm going to phone him." />
<Example fr="Tu veux leur parler ?" en="Do you want to speak to them?" />
<Example fr="Elle peut me montrer le plan." en="She can show me the map." />

## Direct or indirect?

The test is the verb. If it's followed by **à + a person**, use *lui* or *leur*. If there's no *à*, use *le*, *la*, *l'* or *les*.

| No à: direct | With à: indirect |
|---|---|
| *J'écoute Paul.* → *Je **l'**écoute.* (I'm listening to him.) | *Je parle à Paul.* → *Je **lui** parle.* (I'm speaking to him.) |
| *J'attends Marie.* → *Je **l'**attends.* (I'm waiting for her.) | *Je téléphone à Marie.* → *Je **lui** téléphone.* (I'm phoning her.) |
| *Je regarde les enfants.* → *Je **les** regarde.* (I'm watching them.) | *J'écris aux enfants.* → *Je **leur** écris.* (I'm writing to them.) |

Notice that *me*, *te*, *nous* and *vous* are the same in both columns. You only have to choose with the third person.

A thing with *à*, such as *répondre à la lettre*, isn't replaced by *lui*. It takes *y*, in <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-y" text="the next lesson" />. And a few verbs with *à* behave differently, which you'll meet later.

## En français

- *Tu téléphones à ta mère ce soir ?* (Are you phoning your mother tonight?)
- *Oui, je lui téléphone à huit heures.* (Yes, I'm phoning her at eight.)
- *Et tes parents ?* (And your parents?)
- *Je leur écris.* (I'm writing to them.)
- *Tu peux me donner le stylo ?* (Can you give me the pen?)

<Callout type="warning" title="French needs the à">
  English says "phone him", "answer him" and "ask him", with no preposition. It's tempting to reach for *le*: *Je le téléphone*. French verbs here have *à*: *téléphoner à*, *répondre à*, *demander à*. So the pronoun is **lui**: **Je lui téléphone**, **Je lui réponds**, **Je lui demande**. Learn each of these verbs along with its *à*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Say it with à first">
  If you're not sure which pronoun to use, say the whole sentence in your head with the person's name: *Je téléphone à Marie.* Can you hear an *à* before the name? If so, *lui* or *leur* replaces both words. Saying the full version first takes a moment, and it stops you from guessing.
</Callout>
