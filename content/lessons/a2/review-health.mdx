## A model: explaining an injury

Here's Nora, writing to a friend. Look closely at each participle.

<Example fr="Samedi, je suis tombée de vélo." en="On Saturday, I fell off a bike. (said by a woman)" />
<Example fr="Je me suis blessée à la main et je me suis cassé la jambe." en="I hurt my hand and I broke my leg. (said by a woman)" />
<Example fr="J'ai très mal, et je ne peux pas marcher facilement." en="It really hurts, and I can't walk easily." />

*Je me suis blessée* agrees, because *à la main* isn't a direct object. *Je me suis cassé la jambe* doesn't, because *la jambe* is.

And here's the advice that comes back.

## Your turn

Write two short pieces on paper or in a notes app. Invent the details if you like.

**A message about an injury,** in five or six sentences. Include:

1. when it happened, and where you were
2. *tomber* with *être*
3. one reflexive verb that agrees, such as *se blesser*
4. one reflexive verb with a body part after it, such as *se casser le bras*
5. what hurts, using *avoir mal à*
6. an adverb, such as *heureusement* or *vraiment*

**A reply giving advice,** in four or five sentences. Include:

1. *il faut* with an infinitive
2. *il ne faut pas*
3. *tu dois* with a reflexive verb, such as *te reposer*
4. something that isn't necessary, with *tu n'as pas besoin de*

## Check your work

Go through your writing once for each question below.

- Did you use the plain article with *avoir mal à*, and no "my"?
- Does every reflexive verb have *être*, with the pronoun in front?
- Does each participle agree, unless a direct object follows it?
- Does your reflexive pronoun match the subject after *devoir*, and stay *se* after *il faut*?
- Is each short adverb between the two parts of the verb, and each *-ment* adverb after them?

<Callout type="warning" title="Two sentences, two endings">
  These two look so alike that it's easy to treat them the same way. **Elle s'est blessée au genou** agrees, because *au genou* isn't a direct object. **Elle s'est cassé le bras** doesn't, because *le bras* is. Before you add an -e to a reflexive participle, look at what comes straight after it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Tell it in three parts">
  When you need to explain a health problem under pressure, use the same three steps every time: what happened, what hurts, and what you need. *Je suis tombé. J'ai mal au genou. Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous.* Three short sentences give anyone everything they need to help you.
</Callout>
