By the end of this lesson you'll be able to use both French verbs for "to know", and choose the right one for what you mean.

## Two verbs for one English word

English has only "know". French splits it in two. **Savoir** is about knowing facts and how to do things. **Connaître** is about being familiar with people, places and things.

They're both irregular, like the verbs in <LessonLink id="a2-irregular-verbs-present" text="the lesson on common irregular verbs" />.

<VocabTable group="connaitre" />

The *il* form *connaît* has a circumflex over the i. It doesn't separate this word from any other. It's a trace of an s that older French had in the word. You'll also see it in the infinitive *connaître*, and not in other forms.

## Savoir

**Before an infinitive**, *savoir* means you know how to do something, as a skill.

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="Je sais nager." en="I know how to swim." />
<Example fr="Il sait cuisiner." en="He knows how to cook." />
<Example fr="Tu sais danser ?" en="Do you know how to dance?" />

**Before *que* and a simple sentence**, it means you know a fact. Learn this as a pattern for now.

<Example fr="Je sais que tu travailles." en="I know that you're working." />
<Example fr="Nous savons que Paul est à Lyon." en="We know that Paul is in Lyon." />

**On its own**, it's very useful.

<Example fr="Je ne sais pas." en="I don't know." />

## Connaître

Use **connaître** with a person, a place or a thing you're familiar with. It's always followed directly by a noun or a name.

<Example fr="Je connais Paul." en="I know Paul." />
<Example fr="Tu connais Lyon ?" en="Do you know Lyon?" />
<Example fr="Nous connaissons ce café." en="We know this café." />
<Example fr="Vous connaissez Madame Roche ?" en="Do you know Mrs Roche?" />

Sentences like *ce restaurant* use the demonstratives from <LessonLink id="a1-demonstratives" text="the lesson on ce, cet, cette and ces" />.

## Savoir or pouvoir?

Both verbs can go before an infinitive. They're different, and you know *pouvoir* from <LessonLink id="a1-modal-verbs" text="the lesson on pouvoir, vouloir and devoir" />.

- *Je sais nager.* (I know how to swim. It's a skill I have.)
- *Je peux nager.* (I'm able to swim, or I'm allowed to swim. Perhaps the pool is open today.)

## Which one do I need?

Most of the time, the word that comes next decides it.

| What comes next | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| an infinitive | *savoir* | *Je sais cuisiner.* (I know how to cook.) |
| *que* and a sentence | *savoir* | *Je sais que Léa est ici.* (I know that Léa is here.) |
| nothing | *savoir* | *Je sais !* (I know!) |
| a person | *connaître* | *Je connais Karim.* (I know Karim.) |
| a place | *connaître* | *Je connais Nice.* (I know Nice.) |

The table covers the patterns in this lesson. As you read more French, you'll see other uses, but these will get you a long way.

## En français

- *Tu connais Marie ?* (Do you know Marie?)
- *Oui, je connais Marie. Elle travaille à l'hôpital.* (Yes, I know Marie. She works at the hospital.)
- *Elle sait nager ?* (Does she know how to swim?)
- *Je ne sais pas.* (I don't know.)
- *Nous allons à la piscine samedi.* (We're going to the pool on Saturday.)
- *Je sais que Marie adore le sport.* (I know that Marie loves sport.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Two classic mix-ups">
  Mixing up the two verbs is a common mistake. *Je connais nager* is wrong, because a skill needs **savoir**: **Je sais nager**. *Je sais Paul* is wrong too, because a person needs **connaître**: **Je connais Paul**. Look at what comes next. An infinitive or *que* calls for *savoir*. A person or a place calls for *connaître*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Could you meet it?">
  If you could shake hands with it or walk around it, use *connaître*. You can meet Paul, and you can walk around Lyon. You can't shake hands with swimming, or with the fact that someone works. It's a quick picture to carry with you when you can't remember which verb to use.
</Callout>
