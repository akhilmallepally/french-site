By the end of this lesson you'll be able to describe your childhood, and ask other people what theirs was like.

## The phrase that opens every memory

French memories nearly always start the same way. Now that you know <LessonLink id="a2-imparfait-formation" text="the imparfait" />, you can use it.

<VocabTable group="phrases" />

*Petit* matches the speaker: a man says *Quand j'étais petit*, and a woman says *Quand j'étais petite*. The whole phrase sets you up for a list of habits and descriptions.

<Example fr="Quand j'étais petit, j'avais un chien." en="When I was little, I had a dog. (said by a man)" />
<Example fr="Quand j'étais petite, je détestais les carottes." en="When I was little, I hated carrots. (said by a woman)" />

## Home, family and friends

Everything you've learned about people works here. Your family words and <LessonLink id="a1-possessive-adjectives" text="mon, ma and mes" /> come straight back.

<Example fr="Mes parents travaillaient à Rennes." en="My parents worked in Rennes." />
<Example fr="Ma sœur et moi, nous lisions le soir." en="My sister and I used to read in the evening." />

## School and games

Children's games use the patterns from <LessonLink id="a1-hobbies-and-sports" text="the lesson on hobbies and sports" />: *jouer à* for games, and *jouer de* for music.

<VocabTable group="games" />

<Example fr="Je jouais au ballon avec mes copains." en="I played ball with my friends." />
<Example fr="Le maître était très gentil." en="The teacher was very kind." />

## Holidays

Children in France look forward to *les grandes vacances*. You can use the seasons and the weather here, too.

<Example fr="L'été, nous allions à la campagne, et il faisait chaud." en="In summer, we used to go to the countryside, and it was hot." />

## A model text

Here's a woman remembering her childhood. Every verb is in the imparfait, because she's describing how things were and what used to happen.

<Example fr="Quand j'étais petite, j'habitais à la campagne avec mes parents et mon frère." en="When I was little, I lived in the countryside with my parents and my brother." />
<Example fr="Notre maison était petite, mais il y avait un grand jardin." en="Our house was small, but there was a big garden." />
<Example fr="Tous les jours, je jouais avec mon frère." en="Every day, I played with my brother." />
<Example fr="L'été, il faisait chaud et nous allions à la mer." en="In summer, it was hot and we used to go to the seaside." />
<Example fr="Le samedi, ma grand-mère faisait un gâteau. J'adorais ça !" en="On Saturdays, my grandmother made a cake. I loved that!" />

## Asking someone else

To find out about someone's childhood, start with these. The question words are from <LessonLink id="a1-question-words" text="the lesson on question words" />.

<VocabTable group="questions" />

Replies are usually short, and they use the same verb: *J'habitais à Nantes.* (I lived in Nantes.) *Non, je détestais l'école !* (No, I hated school!) *Je jouais au foot.* (I played football.)

## Keeping the conversation going

One memory usually leads to another. When someone tells you about their childhood, respond with a short reaction and then ask a question of your own. *C'était super !* (That was great!) and *Et toi ?* (And you?) are enough to keep things moving. You can also ask for details: *Tu avais quel âge ?* (How old were you?), *Il y avait un parc ?* (Was there a park?), *C'était où ?* (Where was that?). Each of these uses only what you already know, so you can focus on listening.

## En français

- *Tu habitais où, quand tu étais petit ?* (Where did you live when you were little?)
- *À Marseille. Et toi ?* (In Marseille. And you?)
- *À la campagne. Tu aimais l'école ?* (In the countryside. Did you like school?)
- *Oui, j'adorais la maîtresse. Tu jouais à quoi ?* (Yes, I loved the teacher. What did you play?)
- *Je jouais à cache-cache avec ma sœur.* (I played hide-and-seek with my sister.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Quand j'étais, not quand j'ai été">
  In English, "when I was little" looks like any other past. It's natural to try the tense you learned first: *Quand j'ai été petit*. For a description like this, you need the imparfait: **Quand j'étais petit**. Don't use the present either. *Quand je suis petit* can only mean you're still small.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Talk through an old photo">
  Find a photograph from when you were young, and describe it in French. Who was there? How old were you? What was the weather like? *J'avais huit ans. Il faisait beau.* A real picture gives you something specific to say, and it brings back words you might not think of otherwise.
</Callout>
