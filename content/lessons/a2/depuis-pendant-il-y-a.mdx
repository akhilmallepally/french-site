By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say how long something has been going on, how long it lasted, and how long ago it happened.

## Three different questions

French has three short expressions, and each one answers a different question. You've already used *il y a* as a chunk in <LessonLink id="a2-time-expressions-past" text="the lesson on time expressions for the past" />, and *Depuis quand ?* as a fixed phrase in <LessonLink id="a2-at-the-doctor" text="the lesson on the doctor and pharmacy" />. Now you can see how they fit together.

| What you're saying | Use | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| It started in the past and it's still going on. | *depuis* | present | *J'habite ici depuis 2019.* (I've lived here since 2019.) |
| This is how long it lasted, or will last. | *pendant* | any | *J'ai travaillé pendant deux ans.* (I worked for two years.) |
| This is how long ago it happened. | *il y a* | passé composé | *Il y a deux ans, j'ai commencé.* (Two years ago, I started.) |

<VocabTable group="words" />

## Depuis

Use **depuis** for something that began in the past and **is still true now**. The verb is in the **present**, even though English uses "have been".

<Example fr="Elle travaille ici depuis trois ans." en="She's been working here for three years." />
<Example fr="Nous habitons à Nantes depuis 2020." en="We've lived in Nantes since 2020." />
<Example fr="Je suis malade depuis lundi." en="I've been ill since Monday." />

*Depuis* can be followed by a starting point (a date, a day, a year) or by a length of time (three years). English uses "since" for one and "for" for the other, but French uses *depuis* for both. For years, use the numbers from <LessonLink id="a2-numbers-years-ordinals" text="the lesson on numbers over 100, ordinals and years" />.

## Pendant

Use **pendant** to say **how long** something lasted. It's used with the past, the future or a habit, as you'd expect from <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-avoir" text="the lesson on the passé composé with avoir" />. Here, *rester* is the verb you met in <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-etre" text="the lesson on the passé composé with être" />.

<Example fr="J'ai habité à Rome pendant deux ans." en="I lived in Rome for two years." />
<Example fr="Je vais rester pendant trois jours." en="I'm going to stay for three days." />
<Example fr="Il a dormi pendant huit heures." en="He slept for eight hours." />

## Il y a

**Il y a** before a length of time means "ago". It goes with the passé composé, and it usually goes at the start or the end of the sentence.

<Example fr="Il y a un an, nous avons vendu la voiture." en="A year ago, we sold the car." />
<Example fr="Elle est arrivée il y a deux heures." en="She arrived two hours ago." />

Remember that *il y a* has another meaning you've known since A1. The two are easy to tell apart.

| Followed by | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a noun | there is, there are | *Il y a un restaurant.* (There's a restaurant.) |
| a length of time, about the past | ago | *Il y a deux jours.* (Two days ago.) |

## Asking how long

<VocabTable group="questions" />

<Example fr="Depuis combien de temps tu travailles ici ? — Depuis six mois." en="How long have you been working here? — For six months." />
<Example fr="Tu as habité à Lille pendant combien de temps ? — Pendant un an." en="How long did you live in Lille? — For a year." />

## En français

- *Tu travailles ici depuis quand ?* (Since when have you worked here?)
- *Depuis 2021. Et toi ?* (Since 2021. And you?)
- *Je suis arrivée il y a un mois.* (I arrived a month ago.)
- *Tu vas rester ici ?* (Are you going to stay here?)
- *Oui, pendant deux ans.* (Yes, for two years.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't use pendant for 'still going on'">
  English "I've worked here for two years" can trick you into *Je travaille ici pendant deux ans*. That's wrong. For something still going on, it's **Je travaille ici depuis deux ans**. With a positive verb, *depuis* takes the present: **J'habite ici depuis 2019**, and *J'ai habité ici depuis 2019* is wrong. Negative sentences with *depuis* work differently, and they come later.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Is it still going on?">
  Before you choose, ask one question: is it still happening now? If yes, you need *depuis* and the present. If it's over and you're saying how long it took, you need *pendant*. If you're pointing back at when it happened, you need *il y a*. That one question gets you most of the way there.
</Callout>
