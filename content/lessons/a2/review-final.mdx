You've reached the end of A2. *Félicitations !* You've gone from simple sentences in the present to stories, plans, comparisons and emails. This lesson looks back over the whole level, unit by unit.

## Your can-do checklist

Read each line and be honest. If one feels shaky, follow the link and spend a few minutes there.

**Talking about the past.** I can:

- use the passé composé with *avoir* and with *être*
- use common irregular participles
- say what I did at the weekend

Go back to <LessonLink id="a2-review-past" text="the review of talking about the past" /> if you need to.

**Health and the body.** I can:

- name parts of the body and say what hurts
- talk to a doctor or a pharmacist
- give advice with *il faut*

Go back to <LessonLink id="a2-review-health" text="the review of health and the body" /> if you need to.

**Memories and childhood.** I can:

- form and use the imparfait
- choose between the imparfait and the passé composé
- tell a simple story

Go back to <LessonLink id="a2-review-memories" text="the review of memories and childhood" /> if you need to.

**Work and studies.** I can:

- talk about jobs and studies
- choose between *c'est* and *il est*
- choose between *savoir* and *connaître*

Go back to <LessonLink id="a2-review-work" text="the review of work and studies" /> if you need to.

**Pronouns in action.** I can:

- use stressed, direct and indirect object pronouns
- use *y* and *en*
- make the participle agree after a direct object pronoun

Go back to <LessonLink id="a2-review-pronouns" text="the review of pronouns" /> if you need to.

**Travel and plans.** I can:

- use the right preposition with a town or a country
- book a hotel and talk about transport
- talk about the future with the futur simple

Go back to <LessonLink id="a2-review-travel" text="the review of travel and plans" /> if you need to.

**Comparing and describing.** I can:

- compare things with *plus*, *moins*, *aussi* and *autant*
- use *meilleur*, *mieux* and superlatives
- join sentences with *qui*, *que* and *où*

Go back to <LessonLink id="a2-review-comparing" text="the review of comparing and describing" /> if you need to.

**Everyday interactions.** I can:

- say how I feel, and ask someone what's wrong
- give commands with pronouns
- choose the right little word after a verb
- make polite requests, and write an email to a friend

Go back to <LessonLink id="a2-polite-requests" text="the lesson on polite requests" /> or <LessonLink id="a2-writing-an-informal-email" text="the lesson on writing an informal email" /> if you need to.

## Your turn

Write one last piece about yourself, on paper or in a notes app. Aim for around ten sentences, and use at least one thing from each unit. You could include:

1. something you did last weekend
2. how you feel today, and why
3. what you did when you were a child
4. your work or your studies
5. something you've given someone, using a pronoun
6. a trip you'll take next year
7. a comparison between two places you know
8. a polite question, or a piece of advice for a friend

These words may help you to talk about your progress.

<VocabTable />

<Callout type="warning" title="Five last mistakes to check for">
  Read through your paragraph and look for these. **Auxiliary and agreement:** *aller* takes *être*, and the participle agrees: **Elle est allée**. **Qui or que:** a verb follows *qui*, and a subject follows *que*: **le livre que je lis**. **Places:** **à Paris**, **en France**, **au Canada**. **Il ne faut pas** means "you mustn't", not "you don't have to". And **the pronoun goes before the verb**, except in a positive command: **Je le vois**, but **Regarde-le**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Teach it with a mistake">
  Pick one contrast from A2, such as *qui* and *que*, and explain it to someone by writing a wrong sentence first and then correcting it. Choosing a mistake that's realistic is harder than it sounds, and it shows you exactly where the rule's edges are. If you can't invent a good mistake, that's the rule to review.
</Callout>
