By the end of this lesson you'll be able to form the futur simple of many common verbs, and use it to make predictions and promises.

## How it's formed

The future is one of the easiest tenses to build. Take the **infinitive** and add these endings: **-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont**.

<VocabTable group="parler" />

<VocabTable group="finir" />

Verbs ending in **-re** drop the final *-e* first. So *attendre* gives *j'attendrai*, *vendre* gives *je vendrai*, and *prendre* gives *je prendrai*.

<VocabTable group="attendre" />

Other verbs you know follow this rule too: *mettre* (*je mettrai*), *dire* (*je dirai*), *lire* (*je lirai*), *écrire* (*j'écrirai*), *boire* (*je boirai*) and *dormir* (*je dormirai*).

<Example fr="Je parlerai à Marie." en="I will speak to Marie." />
<Example fr="Nous finirons à six heures." en="We will finish at six o'clock." />
<Example fr="Elles prendront le train." en="They will take the train." />

## How it sounds

You can always hear the **r** just before the ending. That's what tells you it's the future. *-ai* and *-ez* sound alike, as in *je parlerai* and *vous parlerez*.

## Two small spelling changes

Some verbs change their spelling slightly in the future.

<VocabTable group="spelling" />

*Acheter* takes a grave accent: *j'achèterai*. *Appeler* doubles its l: *j'appellerai*. *Manger* doesn't change: *je mangerai*.

## Near future or futur simple?

You already know two ways of talking about the future, and they often overlap. In everyday speech, either usually works.

- The **near future** (*aller* + infinitive) is common for plans and things coming soon: *Je vais partir demain*.
- The **futur simple** is common for things further ahead, for promises, for predictions, and in formal writing: *Je partirai en 2030*.

## A weather forecast

- *Quel temps fera-t-il demain ?* (What will the weather be like tomorrow?)
- *Demain, il fera beau à Nice.* (Tomorrow, the weather will be nice in Nice.)
- *Et à Lille ?* (And in Lille?)
- *Il fera froid à Lille, et il y aura du vent.* (It will be cold in Lille, and it will be windy.)

## Travel plans

<VocabTable group="travel" />

*L'année prochaine, j'irai au Canada avec ma sœur. Nous prendrons l'avion en juin. Nous serons à Montréal et nous irons au Québec. Je prendrai des photos et j'écrirai à mes parents.* (Next year, I'll go to Canada with my sister. We'll take the plane in June. We'll be in Montréal, and we'll go to Québec. I'll take some photos and I'll write to my parents.)

<Callout type="warning" title="No extra e in -re verbs">
  Verbs ending in *-re* lose that final *-e* before the endings. It's **je prendrai**, not *je prenderai*, and **je vendrai**, not *je venderai*. And the *nous* and *ils* endings are different in the future: **nous parlerons**, **ils parleront**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="The endings are avoir">
  You already know these endings. Compare *j'ai*, *tu as*, *il a*, *ils ont* with the future endings *-ai*, *-as*, *-a*, *-ont*. They're the same. Only *nous* and *vous* are shorter, using *-ons* and *-ez* on their own. So the future is just the infinitive with a piece of *avoir* attached to it.
</Callout>
