By the end of this lesson you'll be able to avoid repeating a noun after *du*, *des*, a number or a quantity, by using one short word.

## What en replaces

**En** is a pronoun that replaces a noun introduced by *de* in some form. It's different from the preposition *en* that you know from *en été* and *en France*.

<VocabTable group="core" />

**Du, de la, de l', des + noun**, from <LessonLink id="a1-partitive-articles" text="the lesson on du, de la and des" />:

<Example fr="Tu veux du café ? Oui, j'en veux." en="Do you want some coffee? Yes, I want some." />
<Example fr="Il y a des pommes ? Oui, je vais en prendre." en="Are there any apples? Yes, I'm going to take some." />

**A number, or un / une.** The number stays, at the end of the sentence.

<Example fr="J'ai deux frères. J'en ai deux." en="I have two brothers. I have two." />
<Example fr="J'ai une sœur. J'en ai une." en="I have one sister. I have one." />

**A quantity expression**, from <LessonLink id="a2-quantities" text="the lesson on expressions of quantity" />. The quantity stays too.

<Example fr="J'ai beaucoup de livres. J'en ai beaucoup." en="I have a lot of books. I have a lot." />
<Example fr="Tu as combien de frères ? J'en ai trois." en="How many brothers do you have? I have three." />

## Verbs with de

Some verbs are followed by *de*, and *en* replaces *de* + a thing.

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

<Example fr="Tu as besoin de ce livre ? Oui, j'en ai besoin." en="Do you need this book? Yes, I need it." />
<Example fr="Vous parlez du film ? Oui, nous en parlons." en="Are you talking about the film? Yes, we're talking about it." />
<Example fr="Tu as envie de chocolat ? Oui, j'en ai envie." en="Do you feel like some chocolate? Yes, I feel like some." />

For **a person**, French uses *de* with a stressed pronoun instead: *parler de lui* (to talk about him), from <LessonLink id="a2-stressed-pronouns" text="the lesson on stressed pronouns" />.

## Where it goes

*En* follows the same rules as <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-y" text="the pronoun y" />. It goes before the verb. In a negative, *ne* becomes *n'*.

<Example fr="Je n'ai pas de pain. Je n'en ai pas." en="I don't have any bread. I don't have any." />
<Example fr="Je ne veux pas de thé. Je n'en veux pas." en="I don't want any tea. I don't want any." />

After *aller*, *pouvoir* and *vouloir*, it goes before the infinitive.

<Example fr="Je veux en acheter." en="I want to buy some." />

You won't use it in the passé composé in this lesson. That's next, in <LessonLink id="a2-pronouns-in-passe-compose" text="the lesson on pronouns in the passé composé" />.

## Answering with en

*En* is especially useful for short answers, because it saves you from repeating the noun. Listen for *du*, *de la*, *des*, a number or a word of quantity in the question, then start your answer with *j'en*: *Tu as des frères ? Oui, j'en ai un* (Do you have brothers? Yes, I have one). The number you give at the end is usually all the other person wanted to know.

## y or en?

The little word after the verb decides it.

| What it replaces | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *à* + a place or thing | *y* | *Je vais à Lyon.* → *J'**y** vais.* (I'm going there.) |
| *de* + a thing, or a quantity | *en* | *Je parle du film.* → *J'**en** parle.* (I'm talking about it.) |
| *à* + a person | *lui* / *leur* | *Je parle à Paul.* → *Je **lui** parle.* (I'm speaking to him.) |

## En français

- *Tu veux du pain ?* (Do you want some bread?)
- *Oui, j'en veux un peu.* (Yes, I'd like a little.)
- *Tu as des enfants ?* (Do you have children?)
- *Oui, j'en ai deux. Et toi ?* (Yes, I have two. And you?)
- *Je n'en ai pas.* (I don't have any.)

<Callout type="warning" title="You can't just stop at the number">
  English can say "I have two", and leave the noun out. French can't: *J'ai deux* is wrong. You need **J'en ai deux**. And don't use *le*, *la* or *les* in place of *en*: as you saw in <LessonLink id="a2-direct-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on direct object pronouns" />, those replace a noun with a definite word. For *un*, *du* or *des* nouns, it has to be **en**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep the number, lose the noun">
  When you use *en* with a number, think of it as a simple swap. The noun goes, *en* moves to the front of the verb, and the number stays where it was: *J'ai deux chats* becomes *J'en ai deux*. If there's a number or quantity in the original, there has to be one at the end of the new sentence.
</Callout>
