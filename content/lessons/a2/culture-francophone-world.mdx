By the end of this lesson you'll know where French is spoken around the world, and you'll be ready for some of the differences you may meet.

## Beyond France

French isn't only the language of France. It's spoken by hundreds of millions of people, on several continents. In some places it's an official language. In others, it's widely used in education, business or everyday life, alongside other languages. People who speak French are called **francophones**.

<VocabTable group="words" />

You can use the prepositions from <LessonLink id="a2-prepositions-with-places" text="the lesson on prepositions" /> to talk about these places.

<VocabTable group="places" />

## Europe

Besides France, French is spoken in **Belgium**, in **Switzerland**, in **Luxembourg** and in **Monaco**.

<Example fr="On parle français en Belgique et en Suisse." en="French is spoken in Belgium and in Switzerland." />
<Example fr="On parle français au Luxembourg et à Monaco." en="French is spoken in Luxembourg and in Monaco." />

## North America and the Caribbean

In **Canada**, French is especially strong in **Québec**, and it's also spoken elsewhere in the country. In the Caribbean, French is spoken in **Guadeloupe**, **Martinique** and **Haïti**.

<Example fr="On parle français au Québec." en="French is spoken in Québec." />
<Example fr="On parle français en Guadeloupe et en Martinique." en="French is spoken in Guadeloupe and Martinique." />
<Example fr="Haïti est un pays francophone." en="Haiti is a French-speaking country." />

## Africa

French is widely used in many African countries, including **Sénégal**, **Côte d'Ivoire** and **Cameroun**, and in the Maghreb countries of north-west Africa.

<Example fr="On parle français au Sénégal et au Cameroun." en="French is spoken in Senegal and Cameroon." />
<Example fr="Abidjan est en Côte d'Ivoire." en="Abidjan is in Côte d'Ivoire." />

## The Pacific

In the Pacific, French is spoken in **Tahiti** and **New Caledonia**.

<Example fr="On parle français à Tahiti et en Nouvelle-Calédonie." en="French is spoken in Tahiti and New Caledonia." />

## Talking about these places

When you talk about the French-speaking world, the small word in front of each place still follows the patterns you've learned. Most of the countries in this lesson are either masculine, which gives *au Sénégal* and *au Cameroun*, or feminine, which gives *en Belgique* and *en Côte d'Ivoire*. Islands vary, so learn those one at a time, as they come up. *On parle français* (French is spoken) is the most useful phrase in this lesson, and it works with every place on the list.

## Local variation

French isn't exactly the same everywhere. These differences are small, and you'll be understood wherever you go.

Numbers are one example. As you saw in <LessonLink id="a1-numbers-21-100" text="the lesson on numbers 21 to 100" />, *septante* and *nonante* are used in Belgium and Switzerland, in place of *soixante-dix* and *quatre-vingt-dix*.

Meals are another. In parts of Belgium, Switzerland and Québec, **le déjeuner** can be the morning meal, **le dîner** is at midday, and **le souper** is in the evening.

<VocabTable group="local" />

In Québec, you'll also hear *la fin de semaine* for the weekend, and *magasiner* for going shopping.

## En français

- *Tu parles français au Sénégal ?* (Do you speak French in Senegal?)
- *Oui, et je parle aussi wolof.* (Yes, and I also speak Wolof.)
- *Il y a beaucoup de langues au Sénégal ?* (Are there a lot of languages in Senegal?)
- *Oui, beaucoup !* (Yes, a lot!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Dîner can mean lunch">
  If someone in Québec invites you for *le dîner*, it may be in the middle of the day. In France, *le déjeuner* is at midday and *le dîner* is in the evening. In parts of Belgium, Switzerland and Québec, the words shift earlier. When you're making plans there, it's sensible to confirm the time.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="No single correct French">
  Every region has its own words and rhythm, and a word from Dakar or Québec isn't a mistake. When you hear something unfamiliar, treat it as new vocabulary. Ask what it means, try it out in that place, and enjoy it. The French you're learning will carry you across all of these places.
</Callout>
