By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say "the biggest", "the least expensive" and "the best" in French, and say where something is the best.

## From comparative to superlative

You know *plus* and *moins* from <LessonLink id="a2-comparatives" text="the lesson on comparatives" />. To make a superlative, add the article: **le**, **la** or **les**. The article and the adjective both agree with the noun.

<VocabTable group="forms" />

<Example fr="C'est le plus grand pays." en="It's the biggest country." />
<Example fr="C'est la plus petite ville." en="It's the smallest town." />
<Example fr="Ce sont les moins chers." en="They're the cheapest." />

## Where the adjective goes

The position depends on the adjective, as you learned in <LessonLink id="a1-adjective-agreement" text="the lesson on adjective agreement" />.

**Most adjectives** follow the noun. In a superlative, they stay there, and the article is **repeated**.

<Example fr="C'est la ville la plus intéressante." en="It's the most interesting city." />
<Example fr="Le film le plus drôle." en="The funniest film." />

**BAGS adjectives** normally come before the noun. In a superlative, they can stay before it, or they can go after it with the article repeated. Both are correct.

- *la plus grande ville* (the biggest city)
- *la ville la plus grande* (the biggest city)

The article is what makes it a superlative. *Une ville plus grande* is "a bigger city", but *la ville la plus grande* is "the biggest city". In the plural, *les* does the same job, and the adjective takes its plural ending: *les plus grandes villes de France* (the biggest cities in France), *les villes les plus intéressantes* (the most interesting cities).

## "In" is de

After a superlative, French uses **de** for "in" or "of". It joins up with *le* and *les* as usual.

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="C'est la plus belle ville de France." en="It's the most beautiful city in France." />
<Example fr="C'est le plus grand pays du monde." en="It's the biggest country in the world." />
<Example fr="Marie est la meilleure élève de la classe." en="Marie is the best pupil in the class." />

## The best

**Bon** becomes **le meilleur**, which agrees like any adjective, and usually goes before its noun.

<Example fr="C'est le meilleur restaurant de Lille." en="It's the best restaurant in Lille." />
<Example fr="Ce sont les meilleures fraises du marché." en="They're the best strawberries at the market." />

**Bien** becomes **le mieux**. As an adverb, it always takes **le**, even for a woman. So do all superlatives with adverbs.

<Example fr="Léa danse le mieux." en="Léa dances the best." />
<Example fr="Il parle le plus vite." en="He speaks the fastest." />
<Example fr="Anne travaille le moins." en="Anne works the least." />

## Using it

You can use all this with the places and the food you already know.

- *Paris est la plus grande ville de France.* (Paris is the biggest city in France.)
- *Le fromage de ma mère est le meilleur.* (My mother's cheese is the best.)
- *Le train est le moins cher.* (The train is the cheapest.)

## En français

- *Quelle est la plus belle ville de France ?* (What's the most beautiful city in France?)
- *Pour moi, c'est Annecy.* (For me, it's Annecy.)
- *Et le meilleur restaurant d'Annecy ?* (And the best restaurant in Annecy?)
- *C'est le restaurant de mon frère, évidemment !* (It's my brother's restaurant, obviously!)

<Callout type="warning" title="De, not dans or en">
  English says "the best restaurant **in** Paris", so it's tempting to use *dans* or *en*. After a superlative, French uses **de**: **le meilleur restaurant de Paris**, **la plus belle ville de France**, **le plus grand pays du monde**. It's *de* even if you'd normally say *en France* or *à Paris*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask for the best">
  Superlatives are one of the quickest ways to get good advice when you're travelling. Stop in a shop or a café and ask: *Quel est le meilleur restaurant du quartier ?* (What's the best restaurant in the neighbourhood?). People love giving an opinion on this, and you'll find places a guidebook would never mention.
</Callout>
