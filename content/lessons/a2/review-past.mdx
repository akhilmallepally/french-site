## A model: last weekend

Here's Sarah's weekend, line by line. Look at each verb and ask yourself why it uses *avoir* or *être*.

<Example fr="Le week-end dernier, je suis allée à Strasbourg avec Paul." en="Last weekend, I went to Strasbourg with Paul. (said by a woman)" />
<Example fr="Nous avons pris le train samedi matin." en="We took the train on Saturday morning." />
<Example fr="Nous avons visité la cathédrale et nous avons bu un chocolat chaud." en="We visited the cathedral and we drank a hot chocolate." />
<Example fr="Dimanche, je n'ai rien fait, et Paul n'a vu personne." en="On Sunday, I did nothing, and Paul saw nobody." />

## A model: last holiday

Now the same writer goes further back.

<Example fr="L'été dernier, nous sommes partis à la mer." en="Last summer, we went to the seaside." />
<Example fr="Il y a trois mois, je suis allée à la montagne." en="Three months ago, I went to the mountains. (said by a woman)" />
<Example fr="J'ai déjà vu la Bretagne, mais je n'ai jamais vu les Alpes." en="I've already seen Brittany, but I've never seen the Alps." />
<Example fr="C'était super, mais je n'ai pas encore lu mon guide !" en="It was great, but I haven't read my guidebook yet!" />

Notice the agreement. *Nous sommes rentrés* has an -s because the subject is plural and includes a man. *Je suis allée* has an -e because Sarah is the one speaking.

## Your turn

Write two short paragraphs on paper or in a notes app. They can be true or invented.

**Last weekend,** in five or six sentences. Include:

1. a time word for each sentence or two (*samedi*, *dimanche matin*, *hier*)
2. two verbs with *avoir*, one regular and one irregular
3. two verbs with *être*
4. something you didn't do, with *rien*, *personne* or *ne…pas*

**Your last holiday,** in five or six sentences. Include:

1. when it was, with *l'été dernier*, a year, or *il y a* and a length of time
2. where you went
3. something you've never done, with *jamais*
4. something you've already done, with *déjà*

## Check your work

Go through your paragraphs once for each question below.

- Does each verb have the right auxiliary, *avoir* or *être*?
- With *être*, does the participle agree with the subject?
- Is *rien* before the participle, and *personne* after it?
- Are *déjà*, *jamais* and *pas encore* between the two parts of the verb?

<Callout type="warning" title="Agreement only comes with être">
  After practising agreement, it's easy to add endings everywhere. Keep the two groups apart. **Elle est allée** agrees, because the auxiliary is *être*. **Elle a mangé** doesn't, because the auxiliary is *avoir*. Before you add an -e or an -s to a participle, look at the verb just in front of it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Colour your auxiliaries">
  When you've finished a piece of writing, take two coloured pens or highlights and mark every *avoir* in one colour and every *être* in another. Then go along the *être* colour alone, checking each ending. It takes a minute, and seeing the two groups side by side makes mistakes jump out.
</Callout>
