By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say where you are, where you're going and where you come from, and you'll be comfortable with five more useful prepositions.

## Cities and countries

You know many of these countries from <LessonLink id="a1-countries-nationalities" text="the lesson on countries and nationalities" />. The little word in front of a place depends on what kind of place it is.

| Place | "in" or "to" | "from" |
|---|---|---|
| a city | **à** | **de** (or **d'**) |
| a feminine country, or any country starting with a vowel | **en** | **de** (or **d'**) |
| a masculine country starting with a consonant | **au** | **du** |
| a plural country | **aux** | **des** |

These forms come from what you learned in <LessonLink id="a1-contractions-a-de" text="the lesson on à and de with articles" />.

<Example fr="J'habite à Nantes." en="I live in Nantes." />
<Example fr="Elle est en Italie." en="She's in Italy." />
<Example fr="Nous allons au Japon." en="We're going to Japan." />
<Example fr="Ils travaillent aux États-Unis." en="They work in the United States." />

As a rule of thumb, a country ending in *-e* is usually feminine: *la France*, *l'Italie*, *l'Espagne*. A few aren't, and you'll need to learn them. The best known are *le Mexique*, *le Cambodge*, *le Mozambique* and *le Zimbabwe*: *au Mexique*.

<VocabTable group="countries" />

Regions, American states and islands follow other patterns, and you can learn those as you meet them.

## Coming from

To say where someone comes from, use *venir* with **de**, **d'**, **du** or **des**.

<Example fr="Je viens de Bruxelles." en="I come from Brussels." />
<Example fr="Tu viens de France ?" en="Do you come from France?" />
<Example fr="Elle vient d'Espagne." en="She comes from Spain." />
<Example fr="Il vient du Portugal." en="He comes from Portugal." />
<Example fr="Nous venons des Pays-Bas." en="We come from the Netherlands." />

With an infinitive, *venir de* means something else: "to have just done". You'll find that in <LessonLink id="a2-venir-de-en-train-de" text="the lesson on the recent past and actions in progress" />.

## Dans

**Dans** means "in" or "inside". It's used with an article and a place or a container.

<Example fr="Je suis dans la rue." en="I'm in the street." />
<Example fr="Le téléphone est dans le sac." en="The phone is in the bag." />

*À la maison* and *dans la maison* are close in meaning. *À la maison* means "at home", and *dans la maison* puts more stress on being inside the house.

## Chez

**Chez** means "at the home of" or "at the place of". It goes with a person, or a person's place of work.

<Example fr="Je vais chez Paul." en="I'm going to Paul's." />
<Example fr="Elle est chez le médecin." en="She's at the doctor's." />

You've already seen *chez moi* and *chez lui* in <LessonLink id="a2-stressed-pronouns" text="the lesson on stressed pronouns" />.

## Avec, sans, pour

**Avec** means "with", and **sans** means "without". After *sans*, there's no article with a general idea.

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="Un café sans sucre, s'il vous plaît." en="A coffee without sugar, please." />
<Example fr="Je voyage avec des amis." en="I'm travelling with friends." />

**Pour** means "for". It can show a purpose, a destination or a person who receives something.

<Example fr="Ce cadeau est pour toi." en="This present is for you." />
<Example fr="Je suis ici pour apprendre." en="I'm here to learn." />
<Example fr="C'est le train pour Lyon." en="It's the train to Lyon." />

## En français

- *Tu viens d'où ?* (Where do you come from?)
- *Je viens du Canada. Je suis à Lille pour travailler.* (I come from Canada. I'm in Lille to work.)
- *Tu vas au Québec cet été ?* (Are you going to Québec this summer?)
- *Non, je vais aux États-Unis, chez ma sœur.* (No, I'm going to the United States, to my sister's.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Au Canada, but à Montréal">
  Countries and cities take different words. It's **au Canada**, but **à Montréal**, and **en France**, but **à Paris**. Check whether you're naming a city or a country before you choose. And don't mix up this *en* with the pronoun in <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the lesson on the pronoun en" />, which replaces a noun.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Learn each country with le or la">
  The article tells you everything. If you know it's *le Portugal*, you know it's *au Portugal* and *du Portugal*. If you know it's *la Grèce*, you know it's *en Grèce* and *de Grèce*. Each time you learn a new country's name, say it first with its article, and the two prepositions will follow from that.
</Callout>
