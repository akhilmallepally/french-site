By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say what you've just done, what you're busy doing right now, and what you're about to do.

## Just done: venir de

To say something has **just** happened, use the present of *venir*, then **de**, then an infinitive. The present of *venir* is in <LessonLink id="a2-irregular-verbs-present" text="the lesson on common irregular verbs" />.

<VocabTable group="core" />

<Example fr="Je viens de déjeuner." en="I've just had lunch." />
<Example fr="Tu viens de finir ?" en="Have you just finished?" />
<Example fr="Paul vient de rentrer." en="Paul has just got home." />
<Example fr="Nous venons d'arriver." en="We've just arrived." />

*De* becomes *d'* before a vowel or silent *h*. Although the meaning is about the past, the verb is in the **present**.

You'll need a couple of time words here.

<VocabTable group="time" />

<Example fr="Elle vient de sortir à l'instant." en="She went out just this moment." />

## In progress: être en train de

To stress that you're **in the middle of** doing something right now, use the present of <LessonLink id="a1-etre-present" text="être" />, then **en train de**, then an infinitive.

<Example fr="Je suis en train de travailler." en="I'm in the middle of working." />
<Example fr="Ils sont en train de manger." en="They're in the middle of eating." />
<Example fr="Tu es en train de lire ?" en="Are you busy reading?" />

You don't have to use this. The simple present can already mean an action in progress: *Je travaille* can mean "I'm working". *En train de* adds emphasis, so you use it when you want to underline that something's happening at this moment.

With a reflexive verb, from <LessonLink id="a1-daily-routine-reflexive" text="the lesson on daily routines" />, the pronoun matches the subject.

<Example fr="Je suis en train de me préparer." en="I'm getting ready." />

## The trio

Together with the near future, from <LessonLink id="a1-near-future" text="the lesson on the near future" />, you've got three ways to place an action around right now.

| When | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| very soon | *aller* + infinitive | *Je vais manger.* (I'm going to eat.) |
| right now | present, or *être en train de* | *Je mange.* (I'm eating.) |
| only just | *venir de* + infinitive | *Je viens de manger.* (I've just eaten.) |

<Example fr="En ce moment, je suis en train de chercher du travail." en="At the moment, I'm looking for work." />

Notice that each structure's first verb is in the present, and the main verb stays in the infinitive, except in the plain present in the middle row.

## Choosing the right one

To decide which structure you need, think about where the action is compared with right now. If it's over and only a moment ago, use *venir de*. If it's happening as you speak and you want to underline that, use *être en train de*. If it hasn't started yet, use *aller*. *À l'instant* fits naturally with *venir de*, and *en ce moment* fits naturally with the present or *en train de*.

## En français

- *Salut, Léa ! Tu es où ?* (Hi, Léa! Where are you?)
- *Je viens d'arriver. Et Paul ?* (I've just arrived. And Paul?)
- *Il est en train de manger.* (He's in the middle of eating.)
- *Et Marie ?* (And Marie?)
- *Elle vient de finir. Elle va arriver.* (She's just finished. She's going to arrive.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Venir de can also mean 'come from'">
  *Je viens de manger* means "I've just eaten", but *Je viens de Lyon* means "I come from Lyon". With an infinitive, it's about the recent past. With a place, it's about where you're from. That second use comes later, in <LessonLink id="a2-prepositions-with-places" text="the lesson on prepositions with places" />.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="You're coming from it">
  *Venir* means "to come", and you can picture the recent past as somewhere you've just come from. *Je viens de manger*: you've just walked out of the action of eating. *Je vais manger*: you're walking towards it. *Aller* and *venir* are opposites, and so are the two times they point to.
</Callout>
