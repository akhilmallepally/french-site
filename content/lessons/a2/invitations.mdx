By the end of this lesson you'll be able to invite someone out, accept an invitation with enthusiasm, and say no without causing offence.

## Inviting someone

With a friend, there are several easy ways. You can use *vouloir*, from <LessonLink id="a1-modal-verbs" text="the lesson on pouvoir, vouloir and devoir" />, or start by asking if someone's free, as in *Tu es libre samedi ?*.

<VocabTable group="inviting" />

<Example fr="Tu veux venir au cinéma ?" en="Do you want to come to the cinema?" />
<Example fr="Ça te dit d'aller au parc ?" en="Do you fancy going to the park?" />
<Example fr="On se retrouve à 20 h devant le cinéma." en="Let's meet at 8 p.m. outside the cinema." />
<Example fr="Tu viens chez moi samedi ?" en="Are you coming to my place on Saturday?" />

*Chez* comes from <LessonLink id="a2-prepositions-with-places" text="the lesson on prepositions" />. For plans, use the near future, from <LessonLink id="a1-near-future" text="the lesson on the near future" />: *On va manger au restaurant* (We're going to eat at the restaurant).

With someone you call *vous*, keep it more formal: *Vous êtes libre samedi ? Voulez-vous venir dîner ?*

## Saying yes

You know *D'accord !* from <LessonLink id="a1-writing-a-short-message" text="the lesson on writing a short message" />. Here are some warmer ways.

<VocabTable group="accepting" />

<Example fr="Bonne idée ! Je viens avec plaisir." en="Good idea! I'd love to come." />

## Saying no politely

In French, as in English, it's kind to soften a refusal. Thank the person, say you're sorry, and give a short reason. You know *désolé / désolée* and *occupé / occupée* already.

<VocabTable group="declining" />

To give a reason, use this pattern with *parce que* and a simple sentence: *Je ne peux pas parce que je dois travailler* (I can't because I have to work).

<Example fr="Désolé, je ne peux pas. Je suis occupé." en="Sorry, I can't. I'm busy." />
<Example fr="Merci pour l'invitation, mais je dois travailler." en="Thank you for the invitation, but I have to work." />
<Example fr="C'est gentil. Peut-être une autre fois ?" en="That's kind. Maybe another time?" />

## Making the plan clear

Once someone has said yes, settle the details: the day, the time and the place. *On se retrouve à…* takes care of the time, and you can add a place with *devant* (in front of): *devant la gare*, *devant le cinéma*. If you're writing instead of talking, you can use the short styles from your text messages, and end with *À samedi !* (See you on Saturday!). The same three details work for a coffee, a meal or a trip.

## With a friend

- *Salut ! Tu es libre samedi ?* (Hi! Are you free on Saturday?)
- *Oui, pourquoi ?* (Yes, why?)
- *Ça te dit d'aller au restaurant ?* (Do you fancy going to a restaurant?)
- *Avec plaisir ! On se retrouve à quelle heure ?* (With pleasure! What time shall we meet?)
- *À 19 h, devant la gare.* (At 7 p.m., outside the station.)
- *D'accord, à samedi !* (OK, see you on Saturday!)

## More formally

- *Bonjour, madame. Vous êtes libre vendredi soir ? Voulez-vous venir dîner ?* (Hello, madam. Are you free on Friday evening? Would you like to come to dinner?)
- *C'est gentil, merci pour l'invitation. Malheureusement, je ne peux pas parce que je travaille.* (That's kind, thank you for the invitation. Unfortunately, I can't because I'm working.)
- *Peut-être une autre fois ?* (Maybe another time?)
- *Oui, avec plaisir.* (Yes, with pleasure.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't stop at non">
  A bare *Non* or *Je ne veux pas* can sound rude when someone has invited you. French speakers usually soften a refusal, with thanks and a reason: **Merci, c'est gentil, mais je ne peux pas.** Keep *je veux* out of it altogether, and use **je ne peux pas**, which sounds as if you'd have liked to come.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Offer another time">
  Turning someone down is easier when you hand them something back. After your reason, add *Peut-être une autre fois ?* or suggest a different day: *Dimanche, je suis libre.* It shows that you're saying no to the date, and not to the person, and it keeps the friendship going without any awkwardness.
</Callout>
