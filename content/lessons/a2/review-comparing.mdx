## A model comparison

Here's a short comparison of two places. Look at how each sentence uses something from this unit.

<Example fr="Je compare deux villes : Lille et Nice." en="I'm comparing two towns: Lille and Nice." />
<Example fr="J'habite à Lille, mais j'ai habité à Nice pendant trois ans." en="I live in Lille, but I lived in Nice for three years." />
<Example fr="Lille est une ville plus animée que Nice, et il y a plus d'étudiants." en="Lille is a livelier town than Nice, and there are more students." />
<Example fr="À Nice, il fait moins froid, et la mer est magnifique." en="In Nice, it's less cold, and the sea is magnificent." />
<Example fr="Le quartier où j'habite est le plus calme de la ville." en="The neighbourhood where I live is the quietest in the town." />
<Example fr="C'est une ville que j'adore." en="It's a town I love." />
<Example fr="Je préfère Lille." en="I prefer Lille." />
<Example fr="Pour moi, la meilleure ville de France, c'est Lille !" en="For me, the best town in France is Lille!" />

You met *préférer* in <LessonLink id="a2-comparatives" text="the lesson on comparatives" />.

## A model about two homes

You can do the same with homes.

<Example fr="Notre appartement est moins grand que la maison de mes parents." en="Our flat isn't as big as my parents' house." />
<Example fr="Il y a autant de pièces, mais le salon est plus petit." en="There are as many rooms, but the living room is smaller." />
<Example fr="La cuisine que j'ai rangée hier est la plus belle pièce !" en="The kitchen I tidied yesterday is the most beautiful room!" />

## How to organize it

A comparison is easier to follow if you take one topic at a time, and use both places in each sentence. Start by saying what you're comparing. Then choose two or three topics, such as size, noise and shops. Finish with a superlative, or with the one you prefer. Two short phrases help you make your point: *La différence, c'est le bruit* (The difference is the noise) and *L'avantage, c'est la mer* (The advantage is the sea).

## Your turn

Write a paragraph comparing two cities or two homes, on paper or in a notes app. It can be real or invented. Aim for six to eight sentences, and include:

1. at least two comparisons, one with an adjective and one with a noun
2. *meilleur* or *mieux*
3. a superlative, with *de* for "in"
4. a sentence with *qui*, *que* or *où*
5. a participle after *que*, with agreement if it's needed

## Check your work

Go through your paragraph once for each question below.

- Did you use *que* for "than"?
- Does each adjective agree with its subject?
- Did you use *meilleur* with nouns and *mieux* with verbs?
- After a superlative, is "in" written as *de*?
- After *que*, does the participle agree with the noun it stands for?

<Callout type="warning" title="Meilleur or mieux?">
  These two are easy to confuse, because English has only "better". **Meilleur** goes with a noun, and it agrees: **une meilleure ville**. **Mieux** goes with a verb, and it never changes: **elle chante mieux**. If you can't remember which you need, look at what the word describes. A noun takes *meilleur*, and a verb takes *mieux*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="One topic, both places">
  The weakest comparisons describe one place completely, then the other, and leave the reader to do the comparing. Choose one topic at a time, and mention both places in the same sentence: *Il fait plus chaud à Nice qu'à Lille.* Your paragraph gets shorter, and the comparison is clearer.
</Callout>
