By the end of this lesson you'll be able to ask for things and for help, and choose how polite to be.

## Asking someone you call tu

With friends and family, a simple question with *pouvoir* is all you need. *Tu pourrais* is a little softer.

<VocabTable group="informal" />

<Example fr="Tu peux fermer la fenêtre ?" en="Can you close the window?" />
<Example fr="Tu pourrais m'aider ?" en="Could you help me?" />

## Asking someone you call vous

With strangers, in shops and at work, use *vous*. You know *vous pouvez* from <LessonLink id="a1-modal-verbs" text="the lesson on modal verbs" /> and *je voudrais* as a fixed phrase. These forms are more polite.

<VocabTable group="formal" />

The forms ending in *-rais* and *-riez* belong to a verb tense that you'll learn properly at B1. For now, treat them as fixed phrases and don't try to build others. Each one is followed by an infinitive, or by a noun after *Je pourrais avoir*.

<Example fr="Pourriez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ?" en="Could you repeat that, please?" />
<Example fr="Est-ce que vous pourriez m'aider ?" en="Could you help me?" />
<Example fr="Je pourrais avoir un verre d'eau ?" en="Could I have a glass of water?" />
<Example fr="Pourriez-vous m'apporter l'addition ?" en="Could you bring me the bill?" />

## How direct does it sound?

| Form | Example | How it sounds | Use it with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bare command | *Donnez-moi l'addition.* (Give me the bill.) | Abrupt, almost rude | Almost no one |
| *Je voudrais* | *Je voudrais l'addition.* (I'd like the bill.) | Polite and clear | Shops, cafés, hotels |
| *Pourriez-vous* | *Pourriez-vous m'apporter l'addition ?* (Could you bring me the bill?) | Very polite | Strangers, formal situations |

## Polite extras

<VocabTable group="extras" />

You already know *s'il vous plaît*, *merci beaucoup*, *pardon*, *je vous en prie* from <LessonLink id="a1-culture-politeness" text="the lesson on French politeness" />, and *avec plaisir* from <LessonLink id="a2-invitations" text="the lesson on invitations" />. *Merci de* is for signs and written notes: *Merci de fermer la porte* (Please close the door).

## In different places

The same patterns work almost everywhere.

**At a hotel,** you can reuse what you learned in <LessonLink id="a2-booking-a-hotel" text="the lesson on booking a hotel" />: *Je voudrais une chambre pour deux nuits* (I'd like a room for two nights).

**In the street,** start with an apology:

- *Excusez-moi de vous déranger. Pourriez-vous m'indiquer la gare ?* (Sorry to bother you. Could you show me the way to the station?)
- *Oui, c'est tout droit.* (Yes, it's straight ahead.)
- *Merci beaucoup, madame.* (Thank you very much, madam.)
- *Je vous en prie.* (You're welcome.)

**At a restaurant:**

- *Pardon, monsieur. Je pourrais avoir du pain ?* (Excuse me. Could I have some bread?)
- *Bien sûr, tout de suite.* (Of course, right away.)
- *Et pourriez-vous m'apporter l'addition ?* (And could you bring me the bill?)

**On the phone or at the office,** as in <LessonLink id="a2-phone-calls-and-messages" text="the lesson on phone calls and messages" />:

- *Pourriez-vous parler plus lentement ?* (Could you speak more slowly?)
- *Pas de problème.* (No problem.)

**With a friend:**

- *Tu peux me passer le sel ?* (Can you pass me the salt?)
- *Tiens.* (Here you are.)
- *Merci !* (Thanks!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Three ways to sound rude by accident">
  **A bare command is too abrupt.** *Donnez-moi l'addition* may be correct, but it sounds like an order. Use **Je voudrais** or **Pourriez-vous**. **Je veux sounds demanding,** as you saw in <LessonLink id="a1-modal-verbs" text="the lesson on modal verbs" />. Don't use it when you're asking for something. **Don't use tu with a stranger.** *Tu peux m'aider ?* is fine with a friend, but not with someone in the street.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Give a small reason">
  People are much happier to help when you say why you're asking. Add one short sentence: *Pourriez-vous parler plus lentement ? Je ne parle pas très bien français.* You don't need perfect grammar for this, and the other person will usually slow down and make an effort for you.
</Callout>
