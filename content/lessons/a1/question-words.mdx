By the end of this lesson you'll be able to ask where, when, how, why, how many, who and what, and choose a question style that suits the person you're talking to.

## The question words

In <LessonLink id="a1-yes-no-questions" text="the lesson on yes/no questions" /> you learned three ways of asking something that can be answered with *oui* or *non*. Question words let you ask for real information.

<VocabTable group="words" />

A note on *où*: the accent matters. **Où** means "where" and **ou** without an accent means "or".

## Three styles again

The three styles you know all work with question words.

| Style | Example | Sounds |
|---|---|---|
| Word at the end | *Tu habites où ?* | relaxed |
| Word + *est-ce que* | *Où est-ce que tu habites ?* | neutral |
| Word + inversion | *Où habites-tu ?* | formal |

*Pourquoi* is the exception to the first style. It normally goes at the start, even in relaxed speech: *Pourquoi tu danses ?*

Before a vowel or silent *h*, *que* shortens as usual: *Où est-ce qu'il habite ?*

<Example fr="Tu finis quand ?" en="When do you finish?" />
<Example fr="Comment est-ce que tu vas à Nantes ?" en="How are you getting to Nantes?" />
<Example fr="Où vas-tu ?" en="Where are you going?" />

## Why and because

To answer *pourquoi*, use **parce que**. Like *je* and *ne*, it shortens before a vowel or silent *h*.

<Example fr="Pourquoi tu chantes ? — Parce que j'adore la musique !" en="Why are you singing? — Because I love music!" />

## How many

To ask how many of something, use **combien de** with a noun. The *de* stays as it is, whatever the noun: it doesn't change for masculine, feminine or plural.

<Example fr="Combien de films tu regardes ?" en="How many films do you watch?" />

## Who

**Qui** can be the subject of the verb, and then it needs no *est-ce que*: *Qui joue ?* (Who's playing?). It also works at the end of a relaxed question: *Tu regardes qui ?* (Who are you looking at?).

## Three words for "what"

"What" takes a different form depending on where it goes.

<VocabTable group="what" />

- At the **start** of a neutral question, use **qu'est-ce que**: *Qu'est-ce que vous aimez ?*
- At the **end** of a relaxed question, use **quoi**: *Tu fais quoi ?*
- With **inversion**, use **que**: *Que fais-tu ?*

You've already been using *Qu'est-ce que tu fais ?* as a fixed phrase in <LessonLink id="a1-faire-and-aller" text="the lesson on faire and aller" />. Now you can see how it's built.

## Which: quel

When "what" or "which" goes with a noun, use **quel**. It's an adjective, so it matches the noun. All four forms sound the same.

<VocabTable group="quel" />

<Example fr="Quelle chanson tu écoutes ?" en="What song are you listening to?" />
<Example fr="Quels films aimez-vous ?" en="Which films do you like?" />

You've already met it in *Quelle heure est-il ?*, from <LessonLink id="a1-telling-time" text="the lesson on telling the time" />.

## Putting it together

Here's a short exchange after a tennis match. Notice how the questions get more specific.

- *Tu joues au tennis où ?* (Where do you play tennis?)
- *À Lille.* (In Lille.)
- *Et tu joues quand ?* (And when do you play?)
- *Le samedi, à dix heures.* (On Saturdays, at ten.)
- *Pourquoi le samedi ?* (Why Saturdays?)
- *Parce que je ne travaille pas le samedi.* (Because I don't work on Saturdays.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Quel or qu'est-ce que?">
  English uses "what" for both, so it's easy to mix them up. If "what" comes right before a noun, it's **quel**: *Quel film ?* If it's followed by a subject and a verb, it's **qu'est-ce que**: *Qu'est-ce que tu regardes ?* You can't say *Quel tu regardes ?*, and you can't use *qu'est-ce que* straight before a noun.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="When you didn't catch that">
  If you didn't hear what someone said, reach for **Pardon ?** or **Comment ?**. Both work like "sorry?" *Quoi ?* on its own means the same thing, but it's casual and it can sound abrupt, so it's best kept for close friends.
</Callout>
