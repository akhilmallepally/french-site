By the end of this lesson you'll be able to write a short paragraph about your family: who they are, how old the younger ones are, what they look like and what they're like.

## What you've learned

This unit has been about people and things. Skim this list and go back to anything that feels shaky.

- **Name things.** You know *le, la, l', les* from <LessonLink id="a1-definite-articles" text="the lesson on gender and articles" />, and *un, une, des* from <LessonLink id="a1-indefinite-articles" text="the lesson on indefinite articles" />.
- **Make plurals.** You can add -s or -x, and you know *journal* becomes *journaux*, from <LessonLink id="a1-plural-nouns" text="the lesson on plural nouns" />.
- **Talk about family.** You know the words from <LessonLink id="a1-family" text="the family lesson" />.
- **Say what people have.** You know *avoir*, ages, and *il y a* from <LessonLink id="a1-avoir-present" text="the lesson on avoir" />.
- **Say whose it is.** You know *mon, ma, mes* and the rest from <LessonLink id="a1-possessive-adjectives" text="the lesson on possessive adjectives" />.
- **Describe.** You know the words from <LessonLink id="a1-describing-people" text="the lesson on describing people" />, and how to match and place them from <LessonLink id="a1-adjective-agreement" text="the lesson on adjective agreement" />.

## A few words to hold it together

Most of these you've seen before. *Il s'appelle* and *elle s'appelle* are new forms of a phrase you know, *je m'appelle*.

## A model paragraph

Imagine Hugo is writing about family for a French class. Here's the paragraph, line by line.

<Example fr="Dans ma famille, il y a quatre personnes." en="There are four people in my family." />
<Example fr="Mon père s'appelle Marc. Il est grand et très drôle." en="My father's name is Marc. He's tall and very funny." />
<Example fr="Ma mère s'appelle Nadia. Elle a les cheveux longs et les yeux verts." en="My mother's name is Nadia. She has long hair and green eyes." />
<Example fr="Elle est petite, mais elle est sportive." en="She's short, but she's sporty." />
<Example fr="Ma sœur Léa a seize ans. Elle est timide et gentille." en="My sister Léa is sixteen. She's shy and kind." />
<Example fr="Nous avons une petite maison à Toulouse." en="We have a small house in Toulouse." />

Look at how much grammar that small paragraph uses: *il y a*, possessives, *être* and *avoir*, the feature pattern with *les*, adjectives that match a man and a woman, and *petite* before its noun.

Look also at what's missing. There's no age for the father, because you can only count to twenty so far. That's fine. When you can't say something yet, say something else.

## Your turn

Write your own paragraph on paper or in a notes app. It can be about your real family, or an invented one. Aim for six to eight sentences, and include:

1. how many people there are, with *il y a*
2. at least three family members, with their names
3. an age, if someone is twenty or younger
4. what someone looks like, using hair or eyes
5. what someone is like, using a personality word
6. a *mais* or an *aussi* to link two ideas
7. one thing your family has, with *avoir*

## Check your work

Go through your paragraph once for each question below.

- Does each noun have a small word in front of it, like *mon*, *une* or *les*?
- Does the possessive match the noun that follows, and not the owner?
- Did you use *avoir* for ages, hair and eyes?
- Are colours and most other adjectives after the noun, and BAGS adjectives before it?

<Callout type="warning" title="Adjectives follow the person, line by line">
  In a family paragraph you keep switching between men and women, so it's easy to copy the same form down the page. *Mon père est grand* doesn't help the next line: it's **Ma mère est grande**, and **Mes parents sont grands**. Before you finish a sentence, look back at who it's about.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Borrow the skeleton">
  You don't have to invent your sentences. Take one line of Hugo's paragraph, keep its structure, and swap in your own details: *Ma tante s'appelle Fatima. Elle est grande et très calme.* You'll sound more natural, because the skeleton is already correct.
</Callout>
