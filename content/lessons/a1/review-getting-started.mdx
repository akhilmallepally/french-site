By the end of this lesson you'll be able to write a short introduction of yourself in French: your name and how to spell it, your nationality, where you live, your age and your phone number, in the right register for the reader.

## What you've learned

Before you start writing, here's what you can already do. Skim the list, and go back to any lesson that feels shaky.

- **Greet and be polite.** You know *bonjour*, *salut*, and how to behave in French encounters from <LessonLink id="a1-culture-politeness" text="the lesson on politeness and la bise" />.
- **Spell.** You can name letters and accents, thanks to <LessonLink id="a1-alphabet-and-accents" text="the alphabet lesson" />.
- **Give your name.** *Je m'appelle…* comes from <LessonLink id="a1-introducing-yourself" text="the introductions lesson" />.
- **Talk about people.** You know the pronouns from <LessonLink id="a1-subject-pronouns" text="the subject pronouns lesson" /> and every form in <LessonLink id="a1-etre-present" text="the lesson on être" />.
- **Count.** You can give your age and read small numbers, from <LessonLink id="a1-numbers-0-20" text="the numbers lesson" />.

## A model introduction

Imagine Emma has joined an online French class and is posting a message to the group. Here it is line by line.

<Example fr="Bonjour à tous !" en="Hello everyone!" />
<Example fr="Je m'appelle Emma Wilson. Ça s'écrit W, I, L, S, O, N." en="My name is Emma Wilson. It's spelled W, I, L, S, O, N." />
<Example fr="Je suis canadienne, mais j'habite à Lyon." en="I'm Canadian, but I live in Lyon." />
<Example fr="J'ai dix-neuf ans et je suis étudiante." en="I'm nineteen and I'm a student." />
<Example fr="J'apprends le français." en="I'm learning French." />
<Example fr="Mon numéro, c'est le zéro six, douze, quinze, dix-huit, zéro deux." en="My number is 06 12 15 18 02." />
<Example fr="À bientôt !" en="See you soon!" />

Notice how little new French that took. Almost every line is a chunk you've already practised. *Mais* and *et* do the joining.

## Choosing the right register

Emma's message is friendly and addressed to a group, so *Bonjour à tous* works. Who you're writing to changes a few words.

**To a friend of a friend,** you can be relaxed: start with *Salut !*, use *Moi, c'est Emma*, and finish with *Et toi ?*

**To a teacher, a landlord, or anyone you'd call *vous*,** start with *Bonjour, madame* or *Bonjour, monsieur*, use *Je m'appelle*, and if you ask a question, finish with *Et vous ?*

Choose one register and keep it all the way through.

## Your turn

Write your own introduction on paper or in a notes app. Aim for five or six sentences, and include:

1. a greeting that suits your reader
2. your name, with the spelling if it's unusual for French speakers
3. your nationality, and the city you live in
4. your age
5. what you do, if you're a student, or just that you're learning French
6. a sign-off

Then check it against the warning below.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check every je suis">
  Most errors in a first introduction come from translating English sentences with "I am". Read yours again and look for these three: age uses *avoir* (**J'ai vingt ans**, not *Je suis vingt ans*); there's no "a" before a job or status (**Je suis étudiant**, not *Je suis un étudiant*); and nationalities match you and take a small letter (**Je suis anglaise**, if you're a woman).
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep your introduction on a card">
  You'll introduce yourself more often than you'll do almost anything else in French. Copy your finished paragraph onto a card or into your phone, and update it each time you learn something new. By the end of A1 it will have grown into a real description of your life.
</Callout>
