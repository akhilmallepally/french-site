By the end of this lesson you'll be able to write two short dialogues in French, one in a café and one in a clothes shop, from *bonjour* to *au revoir*.

## What you've learned

This unit has been about food and shopping. Skim the list and go back to anything that feels shaky.

- **Name foods and drinks.** You know the words from <LessonLink id="a1-food-and-drink" text="the lesson on food and drink" />.
- **Say "some".** You can choose between *le pain* and *du pain*, from <LessonLink id="a1-partitive-articles" text="the lesson on du, de la and des" />.
- **Order politely.** You know *Je voudrais* and *L'addition, s'il vous plaît*, from <LessonLink id="a1-ordering-at-a-cafe" text="the lesson on ordering at a café" />.
- **Behave at the table.** You know the order of a meal and the role of bread, from <LessonLink id="a1-culture-french-meals" text="the lesson on French meals and mealtimes" />.
- **Shop for clothes.** You know colours, sizes and prices, from <LessonLink id="a1-shopping-for-clothes" text="the lesson on clothes, colours and prices" />.
- **Point things out.** You know *ce*, *cet*, *cette* and *ces*, from <LessonLink id="a1-demonstratives" text="the lesson on demonstratives" />.

## A model café dialogue

Here's a customer ordering lunch. Read it line by line and notice how polite and short each turn is.

<Example fr="Bonjour, madame. Je voudrais un sandwich, s'il vous plaît." en="Hello, madam. I'd like a sandwich, please." />
<Example fr="Et comme boisson ? — De l'eau, s'il vous plaît." en="And to drink? — Some water, please." />
<Example fr="Autre chose ? — Non, c'est tout, merci." en="Anything else? — No, that's all, thank you." />
<Example fr="L'addition, s'il vous plaît. — Ça fait neuf euros cinquante." en="The bill, please. — That comes to nine euros fifty." />
<Example fr="Par carte. Voilà. Merci, au revoir !" en="By card. Here you are. Thank you, goodbye!" />

*Comme boisson* means "as a drink". You can use it as a fixed phrase when you read or write a dialogue like this.

## A model shopping dialogue

Now the same customer goes to a clothes shop. Notice how the demonstratives point to the things on the shelf.

<Example fr="Bonjour. Je cherche un pull gris." en="Hello. I'm looking for a grey jumper." />
<Example fr="Ce pull-ci ? — Non, ce pull-là, s'il vous plaît." en="This jumper? — No, that jumper, please." />
<Example fr="Je fais du 40. Je voudrais essayer ce pull." en="I'm a size 40. I'd like to try on this jumper." />
<Example fr="Combien ça coûte ? — Quarante-neuf euros." en="How much does it cost? — Forty-nine euros." />

## Your turn

Write both of your own dialogues on paper or in a notes app. Decide who the customer is, and what's being bought. Aim for six to eight lines each.

**In the café,** include:

1. a greeting, with *madame* or *monsieur*
2. one order with *je voudrais* and one with *je vais prendre*
3. something with *du*, *de la* or *de l'*
4. a question from the server
5. the bill, and a polite goodbye

**In the shop,** include:

1. a greeting and *je cherche*
2. a colour that matches its noun
3. *ce*, *cet*, *cette* or *ces*, and *-ci* or *-là*
4. your size
5. a price

## Check your work

Go through your dialogues once for each question below.

- Did you use *vous* all the way through, for both the staff and the customer?
- Is each colour after its noun and matching it?
- Did you use *un* or *une* for one item, and *du* or *de la* for an amount?
- Did you start with *bonjour* and end with *merci* or *au revoir*?

<Callout type="warning" title="Three words have to agree">
  In a shop, a single phrase can involve three matching words, and they're all linked to the noun. **Ce pull blanc** and **cette robe blanche** look very different, even though they're the same idea. Before you finish a phrase, find the noun, decide if it's masculine or feminine and singular or plural, and then check each word around it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="The menu is outside">
  French restaurants normally display their menu and prices outside the door. That means you can stand on the pavement, take your time, work out the words you don't know, and decide what you'll order before you go in. By the time the server arrives, you can have your whole sentence ready.
</Callout>
