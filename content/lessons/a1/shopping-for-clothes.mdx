By the end of this lesson you'll be able to name clothes and colours, talk to a shop assistant, give your size, and understand how much something costs.

## Clothes

Here are the everyday words. As usual, learn each one with its article.

<VocabTable group="clothes" />

Two surprises for English speakers: *le pantalon* and *le jean* are **singular** in French. So "the trousers are grey" is *Le pantalon est gris*.

<Example fr="Je cherche une robe noire." en="I'm looking for a black dress." />
<Example fr="Les chaussettes sont roses." en="The socks are pink." />

## Colours

Colours are adjectives, so they follow the rules from <LessonLink id="a1-adjective-agreement" text="the lesson on adjective agreement" />: they go **after** the noun and they match it.

<VocabTable group="colours" />

- Most colours add -e for the feminine and -s for the plural: *une jupe verte*, *des pulls verts*.
- *Blanc* is irregular: *un pull blanc*, *une chemise blanche*.
- Colours that already end in -e, like *rouge*, *jaune* and *rose*, don't change for the feminine, but they do add -s in the plural: *des robes rouges*.
- **Marron** and **orange** never change: *des chaussures marron*, *une veste orange*. This is because they're named after things, a chestnut and an orange.

## In the shop

As always, start with *bonjour*. Then these phrases will get you through.

<VocabTable group="shop" />

*Je voudrais essayer* and *Je fais du 38* are fixed phrases for now, so learn them whole. French clothes sizes are numbers, and they're not the same as British or American sizes, so check the label or ask.

<Example fr="Je voudrais essayer la veste verte, s'il vous plaît." en="I'd like to try on the green jacket, please." />
<Example fr="Quelle taille ? — Je fais du 40." en="What size? — I'm a size 40." />

The question *Quelle taille ?* uses *quel* from <LessonLink id="a1-question-words" text="the lesson on question words" />, and it's feminine, because *taille* is.

## Prices

You'll use the numbers from <LessonLink id="a1-numbers-21-100" text="the lesson on numbers 21 to 100" /> here.

<VocabTable group="prices" />

*Coûter* is a regular -er verb, so it's *le pull coûte* for one thing and *les chaussures coûtent* for several.

<Example fr="Combien ça coûte, le manteau ? — Quatre-vingt-neuf euros." en="How much does the coat cost? — Eighty-nine euros." />
<Example fr="Les chaussures coûtent soixante-dix euros." en="The shoes cost seventy euros." />
<Example fr="La veste est chère." en="The jacket is expensive." />

*Cher* is an adjective, so it matches the noun: *un pull cher*, *une robe chère*. In *Ça coûte cher*, it works differently and doesn't change.

## Putting it together

Here's a complete exchange in a clothes shop.

- *Bonjour. Je cherche un pull bleu.* (Hello. I'm looking for a blue jumper.)
- *Quelle taille ?* (What size?)
- *Je fais du 42. Combien ça coûte ?* (I'm a 42. How much does it cost?)
- *Trente-cinq euros.* (Thirty-five euros.)
- *Je voudrais essayer le pull, s'il vous plaît.* (I'd like to try on the jumper, please.)

<Callout type="warning" title="The comma and the euro sign">
  French prices are written with a comma, not a point, and the euro sign usually comes after the number: **12,50 €**. You say it with the word *euros* in the middle: *douze euros cinquante*. People usually leave out *centimes*. Don't say *douze cinquante euros*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Wait for les soldes">
  If you're shopping in France, look out for **les soldes**, the sales. They take place at set times, usually in January and in summer, and the discounts can be large. A sign saying *soldes* means prices are lower, and you may find what you came for at a much better price.
</Callout>
