By the end of this lesson you'll be able to write about your typical day and week: when you get up, what you do, what you don't do, and how your weekend differs from the working week.

## What you've learned

This unit has been about everyday life. Skim the list and go back to anything that feels shaky.

- **Use regular verbs.** You can use the three families, from <LessonLink id="a1-er-verbs-present" text="the lesson on -er verbs" /> and <LessonLink id="a1-ir-re-verbs-present" text="the lesson on -ir and -re verbs" />.
- **Say what you enjoy.** You know *j'aime*, *j'adore* and *je déteste* from <LessonLink id="a1-likes-and-dislikes" text="the lesson on likes and dislikes" />.
- **Say what isn't so.** You can use *ne…pas* and *pas de*, from <LessonLink id="a1-negation-ne-pas" text="the lesson on negation" />.
- **Count to 100.** That comes from <LessonLink id="a1-numbers-21-100" text="the lesson on numbers 21 to 100" />.
- **Give the date.** You know the days and months from <LessonLink id="a1-days-months-dates" text="the lesson on days, months and dates" />.
- **Tell the time.** You know *il est* and *à huit heures* from <LessonLink id="a1-telling-time" text="the lesson on telling the time" />.
- **Describe your routine.** You know reflexive verbs from <LessonLink id="a1-daily-routine-reflexive" text="the lesson on daily routines" />.

## A model paragraph

Imagine Inès is writing about a typical week for a French class. Here it is, line by line.

<Example fr="Du lundi au vendredi, je travaille à Grenoble." en="From Monday to Friday, I work in Grenoble." />
<Example fr="Je me lève à sept heures et je me dépêche." en="I get up at seven and I hurry." />
<Example fr="Je finis le travail à dix-sept heures trente." en="I finish work at 5:30 p.m." />
<Example fr="Je rentre à six heures et je dîne à huit heures." en="I get home at six and I have dinner at eight." />
<Example fr="Le samedi, je ne travaille pas et je me lève tard." en="On Saturdays, I don't work and I get up late." />
<Example fr="Le dimanche, j'adore cuisiner avec des amis." en="On Sundays, I love cooking with friends." />
<Example fr="Je me couche à onze heures, tous les jours." en="I go to bed at eleven, every day." />

Look at the mix in this paragraph: *-er*, *-ir* and reflexive verbs, a negative, an activity after *j'adore*, and two ways of giving the time. The days with *le* show that these are habits.

## Your turn

Write your own paragraph on paper or in a notes app. It can be about your real week or an invented one. Aim for eight sentences or so, and include:

1. what you do from Monday to Friday, using an *-er* verb
2. at least two reflexive verbs, with times
3. one *-ir* or *-re* verb, such as *je finis* or *j'attends*
4. something you don't do, with *ne…pas*
5. something you love or hate doing
6. a day with *le*, to show a habit
7. one time in 24-hour style

## Check your work

Go through your paragraph once for each question below.

- Does each reflexive verb have a pronoun that matches its subject?
- Is every *heures* in place, and did *et demie* get its -e?
- Are days and months in small letters?
- After a negative verb, did *un*, *une* and *des* change to *de*?

<Callout type="warning" title="Ne goes before the pronoun">
  When you make a reflexive verb negative, the small words stack up and the order is easy to lose. *Ne* comes first, then the pronoun, then the verb, then *pas*: **Je ne me lève pas tôt**. *Je me ne lève pas* is a common slip. Treat *me lève* as one unit and wrap *ne…pas* around it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="One line a day">
  Writing a full paragraph is hard to keep up, but one sentence isn't. Each evening, write one French line about your day in a notebook: what time you got up, or something you didn't do. After a month you'll have thirty sentences, and you'll be able to see how much easier they've become.
</Callout>
