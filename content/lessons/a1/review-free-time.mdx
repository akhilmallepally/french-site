By the end of this lesson you'll be able to write a short interview about free time: asking someone about their hobbies and answering the same questions yourself.

## What you've learned

This unit has been about free time. Skim the list and go back to anything that feels shaky.

- **Use two key verbs.** You know every form from <LessonLink id="a1-faire-and-aller" text="the lesson on faire and aller" />.
- **Join à and de to articles.** You know *au*, *aux*, *du* and *des* from <LessonLink id="a1-contractions-a-de" text="the lesson on à and de with articles" />.
- **Talk about hobbies.** You know *jouer à*, *jouer de* and *faire de* from <LessonLink id="a1-hobbies-and-sports" text="the lesson on hobbies and sports" />.
- **Ask yes/no questions.** You know three styles and when to say *si*, from <LessonLink id="a1-yes-no-questions" text="the lesson on yes/no questions" />.
- **Ask for information.** You know *où*, *quand*, *pourquoi* and the rest, from <LessonLink id="a1-question-words" text="the lesson on question words" />.
- **Talk about the weather.** You know *il fait*, *il pleut* and the seasons, from <LessonLink id="a1-weather-seasons" text="the lesson on weather and seasons" />.

## A few words to hold it together

Most of these are familiar. *Quand* now has a second job: it can join two ideas in one sentence, without asking anything.

## A model interview

Imagine Salma is interviewing Théo for a class newsletter. They've just met, so they use *vous*. Here's the interview, exchange by exchange.

<Example fr="Qu'est-ce que vous faites le week-end ? — Je joue au foot et je fais de la randonnée." en="What do you do at weekends? — I play football and I go hiking." />
<Example fr="Où est-ce que vous jouez ? — Au parc, quand il fait beau." en="Where do you play? — In the park, when the weather's nice." />
<Example fr="Et quand il pleut ? — Je vais au cinéma." en="And when it rains? — I go to the cinema." />
<Example fr="Pourquoi ? — Parce que je déteste la pluie !" en="Why? — Because I hate the rain!" />
<Example fr="Faites-vous du ski ? — Oui, souvent, en hiver." en="Do you ski? — Yes, often, in winter." />
<Example fr="Vous ne jouez pas de la guitare ? — Si, et je joue aussi du piano." en="Don't you play the guitar? — Yes, I do, and I play the piano too." />

Look at the variety here. There's *qu'est-ce que*, *où est-ce que*, a one-word *pourquoi*, an inverted *faites-vous*, and a *si*. The answers don't have to be long. In a real conversation, short answers are natural: *Au parc*, *Oui, souvent*.

## Your turn

Write your own interview on paper or in a notes app. It can be with a friend, a family member, or a famous person you invent answers for. Decide first if you're using *tu* or *vous*, and stay with it. Aim for six questions and answers, and include:

1. one yes/no question with intonation
2. one yes/no question with *est-ce que*
3. a question with *où* or *quand*
4. a *pourquoi* question, answered with *parce que*
5. a question with *quel* or *quelle* and a noun
6. something about what happens when the weather is good or bad

Then answer your own questions, about yourself.

## Check your work

Go through your interview once for each question below.

- Does each sport or instrument have the right *au*, *du* or *de la*?
- Do you have *où* with its accent when you mean "where"?
- Does *quel* match its noun?
- Did you keep *tu* or *vous* the same all the way through?

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't mix the patterns">
  Each question style is complete on its own, and blending two is a common slip. It's **Où est-ce que tu habites ?** or **Où habites-tu ?**, but not *Où est-ce que habites-tu ?* Once you've used *est-ce que*, the words after it stay in normal sentence order.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep pourquoi in your pocket">
  When a conversation runs dry, you don't need a new topic. Whatever someone tells you, ask **Pourquoi ?** It's one word, it works after nearly any answer, and it gets the other person talking for a while. That gives you time to think, and more French to listen to.
</Callout>
