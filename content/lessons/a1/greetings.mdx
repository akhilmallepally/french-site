Greetings are the first thing you'll say in any French conversation, and the French take them seriously. Walking into a shop without saying *bonjour* is considered rude.

## Essential words

| French | Pronunciation | English |
|---|---|---|
| bonjour | bon-ZHOOR | hello / good day |
| bonsoir | bon-SWAHR | good evening |
| salut | sah-LOO | hi / bye (informal) |
| au revoir | oh ruh-VWAHR | goodbye |
| merci | mehr-SEE | thank you |
| s'il vous plaît | seel voo PLEH | please (formal) |

## Formal vs informal

French has two registers. The **formal** one is for strangers, shopkeepers, colleagues, and anyone older than you. The **informal** one is for friends, family, and people your own age in casual settings.

- **Formal:** *bonjour*, *au revoir*, *s'il vous plaît*
- **Informal:** *salut* (works for both hello and goodbye), *s'il te plaît*

<Callout type="tip" title="When in doubt, be formal">
  Nobody will be offended if you say **bonjour** to a friend. Saying **salut** to a stranger can sound too familiar.
</Callout>

## Examples

<Example fr="Bonjour, madame !" en="Hello, ma'am!" />
<Example fr="Salut, ça va ?" en="Hi, how's it going? (informal)" />
<Example fr="Un café, s'il vous plaît." en="A coffee, please." />
<Example fr="Merci, au revoir !" en="Thank you, goodbye!" />

<Callout type="warning" title="Bonsoir isn't goodbye">
  **Bonsoir** is a greeting you use when arriving in the evening. To say goodbye at night, use **au revoir** or **bonne soirée** (have a good evening).
</Callout>
