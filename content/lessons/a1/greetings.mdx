By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say hello and goodbye at any time of day, ask for something politely, and say thank you.

Greetings are the first thing you'll say in any French conversation, and the French take them seriously. Walking into a shop without saying *bonjour* is considered rude.

## Essential words

<VocabTable />

## Hello: bonjour and bonsoir

*Bonjour* is made of *bon* (good) and *jour* (day), so it's literally "good day". It's the standard hello from the morning until the evening, and you can say it to anyone: a friend, a shopkeeper or your boss.

In the evening, switch to *bonsoir*. *Soir* means "evening". There's no exact time to change, but most people switch somewhere around six or seven o'clock, or when it starts to get dark. French has no separate greeting for the afternoon: *bonjour* covers the whole day.

*Salut* is the friendly, informal hello. It also works as an informal goodbye, so you can say it both when you arrive and when you leave.

## Formal vs informal

French has two registers. The **formal** one is for strangers, shopkeepers, colleagues, and anyone older than you. The **informal** one is for friends, family, and people your own age in casual settings.

- **Formal:** *bonjour*, *au revoir*, *s'il vous plaît*
- **Informal:** *salut* (works for both hello and goodbye), *s'il te plaît*

The difference between *s'il vous plaît* and *s'il te plaît* comes from the two words for "you", *vous* and *tu*. You'll learn how they work in <LessonLink id="a1-subject-pronouns" text="the lesson on subject pronouns" />. For now, use *s'il vous plaît* in a shop or a café, and *s'il te plaît* with a friend.

<Callout type="tip" title="When in doubt, be formal">
  Nobody will be offended if you say **bonjour** to a friend. Saying **salut** to a stranger can sound too familiar.
</Callout>

## Please and thank you

*S'il vous plaît* literally means "if it pleases you". Put it at the end of a request: *Un café, s'il vous plaît.* (A coffee, please.) *Merci* means "thank you", and it works with everyone, formal or informal.

## Goodbye

*Au revoir* is the everyday goodbye, and it's polite enough for any situation. It's written as two words, and it comes from *revoir*, "to see again".

When you leave in the evening, you can add *bonne soirée*, "have a good evening". Notice the difference: *bonsoir* is what you say when you arrive, and *bonne soirée* is what you say when you go.

## In a café

Here's a whole short exchange, using the words from this lesson.

- *Bonsoir, madame !* (Good evening, ma'am!)
- *Bonsoir !* (Good evening!)
- *Un café, s'il vous plaît.* (A coffee, please.)
- *Merci, au revoir !* (Thank you, goodbye!)
- *Au revoir, bonne soirée !* (Goodbye, have a good evening!)

## How the words sound

Each word in the table has a pronunciation hint, and the syllable in capitals gets a little more weight. In *bonjour*, the *j* sounds like the *s* in "measure", and the *n* isn't pronounced on its own: it changes the sound of the vowel before it. The last letters of *plaît* and *salut* are silent. You'll learn more about reading these hints in <LessonLink id="a1-alphabet-and-accents" text="the lesson on the alphabet and accents" />.

<Callout type="warning" title="Bonsoir is mainly a greeting">
  *Bonsoir* is mainly an evening hello, but you'll sometimes hear it as a goodbye late in the day. If you're unsure, *au revoir* or *bonne soirée* always works.
</Callout>
