By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say "this", "that", "these" and "those" in French, and point out exactly the item you mean.

## One word for this and that

English separates "this" from "that". French usually doesn't. One small word covers both, and it goes in front of a noun, in the same place as *le* or *un*. It's very useful in a shop, as you'll guess from <LessonLink id="a1-shopping-for-clothes" text="the lesson on clothes, colours and prices" />, because you can talk about what's right in front of you.

<VocabTable group="forms" />

Like other small words that go before a noun, it matches that noun:

- **Ce** for a masculine singular noun: *ce manteau*.
- **Cet** for a masculine singular noun beginning with a vowel or silent *h*: *cet ami*, *cet hôtel*.
- **Cette** for any feminine singular noun: *cette jupe*, *cette école*.
- **Ces** for any plural noun: *ces pulls*, *ces robes*.

*Cet* and *cette* are spelled differently, but they sound exactly the same. *Ces* links with a z sound before a vowel: *ces amis*.

Why have *cet* at all? It keeps two vowel sounds from colliding, in the same way that *l'* does. You'll need one or two new words for practice:

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="Ce magasin est grand." en="This shop is big." />
<Example fr="Cet article coûte vingt euros." en="This item costs twenty euros." />
<Example fr="Cette chemise est chère." en="That shirt is expensive." />
<Example fr="Ces chaussettes sont jaunes." en="These socks are yellow." />

## Adjectives still match

A demonstrative doesn't change anything else in the sentence. Colours and other adjectives still follow the rules from <LessonLink id="a1-adjective-agreement" text="the lesson on adjective agreement" />. So you have three things that have to agree: the demonstrative, the noun and the adjective.

- *ce pull blanc* (masculine)
- *cette robe blanche* (feminine)
- *ces chaussures blanches* (feminine plural)

## This one or that one: -ci and -là

Most of the time, *ce* is enough, and the context shows what you mean. When you need to contrast two things, or point at one in particular, add **-ci** (here, close to me) or **-là** (there, further away). They join on to the noun with a hyphen.

<VocabTable group="pointing" />

<Example fr="Ce pull-ci ou ce pull-là ?" en="This jumper or that jumper?" />
<Example fr="Je voudrais essayer cette veste-là." en="I'd like to try on that jacket." />

In everyday speech, *-là* is more common than *-ci*, and it doesn't always mean that something is far away. So don't be surprised to hear *ce jour-là* (that day) or *cette robe-là* for a dress that's right next to you.

Don't confuse the accents. **Là** with an accent means "there", and **la** without one means "the".

## Ce or c'est?

The little word *ce* appears in two places, and they do different jobs.

- **Ce** before a noun means "this" or "that": *ce sac*.
- **C'est** is *ce* + *est*, and it means "it is" or "that is": *C'est un sac*.

If a noun comes right after *ce*, it's this lesson's word. *C'est* and *ce sont* already contain a verb.

## Putting it together

Here's a short exchange at a market stall. Notice how the words change with the noun.

- *Bonjour. Combien ça coûte, ce chapeau ?* (Hello. How much is this hat?)
- *Ce chapeau-ci ? Trente euros.* (This hat here? Thirty euros.)
- *Et cette chemise-là ?* (And that shirt there?)
- *Vingt-cinq euros. Et ces chaussettes, quatre euros.* (Twenty-five euros. And these socks, four euros.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Cet is only for masculine nouns">
  You learned in <LessonLink id="a1-possessive-adjectives" text="the lesson on mon, ma and mes" /> that feminine nouns before a vowel use *mon*: *mon amie*. That trick doesn't apply here. Feminine nouns always take **cette**, vowel or no vowel: **cette amie**, **cette école**. *Cet amie* looks like the *mon amie* pattern, but it's wrong. The good news is that you won't hear the mistake, only see it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="A shortcut to time phrases">
  This one small word gives you a set of time expressions for free. *Ce matin* is "this morning", *ce soir* is "this evening", *cette semaine* is "this week", and *cet été* is "this summer". Just put the right form in front of a time word you already know, and you've got a new phrase.
</Callout>
