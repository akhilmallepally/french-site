By the end of this lesson you'll be able to describe a typical day from morning to night, and use reflexive verbs with every subject pronoun.

## Doing something to yourself

Many verbs for daily routines come with a small extra word. In the dictionary, it appears as **se** in front of the infinitive: *se lever* (to get up), *se coucher* (to go to bed).

This small word is a **reflexive pronoun**. It shows that the action comes back to the person doing it. Sometimes English has an equivalent, as in "I wash myself". More often it doesn't: "I get up" has nothing extra, but the French is *je me lève*, "I raise myself".

The pronoun changes to match the subject, and it goes straight before the verb.

| Subject | Reflexive pronoun |
|---|---|
| je | me |
| tu | te |
| il / elle / on | se |
| nous | nous |
| vous | vous |
| ils / elles | se |

Apart from that, these are ordinary verbs. The endings are the ones you learned in <LessonLink id="a1-er-verbs-present" text="the lesson on regular -er verbs" />.

<VocabTable group="forms" />

Look closely at the accent. *Se lever* takes a grave accent in the forms with a silent ending: *lève*, *lèves*, *lèvent*. There's no accent in *levons* and *levez*.

## Verbs for your day

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

Before a vowel or silent *h*, *me*, *te* and *se* shorten to **m'**, **t'** and **s'**, as in *je m'habille* or *elle s'habille*.

You've been using one reflexive verb since <LessonLink id="a1-introducing-yourself" text="the introductions lesson" />. *Je m'appelle* literally means "I call myself". Now you can see all of it: *tu t'appelles*, *il s'appelle*, *nous nous appelons*, *vous vous appelez*, *ils s'appellent*. Remember that it has two Ls in the forms with silent endings.

<Example fr="Je me réveille à six heures et demie." en="I wake up at half past six." />
<Example fr="Tu te douches le matin ?" en="Do you have a shower in the morning?" />
<Example fr="Il s'habille et il se dépêche." en="He gets dressed and he hurries." />
<Example fr="Nous nous reposons le soir." en="We rest in the evening." />

## With or without the pronoun

Many of these verbs also exist without the pronoun, and then the action is done to something or someone else.

- *Je me lave.* (I wash myself.)
- *Je lave la voiture.* (I wash the car.)
- *Il se réveille.* (He wakes up.)
- *Il réveille Marc.* (He wakes Marc up.)

So the pronoun is a real part of the meaning, and it's not just decoration.

Your routine also needs a few ordinary verbs. They have no pronoun, and they follow the usual pattern. With the times you learned in <LessonLink id="a1-telling-time" text="the lesson on telling the time" />, you can build a whole day.

<VocabTable group="day" />

<Example fr="Elles déjeunent à midi et elles rentrent à six heures." en="They have lunch at noon and they get home at six." />

If you want to make a reflexive verb negative, *ne* goes before the pronoun and *pas* after the verb: *Je ne me lève pas*.

## Putting it together

Here's one person's morning, in order:

- *Je me réveille à sept heures.* (I wake up at seven.)
- *Je me lève, je me douche et je m'habille.* (I get up, shower and get dressed.)
- *Je me brosse les dents, et je me dépêche !* (I brush my teeth, and I hurry!)

Notice *je me brosse les dents*. English says "my teeth", but French uses *les*, because the pronoun *me* already shows whose they are.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't lose the little pronoun">
  In English, "I get up" and "I go to bed" have no extra word, so it's easy to write *Je lève à sept heures*. That's incomplete: *lever* without a pronoun needs something to lift. Say **Je me lève**. And make the pronoun match the subject: **tu te lèves**, not *tu se lèves*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Narrate your morning">
  These verbs happen in the same order every day, so you get free practice. While you're getting ready, say what you're doing in your head: *Je me lève. Je me douche. Je m'habille.* After a week, the pronouns start to come automatically, and it costs you no extra time at all.
</Callout>
