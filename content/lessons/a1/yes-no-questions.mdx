By the end of this lesson you'll be able to ask a yes/no question in three different ways, pick the one that suits the situation, and answer a negative question correctly.

## Three ways to ask

English questions usually need "do", as in "Do you play tennis?" French has no such word here. Instead it gives you three ways of asking, which differ mainly in how formal they sound. All three ask the same thing. We'll use the activities from <LessonLink id="a1-hobbies-and-sports" text="the lesson on hobbies and sports" />.

| Style | Example | Sounds |
|---|---|---|
| Intonation | *Tu joues au tennis ?* | everyday, relaxed |
| *Est-ce que* | *Est-ce que tu joues au tennis ?* | neutral, works everywhere |
| Inversion | *Joues-tu au tennis ?* | formal, mostly written |

## 1. Intonation

This is the simplest way, and the one you'll hear most. Take a normal sentence and raise your voice at the end. In writing, add a question mark, with a space before it in French.

<Example fr="Vous aimez le ski ?" en="Do you like skiing?" />
<Example fr="Il fait du yoga ?" en="Does he do yoga?" />

## 2. Est-ce que

Put **est-ce que** at the beginning of a normal sentence. Nothing else changes, so you can't make a word-order mistake. It means roughly "is it that…?", but it's not translated: treat it as a spoken question mark.

<VocabTable group="words" />

Like other small words ending in *e*, it shortens before a vowel or silent *h*: **est-ce qu'il…**, **est-ce qu'elle…**, **est-ce qu'on…**.

<Example fr="Est-ce que tu joues aux cartes ?" en="Do you play cards?" />
<Example fr="Est-ce qu'elle fait de la danse ?" en="Does she do dance?" />

## 3. Inversion

For this one, you swap the verb and the pronoun, and join them with a hyphen. It's mostly used in writing and in formal situations, but you need to recognize it.

- *Vous parlez français.* → *Parlez-vous français ?*
- *Tu aimes le café.* → *Aimes-tu le café ?*
- *Nous allons à Lyon.* → *Allons-nous à Lyon ?*

You've already used a fixed example of this in *Comment allez-vous ?*

When the verb form ends in a vowel, and the pronoun is *il*, *elle* or *on*, French adds **-t-** between them, because two vowels side by side are hard to say.

<Example fr="Parle-t-il italien ?" en="Does he speak Italian?" />

For now, use inversion with pronouns only, and don't invert with *je*.

## Answering: oui, non and si

Ordinary answers are simple.

<VocabTable group="answers" />

*Si* is the interesting one. Use it to say "yes" in answer to a **negative** question or statement, when you want to contradict it. You'll remember how negatives work from <LessonLink id="a1-negation-ne-pas" text="the lesson on negation" />.

<Example fr="Tu n'aimes pas le vélo ? — Si, j'adore le vélo !" en="You don't like cycling? — Yes, I love cycling!" />
<Example fr="Elle ne joue pas au foot ? — Si !" en="She doesn't play football? — Yes, she does!" />

If you agree with the negative, use *non*: *Tu n'aimes pas le vélo ? — Non, je n'aime pas le vélo.*

## Putting it together

Here's a short conversation with all three question types. Look at how formal the speaker is in each.

- *Tu aimes la musique ?* (Do you like music?)
- *Oui ! Est-ce que tu joues de la guitare ?* (Yes! Do you play the guitar?)
- *Non. Tu n'aimes pas le sport ?* (No. Don't you like sport?)
- *Si, je joue au foot.* (Yes, I play football.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Say si, not oui">
  English uses "yes" to contradict a negative question: "You don't like it?" "Yes, I do!" In French, *oui* would be the wrong word there. When someone says **Tu n'aimes pas le foot ?** and you do like it, the answer is **Si, j'aime le foot**. *Oui* is for ordinary questions only.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Your answer is already in the question">
  When someone asks you a question, you don't have to build your answer from scratch. Reuse their words and change the verb form: *Tu joues au tennis ?* becomes *Oui, je joue au tennis.* *Vous aimez le yoga ?* becomes *Non, je n'aime pas le yoga.* It buys you thinking time, and the structure is right there in front of you.
</Callout>
