By the end of this lesson you'll be able to use two more families of regular verbs, those ending in *-ir* and *-re*, and see how they compare with the *-er* verbs you already know.

## Two more families

In <LessonLink id="a1-er-verbs-present" text="the lesson on regular -er verbs" /> you learned that you remove the infinitive ending and add a new one. These two families work the same way, with different endings. There are fewer verbs in them, but some of them are very common.

## The -ir family: finir

Remove *-ir* and add these endings. The feature to watch for is **-iss-** in all the plural forms.

<VocabTable group="ir-forms" />

*Je finis*, *tu finis* and *il finit* all sound alike. In the plural, *-iss-* is pronounced, so you can hear the difference between *il finit* and *ils finissent*.

Other verbs in this family:

<VocabTable group="ir-verbs" />

<Example fr="Tu choisis le film ?" en="Are you choosing the film?" />
<Example fr="Les enfants grandissent." en="The children are growing up." />

## The -re family: attendre

Remove *-re* and add these endings. They're the same as in other families in the plural, but notice the **il/elle** form: it has **no ending at all**, just the stem.

<VocabTable group="re-forms" />

*J'attends*, *tu attends* and *il attend* all sound alike, because the final d and s are silent. In the plural you can hear the d: *ils attendent*.

Other verbs in this family:

<VocabTable group="re-verbs" />

<Example fr="Elle attend le bus." en="She's waiting for the bus." />
<Example fr="Nous vendons la maison." en="We're selling the house." />
<Example fr="J'entends la musique." en="I can hear the music." />

## The three families side by side

Here's how the three patterns compare.

| Subject | parler | finir | vendre |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | parle | finis | vends |
| tu | parles | finis | vends |
| il / elle / on | parle | finit | vend |
| nous | parlons | finissons | vendons |
| vous | parlez | finissez | vendez |
| ils / elles | parlent | finissent | vendent |

A few things are the same everywhere. *Nous* ends in **-ons**, *vous* in **-ez**, and *ils/elles* in **-ent**, which is silent. All that really changes is the singular, and the *-iss-* in the -ir family.

## Around the working day

These verbs come up in ordinary sentences about work and getting around. Here are a few nouns to use with them.

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="Je réponds à Marc." en="I'm answering Marc." />
<Example fr="On finit à midi ?" en="Shall we finish at noon?" />

Notice that *répondre* takes *à* before the person you answer.

## Putting it together

Here's a short exchange between colleagues at the end of the day.

- *Tu finis le travail ?* (Are you finishing work?)
- *Oui. Et toi, tu attends le train ?* (Yes. And you, are you waiting for the train?)
- *Non, j'attends Julie. Elle arrive !* (No, I'm waiting for Julie. She's on the way!)

You'll also notice that these verbs work with *ne…pas* in the usual way, as you saw in <LessonLink id="a1-negation-ne-pas" text="the lesson on negation" />: *Je ne finis pas*, *Il n'attend pas*.

<Callout type="warning" title="Not every -ir or -re verb is regular">
  An infinitive ending doesn't guarantee the pattern. *Partir* (to leave) and *dormir* (to sleep) end in -ir but have no *-iss-*, and *prendre* (to take) and *faire* (to do, to make) end in -re but go their own way. You'll learn these separately in <LessonLink id="a1-faire-and-aller" text="the lesson on faire and aller" /> and <LessonLink id="a2-irregular-verbs-present" text="the lesson on irregular verbs" />. For now, trust the pattern only with the verbs in this lesson.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Attendre has its 'for' built in">
  English says "wait *for* someone". French doesn't need a separate word, because *attendre* already means "wait for". So it's **J'attends le bus**, and not *J'attends pour le bus*. Remembering the English meaning as "await" helps: you await the bus, with nothing in between.
</Callout>
