By the end of this lesson you'll be able to tell people your name, ask theirs in both a casual and a polite way, reply naturally when you meet someone, and introduce a friend.

## Saying your name

In <LessonLink id="a1-greetings" text="the greetings lesson" /> you learned to say hello. The next thing you'll usually say is your name. French has two everyday ways to do it.

**Je m'appelle…** is the standard choice in any situation. Literally it means "I call myself": *je* (I) + *m'* (myself) + *appelle* (call). You don't need to analyse it yet. Learn it as one chunk.

**Moi, c'est…** ("Me, it's…") is relaxed and very common among friends, at parties, or anywhere informal.

<VocabTable />

You may also hear *Je suis Paul* (I am Paul). It's correct, and you'll learn the verb behind it soon, but *je m'appelle* is what most people say when they first meet.

## Asking someone's name

How you ask depends on who you're talking to. You saw in the greetings lesson that French has a casual register and a polite one. The same split applies here.

**With friends, children, and people your age in casual settings:**
- *Tu t'appelles comment ?* is the most common way in everyday speech.
- *Comment tu t'appelles ?* means exactly the same.

**With strangers, older people, and anyone in a professional setting:**
- *Comment vous appelez-vous ?* is the more formal form.
- *Vous vous appelez comment ?* is also polite, and a little more relaxed. Yes, *vous* appears twice. The first means "you", the second "yourself".

The quickest way of all is to give your name and turn the question around: *Je m'appelle Sam, et toi ?* or, politely, *et vous ?*

## Responding

When you meet someone for the first time, say **enchanté**: "delighted", or "nice to meet you". Women write it **enchantée**, but it sounds exactly the same. This is an early example of something you'll see everywhere in French: some words change their spelling to match the person they describe.

## Introducing someone else

To introduce a friend, the simplest way is **Voici…** ("Here is…" / "This is…"). For a little more ceremony, use **Je te présente…** with one person you know well, or **Je vous présente…** in formal situations or when you're introducing someone to a group.

## First names and surnames

On forms and at reception desks you'll see **prénom** (first name) and **nom** or **nom de famille** (surname). In formal French paperwork, people often give the surname first and sometimes write it in capitals, for example *MARTIN Sophie*. That's when your spelling skills from <LessonLink id="a1-alphabet-and-accents" text="the alphabet lesson" /> come in handy: *Ça s'écrit comment ?* (How do you spell that?)

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't translate 'My name is' word for word">
  English speakers often say *Mon nom est…*, translating "My name is…" directly. It isn't wrong, but it sounds stiff, like reading from a form. Say **Je m'appelle…** instead. Also keep the little **m'**: without it, *J'appelle Marie* means "I'm calling Marie", as in calling her on the phone. And note the spelling: *je m'appelle* has two Ls, *vous vous appelez* has one.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Say bonjour first">
  In France an introduction almost always starts with *bonjour* (or *salut* among friends), often with a handshake or, between friends, *la bise*, a cheek-to-cheek greeting with a kissing sound. Jumping straight to your name without greeting someone first can come across as abrupt.
</Callout>
