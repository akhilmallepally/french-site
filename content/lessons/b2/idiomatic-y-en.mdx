By the end of this lesson you'll be able to use the common verbs built on *y* and *en*, and the neutral pronoun *le*, which French speakers use all the time in conversation.

## What you already know

You know *y* and *en* as pronouns that replace a place or a quantity, from <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-y" text="the lesson on y" /> and <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the lesson on en" />, and their order with other pronouns from <LessonLink id="b1-double-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on double object pronouns" />. In this lesson, *y* and *en* are part of a fixed verb. They don't replace a noun you could point to: the verb simply has a new meaning with them.

## Verbs with en

<VocabTable group="en" />

These verbs carry *en* as part of their meaning. Many are pronominal, so they take *être* in the passé composé, and both pronouns go before the auxiliary, in the order *me / te / se* + *en*: *je m'en suis allé*, *elle s'en est sortie*.

<Example fr="Il est tard, je m'en vais." en="It's late, I'm off." />
<Example fr="Ne t'en fais pas, tu vas t'en sortir." en="Don't worry, you'll manage." />
<Example fr="Elle a été très malade, mais elle s'en est remise." en="She was very ill, but she got over it." />

*En vouloir à quelqu'un* means to hold something against someone: *je lui en veux* (I'm angry with him). You met *je m'en veux* (I'm annoyed with myself) as a fixed phrase in <LessonLink id="b2-regret-and-reproach" text="the lesson on regret and reproach" />: it's the same verb, used reflexively. *Je n'en reviens pas* means "I can't believe it", for good or bad surprises. *En rester là* means to go no further: *nous allons en rester là pour aujourd'hui* (we'll leave it there for today).

## In the negative and the imperative

In the negative, *ne* goes before both pronouns: *je ne m'en fais pas*, *ne t'en fais pas*, *il ne s'y fait pas*. In the affirmative imperative, the pronouns follow the verb, joined with hyphens: *va-t'en* (go away), and formally *allez-vous-en*. You know *allez-y* (go ahead) from <LessonLink id="a2-imperative-with-pronouns" text="the lesson on the imperative with pronouns" />; it works the same way.

<Example fr="Ne t'en fais pas, ça y est, tout est prêt." en="Don't worry, that's it, everything's ready." />

## Having had enough

<VocabTable group="enough" />

You saw idioms with a register in <LessonLink id="b2-idioms-everyday" text="the lesson on everyday idioms" />. These three need the same care. **En avoir marre** is informal: use it with friends. **En avoir assez** is neutral and fine at work. **Ne plus en pouvoir** means you're exhausted or can't stand something any more.

<Example fr="J'en ai marre de ce bruit !" en="I'm fed up with this noise!" />
<Example fr="Après dix heures de travail, je n'en peux plus." en="After ten hours of work, I'm exhausted." />

The thing you're fed up with follows *de*: *j'en ai assez de ses retards*.

## Verbs with y

<VocabTable group="y" />

*Y tenir* means to care about something: *j'y tiens* (it matters to me). You know *tenir à* + noun from <LessonLink id="b2-relative-prepositions" text="the lesson on prepositions with relative pronouns" />; *y* replaces the *à* phrase. *S'y faire* (to get used to it) and *s'y prendre* (to go about it) are very common when talking about a new situation. In *on s'y fait*, *on* means people in general, as often in these phrases.

<Example fr="Le nouveau logiciel est difficile, mais on s'y fait." en="The new software is hard, but you get used to it." />
<Example fr="Je ne sais pas comment m'y prendre." en="I don't know how to go about it." />

**Ça y est** announces that something is done: *ça y est, j'ai fini !* **Y compris** means "including": *tout le monde, y compris le chef*.

## The neutral le

<VocabTable group="le" />

*Le* can replace a whole idea, not just a noun. English often drops the word "it" or uses "so", but French needs *le*.

<Example fr="— Il part demain ? — Oui, je le sais." en="'Is he leaving tomorrow?' 'Yes, I know.'" />
<Example fr="— Tu crois qu'il viendra ? — Je l'espère." en="'Do you think he'll come?' 'I hope so.'" />
<Example fr="Comme je te l'ai dit, la réunion est annulée." en="As I told you, the meeting is cancelled." />

In *comme je te l'ai dit*, *le* stands for the fact you told. Leaving it out (*comme je t'ai dit*) is common in speech, but the full form is standard. *Je m'en doutais* (I suspected as much) uses *en* in the same way, for an idea: *se douter de quelque chose*.

## En français

Inès asks her friend Thomas about the first month in his new job.

- *Alors, ce premier mois dans ton nouveau travail ?* (So, how was the first month in your new job?)
- *Difficile. Je n'en peux plus, les journées sont trop longues.* (Hard. I'm exhausted, the days are too long.)
- *Ne t'en fais pas, c'est normal au début. Tu vas t'y faire.* (Don't worry, that's normal at the start. You'll get used to it.)
- *Je l'espère. Mais mon chef m'en veut, je crois.* (I hope so. But I think my boss is angry with me.)
- *Pourquoi ? Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ?* (Why? What happened?)
- *J'ai oublié une réunion importante. Il n'a rien dit, mais je m'en doutais : il était fâché.* (I forgot an important meeting. He didn't say anything, but I suspected as much: he was annoyed.)
- *Et tu as parlé avec lui ?* (And did you talk to him?)
- *Oui, je me suis excusé, et nous en sommes restés là.* (Yes, I apologized, and we left it there.)
- *Tu vois, tu t'en es bien sorti. Tu veux garder ce poste ?* (You see, you managed well. Do you want to keep the job?)
- *Oui, j'y tiens. L'équipe est super, y compris mon chef !* (Yes, I care about it. The team is great, including my boss!)
- *Alors ça va aller. Comme je te l'ai dit, le premier mois est toujours le plus difficile.* (Then it'll be fine. As I told you, the first month is always the hardest.)
- *Je le sais. Mais j'en ai un peu marre de rentrer à neuf heures du soir !* (I know. But I'm a bit fed up with getting home at nine in the evening!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Je m'en vais, je m'en suis allé">
  With *s'en aller*, both pronouns stay together before the verb, and before the auxiliary in the passé composé. *Je me suis en allé* and *je me vais* are wrong. Say **je m'en vais** and **je m'en suis allé** (or **allée**). The imperative is **va-t'en** (go away).
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep a reply bank">
  Many of these phrases are replies: *je le sais*, *je l'espère*, *je m'en doutais*, *ne t'en fais pas*, *ça y est*. Write them on a card and, during your next conversation in French, try to use two of them when you answer someone. Short replies like these make your French sound natural quickly.
</Callout>

## En résumé

Dans s'en aller, s'en sortir, s'en faire, s'y faire ou y tenir, en et y font partie du verbe. Le pronom ne remplace pas un objet précis, et le verbe a un sens nouveau. Au passé composé, les deux pronoms vont devant être : je m'en suis allé. J'en ai marre est familier, mais j'en ai assez est courant. Enfin, le peut remplacer toute une idée : je le sais, je l'espère, comme je te l'ai dit.
