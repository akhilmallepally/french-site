## A few new words

These five words come up whenever journalists work with official sources.

<VocabTable />

*Un communiqué* is an official statement sent to the press by a town hall, a company or a ministry. *Un extrait* is a short part of a longer text or interview. *Une interview exclusive* is one that only one paper or channel has; *interview* is borrowed from English but pronounced the French way. *La version officielle* is what the authorities say happened, which a journalist may question. Before publishing, a good journalist *recoupe l'information*: they check it against a second, independent source, as you saw in the newsroom feature.

<Example fr="Un bon journaliste recoupe ses informations avant de publier." en="A good journalist cross-checks their information before publishing." />

## An interview and its report

A local paper publishes an exclusive interview with the mayor, Claire Dubois, about the town's swimming pool. Here is the interview, then the journalist's report of it.

*Journaliste : Madame la maire, pourquoi la piscine est-elle fermée ?*

*Claire Dubois : Elle est fermée parce qu'elle doit être rénovée.*

*Journaliste : Quand sera-t-elle ouverte ?*

*Claire Dubois : Le chantier commencera en mars, et tout aura été rénové d'ici septembre.*

*Journaliste : Pourquoi certains habitants n'ont-ils pas été informés ?*

*Claire Dubois : C'est vrai, il y a eu un problème. Nous publierons un communiqué demain.*

*Rapport : Dans une interview exclusive, la maire a expliqué que la piscine était fermée parce qu'elle devait être rénovée. Elle a annoncé que le chantier commencerait en mars et que tout aurait été rénové d'ici septembre. Le journaliste lui a demandé pourquoi certains habitants n'avaient pas été informés. Elle a admis qu'il y avait eu un problème, et elle a promis qu'un communiqué serait publié le lendemain.*

(Journalist: Madam Mayor, why is the pool closed? Claire Dubois: It's closed because it has to be renovated. Journalist: When will it be open? Claire Dubois: The work will start in March, and everything will have been renovated by September. Journalist: Why weren't some residents informed? Claire Dubois: It's true, there was a problem. We'll publish a press release tomorrow. Report: In an exclusive interview, the mayor explained that the pool was closed because it had to be renovated. She announced that the work would start in March and that everything would have been renovated by September. The journalist asked her why some residents hadn't been informed. She admitted that there had been a problem, and she promised that a press release would be published the next day.)

## A summary of the press release

The next day, the town hall publishes its *communiqué*. This is the model summary a student wrote of it.

*Résumé d'un extrait du communiqué : Le communiqué de la mairie porte sur la fermeture de la piscine. Il ressort que la rénovation commencera en mars. Selon la version officielle, la piscine sera ouverte en septembre. En somme, la mairie veut rassurer les habitants.*

(Summary of an extract from the press release: The town hall's press release is about the closure of the pool. It emerges that the renovation will start in March. According to the official version, the pool will be open in September. In short, the town hall wants to reassure residents.)

## How the texts use the grammar

- **The tense shifts.** *Est fermée* becomes *était fermée*, *commencera* becomes *commencerait*, *aura été rénové* becomes *aurait été rénové*, and *il y a eu* becomes *il y avait eu*.
- **Time words.** *Demain* becomes *le lendemain*, because the report is written later.
- **Reporting verbs.** *A expliqué* is neutral, *a annoncé* introduces news, and *a admis* shows that the mayor conceded a point.
- **The reported question** keeps *pourquoi* and drops the inversion: *pourquoi certains habitants n'avaient pas été informés*.
- **The passive.** *Doit être rénovée*, *aurait été rénové* and *serait publié* put the focus on the pool and the release, not on who does the work.
- **The summary** uses the present, a nominal phrase (*la fermeture de la piscine*), *il ressort que* and *en somme*, with no opinion.

## From interview to report

The journalist's report follows a method you can reuse.

1. **Choose what to keep.** A report doesn't repeat every question: it keeps the answers that matter to the reader.
2. **Choose the verb** for each answer: *expliquer* for a reason, *annoncer* for news, *admettre* for a concession, *promettre* for a commitment.
3. **Shift the tenses and the time words**, because the report is written after the interview.
4. **Credit the source** at the start (*dans une interview exclusive*), and say when a fact comes from one side only (*selon la version officielle*).

The same method works when you report a conversation with a colleague or a friend: you rarely report every word, only what changes something.

## Your turn

Choose a short interview, real or imagined, from a local news story: a mayor, a head teacher or a shop owner. Write two pieces, on paper or in a notes app.

1. **A report of the interview** in five or six sentences, in the past, with at least one reported question, one reported order or promise, and three different reporting verbs.
2. **A summary** of four sentences of the same story, in the present, with *porter sur*, *il ressort que* and *en somme*.

If the facts come from one person only, say so in your report: *selon la version officielle*, *d'après la maire*. And if you can, *recoupez l'information* with a second source.

## Check your work

- After *il a dit que* or *elle a annoncé que*, did you shift the tenses, unless the fact is still true?
- Did you change *demain* to *le lendemain* and *hier* to *la veille*?
- In reported questions, did you drop *est-ce que* and the inversion?
- In reported orders, is it *de* + infinitive, with *ne pas* before it?
- In the passive, does the participle agree with the subject, and is *été* unchanged?
- Is your summary in the present, and free of your own opinion?

<Callout type="warning" title="Demain becomes le lendemain">
  When you report an interview the next day, *demain* no longer means the same day. *Elle a promis qu'un communiqué serait publié demain* is wrong if you write it after the interview: say **elle a promis qu'un communiqué serait publié le lendemain**. Keep *demain* only if you report on the same day.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Write the report before rereading">
  After listening to a French interview on the radio, write your report from memory first, then listen again and correct it. You'll notice what you understood, which tenses you shifted, and which details you'd naturally leave out. That's exactly the skill a summary needs.
</Callout>
