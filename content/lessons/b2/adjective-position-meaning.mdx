By the end of this lesson you'll be able to use the adjectives whose meaning changes with their position, before or after the noun.

## What you already know

You know how adjectives agree, and that a few short ones such as *beau*, *jeune* and *petit* go before the noun, from <LessonLink id="a1-adjective-agreement" text="the lesson on adjective agreement" />. You've met *propre* as "clean" in <LessonLink id="a2-describing-your-town" text="the lesson on describing your town" />, and as "own" in *monter sa propre entreprise*, in <LessonLink id="b1-dreams-and-ambitions" text="the lesson on dreams and ambitions" />. This lesson explains that double meaning, and fifteen others like it.

## The principle

Most French adjectives go after the noun. A small group can go on either side, and the position changes the meaning. The general idea is simple:

- After the noun, the adjective has its literal, objective meaning, and it separates this thing from others: *un homme pauvre* has no money.
- Before the noun, the meaning is figurative or subjective, and the adjective says how the speaker sees it: *un pauvre homme* is a man you feel sorry for.

<Example fr="Ce pauvre homme a perdu son chien." en="This poor man has lost his dog." />
<Example fr="Cette association aide les familles pauvres." en="This association helps poor families." />

The adjective still agrees as usual, wherever it is: *une pauvre femme*, *des familles pauvres*.

## Time and age

<VocabTable group="time" />

*Ancien* before the noun means "former": *mon ancien professeur* no longer teaches me. After the noun, it means "old", for things: *un bâtiment ancien*.

<Example fr="Mon ancien collègue travaille maintenant dans un bâtiment ancien du centre-ville." en="My former colleague now works in an old building in the town centre." />

*Dernier* and *prochain* go after the noun when you count from today: *la semaine dernière* (last week), *la semaine prochaine* (next week). They go before the noun for the last or next one in a series: *la dernière semaine des vacances* (the final week of the holidays), *la prochaine fois* (the next time).

<Example fr="La dernière semaine des vacances, il a plu tous les jours." en="In the final week of the holidays, it rained every day." />
<Example fr="La prochaine fois, je réserverai plus tôt." en="Next time, I'll book earlier." />

*Même* before the noun means "same": *le même jour*. After it, it means "very", and stresses the noun: *le jour même* (that very day).

<Example fr="Nous avons notre anniversaire le même jour." en="We have our birthday on the same day." />
<Example fr="J'ai reçu sa lettre et je lui ai répondu le jour même." en="I got her letter and replied that very day." />

## Feelings and judgements

<VocabTable group="feeling" />

These adjectives say what the speaker feels when they come first. *Cher* before the noun is "dear", and after it "expensive". *Pauvre* before is "unfortunate", and after "without money". *Brave* before is "decent, kind", and after "brave", though *courageux* is more common for that. *Curieux* before is "odd", and after, for a person, "curious" or "inquisitive".

<Example fr="Mon cher ami, ce repas cher était une erreur !" en="My dear friend, this expensive meal was a mistake!" />
<Example fr="Le voisin est un brave homme : il aide tout le monde." en="The neighbour is a decent man: he helps everyone." />
<Example fr="Quelle curieuse idée de partir sans manteau !" en="What an odd idea to go out without a coat!" />
<Example fr="C'est un enfant curieux, qui pose beaucoup de questions." en="He's an inquisitive child, who asks lots of questions." />

*Sale* after the noun means "dirty". Before a person, it's an informal insult: *un sale type* (a nasty guy). Keep that one for friends, or for describing a film villain.

<Example fr="Ne mets pas cette chemise sale !" en="Don't put on that dirty shirt!" />

## Size and number

<VocabTable group="size" />

*Grand* is one of the short adjectives that go before the noun, but with people the position matters. *Un grand homme* is a great man; *un homme grand* is a tall man. For things, *grand* stays before and simply means "big": *une grande maison*.

<Example fr="Notre vieux maire était un grand homme, mais pas un homme grand." en="Our old mayor was a great man, but not a tall man." />

*Seul* before the noun means "only one": *un seul enfant*. After it, it means "on his own": *un enfant seul*. *Certain* before a plural noun means "some" (*certaines personnes*), and after it "sure" (*une chose certaine*). *Différent* before a plural noun means "various" (*différentes personnes*), and after it "different from each other".

<Example fr="Elle n'a qu'un seul enfant, mais il ne reste jamais un enfant seul à la maison." en="She has only one child, but he's never left alone at home." />
<Example fr="Certaines personnes pensent que c'est une chose certaine." en="Some people think it's a sure thing." />
<Example fr="J'ai parlé à différentes personnes, qui ont des avis différents." en="I spoke to various people, who have different opinions." />

## Quality

<VocabTable group="quality" />

*Propre* is the classic case. Before the noun, it means "own": *ma propre voiture*. After it, it means "clean": *une voiture propre*. *Simple* before the noun means "just, mere" (*une simple question*), and after it "easy, plain" (*une question simple*). *Vrai* before the noun means "real", a strong judgement (*un vrai problème*), and after it "true", the opposite of invented (*une histoire vraie*).

<Example fr="J'ai enfin ma propre voiture, et je la garde toujours propre." en="I finally have my own car, and I always keep it clean." />
<Example fr="C'est une simple question, mais ce n'est pas une question simple." en="It's just a question, but it isn't an easy question." />
<Example fr="Ce film raconte une histoire vraie, et c'est un vrai chef-d'œuvre." en="This film tells a true story, and it's a real masterpiece." />

## The whole list

- Before the noun, *ancien* means former, *dernier* the last in a series, *prochain* the next in a series, and *même* the same.
- After the noun, *ancien* means old, *dernier* and *prochain* count from today, and *même* means very.
- Before the noun, *cher*, *pauvre*, *brave*, *curieux* and *sale* give a feeling: dear, unfortunate, decent, odd, nasty.
- After the noun, they're literal: expensive, without money, brave, inquisitive, dirty.
- *Grand*, *seul*, *certain* and *différent* before the noun mean great, only one, some and various; after it, tall, on his own, sure and different.
- *Propre*, *simple* and *vrai* before the noun mean own, just and real; after it, clean, easy and true.

## En français

Inès and Hugo are at a *vernissage*, the opening night of an exhibition.

- *Tu vois l'homme près du grand tableau ? C'est mon ancien professeur de français.* (Do you see the man near the big painting? He's my former French teacher.)
- *Ton ancien professeur ? Il est jeune !* (Your former teacher? He's young!)
- *Il a quitté l'école l'année dernière. C'est un brave homme, toujours gentil.* (He left the school last year. He's a decent man, always kind.)
- *Et la femme à côté de lui, c'est qui ? C'est une femme grande !* (And the woman next to him, who is she? She's a tall woman!)
- *Oui, et c'est aussi une grande femme : c'est la peintre de l'exposition.* (Yes, and she's also a great woman: she's the painter of the exhibition.)
- *Ah, c'est elle ! J'aime ses toiles, mais certaines sont très chères.* (Oh, it's her! I like her canvases, but some are very expensive.)
- *C'est vrai. Mais c'est la seule exposition de l'année dans notre ville.* (True. But it's the only exhibition of the year in our town.)
- *Tu veux lui parler ? Tu la connais ?* (Do you want to talk to her? Do you know her?)
- *Un peu. C'est une curieuse personne : elle ne parle à personne pendant ses vernissages !* (A little. She's an odd person: she doesn't talk to anyone at her openings!)
- *Alors je lui écrirai la prochaine fois !* (Then I'll write to her next time!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Ma propre voiture, not ma voiture propre">
  To say "my own car", learners often put *propre* after the noun, as with most adjectives. *Ma voiture propre* means "my clean car". For "own", put it first: **ma propre voiture**, **sa propre chambre**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="One sentence, two meanings">
  For each pair, write one sentence that uses both positions, as in *ma propre voiture est enfin une voiture propre*. The contrast inside one sentence is easier to remember than two separate rules. Say it aloud a few times, and you'll hear which order sounds right.
</Callout>

## En résumé

En français, beaucoup d'adjectifs sont après le nom. Mais certains adjectifs changent de sens selon leur place. Après le nom, l'adjectif a son sens simple et concret : un bâtiment ancien, un repas cher, un homme pauvre, une voiture propre, une histoire vraie. Avant le nom, le sens est moins concret : il montre souvent ce que pense la personne qui parle : un ancien ami, mon cher ami, un pauvre homme, ma propre voiture, un vrai problème. Un grand homme est important, et un homme grand est grand, c'est tout. Un seul enfant veut dire un enfant, et un enfant seul n'a personne avec lui. L'adjectif s'accorde toujours avec le nom, avant ou après.
