By the end of this lesson you'll be able to connect a past choice with a present situation in one sentence, and to use *même si*, *sauf si* and *au cas où* in any tense.

## What you already know

In <LessonLink id="b2-si-plus-que-parfait" text="the last lesson" />, you learned the three *si* patterns: real, unreal present and unreal past. In each one, both halves of the sentence are in the same time frame. Real life is often less tidy: a choice you made ten years ago shapes your life today. This lesson lets you say that.

## A past condition, a present result

When the condition is in the past and its result is still true now, use **si + plus-que-parfait** with the **conditionnel présent**.

<Example fr="Si j'avais fait des études supérieures, j'aurais un meilleur salaire." en="If I'd gone to university, I'd have a better salary." />
<Example fr="Si elle s'était expatriée, elle vivrait au Canada maintenant." en="If she'd moved abroad, she'd be living in Canada now." />

Words such as *aujourd'hui*, *maintenant* or *aujourd'hui encore* often appear in the result, to show that it's about the present.

## A present condition, a past result

The other mix starts from a hypothesis about the present: something that isn't true now, such as speaking German, and imagines how the past would have gone with it. Use **si + imparfait** with the **conditionnel passé**.

<Example fr="Si je parlais allemand, j'aurais accepté ce poste." en="If I spoke German, I'd have accepted that job." />
<Example fr="Si tu étais plus prudent, tu n'aurais pas perdu ton argent." en="If you were more careful, you wouldn't have lost your money." />

The imparfait here doesn't describe the past: *si je parlais allemand* means "if I spoke German", which I don't. To check which tense you need, ask about each half separately: is it about now or about a moment in the past?

## Même si in every tense

<VocabTable group="conj" />

You know *même si* + present from <LessonLink id="b2-concession-opposition" text="the lesson on concession and opposition" />. It follows exactly the same tense rules as *si*, so it can introduce all three patterns and both mixes. As with *si*, the conditionnel never goes straight after it.

<Example fr="Même si j'étais riche, je continuerais à travailler." en="Even if I were rich, I'd keep working." />
<Example fr="Même si j'avais su, je serais parti." en="Even if I'd known, I'd have left." />

## Sauf si and au cas où

**Sauf si** (unless) gives the one case that would change things. It takes the indicative, like *si*. It's the everyday partner of *à moins que* + subjunctive, from <LessonLink id="b2-subjunctive-conjunctions" text="the lesson on conjunctions with the subjunctive" />.

<Example fr="Je viendrai, sauf s'il y a une grève." en="I'll come, unless there's a strike." />

**Au cas où** (in case) prepares for something that might happen. Unlike *si*, it takes the **conditionnel**.

<Example fr="Prends ton téléphone, au cas où tu aurais un problème." en="Take your phone, in case you have a problem." />

Don't mix the two up: *sauf si* says when the plan stops; *au cas où* says what you do just in case.

## Talking about your path

<VocabTable group="path" />

Mixed sentences are natural when you talk about careers and life choices. You know *une carrière* from <LessonLink id="b1-dreams-and-ambitions" text="the lesson on dreams and ambitions" />. *Un parcours* is the whole path, with its studies, jobs and changes. *Tracer sa route* means to follow your own way rather than other people's plans, and *rebondir* (to bounce back) is what you do after a failure.

<Example fr="Après son diplôme, il a tenté sa chance à Paris." en="After his degree, he tried his luck in Paris." />
<Example fr="Elle a perdu son travail, mais elle a rebondi." en="She lost her job, but she bounced back." />

*Réussir sa vie* (to make a success of your life) is a common phrase in debates about work and happiness. Notice *sa*: the possessive changes with the person, as in *réussir ma vie*.

<Example fr="Pour lui, réussir sa vie, c'est avoir du temps pour sa famille." en="For him, making a success of his life means having time for his family." />

## Looking back

<VocabTable group="look" />

*Un regret* is the noun for what you would change. *Le destin* is fate: some people believe in it, others prefer to say that they made their own choices.

<Example fr="Je ne crois pas au destin. Je préfère dire que j'ai fait des choix." en="I don't believe in fate. I prefer to say I made choices." />

*Prudent* means careful or cautious, as in *sois prudent sur la route* (be careful on the road).

<Example fr="Je n'ai qu'un regret : je n'ai pas appris l'anglais plus tôt." en="I've only got one regret: I didn't learn English sooner." />

## The whole list

- Past condition, present result: *si* + plus-que-parfait, conditionnel présent.
- Present condition, past result: *si* + imparfait, conditionnel passé.
- *Même si* follows the same rules as *si*.
- *Sauf si* + indicative: unless.
- *Au cas où* + conditionnel: in case.

## En français

Julien, thirty-five, meets a careers adviser, Madame Martin.

- *Alors, Julien, pourquoi voulez-vous changer de métier ?* (So, Julien, why do you want to change careers?)
- *J'aime cuisiner. Si j'avais suivi mon envie à vingt ans, je serais cuisinier aujourd'hui.* (I love cooking. If I'd followed my wish at twenty, I'd be a cook today.)
- *Et pourquoi ne l'avez-vous pas fait ?* (And why didn't you?)
- *Mes parents voulaient que j'aie un diplôme. Et si j'étais moins prudent, j'aurais tenté ma chance plus tôt.* (My parents wanted me to get a degree. And if I were less cautious, I'd have tried my luck sooner.)
- *Je comprends. Mais aujourd'hui, vous avez des regrets ou envie de rebondir ?* (I understand. But today, do you have regrets, or do you want to bounce back?)
- *Envie de rebondir ! Même si j'ai trente-cinq ans, il n'est pas trop tard, non ?* (I want to bounce back! Even though I'm thirty-five, it's not too late, is it?)
- *Pas du tout. Beaucoup de gens changent de parcours à votre âge.* (Not at all. Lots of people change path at your age.)
- *Et si je ne trouve pas de travail après ma formation ?* (And what if I don't find a job after my training?)
- *Gardez votre poste à temps partiel, au cas où la formation serait difficile.* (Keep your job part-time, in case the training is hard.)
- *Bonne idée. Je commence en septembre, sauf si l'école est complète.* (Good idea. I'll start in September, unless the school is full.)
- *Parfait. Il n'est jamais trop tard pour tracer sa route.* (Perfect. It's never too late to go your own way.)
- *Merci. Aujourd'hui encore, je me demande pourquoi j'ai attendu quinze ans !* (Thank you. Even today, I wonder why I waited fifteen years!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Au cas où takes the conditionnel">
  *Au cas où* looks like *si*, but it doesn't follow the *si* rule. Don't say *au cas où il pleut*: say **au cas où il pleuvrait**. And don't use the conditionnel after *si* meaning "if", *même si* or *sauf si*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Draw a timeline">
  Before you write a mixed sentence, draw a short line with two marks: "then" and "now". Put the condition on one mark and the result on the other. A condition on "then" needs the plus-que-parfait; a condition on "now" needs the imparfait. A result on "now" needs the conditionnel présent; a result on "then" needs the conditionnel passé.
</Callout>

## En résumé

Dans une phrase avec si, le passé et le présent peuvent se mélanger. Si j'avais fait des études, j'aurais un meilleur salaire : la condition est dans le passé, la conséquence est dans le présent. Si je parlais allemand, j'aurais accepté ce poste : la condition est imaginée, parce qu'en réalité je ne parle pas allemand, et la conséquence est dans le passé. Avec même si, on choisit les temps comme avec si. Sauf si prend l'indicatif, mais au cas où prend le conditionnel.
