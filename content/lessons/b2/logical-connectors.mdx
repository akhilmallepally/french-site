By the end of this lesson you'll be able to link the ideas in a paragraph so that a reader can follow your reasoning: adding, explaining, drawing consequences and giving causes.

## What you already know

You know *car*, *donc* and *c'est pourquoi* from <LessonLink id="b1-cause-and-consequence" text="the lesson on cause and consequence" />, and you can order arguments with *premièrement* and *deuxièmement*, from <LessonLink id="b1-presenting-an-argument" text="the lesson on presenting an argument" />. In <LessonLink id="b2-concession-opposition" text="the lesson on concession and opposition" />, you learned the connectors that say "but". This lesson covers all the others, the ones that make written French sound organized.

## Adding an idea: de plus, en outre, d'ailleurs

<VocabTable group="add" />

*De plus* (moreover), which you know from <LessonLink id="b1-review-opinions" text="the review of opinions" />, adds an argument in the same direction. *En outre* (furthermore) does the same in formal writing. *D'ailleurs* (besides) adds an extra argument that supports the first one, often as an afterthought; in speech it can also mean "by the way".

<Example fr="Ce quartier est calme. En outre, il y a beaucoup de parcs." en="This area is quiet. Furthermore, there are lots of parks." />
<Example fr="Je n'irai pas à la fête. D'ailleurs, personne ne m'a invité." en="I won't go to the party. Besides, nobody invited me." />

## Explaining: en effet, c'est-à-dire, autrement dit

<VocabTable group="explain" />

**En effet** is a false friend. It doesn't mean "in effect": it confirms or explains what you've just said, like "indeed" or "that's because". *C'est-à-dire* (that is to say) and *autrement dit* (in other words) rephrase an idea more clearly.

<Example fr="Il est fatigué. En effet, il a mal dormi." en="He's tired. That's because he slept badly." />
<Example fr="Elle travaille à temps partiel, c'est-à-dire trois jours par semaine." en="She works part-time, that is to say three days a week." />

**Or** is a short but important word in argument. It introduces a new fact that changes the reasoning, like "now" or "yet" at the start of an English sentence. It's mostly written, and it usually comes after a full stop, followed by a comma.

<Example fr="Il voulait partir en train. Or, la gare était fermée." en="He wanted to leave by train. Yet the station was closed." />

## Drawing a consequence

<VocabTable group="result" />

*Ainsi* (thus) and *par conséquent* (consequently) start a sentence that follows from the one before. *Par conséquent* is a more formal *donc*. **Si bien que** joins the consequence in the same sentence, with the indicative. **D'où** is followed by a noun, not a verb: *d'où le retard* (hence the delay).

<Example fr="Les trains étaient en grève. Par conséquent, beaucoup de gens ont travaillé de chez eux." en="The trains were on strike. Consequently, many people worked from home." />
<Example fr="Il a raté le bus, si bien qu'il est arrivé en retard." en="He missed the bus, so he arrived late." />
<Example fr="Les loyers sont très chers, d'où les manifestations de samedi." en="Rents are very expensive, hence Saturday's demonstrations." />

*Tellement… que* (so much… that) puts the reason and the result in one sentence. *Tellement* goes before the adjective, the adverb or the participle; *si* can replace it before an adjective: *il est si fatigué qu'il dort*.

<Example fr="Il a tellement travaillé qu'il est fatigué." en="He worked so much that he's tired." />

## Giving a cause: étant donné que, vu que, du fait que

<VocabTable group="cause" />

*Étant donné que* (given that), *vu que* (seeing that) and *du fait que* (because of the fact that) all introduce a cause and take the indicative. *Vu que* is common in speech; *étant donné que* and *du fait que* are more formal.

<Example fr="Étant donné qu'il pleut, le match est annulé." en="Given that it's raining, the match is cancelled." />

**Faute de** (for lack of) and **à force de** (by doing something again and again) are followed by a noun or an infinitive. *Faute de* gives a cause that's missing; *à force de* gives one that's repeated.

<Example fr="Faute de temps, je n'ai pas fini le livre." en="For lack of time, I didn't finish the book." />
<Example fr="À force de travailler, elle a réussi son examen." en="By working hard, she passed her exam." />

## Summing up: en somme

<VocabTable group="sum" />

*En somme* (in short) introduces a conclusion. It's a little more formal than *bref*, which you know from <LessonLink id="b1-telling-an-anecdote" text="the lesson on telling an anecdote" />.

## Punctuation

In writing, most of these connectors start a sentence and are followed by a comma: *En outre, …*, *Par conséquent, …*. *Si bien que* joins two clauses inside one sentence, usually after a comma; careful writers don't start a sentence with it. *Étant donné que* and *vu que* can start the sentence or follow the main clause.

## Either… or

| Structure | Example |

| *ou bien* | *On prend le train, ou bien on y va en voiture.* |

## En français

Clara asks her teacher, Monsieur Rivière, to look at a paragraph of her essay.

- *Monsieur, vous pouvez regarder mon paragraphe ? Il me semble qu'il manque des liens entre les idées.* (Sir, could you look at my paragraph? It seems to me that the links between the ideas are missing.)
- *Voyons. Vous écrivez : « Le vélo est pratique en ville. Il ne pollue pas. » Quel est le lien ?* (Let's see. You write: "Bikes are practical in town. They don't pollute." What's the link?)
- *C'est une deuxième idée. Je peux mettre « en outre » ?* (It's a second idea. Can I put "en outre"?)
- *Oui, ou « de plus ». Et ici : « Il y a de plus en plus de parkings à vélos. Beaucoup de gens prennent le vélo. »* (Yes, or "de plus". And here: "There are more and more bike racks. Lots of people cycle.")
- *C'est une conséquence. Alors « par conséquent » ?* (It's a consequence. So "par conséquent"?)
- *Exactement. Et à la fin, vous dites la même idée deux fois.* (Exactly. And at the end, you say the same idea twice.)
- *Alors je mets « autrement dit » ?* (So I put "autrement dit"?)
- *Oui, ou vous supprimez la deuxième phrase. C'est encore plus clair.* (Yes, or you delete the second sentence. That's even clearer.)
- *D'accord. En somme, il faut un connecteur entre chaque idée ?* (OK. In short, I need a connector between every idea?)
- *Pas toujours. Mais il faut qu'on comprenne le lien.* (Not always. But the reader has to understand the link.)

<Callout type="warning" title="En effet confirms, it doesn't contrast">
  *En effet* looks like "in effect", but it means "indeed" or "that's because": it confirms the sentence before. Don't use it for a contrast: *Il fait beau. En effet, nous restons à la maison* makes no sense. Write **Il fait beau. Pourtant, nous restons à la maison**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Highlight by function">
  Take a short French article and highlight every connector in one of four colours: adding, explaining, consequence, cause. Then look at where each colour falls in the paragraph. You'll see that good writers use few connectors, and often at the start of a sentence, although some, such as *si bien que*, *d'où* or *étant donné que*, can also sit in the middle. Copy that habit in your own writing.
</Callout>

## En résumé

Pour ajouter une idée, on dit de plus, en outre ou d'ailleurs. En effet explique ou confirme ce qu'on vient de dire. Or présente un fait qui change la situation. Pour une conséquence, on peut dire ainsi, par conséquent, si bien que, ou d'où avec un nom. Étant donné que, vu que et du fait que donnent une cause, avec l'indicatif. Faute de et à force de sont suivis d'un nom ou d'un infinitif. Pour finir, on dit en somme.
