By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say why you do something, and what you want to avoid, with both an infinitive and the subjunctive.

## What you already know

You've used *pour* + infinitive since A2: *Je suis ici pour apprendre* (I'm here to learn), from <LessonLink id="a2-prepositions-with-places" text="the lesson on prepositions with places" />. In <LessonLink id="b1-subjunctive-vs-infinitive" text="the lesson on the subjunctive versus the infinitive" />, you learned the key rule: same subject, infinitive; different subjects, *que* + subjunctive. And in <LessonLink id="b2-subjunctive-conjunctions" text="the lesson on conjunctions with the subjunctive" />, you met *bien que*, *avant que* and the expletive *ne*. This lesson applies all of that to purpose.

## Pour, afin de, pour que, afin que

<VocabTable group="aim" />

The rule is the same for every purpose expression. If the person who acts and the person who benefits are the same, use **pour** or **afin de** + infinitive. If they're different, use **pour que** or **afin que** + subjunctive.

<Example fr="Je prends des notes pour ne rien oublier." en="I take notes so as not to forget anything." />
<Example fr="Je prends des notes pour que tu puisses les lire." en="I take notes so that you can read them." />

*Afin de* and *afin que* mean the same as *pour* and *pour que*, but they're formal. You'll find them in letters, reports and official texts. In conversation, *pour* is the normal choice.

<Example fr="Merci de répondre avant vendredi afin que nous puissions organiser la réunion." en="Please reply before Friday so that we can organize the meeting." />

Notice the negative with the infinitive: *pour ne rien oublier*, *pour ne pas être en retard*. Both parts of the negative come before the infinitive.

## Saying what you want to avoid

<VocabTable group="fear" />

Sometimes the purpose is to stop something from happening. *De peur de* (for fear of) + infinitive works with one subject. *De peur que* + subjunctive works with two. *De crainte que* means the same, in a more formal register.

<Example fr="Il ne dit rien de peur de se tromper." en="He says nothing for fear of being wrong." />
<Example fr="Elle prend un parapluie de peur qu'il ne pleuve." en="She takes an umbrella in case it rains." />

After *de peur que* and *de crainte que*, you'll often see the expletive *ne*, which doesn't make the sentence negative. It's common in writing and optional in speech: *de peur qu'il pleuve* means the same thing.

*Éviter que* (to avoid something happening) also takes the subjunctive. With one subject, use *éviter de* + infinitive.

<Example fr="Nous voulons éviter que les supermarchés gaspillent les produits." en="We want to stop supermarkets from wasting products." />

## De sorte que: two meanings

<VocabTable group="sorte" />

**De sorte que** changes meaning with the mood after it. With the subjunctive, it gives a purpose (so that). With the indicative, it gives a result (so, with the result that), like *si bien que* from <LessonLink id="b2-logical-connectors" text="the lesson on logical connectors" />.

<Example fr="Parle fort, de sorte que tout le monde t'entende." en="Speak loudly, so that everyone can hear you." />
<Example fr="Il a parlé fort, de sorte que tout le monde l'a entendu." en="He spoke loudly, so everyone heard him." />

The first sentence describes an aim: it hasn't happened yet. The second describes a fact. If you can't decide which mood to use, ask yourself whether the second part is a hope or something that really happened.

## Formal goals: dans le but de, en vue de, viser à

<VocabTable group="goal" />

These are useful in reports, project descriptions and opinion texts. *Dans le but de* (with the aim of) + infinitive is a heavier *pour*. *En vue de* (with a view to) is usually followed by a noun: *en vue des élections* (with the elections in mind). *Viser à* + infinitive means "to aim to". *En faveur de* (in favour of) doesn't express a purpose, but it often appears next to these words: *une campagne en faveur du vélo* (a campaign in favour of cycling).

<Example fr="La ville a ouvert un centre d'accueil dans le but d'aider les sans-abri." en="The city opened a drop-in centre with the aim of helping homeless people." />
<Example fr="Ce projet vise à réduire les inégalités." en="This project aims to reduce inequality." />

## Talking about a campaign

<VocabTable group="campaign" />

*Une campagne* (a campaign) usually has a goal, so it brings purpose structures with it. *Encourager quelqu'un à* + infinitive pushes people to do something; *dissuader quelqu'un de* + infinitive pushes them not to. You know *sensibiliser* (to raise awareness) from <LessonLink id="b1-review-society" text="the review of society" />, and *convaincre* (to convince) from <LessonLink id="b1-review-opinions" text="the review of opinions" />.

<Example fr="Cette campagne encourage les jeunes à voter." en="This campaign encourages young people to vote." />
<Example fr="Les affiches visent à dissuader les gens de fumer." en="The posters aim to deter people from smoking." />

## The whole list

- Same subject: *pour*, *afin de*, *de peur de*, *dans le but de*, *éviter de* + infinitive.
- Different subjects: *pour que*, *afin que*, *de peur que*, *de crainte que*, *éviter que* + subjunctive.
- With a noun: *en vue de*, *en faveur de*.
- With two moods: *de sorte que* + subjunctive (purpose) or + indicative (result).

## En français

Two members of a charity plan a campaign against food waste.

- *Bon, notre campagne contre le gaspillage commence le mois prochain. Quel est notre objectif ?* (Right, our campaign against food waste starts next month. What's our goal?)
- *Je dirais qu'on vise à changer les habitudes, pas seulement à informer.* (I'd say we aim to change habits, not just to inform.)
- *D'accord. Alors on fait des affiches pour que les gens voient le problème ?* (OK. So we make posters so that people see the problem?)
- *Oui, mais aussi des ateliers de cuisine, afin de montrer comment cuisiner les restes.* (Yes, but also cooking workshops, to show how to cook leftovers.)
- *Bonne idée. Et les supermarchés ? Il faut éviter qu'ils gaspillent les produits.* (Good idea. And the supermarkets? We have to stop them wasting products.)
- *On peut leur écrire, de sorte qu'ils donnent les produits à des associations.* (We can write to them, so that they give the products to charities.)
- *Oui, mais pas tout de suite, de peur qu'ils ne disent non.* (Yes, but not straight away, in case they say no.)
- *Tu as raison. On commence par les familles, et on leur écrit en vue de la réunion de mars.* (You're right. We start with families, and we write to them with the March meeting in mind.)
- *Parfait. Je prépare un texte pour que tout le monde puisse le lire.* (Perfect. I'll prepare a text so that everyone can read it.)
- *Super. Envoie-le avant jeudi, pour qu'on ait le temps d'en parler.* (Great. Send it before Thursday, so that we have time to talk about it.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Pour + infinitive with one subject">
  Don't use *pour que* when both parts have the same subject: *Je travaille pour que je réussisse* is wrong. With one subject, use the infinitive: **Je travaille pour réussir** (I work to succeed).
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask who benefits">
  Before you write a purpose sentence, ask yourself two questions: who acts, and who benefits? If the answer is the same person, reach for an infinitive. If it's two different people, you need *que* and the subjunctive. This quick check works for every expression in this lesson.
</Callout>

## En résumé

Pour dire pourquoi on fait quelque chose, on dit pour ou afin de avec l'infinitif quand c'est la même personne. Avec deux personnes, on dit pour que ou afin que avec le subjonctif. Afin de et afin que sont du registre soutenu. De peur de et de peur que expriment une crainte, souvent avec ne après que. Éviter que prend aussi le subjonctif. De sorte que change de sens : avec le subjonctif, c'est un but, et avec l'indicatif, c'est une conséquence.
