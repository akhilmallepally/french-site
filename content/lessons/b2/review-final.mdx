By the end of this lesson you'll be able to look back over the whole B2 level, check what you can do, and write your own stock-take in French.

## What you've learned

B2 has eight units. Skim this checklist, one line per unit, and go back to the review of any unit that feels shaky.

- **Unit 1, nuance and society.** You can link ideas with *bien que*, *pour que* and *pourtant*, and nuance an opinion, from <LessonLink id="b2-review-nuance" text="the review of nuance" />.
- **Unit 2, what might have been.** You can talk about the unreal past with *si j'avais su, je serais venu*, and use the futur antérieur, from <LessonLink id="b2-review-what-might-have-been" text="the review of what might have been" />.
- **Unit 3, news and reporting.** You can report speech in the past, use the passive and write in a nominal style, from <LessonLink id="b2-review-news" text="the review of the news" />.
- **Unit 4, precision with pronouns.** You can use *lequel*, *dont*, the subjunctive in relative clauses, idiomatic *y* and *en*, and the mise en relief, from <LessonLink id="b2-review-pronouns" text="the review of pronouns" />.
- **Unit 5, work and the economy.** You can use *faire* + infinitive, participles and the past infinitive, and write a report, from <LessonLink id="b2-review-work" text="the review of work" />.
- **Unit 6, science and health.** You can say how sure you are, from *il est certain que* to *il se peut que*, and write an argumentative essay, from <LessonLink id="b2-review-science" text="the review of science" />.
- **Unit 7, culture and the arts.** You can recognize the passé simple, use adjectives that change meaning and the forms of *tout*, and describe a work of art, from <LessonLink id="b2-review-arts" text="the review of culture and the arts" />.
- **Unit 8, registers and debate.** You can choose a register, understand spoken French and slang, avoid false friends, and debate and persuade, from <LessonLink id="b2-registers" text="the lesson on registers" /> to <LessonLink id="b2-persuasion-and-hedging" text="the lesson on persuasion" />.

## One sentence per unit

Here is one model sentence for each unit, built on its key structure. Read them aloud: if one of them feels difficult, that unit is the one to review.

<Example fr="Si nous étions partis plus tôt, nous n'aurions pas raté le train." en="If we had left earlier, we wouldn't have missed the train. (Unit 2: the unreal past)" />
<Example fr="Le ministre a annoncé que la loi serait votée en mai." en="The minister announced that the law would be passed in May. (Unit 3: reported speech and the passive)" />

## A few new words

These six words help you talk about your own learning.

<VocabTable />

*L'aisance* is the ease with which you speak: fluency without effort. To *perfectionner* something is to make it better, and to *approfondir* a subject is to go further into it. *Une lacune* is a gap in what you know. A learner *se fixe un objectif* and follows *un parcours d'apprentissage*, a learning path.

<Example fr="Je parle avec plus d'aisance qu'il y a un an." en="I speak more fluently than a year ago." />
<Example fr="Elle veut perfectionner sa prononciation." en="She wants to improve her pronunciation." />
<Example fr="J'ai encore quelques lacunes en grammaire." en="I still have a few gaps in grammar." />
<Example fr="Cette année, je vais approfondir la littérature." en="This year, I'm going to study literature in more depth." />
<Example fr="Je me suis fixé un objectif : lire un roman par mois." en="I've set myself a goal: to read one novel a month." />
<Example fr="Chaque personne a son propre parcours d'apprentissage." en="Each person has their own learning path." />

## A model stock-take

This is an original model text, *Mon bilan de B2*: a learner looks back on the level. Each paragraph keeps one time frame, and together they use a structure from every unit.

*Mon bilan de B2*

*Il y a un an, je parlais déjà assez bien, mais j'avais beaucoup de lacunes. Je ne savais pas nuancer mes opinions, et je comprenais mal les journaux.*

*Aujourd'hui, je lis la presse sans difficulté : je reconnais le passif et le discours indirect. Je cherche des livres qui me fassent réfléchir, et je reconnais même le passé simple dans les romans. Bien que mon accent ne soit pas parfait, je parle avec plus d'aisance, et je sais choisir mon registre.*

*Si j'avais commencé plus tôt, j'aurais sans doute progressé plus vite.*

*L'année prochaine, je ferai lire mes textes à une amie, et j'approfondirai la littérature. Il est probable que je passerai l'examen de C1 dans deux ans.*

*Tout compte fait, mon parcours d'apprentissage est long, mais c'est un vrai plaisir. Je me suis fixé un nouvel objectif : perfectionner mon français, quoi qu'on en dise !*

(My B2 stock-take. A year ago, I already spoke quite well, but I had a lot of gaps. I couldn't nuance my opinions, and I understood the newspapers badly. Today, I read the press without difficulty: I recognize the passive and reported speech. I look for books that make me think, and I even recognize the passé simple in novels. Although my accent isn't perfect, I speak more fluently, and I know how to choose my register. If I had started earlier, I would probably have made faster progress. Next year, I'll have a friend read my texts, and I'll study literature in more depth. I'll probably take the C1 exam in two years. All things considered, my learning path is long, but it's a real pleasure. I've set myself a new goal: to perfect my French, whatever people say!)

## How the text uses the grammar

- The first paragraph uses the imparfait for a past situation, and the second uses the present.
- *Bien que mon accent ne soit pas parfait* is a subjunctive conjunction (Unit 1).
- *Si j'avais commencé…, j'aurais progressé* is the unreal past (Unit 2).
- *Le passif* and *le discours indirect* are the news tools of Unit 3.
- *Des livres qui me fassent réfléchir* is a subjunctive in a relative clause, for something you're looking for (Unit 4).
- *Je ferai lire mes textes à une amie* is *faire* + infinitive (Unit 5).
- *Il est probable que je passerai*, with the indicative, comes from Unit 6.
- *Tout compte fait* and *un vrai plaisir* (adjective before the noun) come from Unit 7.
- *Quoi qu'on en dise* is a persuasion phrase from Unit 8.

## Your turn

Write your own *bilan de B2*, of five short paragraphs, on paper or in a notes app.

1. **Before**, in the imparfait: what you could and couldn't do a year ago.
2. **Now**, in the present: three things you can do, with *bien que* and a relative clause.
3. **A regret**, with *si* + plus-que-parfait and the conditionnel passé.
4. **Plans**, in the futur simple, with *faire* + infinitive and *il est probable que*.
5. **A conclusion**, with *tout compte fait*, *se fixer un objectif* and one persuasion phrase.

## Check your work

- Does each paragraph keep one time frame?
- After *bien que*, *avant que* and *il se peut que*, is the verb in the subjunctive?
- After *il est probable que*, is it in the indicative?
- In your regret, is the *si* clause in the plus-que-parfait and the main clause in the conditionnel passé?
- Did you keep the standard register throughout, with *ne* in every negation?

<Callout type="warning" title="Je me suis fixé un objectif">
  With *se fixer un objectif*, learners often make the participle agree with the speaker: *je me suis fixée un objectif*, said by a woman. Here *se* isn't the direct object; *un objectif* is, and it comes after the verb. So the participle doesn't change: **je me suis fixé un objectif**, for a man or a woman.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Plan your next three months">
  Finish B2 by writing down three precise goals for the next three months: one for reading, one for speaking, one for writing. For each, write one sentence in French with *je me suis fixé un objectif*. Check them again at the end of each month.
</Callout>
