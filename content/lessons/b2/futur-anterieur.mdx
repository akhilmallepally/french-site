By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say what will be done by a deadline, to put two future actions in order, and to guess what has probably happened.

## What you already know

You know the futur simple (*je finirai*, *nous serons*) from <LessonLink id="a2-futur-simple" text="the lesson on the futur simple" />. In <LessonLink id="b1-time-connectors" text="the lesson on time connectors" />, you learned that *quand*, *lorsque* and *dès que* take the futur when the main verb is in the futur: *quand j'arriverai, je t'appellerai*. This lesson adds a compound tense for the action that comes first.

## Forming the futur antérieur

<VocabTable group="form" />

The futur antérieur is *avoir* or *être* in the futur simple, followed by the past participle. The choice of auxiliary and the agreement work as in the passé composé.

| Person | avoir + participle | être + participle |
|---|---|---|
| je | *j'aurai fini* | *je serai parti(e)* |
| tu | *tu auras fini* | *tu seras parti(e)* |
| il / elle | *il aura fini* | *elle sera partie* |
| nous | *nous aurons fini* | *nous serons parti(e)s* |
| vous | *vous aurez fini* | *vous serez parti(e)(s)* |
| ils / elles | *ils auront fini* | *elles seront parties* |

Be careful with the spelling: *j'aurai* (futur) has no *s*, while *j'aurais* (conditionnel, from <LessonLink id="b2-conditionnel-passe" text="the lesson on the conditionnel passé" />) does. The two sound almost the same.

## Done by a point in the future

<VocabTable group="time" />

The first use describes an action that will be finished before a moment in the future. **D'ici** + a date or a time gives the deadline: *d'ici vendredi* (by Friday), *d'ici la fin du mois* (by the end of the month).

<Example fr="D'ici la fin du mois, nous aurons fini le rapport." en="By the end of the month, we'll have finished the report." />
<Example fr="À huit heures, elle sera déjà partie." en="At eight o'clock, she'll already have left." />

## Two future actions

When two actions are both in the future, French marks the one that comes first with the futur antérieur, after *quand*, *lorsque*, *dès que* or **une fois que** (once). The later action is in the futur or the imperative. English uses the present or the present perfect here, so this is easy to forget.

<Example fr="Dès que j'aurai fini, je partirai." en="As soon as I've finished, I'll leave." />
<Example fr="Une fois que tu auras lu le dossier, appelle-moi." en="Once you've read the file, call me." />

*Après que*, which you met in <LessonLink id="b2-subjunctive-conjunctions" text="the lesson on conjunctions with the subjunctive" />, takes the indicative, so it works in the same way: *après que tu seras parti, je fermerai la porte*.

## Since: depuis que

<VocabTable group="since" />

You know *depuis* + a time or a noun from <LessonLink id="a2-depuis-pendant-il-y-a" text="the lesson on depuis, pendant and il y a" />: *depuis lundi*. **Depuis que** introduces a whole clause instead, with the indicative. The situation started in the past and still continues, so the main verb is usually in the present.

<Example fr="Depuis qu'il habite à Lyon, il est plus heureux." en="Since he's been living in Lyon, he's happier." />
<Example fr="Depuis que j'ai changé de travail, je dors mieux." en="Since I changed jobs, I've been sleeping better." />

## Projects and deadlines

<VocabTable group="project" />

The futur antérieur is the language of planning. *Une échéance* is a due date, and *respecter un délai* means to meet a deadline. When something goes wrong, you *repousser* (postpone) a stage. You know *un imprévu* (something unexpected) from <LessonLink id="b1-si-present-futur" text="the lesson on si with the present" />.

<Example fr="Nous aurons livré le projet avant l'échéance." en="We'll have delivered the project before the deadline." />
<Example fr="La réunion prévue lundi est repoussée à jeudi." en="The meeting planned for Monday is put back to Thursday." />

*Boucler un projet* is an informal but common way to say "to wrap up a project" at work.

## Guessing about the past

<VocabTable group="guess" />

The futur antérieur can also give a guess about something that has probably already happened. *Il aura oublié* means the same as *il a sûrement oublié* (he must have forgotten), with *sûrement* from <LessonLink id="b1-expressing-doubt-certainty" text="the lesson on doubt and certainty" />. It's common in speech when someone is late or missing.

<Example fr="Marie n'est pas là ? Elle se sera trompée de salle." en="Marie isn't here? She must have gone to the wrong room." />

*Se tromper de* + noun means to get the wrong one: *se tromper de date*, *se tromper de numéro*.

## The whole list

- Formation: *avoir* or *être* in the futur simple + past participle.
- By a deadline: *d'ici vendredi, j'aurai fini*.
- The earlier of two future actions, after *quand*, *lorsque*, *dès que*, *une fois que* and *après que*.
- A guess about the past: *il aura oublié*.
- *Depuis que* + indicative: a situation that started in the past and continues.

## En français

Madame Dupont, a team leader, checks a project with her colleague Antoine.

- *Antoine, où en est le projet ? L'échéance, c'est le 30.* (Antoine, how's the project going? The deadline is the 30th.)
- *Nous avons fini la première étape. D'ici vendredi, nous aurons fini la deuxième.* (We've finished the first stage. By Friday, we'll have finished the second.)
- *Très bien. Et le rapport pour le client ?* (Very good. And the report for the client?)
- *Je l'écrirai dès que Sophie aura envoyé les documents.* (I'll write it as soon as Sophie has sent the documents.)
- *Elle n'a pas encore envoyé les documents ? Elle aura oublié.* (She hasn't sent the documents yet? She must have forgotten.)
- *Peut-être. Depuis qu'elle travaille sur l'autre projet, elle est très occupée.* (Maybe. Since she's been working on the other project, she's very busy.)
- *Je l'appellerai. Une fois que vous aurez le rapport, pourrez-vous livrer le projet lundi ?* (I'll call her. Once you have the report, will you be able to deliver the project on Monday?)
- *Lundi, c'est difficile. Il y a toujours un imprévu. Nous pourrions repousser à mercredi ?* (Monday is difficult. There's always something unexpected. Could we put it back to Wednesday?)
- *D'accord, mais il faut respecter ce délai : le client a prévu une réunion jeudi.* (OK, but we have to meet that deadline: the client has planned a meeting on Thursday.)
- *Compris. Je vais changer le calendrier tout de suite.* (Understood. I'll change the schedule straight away.)
- *Merci. Et attention à ne pas vous tromper de jour dans le message !* (Thanks. And be careful not to get the day wrong in the message!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Quand j'aurai fini, not quand j'ai fini">
  English says "when I've finished", so learners often write *je t'appellerai quand j'ai fini*. When the main verb is in the futur, the earlier action after *quand* or *dès que* goes in the futur antérieur: **je t'appellerai quand j'aurai fini**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Plan your week backwards">
  On Monday morning, write three sentences about Friday evening with *d'ici vendredi* or *vendredi soir*: *vendredi soir, j'aurai fini mon rapport, j'aurai appelé ma sœur et je serai allé à la piscine*. On Friday, check which ones came true. It's a planning habit and a grammar drill at the same time.
</Callout>

## En résumé

Le futur antérieur se forme avec avoir ou être au futur, puis le participe passé : j'aurai fini, elle sera partie. Il montre qu'une action sera finie avant un moment du futur : d'ici vendredi, nous aurons fini. Après quand, dès que ou une fois que, il marque l'action qui vient en premier : dès que j'aurai fini, je partirai. Il permet aussi de dire ce qui est sûrement arrivé : il aura oublié.
