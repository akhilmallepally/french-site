By the end of this lesson you'll be able to read a French news article, pick out its main idea and key points, and summarise it neutrally, crediting the source.

## What you already know

You know the press vocabulary (*un quotidien*, *à la une*, *une source*) from <LessonLink id="b1-media-and-news" text="the lesson on media and news" />, and *en résumé* from <LessonLink id="b1-presenting-an-argument" text="the lesson on presenting an argument" />. In this unit, you've learned to report speech, to choose a reporting verb, and to write in a nominal style, in <LessonLink id="b2-nominalisation" text="the lesson on nominalisation" />. A summary uses all three.

## What a summary contains

<VocabTable group="topic" />

A good summary of a news article, *un résumé*, answers four questions in a few sentences.

- **The source:** which paper, and what kind of text (an article, an interview, an editorial).
- **The main idea:** what the article *porte sur* (is about), in one sentence.
- **The key points:** two or three *points clés*, the facts or arguments that support the main idea.
- **The writer's view**, if the article has one: what the journalist thinks, reported neutrally.

To find the main idea, read the headline and *le chapeau*, the short text in bold under the headline. They usually give the topic. Then *dégager* (bring out) the key points, paragraph by paragraph. **Il ressort que** (it emerges that) + indicative introduces what you've concluded from the whole text.

<Example fr="L'article porte sur la hausse des loyers étudiants." en="The article is about the rise in student rents." />
<Example fr="Il ressort de l'article que beaucoup de jeunes ont du mal à trouver un logement." en="It emerges from the article that many young people struggle to find a home." />

## Writing it neutrally

<VocabTable group="summary" />

A summary is **objectif** and **neutre**: it says what the article says, not what you think about it. Three habits help.

<Example fr="Ce résumé est objectif : il ne donne pas d'opinion." en="This summary is objective: it gives no opinion." />

- **Leave yourself out.** Don't write *je pense* or use words that judge, such as *heureusement* or *c'est une honte*.
- **Use neutral reporting verbs:** *l'article explique que*, *l'auteur estime que*, *la journaliste rappelle que*. Keep *prétendre* only if the article itself doubts the claim, as you saw in <LessonLink id="b2-reporting-verbs" text="the lesson on reporting verbs" />.
- **Rephrase rather than copy.** *Reformuler* means using your own words. If you quote, put the words in *« … »* and say who said them.

<Example fr="L'autrice de l'article explique que les loyers augmentent." en="The author of the article explains that rents are rising." />

If the article gives two sides, give both, in the same order and with a similar amount of space. Leaving one side out is a way of taking sides, even when every sentence you write is neutral.

Write the summary in the present: *l'article explique que…*, *la ville prévoit…*. With a reporting verb in the present, the tenses don't shift, as in <LessonLink id="b1-reported-speech-present" text="the lesson on reported speech" />.

A summary is much shorter than the article, so you have to *passer sous silence* (leave out) examples, details and most quotations. *Mentionner* an example is fine if it's essential. Nominal phrases keep it short: *la hausse des loyers* instead of *les loyers augmentent beaucoup*. For the last sentence, *en somme*, from <LessonLink id="b2-logical-connectors" text="the lesson on logical connectors" />, introduces the conclusion.

## Step by step

When you have an article to summarise, work in this order.

1. **Read the headline and the chapeau**, and write the topic in one line: *l'article porte sur…*
2. **Read the whole article once** without taking notes, to understand the main idea.
3. **Underline one key sentence per paragraph.** These become your *points clés*.
4. **Write the summary with your own words**, in the present, starting with the source.
5. **Cut** anything that isn't essential, then end with *en somme* and the conclusion of the article.

Credit the source in the first sentence: *Dans un article du Monde, la journaliste explique que…*, or *Selon Ouest-France…*. If the article quotes several people, name the side, not every person: *les associations estiment que…*, *selon la mairie…*. That keeps the summary short and fair to each side.

## A false friend

**Un résumé** is a false friend: it's a summary, not a CV, which is *un CV*. You know *un auteur* from <LessonLink id="b1-cinema-and-books" text="the lesson on cinema and books" />; for an article, people also say *le journaliste* or *la journaliste*.

## En français

Clara and Lucas, two students, prepare a presentation on a news article for Friday.

- *Pour la présentation de vendredi, on choisit quel article ?* (For Friday's presentation, which article shall we choose?)
- *Celui sur la hausse des loyers étudiants. Il porte sur un vrai problème.* (The one on the rise in student rents. It's about a real problem.)
- *D'accord. Quelle est l'idée principale, selon toi ?* (OK. What's the main idea, in your view?)
- *Il ressort que beaucoup d'étudiants ont du mal à trouver un logement.* (It emerges that many students struggle to find housing.)
- *Et les points clés ? Il faut les dégager avant d'écrire le résumé.* (And the key points? We need to bring them out before we write the summary.)
- *Les loyers augmentent, les propriétaires parlent de leurs charges, et la ville promet de nouvelles résidences.* (Rents are rising, landlords talk about their costs, and the town is promising new residences.)
- *Attention, dans le résumé, on ne donne pas notre avis. On reste neutres.* (Careful, in the summary we don't give our opinion. We stay neutral.)
- *Oui, et on cite la source au début : le nom du journal.* (Yes, and we name the source at the start: the name of the paper.)
- *On mentionne aussi l'étudiante de Lyon ?* (Do we mention the student from Lyon too?)
- *En une phrase seulement. Le reste, on peut le passer sous silence.* (In one sentence only. The rest, we can leave out.)

## An article and its summary

Here is an original article from a local news site, followed by a model summary. Four words or phrases are new.

<VocabTable group="glossary" />

*La hausse des loyers étudiants inquiète les familles*

*À quelques semaines de la rentrée, trouver un logement devient difficile pour beaucoup d'étudiants. Les associations demandent des mesures.*

*Dans les grandes villes, les loyers des petits appartements ont beaucoup augmenté. Selon une association étudiante, beaucoup de jeunes doivent consacrer une grande partie de leur budget au logement. « Je cherche depuis juin, et tout est trop cher », explique Léa, étudiante à Lyon. Les propriétaires, de leur côté, rappellent que leurs charges augmentent aussi.*

*La ville a annoncé l'ouverture de nouvelles résidences étudiantes d'ici deux ans. Pour les associations, cette mesure est nécessaire, mais trop lente : elles demandent le maintien des aides au logement.*

*Résumé : L'article porte sur la hausse des loyers étudiants dans les grandes villes. Il ressort que beaucoup de jeunes ont du mal à trouver un logement. Les propriétaires soulignent que leurs charges augmentent aussi. La ville prévoit l'ouverture de nouvelles résidences, mais les associations estiment que cette mesure est trop lente. En somme, le logement reste un enjeu important pour les étudiants.*

(Rising student rents worry families. A few weeks before the start of the academic year, finding housing is becoming difficult for many students. Charities are asking for measures. In big cities, rents for small flats have risen a lot. According to a student association, many young people have to spend a large part of their budget on housing. "I've been looking since June, and everything's too expensive," explains Léa, a student in Lyon. Landlords, for their part, point out that their costs are rising too. The town has announced the opening of new student residences within two years. For the charities, this measure is necessary but too slow: they're asking for housing benefits to be kept. Summary: The article is about rising student rents in big cities. It emerges that many young people struggle to find housing. Landlords stress that their costs are rising too. The town is planning to open new residences, but the charities consider this measure too slow. In short, housing remains a major issue for students.)

Notice what the summary leaves out: Léa's quotation and the detail about the budget. It keeps the topic, the two sides and the town's answer, and it ends with *en somme*.

<Callout type="warning" title="No je in a summary">
  Learners often add their opinion at the end: *L'article explique que les loyers augmentent, et je pense que c'est une honte.* In a summary, the last sentence concludes what the article says: **En somme, le logement reste un enjeu important pour les étudiants.** Keep your opinion for a separate paragraph, if you're asked for one.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Summarise in one sentence first">
  Before you write the summary, say the article in one sentence, out loud, without looking at it: *L'article porte sur…, et il ressort que…*. If you can't, read the headline and the chapeau again. That one sentence becomes the start of your summary, and everything else must support it.
</Callout>
