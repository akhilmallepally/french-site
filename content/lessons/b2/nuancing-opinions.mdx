## How strong is your view?

<VocabTable group="degree" />

*Plutôt* (rather, fairly) and *relativement* (relatively) soften an adjective or a verb. *Dans une certaine mesure* (to some extent) and *jusqu'à un certain point* (up to a point) limit how far you agree.

<Example fr="L'idée est plutôt bonne, mais elle coûte cher." en="The idea is fairly good, but it's expensive." />
<Example fr="Je suis d'accord avec toi jusqu'à un certain point." en="I agree with you up to a point." />

*Plutôt* is very common in speech. *Relativement* sounds a little more careful and is frequent in writing: *un prix relativement bas* (a relatively low price).

## First impressions

<VocabTable group="first" />

These four phrases say that your view could change once you know more. *A priori* and *à première vue* (at first sight) introduce a first impression. *En principe* means "normally, if everything goes to plan". *Sur le fond* (basically, on the substance) sets aside the details and talks about the main idea.

<Example fr="A priori, le projet me plaît." en="On the face of it, I like the project." />
<Example fr="En principe, la réunion a lieu jeudi." en="In principle, the meeting is on Thursday." />
<Example fr="Sur le fond, nous sommes d'accord : il faut changer." en="Basically, we agree: things have to change." />

*A priori* is written without an accent on the *a*, because it's Latin. *À première vue* has one.

## It seems: indicative or subjunctive?

<VocabTable group="seem" />

Two very similar phrases take different moods. **Il me semble que** gives your own impression, and it takes the indicative. **Il semble que** reports a general impression, as if from the outside, and it takes the subjunctive, which you know from the B1 subjunctive lessons. This holds for affirmative sentences: in the negative, *il ne me semble pas que* takes the subjunctive too (*il ne me semble pas que ce soit vrai*, it doesn't seem to me that it's true).

<Example fr="Il me semble que tu as raison." en="It seems to me that you're right." />
<Example fr="Il semble que le projet soit trop cher." en="It seems that the project is too expensive." />

*Je dirais que* (I'd say that) uses the conditionnel, from <LessonLink id="b1-conditionnel-present" text="the lesson on the conditionnel présent" />, to make any opinion sound less absolute. It's followed by the indicative.

<Example fr="Je dirais que c'est un bon début." en="I'd say it's a good start." />

## Conceding a point

<VocabTable group="concede" />

To sound fair, accept part of the other view before you answer it. You know *il est vrai que…, mais…* from <LessonLink id="b1-presenting-an-argument" text="the lesson on presenting an argument" />. **Reconnaître que** (to acknowledge that) does the same job, in a slightly more personal way.

<Example fr="Je reconnais que le prix est élevé, mais la qualité est excellente." en="I acknowledge that the price is high, but the quality is excellent." />

If you can't decide, say you're *partagé* (torn), or that your view is *mitigé* (mixed). To *nuancer* is to add a *nuance* to a view; a *contre-argument* is the point the other side will make.

## In a debate

<VocabTable group="topic" />

The examples and the texts below discuss *la semaine de quatre jours*, a topic often debated in France and elsewhere. Notice how often each speaker softens a claim instead of contradicting the other directly.

## En français

Inès and Thomas, two colleagues, talk about a plan at their company.

- *Tu as vu ? L'entreprise parle de la semaine de quatre jours.* (Have you seen? The company is talking about the four-day week.)
- *Oui. A priori, c'est une bonne idée, non ?* (Yes. On the face of it, it's a good idea, isn't it?)
- *Plutôt, oui. Mais il me semble qu'on travaillerait plus chaque jour.* (Fairly, yes. But it seems to me we'd work more each day.)
- *C'est vrai, dans une certaine mesure. Les journées seraient plus longues.* (That's true, to some extent. The days would be longer.)
- *Et pour les clients ? Il semble que certains ne soient pas contents.* (And what about the customers? It seems some aren't happy.)
- *Je dirais que c'est un contre-argument sérieux, mais pas le plus important.* (I'd say that's a serious counter-argument, but not the most important one.)
- *Moi, je suis partagée. Sur le fond, j'aime l'idée.* (I'm torn. Basically, I like the idea.)
- *Je reconnais que trois jours de repos, ce serait génial.* (I admit that three days off would be great.)
- *Jusqu'à un certain point ! Moi, j'aime bien le vendredi au bureau.* (Up to a point! I quite like Fridays at the office.)
- *En principe, tu pourrais choisir ton jour libre.* (In principle, you could choose your day off.)
- *Alors mon avis est mitigé. On en parle à la réunion ?* (Then my opinion is mixed. Shall we talk about it at the meeting?)
- *D'accord. Je le note pour lundi.* (OK. I'll note it down for Monday.)

## A forum thread

Julie asks other employees what they think. One word in the thread is new.

<VocabTable group="glossary" />

- **Julie :** *Notre entreprise va essayer la semaine de quatre jours. A priori, je trouve l'idée excellente : un jour de plus avec ma famille et moins de transports. Mais je me demande si c'est possible dans tous les métiers. Qu'est-ce que vous en pensez ?*
- **Marc :** *Il me semble que c'est plutôt une bonne nouvelle. Je reconnais que les journées seront plus longues, mais je préfère travailler un peu plus le lundi et avoir le vendredi libre. Pour moi, c'est relativement simple : moins de transports, c'est plus de temps libre.*
- **Sophie :** *Mon avis est plus mitigé. À première vue, tout le monde est gagnant. Sur le fond, je ne suis pas sûre que nos clients soient d'accord : il semble que beaucoup d'entre eux aient besoin de nous le vendredi. En principe, l'idée me plaît, mais jusqu'à un certain point.*
- **Julie :** *Merci à vous deux ! Vous m'aidez à nuancer mon point de vue. Je vais poser la question à la prochaine réunion.*

(Julie: Our company is going to try the four-day week. On the face of it, I think the idea is excellent: one more day with my family and less commuting. But I wonder whether it's possible in every job. What do you think? Marc: It seems to me that it's fairly good news. I admit that the days will be longer, but I'd rather work a bit more on Monday and have Friday free. For me, it's relatively simple: less commuting means more free time. Sophie: My opinion is more mixed. At first sight, everyone wins. Basically, I'm not sure our customers will agree: it seems that many of them need us on Fridays. In principle, I like the idea, but up to a point. Julie: Thank you both! You're helping me to qualify my point of view. I'll raise the question at the next meeting.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Il semble que, il me semble que">
  The two phrases look almost identical, but they take different moods. **Il me semble que tu as raison** (indicative), but **il semble que tu aies raison** (subjunctive). In affirmative sentences, learn the pair as a rule: with *me*, the indicative; without *me*, the subjunctive. In the negative, both take the subjunctive: **il ne me semble pas que tu aies raison**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Add one qualifier">
  For one week, every time you give an opinion in French, add exactly one word or phrase from this lesson: *plutôt*, *a priori*, *dans une certaine mesure*. One qualifier is enough to sound thoughtful. More than two in a sentence starts to sound as if you have no opinion at all.
</Callout>
