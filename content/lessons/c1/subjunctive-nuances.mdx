By the end of this lesson you'll be able to use the subjunctive where it changes the tone or the meaning of a sentence: in questions, at the start of a sentence, and after verbs that take either mood.

## What you already know

You know that *je pense que* takes the indicative and *je ne pense pas que* the subjunctive from <LessonLink id="b1-expressing-doubt-certainty" text="the lesson on doubt and certainty" />, and *le seul… qui* + subjunctive from <LessonLink id="b2-subjunctive-relative-clauses" text="the lesson on the subjunctive in relative clauses" />. You know *il se peut que* + subjunctive from <LessonLink id="b2-certainty-and-probability" text="the lesson on certainty and probability" />, and *ne pensez-vous pas que* from <LessonLink id="b2-persuasion-and-hedging" text="the lesson on persuasion and hedging" />. This lesson covers the cases where both moods are possible and the choice says something.

## Mood and meaning

<VocabTable group="terms" />

French calls the indicative, the subjunctive and the conditionnel *les modes* (moods). Most triggers decide *le mode* for you: *il faut que* always takes the subjunctive. But in some places, you choose, and the choice changes the tone or the meaning. In every pair below, the two verb forms differ (*vienne* / *viendra*, *sois* / *es*), so you can see the choice.

<Example fr="Dans cette phrase, le mode change le sens." en="In this sentence, the mood changes the meaning." />

## Penser and croire in questions

<VocabTable group="questions" />

In a question with inversion, *penser que* and *croire que* can take either mood. The subjunctive shows doubt: the speaker isn't sure. The indicative is a neutral question, and it's what you'll hear most with *est-ce que*.

<Example fr="Pensez-vous qu'il vienne demain ?" en="Do you think he'll come tomorrow? (doubtful, formal)" />
<Example fr="Pensez-vous qu'il viendra demain ?" en="Do you think he'll come tomorrow? (neutral)" />
<Example fr="Est-ce que tu crois qu'elle viendra ?" en="Do you think she'll come? (everyday)" />

With a negative question, *ne pensez-vous pas que*, the speaker expects "yes", so the indicative is usual: *ne pensez-vous pas qu'il a raison ?*

## A que clause at the front

<VocabTable group="front" />

When a *que* clause comes first in the sentence, it takes the subjunctive, even if the verb that follows would normally take the indicative. The main clause takes it up with *le* or *cela*.

<Example fr="Qu'il soit fatigué, je le comprends." en="That he's tired, I understand." />
<Example fr="Qu'elle ait raison, personne ne le nie." en="That she's right, nobody denies." />

Compare the usual order: *je comprends qu'il soit fatigué*. The fronted version, typical of debates and speeches, puts the point being conceded first.

**Que… ou non** + subjunctive means "whether… or not". **Qu'il** + subjunctive, alone, gives a wish or an order about a third person: *qu'il entre !* (let him come in!).

<Example fr="Que tu le veuilles ou non, il faut partir." en="Whether you like it or not, we have to leave." />
<Example fr="Qu'elle attende, je suis en réunion." en="She'll have to wait, I'm in a meeting." />

## Verbs that change meaning with the mood

<VocabTable group="meaning" />

A few verbs take the indicative for a fact and the subjunctive for an attitude to that fact.

- **Comprendre que** + indicative: to realize. + subjunctive: to understand why, to find it normal.
- **Admettre que** + indicative: to acknowledge a fact. + subjunctive: to accept an idea or a possibility.
- **Dire que** + indicative: to report. + subjunctive: to give an order, like *dire de* + infinitive.

<Example fr="Il comprend que tu es parti." en="He realizes that you've left." />
<Example fr="Il comprend que tu sois parti." en="He understands why you left." />
<Example fr="J'admets qu'il a raison." en="I acknowledge that he's right." />
<Example fr="J'admets qu'il ait pu se tromper." en="I accept that he may have made a mistake." />
<Example fr="Il dit que tu viens." en="He says you're coming." />
<Example fr="Il dit que tu viennes." en="He says you should come." />

<VocabTable group="words" />

Accepting a fact that concerns someone else also takes the subjunctive: *accepter que*, *il est normal que*.

<Example fr="Il accepte que tu partes plus tôt." en="He accepts that you'll leave earlier." />
<Example fr="Il est normal qu'il soit déçu : il voulait ce poste." en="It's understandable that he's disappointed: he wanted that job." />

*S'attendre à ce que* (to expect) and *tenir à ce que* (to insist that) always take the subjunctive. They're the forms of *s'attendre à* and *tenir à* before a clause.

<Example fr="Je m'attends à ce que la réunion soit longue." en="I expect the meeting to be long." />
<Example fr="Je tiens à ce que tout soit prêt à l'avance." en="I insist that everything be ready in advance." />

## In a debate

These nuances are useful when you argue. Fronting with *que* concedes a point before you turn it: *qu'il y ait des risques, je l'admets ; mais…*. *Que… ou non* sets a condition aside. And the choice of mood after *comprendre* or *admettre* shows whether you merely note a fact or accept it.

<Example fr="Qu'il y ait des risques, je l'admets. Mais ils sont limités." en="That there are risks, I admit. But they're limited." />

## The whole list

| Structure | Mood | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *pensez-vous que… ?* | subjunctive or indicative | doubt, or a neutral question |
| *que* clause at the front | subjunctive | the point conceded first |
| *que… ou non* | subjunctive | whether… or not |
| *qu'il* + verb ! | subjunctive | a wish or an order |
| *comprendre que* | indicative / subjunctive | realize / understand why |
| *admettre que* | indicative / subjunctive | acknowledge / accept the idea |
| *dire que* | indicative / subjunctive | report / order |
| *s'attendre à ce que*, *tenir à ce que* | subjunctive | expect, insist |

## En français

Sophie tells her manager, Monsieur Martin, that a colleague is leaving.

- *Vous savez que Paul quitte l'équipe ?* (Do you know that Paul is leaving the team?)
- *Oui, il me l'a dit hier. Je comprends qu'il veuille partir : on lui propose un meilleur poste.* (Yes, he told me yesterday. I understand why he wants to leave: he's been offered a better job.)
- *Pensez-vous qu'il revienne un jour ?* (Do you think he'll ever come back? (doubtful))
- *Ça, je ne sais pas. Qu'il soit déçu par notre projet, je l'admets.* (That, I don't know. That he's disappointed with our project, I admit.)
- *Et l'équipe ? Elle va le prendre mal.* (And the team? They'll take it badly.)
- *Que l'équipe le veuille ou non, il faut avancer.* (Whether the team likes it or not, we have to move on.)
- *Vous allez annoncer la nouvelle ?* (Are you going to announce the news?)
- *Paul tient à ce que je l'annonce moi-même.* (Paul insists that I announce it myself.)
- *Et qui va le remplacer ?* (And who will replace him?)
- *Je m'attends à ce que la directrice choisisse quelqu'un de l'équipe.* (I expect the director to choose someone from the team.)
- *Elle m'a dit que je sois prête à aider.* (She told me to be ready to help.)
- *Qu'elle vienne vous voir, alors !* (Then let her come and see you!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Il se rend compte que tu es parti, not que tu sois parti">
  Learners who have just met *comprendre que* + subjunctive sometimes use the subjunctive after every verb of understanding. *Se rendre compte que* (to realize) states a fact and takes the indicative: **il se rend compte que tu es parti**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask: fact or attitude?">
  When a verb could take either mood, ask whether you're stating a fact or giving your attitude to it. Fact: indicative (*il comprend que tu es parti*). Attitude, acceptance, order or doubt: subjunctive (*il comprend que tu sois parti*).
</Callout>

## En résumé

Le mode change parfois le sens ou le ton de la phrase. Dans une question avec inversion, pensez-vous que peut prendre le subjonctif, qui montre un doute, ou l'indicatif, qui est neutre. Quand une proposition avec que vient au début de la phrase, elle prend le subjonctif : qu'il soit fatigué, je le comprends. Que… ou non prend aussi le subjonctif : que tu le veuilles ou non. Qu'il, suivi du subjonctif, exprime un souhait ou un ordre. Certains verbes changent de sens avec le mode : comprendre que, suivi de l'indicatif, veut dire se rendre compte, mais avec le subjonctif, il veut dire trouver normal. Admettre que et dire que fonctionnent de la même façon. S'attendre à ce que et tenir à ce que prennent toujours le subjonctif.
