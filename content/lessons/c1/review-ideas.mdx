## A few new words

<VocabTable group="new" />

These four words help you frame a debate. *Un débat d'idées* is a debate about principles rather than facts. *Une remise en cause* is a calling into question; it's very close to *la remise en question*, which you met in the lesson on abstract nouns, and the two are often interchangeable. *Un courant de pensée* is a school of thought, such as the Enlightenment or existentialism. *Le progrès technique* (technological progress) is the theme of the model below.

## Writing about a debate of ideas

An essay introduction on a debate of ideas follows the method you know, with one extra demand: fairness. As a rule of thumb, build it in five moves:

1. **The amorce**: start from a precise moment in the history of ideas, such as an Enlightenment thinker or May 68, then bring it to today.
2. **The terms**: define the key words of the question in one sentence each. In a debate of ideas, the definitions often decide the answer.
3. **The positions**: give each side its strongest argument, in the same tone and at similar length, and attribute it: *pour certains…, pour d'autres…*.
4. **The problématique**: turn the question into an indirect question, with no inversion: *on peut dès lors se demander dans quelle mesure…*.
5. **The plan**: announce the parts with *nous verrons d'abord…, nous montrerons ensuite…, nous nous demanderons enfin…*.

Each lesson of this unit can give you an amorce. The Enlightenment gives you reason, tolerance and progress; May 68 gives you the challenge to authority; the lesson on technology gives you the debates of today; auteur cinema shows how a group of critics changed the way people judge a work. Choose the one closest to your question, and keep it short.

Three habits keep the tone neutral. Don't say what a group "always" thinks; say what *some* people argue. Give no figures you can't check. And keep your own view for the conclusion: the introduction asks the question, it doesn't answer it.

## A model introduction

The introduction below is an original text written for this lesson. It answers the question: *Le progrès technique nous rend-il plus libres ?*

*Introduction*

*Au dix-huitième siècle, un courant de pensée, les Lumières, voyait dans le progrès des sciences un moyen de libérer les hommes des préjugés. Dans l'Encyclopédie, Diderot et d'Alembert voulaient rassembler tous les savoirs. Deux siècles plus tard, en Mai 68, des étudiants ont contesté l'autorité au nom de la liberté. Aujourd'hui, nos téléphones nous donnent accès aux savoirs en quelques secondes, mais ils collectent aussi nos données et savent où nous allons.*

*Le progrès technique, ce sont ici les outils nouveaux qui transforment notre vie quotidienne, des réseaux sociaux à l'intelligence artificielle. Être libre, c'est pouvoir penser et agir par soi-même, sans que d'autres décident à notre place. La question n'est donc pas seulement technique : elle touche à notre façon de vivre ensemble.*

*Ce débat d'idées n'est pas nouveau, mais il prend une forme nouvelle. Pour certains, la technique continue l'héritage des Lumières : elle rassemble les savoirs et donne la parole à chacun. Pour d'autres, c'est une remise en cause de nos libertés : la surveillance, les algorithmes et leurs biais décident de plus en plus souvent à notre place.*

*On peut dès lors se demander dans quelle mesure le progrès technique, qui promet de nous libérer, crée aussi de nouvelles formes de contrôle.*

*Nous verrons d'abord que la technique a vraiment élargi nos libertés, en particulier l'accès au savoir et la liberté d'expression. Nous montrerons ensuite qu'elle les menace aussi, par la collecte des données et la surveillance. Nous nous demanderons enfin à quelles conditions, et avec quels garde-fous, elle peut rester au service de la liberté.*

(Introduction. In the eighteenth century, a school of thought, the Enlightenment, saw in the progress of science a way to free people from prejudice. In the Encyclopédie, Diderot and d'Alembert wanted to bring all knowledge together. Two centuries later, in May 68, students challenged authority in the name of freedom. Today, our phones give us access to knowledge in a few seconds, but they also collect our data and know where we go. Technological progress here means the new tools that are transforming our daily lives, from social networks to artificial intelligence. Being free means being able to think and act for yourself, without others deciding for you. So the question isn't only a technical one: it concerns the way we live together. This debate of ideas isn't new, but it is taking a new form. For some, technology continues the legacy of the Enlightenment: it brings knowledge together and gives everyone a voice. For others, it is a challenge to our freedoms: surveillance, algorithms and their biases decide for us more and more often. One may therefore ask to what extent technological progress, which promises to free us, also creates new forms of control. We will first see that technology has really broadened our freedoms, in particular access to knowledge and freedom of expression. We will then show that it also threatens them, through data collection and surveillance. Finally, we will ask under what conditions, and with what safeguards, it can remain at the service of freedom.)

## How the introduction uses the unit

- **The amorce**: two sentences on the Enlightenment and the *Encyclopédie*, one on May 68, then *aujourd'hui* to bring the subject to the present.
- **The terms**: *le progrès technique* and *être libre*, defined in one sentence each.
- **The positions**: two sentences of similar length, each attributed (*pour certains…*, *pour d'autres…*), each with its strongest argument; no figures, and no "always" about any group.
- **The problématique**: *on peut dès lors se demander dans quelle mesure…*, with no inversion.
- **The plan**: three parts announced with *nous verrons d'abord*, *nous montrerons ensuite* and *nous nous demanderons enfin*, ending with the safeguards from the lesson on technology.
- **The new words**: *un courant de pensée*, *le progrès technique*, *un débat d'idées* and *une remise en cause*, each in a complete sentence.

## Your turn

Write the introduction of an essay on one of these questions: *La liberté d'expression doit-elle avoir des limites ?* or *Faut-il remettre en question l'autorité ?* Use the five moves: an amorce from the Enlightenment or May 68, two defined terms, two attributed positions of similar length, a problématique with *dans quelle mesure*, and a plan in three parts.

## Check your work

- Does the amorce start from a precise moment, and does it reach the present?
- Are the key terms defined, each in one sentence?
- Are both positions attributed, in the same tone, and of similar length?
- Is the problématique an indirect question, with no inversion?
- Does the introduction stay neutral, with no figures and no verdict?

<Callout type="warning" title="Pour d'autres, not pour autres">
  When learners balance two positions, they often drop the *d'*. The pair is **pour certains…, pour d'autres…**, just as you say *d'autres personnes*. *Les autres* means "all the others", which changes the meaning.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Date your amorce">
  Avoid starting with *depuis toujours* or *de nos jours*, which say nothing. Start instead from one dated fact: a book (*Du contrat social*, 1762), an event (May 1968) or a law. A precise first sentence tells the reader at once that you know your subject.
</Callout>
