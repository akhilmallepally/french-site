By the end of this lesson you'll be able to chair a meeting in French: open it, give the floor, keep to time, calm a disagreement, put a proposal to the vote and record the decisions.

## What you already know

You know *l'ordre du jour*, *prendre la parole*, *passer la parole à*, *trancher*, *à l'unanimité* and *un compte rendu* from <LessonLink id="b2-meetings-and-calls" text="the lesson on meetings and professional calls" />, and *couper la parole à* and *revenons-en à* from <LessonLink id="b2-debating" text="the lesson on taking part in a debate" />. *La séance*, *lever la séance* and *le procès-verbal* come from <LessonLink id="c1-participle-clauses" text="the lesson on participle clauses" />. At B2 you took part in meetings; here you lead them.

## The role of the chair

*Le président* or *la présidente de séance* has three jobs: keep the meeting to its agenda and its time, make sure everyone can speak, and turn the discussion into decisions. Each job has its own formulas, and they're more formal than everyday conversation, even among colleagues who know each other well.

## Opening

<VocabTable group="open" />

A formal meeting opens with a fixed sentence, then the purpose and the agenda.

<Example fr="Il est dix-huit heures : je déclare la séance ouverte." en="It's six o'clock: I declare the meeting open." />
<Example fr="Nous sommes réunis pour décider de l'aménagement du parking." en="We're here to decide how to fit out the car park." />

In a less formal team meeting, *bon, on commence ?* does the same job in speech. Choose by the setting: a board or an association's general meeting needs the formula; a weekly team meeting doesn't.

## Giving the floor and managing time

<VocabTable group="floor" />

The chair *donne la parole à* each speaker; speakers *prennent la parole*. *Un tour de table* gives everyone a turn, often at the start. To keep to time, interrupt politely and postpone: *je me permets de vous interrompre* and *nous y reviendrons* are the two most useful sentences of a chair.

<Example fr="Je donne la parole à Madame Martin." en="I give the floor to Madame Martin." />
<Example fr="Je me permets de vous interrompre : nous y reviendrons après le vote." en="May I interrupt you: we'll come back to that after the vote." />

## Managing disagreement

<VocabTable group="conflict" />

When a discussion gets heated, the chair *apaise* (calms) and *recadre le débat* (brings it back to the point). The diplomatic tools from the last lessons work here too: acknowledge the point, name a possible misunderstanding, and return to the agenda.

<Example fr="Je comprends tout à fait votre position. Il y a peut-être un malentendu." en="I fully understand your position. There may be a misunderstanding." />
<Example fr="Revenons-en à l'ordre du jour : nous devons décider aujourd'hui." en="Let's get back to the agenda: we have to decide today." />

<Example fr="La présidente a su apaiser les échanges." en="The chair managed to calm the discussion." />

*Les échanges* is the neutral word for the discussion itself: *les échanges ont été vifs* (the discussion was heated).

## Deciding

<VocabTable group="decide" />

When the discussion is over, the chair *met la proposition aux voix* (puts it to the vote). A vote *à main levée* (by a show of hands) is common in small meetings. Some participants may *s'abstenir*. The result is announced, and the chair *acte* the decision: records it formally.

<Example fr="Je mets la proposition aux voix. Qui est pour ?" en="I'm putting the proposal to the vote. Who is in favour?" />
<Example fr="La proposition est adoptée ; deux participants se sont abstenus." en="The proposal is adopted; two participants abstained." />
<Example fr="Nous actons donc la création d'un parking à vélos." en="We therefore record the decision to create a bike park." />

If the vote isn't needed, the chair can also *trancher*, as you saw at B2, or note that everyone agrees: *la décision est prise à l'unanimité*.

## Closing and the written record

<VocabTable group="close" />

Before closing, the chair *fait la synthèse des échanges* in two or three sentences, then reads the decisions. Afterwards, someone writes it up. *Un procès-verbal* is the full, official record; *un compte rendu* reports the discussion; *un relevé de décisions* lists mainly what was decided, who will do it and by when, with very little about the discussion.

<Example fr="Pour faire la synthèse des échanges : nous sommes d'accord sur le principe, mais pas sur le calendrier." en="To sum up the discussion: we agree on the principle, but not on the schedule." />
<Example fr="L'ordre du jour étant épuisé, je lève la séance." en="As the agenda has been covered, I close the meeting." />

## Chairing online

Many meetings now take place by *visioconférence*, which you know from B2. The chair's job is the same, but the signals change. Ask participants to *couper leur micro* when they're not speaking, and to raise their hand in the tool rather than interrupt. Give the floor by name, because people can't see who you're looking at. Before a vote, say clearly how it will work.

<Example fr="Pour voter, je vous propose de lever la main dans l'outil, et je compterai les voix." en="To vote, I suggest you raise your hand in the tool, and I'll count the votes." />

Online, silence is harder to read than in a room. As a rule of thumb, after each important point, ask a direct question (*avez-vous des remarques ?*) and wait a few seconds before moving on.

## A relevé de décisions

This record is an original text written for this lesson. The association and the people are invented.

*Association des habitants du quartier Saint-Jacques*

*Relevé de décisions de la réunion du 12 mars*

*Présents : douze membres du bureau. Présidente de séance : Madame Lefèvre.*

*La séance est ouverte à dix-huit heures. Après un tour de table, les échanges portent sur quatre points.*

*Point 1 : l'aménagement de la cour de l'immeuble. Les membres s'accordent sur le principe d'un parking à vélos. La proposition de Monsieur Petit, qui prévoit vingt places, est mise aux voix à main levée : elle est adoptée par dix voix, deux membres s'étant abstenus. Le coût sera partagé entre l'association et le propriétaire de l'immeuble, sous réserve de l'accord de celui-ci. Madame Morel est chargée de demander trois devis avant le 15 avril.*

*Point 2 : la fête du quartier. Le 14 juin, proposé par Monsieur Petit, est aussi le jour de la fête de l'école ; après un échange assez vif, la présidente recadre le débat. La fête aura lieu le samedi 7 juin. Décision prise à l'unanimité. Monsieur Petit est chargé de demander l'accord de la mairie avant le 30 avril.*

*Point 3 : le bruit des travaux de la rue voisine. Madame Lefèvre écrira à la mairie avant le 30 mars pour demander combien de temps les travaux vont durer. Ce point sera traité en priorité à la prochaine réunion.*

*La présidente fait la synthèse des échanges, puis l'ordre du jour étant épuisé, lève la séance à vingt heures.*

*Point 4 : questions diverses. Aucune question n'a été posée.*

*Prochaine réunion : le 9 avril, à dix-huit heures. Ce relevé sera envoyé à tous les membres de l'association.*

<VocabTable group="glossary" />

(Saint-Jacques Residents' Association. Record of decisions of the meeting of 12 March. Present: twelve committee members. Chair: Madame Lefèvre. The meeting opens at 6 p.m. After a round, the discussion covers four points. Point 1: fitting out the building's courtyard. The members agree on the principle of a bike park. Monsieur Petit's proposal, which provides for twenty spaces, is put to a show-of-hands vote: it is adopted by ten votes, two members having abstained. The cost will be shared between the association and the owner of the building, subject to the owner's agreement. Madame Morel is to ask for three quotes before 15 April. Point 2: the neighbourhood party. 14 June, proposed by Monsieur Petit, is also the day of the school party; after a rather heated exchange, the chair brings the debate back on track. The party will take place on Saturday 7 June. Decision taken unanimously. Monsieur Petit is to ask for the town hall's agreement before 30 April. Point 3: the noise from the works in the next street. Madame Lefèvre will write to the town hall before 30 March to ask how long the works will last. This point will be dealt with as a priority at the next meeting. The chair sums up the discussion, then, the agenda having been covered, closes the meeting at 8 p.m. Point 4: any other business. No questions were raised. Next meeting: 9 April, at 6 p.m. This record will be sent to all the members of the association.)

## En français

The meeting of 12 March, during point 2.

- *Je donne la parole à Monsieur Petit.* (I give the floor to Monsieur Petit.)
- *Merci. Pour la fête, je propose le 14 juin : il y a moins de monde en vacances.* (Thank you. For the party, I suggest 14 June: fewer people are on holiday.)
- *Le 14 ? C'est le jour de la fête de l'école ! Les familles ne viendront pas.* (The 14th? That's the day of the school party! Families won't come.)
- *Ce n'est pas vrai, l'an dernier…* (That's not true, last year…)
- *Je me permets de vous interrompre. Je comprends tout à fait vos deux positions.* (May I interrupt you. I fully understand both your positions.)
- *Mais la fête de l'école, c'est important pour les familles.* (But the school party matters to families.)
- *Il y a peut-être un malentendu. Monsieur Petit, vous tenez au 14, ou seulement à un jour avant les vacances ?* (There may be a misunderstanding. Monsieur Petit, do you insist on the 14th, or simply on a day before the holidays?)
- *Avant les vacances, surtout.* (Before the holidays, mainly.)
- *Revenons-en à l'ordre du jour : nous devons choisir un jour aujourd'hui. Le 7 juin est avant les vacances, et l'école n'a pas de fête ce jour-là. Je propose le 7 juin.* (Let's get back to the agenda: we have to choose a day today. 7 June is before the holidays, and the school has no party that day. I suggest 7 June.)
- *Le 7, ça me va.* (The 7th suits me. (spoken))
- *Moi aussi.* (Me too.)
- *Je mets la proposition aux voix, à main levée. Qui est pour le 7 juin ?* (I'm putting the proposal to a show-of-hands vote. Who is in favour of 7 June?)
- *Nous sommes tous pour.* (We're all in favour.)
- *La décision est prise à l'unanimité. Nous l'actons.* (The decision is taken unanimously. We record it.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Je donne la parole à…, not je prends la parole à…">
  Learners often use *prendre la parole* for the chair. The chair **donne** or **passe la parole à** someone; a speaker **prend la parole**. *Je prends la parole à Madame Martin* means nothing.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Prepare three sentences">
  Before you chair a meeting, write three sentences on a card: one to open (*je déclare la séance ouverte*), one to interrupt (*je me permets de vous interrompre : nous y reviendrons*), and one to decide (*je mets la proposition aux voix*). Under pressure, these are the ones you'll need.
</Callout>
