By the end of this lesson you'll be able to use verbs whose meaning changes with the preposition, choose between *de* and *par* after a passive, and use the right preposition with islands, overseas regions and cities that have an article.

## What you already know

You learned that verbs take *à* or *de* before an infinitive in <LessonLink id="a2-verbs-with-a-and-de" text="the lesson on verbs with à and de" />, and the prepositions with cities and countries (*à Nantes*, *en Italie*, *au Japon*, *aux États-Unis*) in <LessonLink id="a2-prepositions-with-places" text="the lesson on prepositions with places" />. In <LessonLink id="b2-passive-voice" text="the lesson on the passive voice" />, you saw *par* for the agent of an action and *de* for a state or a feeling (*entouré de*, *respecté de*). This lesson adds the cases where the choice changes the meaning.

## One verb, two meanings

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

**Manquer à** and **manquer de** are the classic trap. *Manquer de* means "to lack", which you know from <LessonLink id="b1-relative-dont" text="the lesson on dont" />: *il manque de temps* (he's short of time). *Manquer à* means "to be missed by", so the roles are the reverse of English. You know *tu me manques* (I miss you) from <LessonLink id="a2-writing-an-informal-email" text="the lesson on writing an informal email" />.

<Example fr="Paris manque beaucoup à Julie." en="Julie misses Paris a lot." />
<Example fr="Mes amis me manquent." en="I miss my friends." />
<Example fr="Cette équipe manque d'expérience." en="This team lacks experience." />

With no preposition, *manquer* + object means "to miss" in the sense of not catching: *il a manqué son train*.

**Penser à** means to think about, to have in mind; you met it with *y* in <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-y" text="the lesson on the pronoun y" />. **Penser de** asks for or gives an opinion, mostly in questions and with *en*, as in *qu'est-ce que tu en penses ?* With *penser à*, a person stays after *à* as a stressed pronoun: *je pense à lui*, not *je lui pense*.

<Example fr="Je pense souvent à mes grands-parents." en="I often think about my grandparents." />
<Example fr="Que pensez-vous de ce projet ?" en="What do you think of this project?" />

**Croire à** and **croire en** both translate as "to believe in". As a rule of thumb, *croire à* is about whether something exists or is true, and *croire en* is about trust or faith in a person or a value. Usage overlaps with some nouns (*croire à l'avenir*, *croire en l'avenir*). *Croire quelqu'un*, with no preposition, means to believe what they say. With a thing and no preposition, *croire* means to believe it as it's told: *je ne crois pas son histoire* (I don't believe his story).

<Example fr="Il croit aux fantômes." en="He believes in ghosts." />
<Example fr="Je ne crois pas à son excuse." en="I don't believe his excuse." />
<Example fr="Ma directrice croit en moi." en="My manager believes in me." />

**Rêver de** is the everyday verb, for a wish or a night's dream: *je rêve de voyager*, *j'ai rêvé de toi*. **Rêver à** is literary: to muse on, to let your mind wander to something.

<Example fr="Assis devant la mer, il rêvait à son enfance." en="Sitting by the sea, he was musing on his childhood. (literary)" />

**Tenir à** means to care about, or to insist on, as you saw in <LessonLink id="b2-relative-prepositions" text="the lesson on relatives after prepositions" />. **Tenir de** means to take after a relative.

<Example fr="Je tiens beaucoup à cette maison." en="This house means a lot to me." />
<Example fr="Elle tient de sa mère : elle adore voyager." en="She takes after her mother: she loves travelling." />

**Jouer à** and **jouer de** you've known since A1: *jouer au tennis* for games and sports, *jouer du piano* for instruments. Remember the contractions: *jouer aux cartes*, *jouer de la guitare*.

## De or par after a passive

<VocabTable group="passive" />

The rule from B2 still holds: *par* for an agent that performs an action, *de* for a state, a position or a feeling. At this level, notice that some verbs take both, with a change of meaning.

<Example fr="La maison est entourée d'un grand jardin." en="The house is surrounded by a large garden." />
<Example fr="À six heures du matin, la maison a été entourée par la police." en="At six in the morning, the house was surrounded by the police." />
<Example fr="Le film sera suivi d'un débat." en="The film will be followed by a debate." />
<Example fr="Le suspect a été suivi par deux policiers." en="The suspect was followed by two police officers." />

In each pair, the first sentence describes how things are or come in order, and the second tells what someone did. With verbs of feeling (*aimé*, *respecté*), *de* is the more elegant choice, and *par* is also correct.

## Islands, overseas regions and cities with an article

<VocabTable group="places" />

You know the rule for countries: *en* before a feminine country or one that starts with a vowel, *au* before a masculine one, *aux* before a plural one. So masculine countries that start with a vowel take *en*: *en Iran*, *en Israël*.

Islands follow other habits. Take these as rules of thumb, and learn each name with its preposition.

- **An island whose name has no article** usually takes *à*, whatever its size: *à Cuba*, *à Madagascar*, *à Malte*.
- **A feminine name used with an article** (*la Corse*, *la Sicile*) usually takes *en*: *en Corse*, *en Sicile*.
- **An exception: La Réunion** keeps its article after *à*: *à La Réunion*.
- **Plural groups of islands** take *aux*: *aux Antilles*.

For Martinique and Guadeloupe, both *en Martinique* and *à la Martinique* are correct; *en* is the more usual today.

<Example fr="Elle a passé un an à Madagascar." en="She spent a year in Madagascar." />
<Example fr="Nous partons en Corse cet été." en="We're going to Corsica this summer." />
<Example fr="Mon frère habite à La Réunion." en="My brother lives on Réunion." />

Cities whose name has an article keep it, and *à* or *de* contracts with *le*: *au Havre*, *au Caire*, *du Havre*, *du Caire*. A feminine article doesn't contract: *à La Rochelle*.

To say where someone comes from, use the same contractions with *de*: *du Mexique*, *des Antilles*, *d'Iran*, *de Corse*, *de La Réunion*.

<Example fr="Il est rentré des Antilles la semaine dernière." en="He came back from the Antilles last week." />

## The whole list

- Missing and lacking: *manquer à* (to be missed by), *manquer de* (to lack), *manquer* + object (to miss a train).
- Thinking: *penser à* (to think about; *à lui*), *penser de* (an opinion; *en*).
- Believing: *croire à* (true, real), *croire en* (trust, faith), *croire quelqu'un* (believe what they say), *croire quelque chose* (believe it's true: *je ne crois pas son histoire*).
- Dreaming: *rêver de* (everyday), *rêver à* (literary).
- Holding: *tenir à* (to care about), *tenir de* (to take after).
- Passive: *par* for an action, *de* for a state or a feeling.
- Islands: *à Cuba*, *en Corse*, *à La Réunion*, *aux Antilles*; *en* or *à la Martinique*. Cities with *le*: *au Havre*, *du Caire*.

## En français

Sophie has just moved to Réunion for work. She's on a video call with her friend Karim, in Lyon.

- *Alors, la vie à La Réunion ?* (So, how's life on Réunion?)
- *C'est magnifique, mais Lyon me manque un peu.* (It's beautiful, but I miss Lyon a bit.)
- *Et nous, on ne te manque pas ?* (And what about us, don't you miss us? (spoken))
- *Bien sûr que si ! Je pense souvent à vous.* (Of course I do! I often think about you.)
- *Et ton nouveau travail, qu'est-ce que tu en penses ?* (And your new job, what do you think of it?)
- *Il me plaît. Ma directrice croit en moi, et ça aide.* (I like it. My manager believes in me, and that helps.)
- *Tu vas voyager un peu ? Madagascar n'est pas loin.* (Are you going to travel a bit? Madagascar isn't far.)
- *Oui, je rêve d'aller à Madagascar, et un jour à Cuba.* (Yes, I dream of going to Madagascar, and one day to Cuba.)
- *Toujours l'envie de partir ! Tu tiens de ton père.* (Always wanting to go somewhere! You take after your father.)
- *C'est vrai. Lui, il a habité en Martinique pendant dix ans.* (That's true. He lived in Martinique for ten years. (spoken))
- *Et ta sœur, elle vit toujours aux Antilles ?* (And your sister, does she still live in the Antilles? (spoken))
- *Non, elle est rentrée des Antilles l'année dernière. Elle vit au Havre maintenant.* (No, she came back from the Antilles last year. She lives in Le Havre now.)
- *Au Havre ? Elle doit manquer de soleil !* (In Le Havre? She must be short of sun!)
- *Un peu ! Mais elle joue du piano dans un groupe, et elle est heureuse.* (A bit! But she plays the piano in a band, and she's happy.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Tu me manques means I miss you">
  English speakers often build *manquer à* like "to miss" and swap the roles. The person or thing that's missed is the subject: to say "I miss you", write **tu me manques**, and for "Julie misses Paris", write **Paris manque à Julie**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Learn the place with its preposition">
  When you meet a new island, region or city, note it with its preposition and its "from" form, as one unit: *à Cuba, de Cuba*; *en Corse, de Corse*; *au Havre, du Havre*. You'll never have to work out the rule again for that place.
</Callout>

## En résumé

Certains verbes changent de sens avec la préposition. Manquer de veut dire ne pas avoir assez de quelque chose, mais manquer à veut dire que quelqu'un est triste sans une personne ou un lieu : Paris manque à Julie, tu me manques. Penser à, c'est avoir quelqu'un ou quelque chose en tête, et penser de donne un avis : que pensez-vous de ce projet ? Croire à porte sur ce qui est vrai, et croire en porte sur la confiance : il croit aux fantômes, elle croit en ses enfants. Sans préposition, on dit aussi : je ne crois pas son histoire. Rêver à est littéraire, et tenir de sa mère, c'est être comme sa mère. Après un passif, on met par pour une action et de pour un état. Avec les îles, on dit à Cuba, en Corse, à La Réunion et aux Antilles, et on dit en Martinique ou à la Martinique. Avec Le Havre ou Le Caire, on dit au Havre et du Caire.
