## The director as author

<VocabTable group="idea" />

In the 1950s, a group of young critics wrote for *une revue* (a specialist magazine), *les Cahiers du cinéma*, founded in 1951. They argued that the real author of a film is its director, not the writer of the screenplay or the studio. A true *cinéaste* (film-maker), they said, leaves a personal mark on every film: themes that return, a way of placing the camera, a rhythm. This idea is called *la politique des auteurs* (the auteur theory). Truffaut defended it in a famous 1954 article that attacked the well-made but impersonal French films of the time.

<Example fr="Les Cahiers du cinéma sont une revue de critique de cinéma." en="The Cahiers du cinéma is a film criticism magazine." />
<Example fr="Pour les critiques des Cahiers du cinéma, un vrai cinéaste est l'auteur de ses films." en="For the Cahiers du cinéma critics, a true film-maker is the author of his or her films." />

Several of these critics then became directors themselves: Truffaut, Godard, Rohmer, Chabrol. Their first films, at the end of the 1950s, launched *la Nouvelle Vague*. Agnès Varda, often called a forerunner of the movement, had already made a feature film in 1955; her *Cléo de 5 à 7* (1962) follows a young singer through part of an afternoon in Paris, almost in real time.

<Example fr="Agnès Varda a tourné Cléo de 5 à 7 dans les rues de Paris." en="Agnès Varda shot Cléo de 5 à 7 in the streets of Paris." />

Today, French uses *le cinéma d'auteur* (auteur or art-house cinema) for films that express a director's personal vision, as opposed to *le cinéma grand public* (mainstream cinema), made first of all to attract a large audience. The line between the two isn't strict: some auteur films are popular, and some mainstream films have a clear style. As a rule of thumb, a critic calls a film *une œuvre personnelle* (a personal work) when you could recognize its director without seeing the credits.

<Example fr="Ce cinéaste fait un cinéma d'auteur exigeant, loin du cinéma grand public." en="This film-maker makes demanding auteur cinema, far from mainstream cinema." />
<Example fr="Chaque film de cette réalisatrice est une œuvre très personnelle." en="Each film by this director is a very personal work." />

France supports this kind of cinema with public funding, managed by a national film centre, and with many art-house cinemas and film festivals. Supporters say this protects creativity and diversity; critics answer that some subsidized films find very few viewers. Both arguments come up whenever the system is discussed.

## Describing a film's style

<VocabTable group="style" />

To talk about style, critics use a precise vocabulary. *Un plan* is a shot: everything between two cuts. *Un gros plan* (a close-up) shows a face or an object very near. *Un plan-séquence* (a long take) films a whole scene without a cut, and often creates tension or a feeling of real time.

*Le montage* (the editing) is the way the shots are put together. A fast *montage* gives energy; a slow one gives weight. In *À bout de souffle*, Godard used *des faux raccords* (jump cuts): he cut inside the same shot, so the image jumps, and the audience notices the editing instead of forgetting it.

<Example fr="Dans À bout de souffle, Godard utilise des faux raccords que le spectateur remarque tout de suite." en="In À bout de souffle, Godard uses jump cuts that the viewer notices at once." />
<Example fr="La scène est filmée en un seul plan-séquence de plusieurs minutes." en="The scene is filmed in a single long take lasting several minutes." />

The New Wave liked to *filmer* (film) in real places, often with *la caméra à l'épaule* (a hand-held camera) and *la lumière naturelle* (natural light), which gave its films a feeling of freedom and of life caught in the moment.

*Le hors-champ* (off-screen space) is everything the camera doesn't show: a voice, a noise, a character who has just left the frame. Directors use it to suggest rather than show. *La voix off* (the voice-over) is a voice heard over the images, often a narrator's.

<Example fr="Le cinéaste filme caméra à l'épaule, au milieu de la foule." en="The film-maker films with a hand-held camera, in the middle of the crowd." />
<Example fr="Une voix off raconte l'histoire, et le danger reste dans le hors-champ." en="A voice-over tells the story, and the danger stays off screen." />

Critics also judge rhythm. *La lenteur* (slowness) can be a choice, to let the viewer look and think, or a weakness, when nothing happens. A good review says which, and why.

<Example fr="La lenteur du film est voulue : elle laisse le temps de regarder." en="The film's slowness is deliberate: it leaves time to look." />
<Example fr="Cette scène est filmée en gros plan, avec la lumière naturelle du matin." en="This scene is filmed in close-up, with the natural light of morning." />

## A film review

The review below is an original text written for this lesson. The film, the director and the actors are invented.

*Le Silence des quais, de Claire Morand*

*Avec son troisième film, Claire Morand confirme qu'elle est une vraie cinéaste. Le Silence des quais suit une jeune femme qui attend, pendant toute une nuit, un bateau qui ne vient pas. Le scénario tient en deux lignes ; tout le reste est mise en scène.*

*Le film s'ouvre sur un plan-séquence de plusieurs minutes, caméra à l'épaule, qui suit l'héroïne sur le port. Puis le rythme change : le montage devient plus lent, les gros plans sont de plus en plus nombreux, et la lumière naturelle de la nuit donne à chaque visage une couleur étrange. La cinéaste filme surtout ce qu'on ne voit pas. Le bateau reste dans le hors-champ, et une voix off, celle d'un homme qu'on ne verra jamais, lit de vieilles lettres.*

*Les acteurs, que le public ne connaît pas encore, parlent peu, et leurs silences disent plus que leurs mots. L'actrice principale, souvent filmée en gros plan pendant de longues minutes, porte tout le film sur son visage.*

*Cette lenteur est le choix le plus fort du film, mais aussi sa limite. Certains spectateurs aimeront cette attente ; d'autres trouveront que l'histoire n'avance pas. Il faut reconnaître que la dernière demi-heure perd un peu de sa force.*

*Reste une œuvre personnelle, qui fait confiance au spectateur et lui laisse le temps de regarder. Dans un cinéma français partagé entre le cinéma d'auteur et le cinéma grand public, Le Silence des quais fait clairement son choix. On n'est pas obligé de le suivre, mais on ne l'oubliera pas.*

(The Silence of the Quays, by Claire Morand. With her third film, Claire Morand confirms that she is a true film-maker. The Silence of the Quays follows a young woman who waits, for a whole night, for a boat that doesn't come. The screenplay fits in two lines; everything else is direction. The film opens with a long take lasting several minutes, with a hand-held camera, that follows the heroine around the port. Then the rhythm changes: the editing slows down, the close-ups multiply, and the natural light of the night gives each face a strange colour. The film-maker mostly films what we don't see. The boat stays off screen, and a voice-over, that of a man we will never see, reads old letters. The actors, whom the public doesn't know yet, speak little, and their silences say more than their words. The lead actress, often filmed in close-up for long minutes, carries the whole film on her face. This slowness is the film's strongest choice, but also its limit. Some viewers will love this waiting; others will find that the story doesn't move forward. It has to be said that the last half-hour loses some of its strength. What remains is a personal work, which trusts the viewer and leaves them time to look. In a French cinema divided between auteur cinema and mainstream cinema, The Silence of the Quays clearly makes its choice. You don't have to follow it, but you won't forget it.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Un cinéaste, not un directeur">
  Learners often call a film director *un directeur*. In French, *un directeur* runs a company or a school. For films, say **un réalisateur**, **une réalisatrice**, or, for an auteur, **un cinéaste**, **une cinéaste**.
</Callout>
