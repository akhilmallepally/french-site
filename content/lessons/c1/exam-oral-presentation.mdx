By the end of this lesson you'll be able to prepare a short exposé from a short document, give it with a clear plan, and answer questions calmly.

## What you already know

You know how to open and close a speech (*mesdames et messieurs*, *j'en viens à*, *permettez-moi de conclure*) from <LessonLink id="c1-public-speaking" text="the lesson on speeches and presentations" />, and how to grant and turn a point (*je vous l'accorde*, *il n'en demeure pas moins que*) from <LessonLink id="c1-formal-debate" text="the lesson on formal debate" />. You know *la problématique* with *dans quelle mesure* and the kinds of plan from <LessonLink id="c1-dissertation" text="the lesson on the dissertation" />, and *un exposé* from <LessonLink id="c1-abstract-nouns" text="the lesson on abstract nouns" />. This practice lesson puts them together for a short talk based on a document. It says nothing about any exam's rules: it trains the skill.

## Reading the document

<VocabTable group="prepare" />

A short document, such as a newspaper article, an opinion piece or an interview, is *le point de départ* (the starting point), not the subject itself. As a rule of thumb, read it twice. The first time, find the theme and the author's position. The second time, *prendre des notes* (take notes): underline *les mots-clés* (keywords), note the main facts, and ask what question the document *soulève* (raises) beyond its own case.

<Example fr="Le document n'est qu'un point de départ : l'exposé va plus loin." en="The document is only a starting point: the exposé goes further." />
<Example fr="Pendant la deuxième lecture, je prends des notes et je souligne chaque mot-clé." en="During the second reading, I take notes and underline each keyword." />
<Example fr="Ce document soulève une question de société : comment partager la ville ?" en="This document raises a social question: how should the city be shared?" />

Then work on *un brouillon* (rough notes). Don't write the exposé in full sentences: write the problématique, the plan and a few keywords, so that you speak rather than read. Find one or two concrete examples to *illustrer* (illustrate) each part: something you have seen, a fact about your own country, or the document's own case.

<Example fr="Sur mon brouillon, j'écris seulement le plan et quelques mots-clés." en="On my rough notes, I write only the plan and a few keywords." />
<Example fr="Pour illustrer cette idée, je prendrai l'exemple de ma ville." en="To illustrate this idea, I'll take the example of my town." />

## Building the exposé

<VocabTable group="speak" />

A short exposé follows the shape of an essay, spoken:

1. **Introduction**: present the document in a sentence (its theme, its source if you have it), then state the problématique as an indirect question. *En guise d'introduction* (by way of introduction) can open it.
2. **The plan**: announce it in one sentence. *Mon exposé s'articulera autour de deux parties* (my presentation will be organized around two parts).
3. **The parts**: two or three, each with one idea and one example. Two parts is often enough for a short exposé: advantages and limits, causes and consequences, or facts and solutions.
4. **Conclusion**: *pour conclure* (to conclude), answer the problématique in one or two sentences, then open the subject with *une piste de réflexion* (a line of thought for the future).

<Example fr="En guise d'introduction, je voudrais présenter rapidement le document." en="By way of introduction, I'd like to present the document briefly." />
<Example fr="Mon exposé s'articulera autour de deux parties : les avantages, puis les limites." en="My presentation will be organized around two parts: the advantages, then the limits." />
<Example fr="Pour conclure, je proposerais une piste de réflexion : et si chaque quartier choisissait ?" en="To conclude, I'd suggest a line of thought: what if each neighbourhood chose?" />

Speak from your notes, look at the people listening, keep a steady pace, and use the discourse markers of the previous lesson sparingly: *bon* or *voilà* once can make you sound natural, but not in every sentence.

## Answering questions

<VocabTable group="answer" />

Questions after an exposé aren't a trap: they show that people listened. Four habits help.

- **Reformulate** the question before you answer: *si je comprends bien* (if I understand correctly), *vous me demandez si…*. It shows you've understood, and it gives you a moment.
- **Gain time** politely when you need to think: *c'est une question intéressante ; laissez-moi réfléchir un instant*. That's a polite way to *gagner du temps* (buy time).
- **Nuance** rather than contradict: *je n'irais pas jusqu'à dire que…* (I wouldn't go as far as to say that…).
- **Admit a limit** honestly, then offer what you can: *je ne connais pas bien ce sujet, mais…*.

<Example fr="Si je comprends bien, vous me demandez si cette mesure est juste pour tous." en="If I understand correctly, you're asking me whether this measure is fair to everyone." />
<Example fr="Je n'irais pas jusqu'à dire que les voitures doivent disparaître." en="I wouldn't go as far as to say that cars should disappear." />
<Example fr="Je ne connais pas bien ce sujet, mais je peux vous donner un exemple." en="I don't know this subject well, but I can give you an example." />
<Example fr="Cette question est importante ; en la reformulant, on gagne du temps pour réfléchir." en="This question is important; by reformulating it, you buy time to think." />

## A document and a model exposé

The document and the exposé below are original texts written for this lesson. The town is invented.

*Le document : « Dimanche sans voitures : une ville qui respire »*

*Depuis le printemps, le centre de Montval est fermé aux voitures un dimanche par mois. Ce jour-là, les rues appartiennent aux piétons et aux vélos. Les familles marchent dans les rues, les enfants jouent sur la place, et la pollution baisse. Les commerçants, eux, sont partagés : certains vendent plus, d'autres disent que leurs clients ne viennent plus. La mairie souhaite maintenant fermer le centre tous les dimanches, mais elle veut d'abord demander l'avis des habitants.*

*L'exposé*

*En guise d'introduction, je voudrais présenter ce document. Il s'agit d'un article sur une ville, Montval, qui ferme son centre aux voitures un dimanche par mois. Ce document soulève une question : on peut se demander dans quelle mesure une ville peut réduire la place de la voiture sans gêner ceux qui en ont besoin. Mon exposé s'articulera autour de deux parties : les avantages de cette mesure, puis ses limites.*

*D'abord, cette mesure a de vrais avantages. La pollution baisse, et les habitants retrouvent leurs rues. Pour illustrer cette idée, je prendrai l'exemple de ma ville, où une rue fermée aux voitures est devenue un lieu de rencontre.*

*Ensuite, il faut reconnaître ses limites. Les commerçants n'en profitent pas tous, et certaines personnes, âgées ou qui habitent loin, ont besoin de leur voiture.*

*Pour conclure, je dirais que la mesure est utile, à condition de proposer d'autres solutions, comme des transports gratuits ce jour-là. On pourrait ouvrir une piste de réflexion : et si chaque quartier choisissait ses propres règles ?*

<VocabTable group="glossary" />

(The document: "Car-free Sunday: a town that breathes". Since the spring, the centre of Montval has been closed to cars one Sunday a month. That day, the streets belong to pedestrians and bikes. Families walk in the streets, children play in the square, and pollution falls. Shopkeepers, for their part, are divided: some sell more, others say their customers no longer come. The town hall now wants to close the centre every Sunday, but it first wants to ask the residents' opinion. The exposé. By way of introduction, I'd like to present this document. It's an article about a town, Montval, that closes its centre to cars one Sunday a month. This document raises a question: one may ask to what extent a town can reduce the place of the car without inconveniencing those who need one. My presentation will be organized around two parts: the advantages of this measure, then its limits. First, this measure has real advantages. Pollution falls, and residents take back their streets. To illustrate this idea, I'll take the example of my town, where a street closed to cars has become a meeting place. Next, its limits must be acknowledged. Not all shopkeepers benefit from it, and some people, older people or those who live far away, need their car. To conclude, I'd say that the measure is useful, provided other solutions are offered, such as free public transport that day. One could open a line of thought: what if each neighbourhood chose its own rules?)

## En français

After the exposé, Clara, who listened, asks Malik some questions.

- *Merci. Vous dites que la mesure est utile. Et pour les commerçants ?* (Thank you. You say the measure is useful. What about the shopkeepers?)
- *Si je comprends bien, vous me demandez si les commerçants y perdent ?* (If I understand correctly, you're asking whether the shopkeepers lose out?)
- *Exactement.* (Exactly.)
- *Le document dit qu'ils sont partagés : certains vendent plus, d'autres disent que leurs clients ne viennent plus.* (The document says they're divided: some sell more, others say their customers no longer come.)
- *Et vous, qu'en pensez-vous ?* (And what do you think?)
- *C'est une question intéressante ; laissez-moi réfléchir un instant.* (That's an interesting question; let me think for a moment.)
- *Prenez votre temps.* (Take your time.)
- *Je pense qu'un dimanche par mois ne pose pas de problème. Tous les dimanches, en revanche, ce serait plus difficile pour eux.* (I think one Sunday a month isn't a problem. Every Sunday, on the other hand, would be harder for them.)
- *Certains disent que les voitures devraient disparaître des centres-villes.* (Some people say that cars should disappear from town centres.)
- *Je n'irais pas jusqu'à dire que les voitures doivent disparaître : certaines personnes en ont besoin.* (I wouldn't go as far as to say that cars must disappear: some people need them.)
- *Et dans votre pays, ça existe ?* (And does this exist in your country?)
- *Je ne connais pas bien ce sujet, mais je sais que ma ville a déjà fermé une rue aux voitures.* (I don't know this subject well, but I know my town has already closed a street to cars.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Mon exposé s'articule autour de, not mon exposé, il a">
  Learners often announce the plan in spoken style, with a dislocation: *mon exposé, il a deux parties*. In an exposé, use the formal sentence: **mon exposé s'articule autour de deux parties**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Rehearse with a stranger's question">
  After you rehearse an exposé, ask a friend to ask you one question you haven't prepared. Answer it with the four habits: reformulate, take a moment, nuance, admit a limit if you must. Answering one surprise question a day builds more confidence than rereading your notes.
</Callout>
