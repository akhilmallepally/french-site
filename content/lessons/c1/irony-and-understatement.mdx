By the end of this lesson you'll be able to recognize irony and understatement in spoken and written French, understand what speakers really mean, and decide when not to use irony yourself.

## What you already know

You know *la litote* and *l'hyperbole* from <LessonLink id="c1-rhetorical-devices" text="the lesson on rhetorical devices" />, and spoken features such as *quoi* and dropped *ne* from <LessonLink id="b2-colloquial-french" text="the lesson on spoken, everyday French" />. You also know *se moquer de* from <LessonLink id="b1-relative-dont" text="the lesson on dont" />. This lesson is about meaning that isn't in the words.

## Irony

<VocabTable group="irony" />

*L'ironie* says one thing and means another, usually the opposite, to criticize or to amuse. Its simplest form is *l'antiphrase*: praise that is really blame.

<Example fr="Cette phrase est une antiphrase : elle dit le contraire de ce que pense la personne qui parle." en="This sentence is an antiphrasis: it says the opposite of what the speaker thinks." /> A sentence *au second degré* isn't meant literally; a listener who *prend au premier degré* misses the point, and may even be *vexé*.

<Example fr="Quel voyage agréable !" en="What a pleasant journey! (ironic, after a three-hour delay)" />
<Example fr="Bravo, quelle organisation !" en="Well done, what organization! (ironic, after a disaster)" />
<Example fr="Il a pris ma remarque au premier degré et il s'est vexé." en="He took my remark literally and was offended." />

*Pince-sans-rire* describes someone who makes jokes with a straight face, or that style of humour. It's admired in French conversation, and it's also the hardest to read for a learner.

## Understatement in everyday French

<VocabTable group="less" />

French conversation uses understatement constantly, and many phrases are fixed. They work like the litote: they say less, and mean more.

- **Ce n'est pas mal** means "it's good", often "it's very good".
- **Pas fameux** and **ce n'est pas terrible** mean "not great", often "bad". *Terrible* here is a false friend: *pas terrible* doesn't mean "not awful".
- **Il ne fait pas chaud** means "it's cold".
- **Je ne dis pas non** means "yes, please".

<Example fr="Ton gâteau ? Ce n'est pas mal du tout !" en="Your cake? It's really good!" />
<Example fr="Le film n'était pas terrible." en="The film wasn't great." />
<Example fr="Un café ? Je ne dis pas non." en="A coffee? I wouldn't say no." />

The same words can be sincere or ironic: *ce n'est pas mal* about a burnt cake is ironic. Only the context decides.

<Example fr="Ce n'est pas terrible, ce film." en="This film isn't great. (spoken)" />

## Reading the signals

<VocabTable group="signals" />

In speech, *le ton* (the tone) carries the irony: a flat voice, a pause, a look. In writing, readers rely on other signals:

- **A mismatch with the facts**: praise for something that obviously went wrong.
- **Exaggeration**: *le meilleur voyage de ma vie* after a breakdown.
- **Set words**: *évidemment*, *comme d'habitude*, *quelle chance*.
- **Typography**: *les guillemets* around a word (*une « petite » panne*) or *un émoji* at the end of a message.

<Example fr="Les guillemets montrent souvent l'ironie à l'écrit." en="Quotation marks often show irony in writing." />
<Example fr="Un émoji à la fin du message montre que c'est une blague." en="An emoji at the end of the message shows it's a joke." />

<Example fr="Le train a encore eu une « petite » panne de trois heures, comme d'habitude." en="The train had another \"little\" three-hour breakdown, as usual. (ironic)" />

## Why irony fails in writing

Without the voice, many readers take irony *au premier degré*. In an email to a colleague, *bravo pour ta ponctualité* after a late report can be read as sincere, or as an attack. As a rule of thumb, avoid irony in professional writing and with people you don't know well, and say the criticism plainly and politely. Keep irony for friends, and for texts where the context makes it obvious: a humorous column, a sketch, a blog post.

<Example fr="Le rapport est arrivé avec deux jours de retard." en="The report arrived two days late. (plain and safe)" />

## Answering irony

When someone is ironic with you, you have three choices. You can show that you understood by answering in the same tone: *ah oui, vraiment inoubliable !*. You can answer the real meaning: *je suis désolé pour le retard*. Or, if you're not sure, you can ask: *tu es sérieux ?*, *c'est au second degré ?*. Asking is always safer than taking a remark *au premier degré* and being hurt by it.

<Example fr="Tu es sérieux, ou c'est au second degré ?" en="Are you serious, or is that tongue in cheek?" />

In France, irony and understatement are part of everyday humour among friends, between colleagues who know each other, and in the media. They aren't a sign of hostility. As a rule of thumb, a learner should recognize all of them and use the fixed understatements (*ce n'est pas mal*, *je ne dis pas non*) freely, but keep real irony for people who know you well.

## A humorous review

This restaurant review is an original text written for this lesson, in the style of a humorous blog. The restaurant is invented. Most of the text is ironic: the translation marks it.

*Sur notre blog : Le Petit Gourmet, une soirée inoubliable*

*Samedi soir, nous avons enfin testé Le Petit Gourmet, le restaurant dont tout le quartier parle. Quelle soirée !*

*D'abord, l'accueil. Nous avions réservé pour vingt heures ; on nous a installés à vingt et une heures, ce qui nous a laissé tout le temps d'admirer la porte d'entrée. Une attention délicate, vraiment.*

*Ensuite, la carte. Le chef propose une « cuisine créative ». En effet, notre poisson était créatif : froid à l'extérieur, encore plus froid à l'intérieur. Le serveur, très pince-sans-rire, nous a expliqué que c'était voulu. Nous l'avons remercié, évidemment. Il est revenu trois fois pour nous demander si tout allait bien. Tout allait très bien, merci : nous attendions toujours le plat suivant.*

*Le cadre, lui aussi, mérite quelques mots. La musique était si forte que nous avons pu suivre les conversations de toutes les tables voisines. Quel plaisir de partager la vie des autres !*

*Le pain n'était pas fameux, mais il était au moins frais. Nous avons beaucoup ri, surtout en attendant le plat suivant.*

*Le dessert, lui, n'était pas mal du tout : une tarte aux pommes simple et chaude. Le chef sait donc cuisiner quand il veut.*

*L'addition ? Il ne fait pas chaud dans ce restaurant, mais les prix, eux, sont brûlants.*

*Bref, une soirée inoubliable, que nous recommandons à tous ceux qui ont beaucoup de patience. Nous y retournerons… peut-être, quand nous aurons oublié le poisson. Et si vous aimez attendre, je ne dis pas non : réservez pour nous aussi.*

<VocabTable group="glossary" />

(On our blog: Le Petit Gourmet, an unforgettable evening (ironic). On Saturday evening, we finally tried Le Petit Gourmet, the restaurant the whole neighbourhood is talking about. What an evening! (ironic) First, the welcome. We had booked for eight o'clock; we were seated at nine, which gave us plenty of time to admire the front door. A thoughtful touch, really. (ironic) Next, the menu. The chef offers "creative cooking". Indeed, our fish was creative: cold on the outside, even colder on the inside. (ironic) The waiter, very deadpan, explained that it was intentional. We thanked him, of course. (ironic) He came back three times to ask if everything was all right. Everything was fine, thank you: we were still waiting for the next course. (ironic) The setting, too, deserves a few words. The music was so loud that we could follow the conversations at all the neighbouring tables. What a pleasure to share other people's lives! (ironic) The bread wasn't great, but at least it was fresh. (sincere) We laughed a lot, especially while waiting for the next course. The dessert, on the other hand, was really good: a simple, warm apple tart. (sincere) So the chef can cook when he wants to. The bill? It's not warm in this restaurant, but the prices are scorching. Anyway, an unforgettable evening, which we recommend to everyone with a great deal of patience. (ironic) We'll go back… perhaps, once we've forgotten the fish. And if you like waiting, I wouldn't say no: book for us too.)

## How the review works

- **Antiphrasis**: the title and the first lines praise a bad evening (*une soirée inoubliable*, *quelle soirée !*).
- **Mismatch with the facts**: *une attention délicate* for an hour's wait, *tout allait très bien* while the food didn't come, *quel plaisir* for loud music.
- **Quotation marks**: *une « cuisine créative »*.
- **A deadpan character**: *le serveur, très pince-sans-rire*.
- **Set words**: *évidemment*.
- **Understatement used sincerely**: *n'était pas fameux* for the bread, and *n'était pas mal du tout* for the one good dish.
- **Understatement used ironically**: *il ne fait pas chaud* (the room is cold) and *je ne dis pas non* at the end.

## En français

Emma shows the review to her friend Marc, who isn't French.

- *Tu as lu la critique du Petit Gourmet ? Elle est géniale.* (Have you read the review of Le Petit Gourmet? It's brilliant.)
- *Oui, j'ai même réservé : ils disent que c'est une soirée inoubliable !* (Yes, I've even booked: they say it's an unforgettable evening!)
- *Marc… tu l'as prise au premier degré !* (Marc… you took it literally!)
- *Comment ça ?* (What do you mean?)
- *C'est de l'ironie. Ils ont attendu une heure, et le poisson était froid.* (It's irony. They waited an hour, and the fish was cold.)
- *Mais ils écrivent « une attention délicate » !* (But they write "a thoughtful touch"!)
- *Justement. C'est le contraire de ce qu'ils pensent.* (Exactly. It's the opposite of what they think.)
- *Et « le dessert n'était pas mal », c'est ironique aussi ?* (And "the dessert wasn't bad", is that ironic too?)
- *Non, là, c'est sincère : ça veut dire que c'était bon.* (No, that one's sincere: it means it was good.)
- *Le français, ce n'est pas simple !* (French isn't simple! (understatement))
- *Tu vois, toi aussi tu fais des litotes. Tu annules ta réservation ?* (You see, you're using litotes too. Are you cancelling your booking?)
- *Je ne dis pas non.* (I wouldn't say no.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Pas terrible means not great">
  Learners often read *pas terrible* as "not terrible", that is, fine. It means the opposite: **le film n'était pas terrible** is a criticism. To praise, say **ce n'était pas mal** or **c'était très bien**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Check the facts against the words">
  When you suspect irony, compare the words with the facts around them. If the words praise and the facts are bad, it's irony; if they match, it's sincere. In the review above, *pas mal* matches a good dessert, so it's sincere.
</Callout>
