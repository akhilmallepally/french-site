By the end of this lesson you'll be able to check your level against everything C1 has taught, and write a short, well-built text about your own progress.

## What C1 has covered

This checklist goes unit by unit. For each one, read the statement, read the model sentence, and ask yourself whether you could write a sentence like it without help. If not, go back to the linked lesson.

**Unit 1, Literary French.** I can read a story told in the passé simple, recognize the passé antérieur and the imperfect subjunctive, and read a classical line of verse. See <LessonLink id="c1-passe-simple" text="the lesson on the passé simple" />.

<Example fr="Le voyageur entra dans l'auberge et demanda pourquoi la cloche se taisait." en="The traveller entered the inn and asked why the bell was silent. (literary)" />

**Unit 2, Style and emphasis.** I can use stylistic inversion, the ne explétif, literary negation and the precise verbs and collocations of careful writing. See <LessonLink id="c1-stylistic-inversion" text="the lesson on stylistic inversion" />.

<Example fr="Peut-être faut-il attendre la fin de l'année pour juger." en="Perhaps we should wait until the end of the year to judge. (formal)" />

**Unit 3, Academic writing.** I can use participle clauses, formal relatives, apposition and concession, and write a synthesis and a dissertation. See <LessonLink id="c1-participle-clauses" text="the lesson on participle clauses" />.

<Example fr="Le rapport terminé, l'équipe a présenté ses conclusions." en="With the report finished, the team presented its conclusions." />

**Unit 4, Debate and rhetoric.** I can concede a point and turn it, use a few rhetorical devices, and recognize irony. See <LessonLink id="c1-formal-debate" text="the lesson on formal debate" />.

<Example fr="Le projet a un coût, je vous l'accorde. Il n'en demeure pas moins qu'il répond à un vrai besoin." en="The project has a cost, I grant you. The fact remains that it meets a real need." />

**Unit 5, Professional mastery.** I can write to an institution in the high register, lead a meeting and use the language of law and finance. See <LessonLink id="c1-formal-correspondence" text="the lesson on high-register correspondence" />.

<Example fr="Je vous saurais gré de bien vouloir m'envoyer le dossier complet." en="I would be grateful if you would send me the complete file. (formal)" />

**Unit 6, Ideas and society.** I can discuss ideas, history and technology, and present a debate neutrally, attributing each position. See <LessonLink id="c1-philosophy-and-ideas" text="the lesson on philosophy and ideas" />.

<Example fr="Pour certains, nous avons un libre arbitre ; pour d'autres, nos choix ont des causes." en="For some, we have free will; for others, our choices have causes." />

**Unit 7, Living the language.** I can use proverbs and idioms where they fit, recognize regional French, and move between registers on purpose. See <LessonLink id="c1-register-shifting" text="the lesson on shifting register" />.

<Example fr="Le chauffage est mort depuis lundi ; le chauffage ne fonctionne plus depuis lundi." en="The heating's been dead since Monday (familiar); the heating has not been working since Monday (formal)." />

**Unit 8, Towards DALF C1.** I can choose words by strength and register, use spoken markers, explain laïcité as a set of rules, give a short exposé and write a synthesis or an argued text. See <LessonLink id="c1-exam-oral-presentation" text="the practice lesson on the short exposé" />.

<Example fr="Si je comprends bien, vous me demandez si cette mesure est juste pour tous." en="If I understand correctly, you're asking me whether this measure is fair to everyone." />

## A few new words

<VocabTable group="new" />

These three words help you talk about your progress. *Les acquis* (always plural in this sense) are what you have learned and can rely on. *La fluidité* (fluency) is speaking or writing without searching for words. To *persévérer* is to keep going when it's difficult, which is the main advice at the end of any level.

<Example fr="Mes acquis sont nombreux à l'écrit, mais ma fluidité à l'oral peut encore progresser." en="My achievements are solid in writing, but my fluency in speaking can still improve." />
<Example fr="Pour atteindre le niveau suivant, il faut persévérer." en="To reach the next level, you have to keep going." />

## Writing your bilan

*Un bilan* (a review, a stock-take) of your learning is a short text in your own voice, in the first person. As a rule of thumb, build it in four paragraphs:

1. **Where you started and where you are**: one concrete before-and-after.
2. **Your strengths**: two or three precise skills, each with an example.
3. **What remains**: one or two weak points, stated honestly and precisely.
4. **What's next**: an objective you can check, and why you'll keep going.

Write it in standard written French: the passé composé and the imparfait for the past, not the passé simple, which belongs to stories. Use a few of the structures of the level where they fit naturally, not all of them: a text that piles up every C1 structure sounds like an exercise.

## The model

The text below is an original text written for this lesson.

*Mon bilan de C1*

*Il y a un an, je lisais des romans français avec un dictionnaire à côté de moi. Aujourd'hui, je lis des nouvelles du dix-neuvième siècle presque sans aide, et je reconnais le passé simple dès la première lecture. Je comprends aussi les films de la Nouvelle Vague sans sous-titres.*

*Mes acquis sont nombreux. J'écris des synthèses sans donner mon avis, je construis une dissertation autour d'une problématique, et je sais passer d'un registre à l'autre : je n'écris plus au propriétaire comme à mes amis. Peut-être mon plus grand progrès est-il à l'oral : je prends la parole en réunion, je reformule les questions et je gagne du temps quand il le faut. Ma professeure, grande lectrice de poésie, m'a aussi fait découvrir Baudelaire. Au travail, j'écris des courriels plus précis, et j'ose enfin dire non poliment.*

*Il me reste cependant des lacunes. La fluidité, que je croyais acquise, disparaît encore quand je suis fatigué. Les accords du participe passé, dont je connais pourtant les règles, me posent encore des problèmes à l'écrit. Je cherche aussi parfois mes mots dans une discussion rapide, surtout quand plusieurs personnes parlent en même temps.*

*Mon objectif pour l'année prochaine est simple : lire un classique par mois et faire un exposé devant des amis chaque semaine. Quand bien même j'atteindrais un jour le niveau C2, je continuerais à lire chaque jour. Je garderai aussi ma grille de relecture près de mon bureau. Je vais donc persévérer, car c'est en forgeant qu'on devient forgeron.*

(My C1 review. A year ago, I read French novels with a dictionary next to me. Today, I read nineteenth-century short stories almost without help, and I recognize the passé simple on the first reading. I also understand New Wave films without subtitles. My achievements are many. I write syntheses without giving my opinion, I build a dissertation around a problématique, and I can move from one register to another: I no longer write to the landlord as I write to my friends. Perhaps my greatest progress is in speaking: I speak up in meetings, I reformulate questions and I buy time when I need to. My teacher, a great reader of poetry, also introduced me to Baudelaire. At work, I write more precise emails, and I finally dare to say no politely. I still have gaps, however. Fluency, which I thought I had acquired, still disappears when I'm tired. Past participle agreements, whose rules I do know, still cause me problems in writing. I also sometimes search for my words in a fast discussion, especially when several people are talking at once. My objective for next year is simple: read one classic a month and give a presentation to friends every week. Even if I one day reached the C2 level, I would go on reading every day. I'll also keep my rereading checklist near my desk. So I'm going to keep going, because practice makes perfect.)

## How the bilan uses the course

- **The plan**: four paragraphs, as above: before and after, strengths, what remains, objective.
- **Unit 2**: inversion after *peut-être* (*peut-être mon plus grand progrès est-il à l'oral*).
- **Unit 3**: an apposition (*ma professeure, grande lectrice de poésie,*), the relatives *que* and *dont*, and *quand bien même* with the conditionnel in both clauses.
- **Units 5 and 8**: the skills of the meeting and the exposé (*je prends la parole*, *je reformule les questions*, *je gagne du temps*).
- **Unit 7**: a proverb in its place, at the end (*c'est en forgeant qu'on devient forgeron*), and the idea of moving between registers.
- **The new words**: *mes acquis*, *la fluidité* and *persévérer*.
- **No passé simple**: a bilan is not a story, so it uses the passé composé and the imparfait.

The writer is a man here (*fatigué*); a woman would write *fatiguée*.

## Your turn

Write your own *bilan de C1* in about two hundred words, in four paragraphs. Use the three new words, one structure from at least three different units, and an objective you can check in a month. Then reread it with the checklist from the practice lesson on synthesis and essay.

## Check your work

- Does each paragraph do one job: before and after, strengths, what remains, objective?
- Is each strength backed by an example?
- Did you use the passé composé and the imparfait, and no passé simple?
- Is every structure you borrowed correct (inversion, apposition, *quand bien même*)?
- Is your objective concrete enough to check?

<Callout type="warning" title="Mes acquis, not mes acquises">
  Learners sometimes make *acquis* agree with the things learned. In this sense, the noun is masculine plural: **mes acquis sont nombreux**. The participle does agree, as in *la fluidité, que je croyais **acquise***.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Reread your first C1 text">
  Find the first text you wrote at the start of C1 and reread it next to your bilan. Note three things you would now write differently. Seeing your own progress, sentence by sentence, is the best reason to persevere.
</Callout>
