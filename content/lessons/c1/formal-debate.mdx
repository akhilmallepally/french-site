By the end of this lesson you'll be able to take part in a formal debate in French: present an opening statement, reply to the other side, concede a point and turn it, and conclude within your time.

## What you already know

You know *un modérateur*, *le temps de parole*, *couper la parole à*, *permettez-moi de*, *réfuter*, *objecter que* and *je maintiens que* from <LessonLink id="b2-debating" text="the lesson on taking part in a debate" />. You can concede with *quand bien même* and *encore que* from <LessonLink id="c1-concession-and-hypothesis" text="the lesson on concession and hypothesis" />, and use rhetorical questions and anaphora from <LessonLink id="c1-rhetorical-devices" text="the lesson on rhetorical devices" />. A B2 debate is a discussion; a formal debate has rules, roles and a clock.

## The format

<VocabTable group="format" />

A formal debate starts from *une motion*, a statement such as *les musées nationaux doivent être gratuits*. One team *défend la motion*; the other *s'oppose à la motion*. A moderator gives the floor, and each *intervenant* has a fixed *temps imparti*.

As a rule of thumb, the debate has three rounds:

1. **La déclaration d'ouverture**: each side presents its position and its two or three main arguments.
2. **La réplique**: each side answers the other's arguments, not just repeats its own.
3. **La conclusion**: each side sums up why its position is stronger, with no new argument.

<Example fr="Notre équipe défend la motion : les musées nationaux doivent être gratuits." en="Our team speaks for the motion: national museums should be free." />
<Example fr="Il vous reste une minute sur le temps imparti." en="You have one minute left of your allotted time." />

## Conceding without losing

<VocabTable group="concede" />

In a formal debate, a good concession makes you more convincing, not less: you show that you've heard the other side. The pattern has two steps. First, grant the point: *je vous l'accorde*, *je vous rejoins sur ce point*, or *certes*. Then turn: *il n'en demeure pas moins que* (more formal than *il n'en reste pas moins que*), *mais*, *cependant*.

<Example fr="La gratuité a un coût, je vous l'accorde. Il n'en demeure pas moins qu'elle attire un nouveau public." en="Free entry has a cost, I grant you. The fact remains that it attracts a new public." />

The strongest move is to *retourner l'argument*: you take the other side's point and show that it supports yours.

<Example fr="Vous dites que les musées manquent d'argent. Justement : plus de visiteurs, c'est plus de dons et plus de soutien public." en="You say museums are short of money. Exactly: more visitors means more donations and more public support." />

Use *concéder* to describe the move: *l'équipe adverse a concédé un point important*.

## Replying, not repeating

In the réplique, quote or summarize the other side's argument in one sentence, then answer it. As a rule of thumb, answer the strongest argument of the other side, not the weakest: the jury notices the difference.

<Example fr="Vous affirmez que la gratuité profite surtout aux visiteurs déjà habitués. Or, la fréquentation des jeunes a nettement augmenté." en="You claim that free entry mainly benefits regular visitors. But attendance among young people has clearly increased." />

*Or*, from the lesson on advanced connectors, introduces the fact that changes the reasoning.

## Keeping to time and to the rules

The moderator keeps order with the formulas you know from B2 (*laissez-le finir*, *revenons-en à la motion*). As a speaker, announce your plan in one sentence, keep one idea per minute, and finish before the moderator stops you. A conclusion cut off in the middle loses the debate.

## Formulas for each round

Each round has its own set phrases. Learn one or two for each, so that you never lose time looking for words.

- **To open**: *notre équipe défend la motion pour trois raisons*; *nous nous opposons à la motion, et voici pourquoi*.
- **To reply**: *vous affirmez que… or…*; *l'équipe adverse oublie que…*; *vous dites que… justement…*.
- **To concede and turn**: *je vous l'accorde… il n'en demeure pas moins que…*; *je vous rejoins sur ce point, mais…*.
- **To conclude**: *en définitive*, *pour toutes ces raisons*, *nous vous invitons à voter pour la motion*.

<Example fr="Pour toutes ces raisons, nous vous invitons à voter contre la motion." en="For all these reasons, we invite you to vote against the motion." />

In the conclusion, don't add a new argument: if you do, the other side has no time to answer it, and many juries count it against you. Repeat your strongest point in one sentence, and end with a clear request to the audience or the jury.

## An extract from a debate

<VocabTable group="topic" />

The debate below is an original text written for this lesson. The speakers are invented, and each side is given its strongest arguments.

*Motion : « Les musées nationaux doivent être gratuits. »*

- **La modératrice :** *Je donne la parole à l'équipe qui défend la motion. Vous avez trois minutes.*
- **Léa :** *Merci. Un musée, c'est un bien commun. Les collections appartiennent à tous ; pourquoi faudrait-il payer pour les voir ? La gratuité ouvre les portes à ceux qui n'entrent jamais : les jeunes, les familles modestes, les habitants qui n'y sont jamais allés.*
- **La modératrice :** *Merci. La parole est à l'équipe qui s'oppose à la motion.*
- **Karim :** *La culture doit être accessible, je vous l'accorde. Il n'en demeure pas moins qu'un musée a un coût : l'entretien des bâtiments, la conservation des collections, le personnel. Si l'entrée est gratuite, qui paiera ? Le contribuable, y compris celui qui ne va jamais au musée.*
- **La modératrice :** *Réplique de l'équipe qui défend la motion.*
- **Léa :** *Vous dites que les musées manquent d'argent. Justement : plus de visiteurs, c'est plus de dons et plus de soutien public. Or, là où l'entrée est devenue gratuite pour les jeunes, leur fréquentation a nettement augmenté.*
- **Karim :** *Je vous rejoins sur ce point : la gratuité pour les jeunes est une bonne mesure. Mais pour tous, elle profite surtout aux visiteurs qui venaient déjà. Une gratuité ciblée coûte moins et aide mieux.*
- **La modératrice :** *Il vous reste une minute chacun pour conclure.*
- **Léa :** *Pour toutes ces raisons, nous vous invitons à voter pour la motion : un musée gratuit est un musée ouvert à tous.*
- **Karim :** *Nous partageons le même but, mais pas le même moyen. Une gratuité ciblée protège les musées et aide ceux qui en ont besoin. Nous vous invitons donc à voter contre la motion.*

<VocabTable group="glossary" />

(Motion: "National museums should be free." The moderator: I give the floor to the team for the motion. You have three minutes. Léa: Thank you. A museum is a common good. The collections belong to everyone; why should people have to pay to see them? Free entry opens the doors to those who never go in: young people, families on low incomes, residents who have never been. The moderator: Thank you. The floor goes to the team against the motion. Karim: Culture must be accessible, I grant you. The fact remains that a museum has a cost: the upkeep of the buildings, the conservation of the collections, the staff. If entry is free, who will pay? The taxpayer, including the one who never goes to museums. The moderator: Rebuttal from the team for the motion. Léa: You say museums are short of money. Exactly: more visitors means more donations and more public support. And where entry has become free for young people, their attendance has clearly increased. Karim: I agree with you on that point: free entry for young people is a good measure. But for everyone, it mainly benefits visitors who already came. Targeted free entry costs less and helps better. The moderator: You each have one minute left to conclude. Léa: For all these reasons, we invite you to vote for the motion: a free museum is a museum open to all. Karim: We share the same goal, but not the same means. Targeted free entry protects museums and helps those who need it. We therefore invite you to vote against the motion.)

## How the debate works

- **The format**: the moderator gives the floor, sets the time (*trois minutes*, *une minute*) and announces each round.
- **The openings**: Léa uses a rhetorical question (*pourquoi faudrait-il payer… ?*) and a list of three groups; Karim lists three costs.
- **The concessions**: Karim grants a point (*je vous l'accorde*, *je vous rejoins sur ce point*) and turns it (*il n'en demeure pas moins que*, *mais*).
- **The réplique**: Léa turns Karim's argument round (*vous dites que… justement*) and adds a fact with *or*.
- **The conclusions**: each side repeats its strongest point and asks for the vote (*nous vous invitons à voter pour / contre la motion*), with no new argument.
- **The outcome**: the debate moves from two opposed positions to a narrower disagreement: free entry for everyone, or only for some groups.

## En français

After the debate, Léa and her teammate Hugo talk about how it went.

- *Alors, comment tu nous trouves ?* (So, how do you think we did?)
- *Ta réplique était très forte. Tu as retourné son argument.* (Your rebuttal was very strong. You turned his argument round.)
- *Oui, mais Karim a été très fort : il a concédé un point pour mieux revenir.* (Yes, but Karim was very good: he conceded a point to come back stronger.)
- *« Je vous rejoins sur ce point »… Le jury a aimé.* ("I agree with you on that point"… The jury liked it.)
- *Et ma conclusion ? J'ai dépassé le temps imparti ?* (And my conclusion? Did I go over the allotted time?)
- *De dix secondes. La modératrice t'a laissée finir.* (By ten seconds. The moderator let you finish.)
- *La prochaine fois, je prépare une conclusion plus courte.* (Next time, I'll prepare a shorter conclusion.)
- *Et réponds à son argument le plus fort, celui du contribuable.* (And answer his strongest argument, the one about the taxpayer.)
- *Tu as raison, je l'ai un peu évité.* (You're right, I avoided it a bit.)
- *Pourtant, tu avais la réponse : les dons et le soutien public.* (And yet you had the answer: donations and public support.)
- *Je vous l'accorde, Monsieur le Président !* (I grant you that, Mr President! (joking))
- *Très drôle. On s'entraîne demain ?* (Very funny. Shall we practise tomorrow? (spoken))

<Callout type="warning" title="Je vous l'accorde, not je vous accorde">
  Learners often drop the pronoun in this formula. *Accorder* needs its object: **je vous l'accorde**, where *l'* stands for the point you grant.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Write their best argument first">
  Before a debate, write the other side's strongest argument in one sentence, then your answer to it. If you can't answer it, prepare a concession instead (*je vous l'accorde…, il n'en demeure pas moins que…*). You'll never be caught without a reply.
</Callout>
