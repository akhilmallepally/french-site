By the end of this lesson you'll be able to understand and use the small words that organize spoken French, and know which of them to keep out of formal writing.

## What you already know

You know the fillers and reductions of everyday speech (*ben*, *du coup*, *en gros*, *genre*, *quoi*) from <LessonLink id="b2-colloquial-french" text="the lesson on spoken, everyday French" />, *quand même* (all the same) from <LessonLink id="b2-concession-opposition" text="the lesson on concession and opposition" />, and *justement* from <LessonLink id="b2-mise-en-relief" text="the lesson on emphasis" />. You know how to move between registers from <LessonLink id="c1-register-shifting" text="the lesson on shifting register" />. This lesson looks at the markers that structure a conversation.

## What markers do

*Un marqueur de discours* (a discourse marker) adds no information; it tells the listener how to take what you're saying: you're starting, correcting yourself, hesitating, reacting or checking that they follow. Native speakers use them constantly, and a learner who never does sounds stiff. Used too often, they sound like hesitation. As a rule of thumb, one or two per sentence is plenty.

<Example fr="Ces petits mots sont des marqueurs de discours : ils organisent la conversation." en="These small words are discourse markers: they organize the conversation." />

## Opening and closing

<VocabTable group="frame" />

*Bon, alors…* opens a new topic or moves things on, often at the start of a meeting or a call. *Eh bien…* (well…) starts an answer, especially when you need a second to think. *Et voilà* closes a point, a list or an explanation: there you go, that's it. You know *voilà* (here you are) from <LessonLink id="a1-review-food-and-shopping" text="the A1 review on food and shopping" />; this is its other use.

<Example fr="Bon, alors, on commence par le budget ?" en="Right, so, shall we start with the budget? (spoken)" />
<Example fr="Eh bien, je ne m'attendais pas à cette question." en="Well, I wasn't expecting that question. (spoken)" />
<Example fr="On envoie le dossier, on attend la réponse, et voilà." en="We send the file, we wait for the answer, and that's it. (spoken)" />

## Correcting, approximating and hedging

<VocabTable group="adjust" />

*Enfin*, which you know as "finally", has another use in speech: **enfin, je veux dire…** corrects what you've just said. *En fait* (actually) corrects an idea, yours or the listener's. *Disons que…* (let's say that…) gives an approximation or softens a judgment. *Si tu veux* or *si vous voulez* (if you like, in a way) hedges a word you're not sure fits. *À la limite* (at a pinch) presents a last option.

<Example fr="La réunion est à dix heures, enfin, je veux dire à onze heures." en="The meeting is at ten, well, I mean at eleven. (spoken)" />
<Example fr="On croit que c'est simple. En fait, c'est assez difficile." en="People think it's simple. Actually, it's fairly difficult. (spoken)" />
<Example fr="Disons que ce n'était pas sa meilleure présentation." en="Let's say it wasn't her best presentation. (spoken)" />
<Example fr="C'est une sorte de chef d'équipe, si vous voulez." en="He's a kind of team leader, if you like. (spoken)" />
<Example fr="Je préfère jeudi. À la limite, vendredi matin." en="I prefer Thursday. At a pinch, Friday morning. (spoken)" />

Note that *disons que* is a form of politeness: like the euphemism, it softens. *Disons que le résultat n'est pas très bon* is gentler than *le résultat est mauvais*.

## Reacting and adding a nuance

<VocabTable group="react" />

**Quand même !** at the end of a sentence expresses indignation, a reproach or insistence: *tu aurais pu prévenir, quand même !* (honestly, you could have warned me!). It's different from *quand même* meaning "all the same", which you know.

**Tiens !** marks surprise (*tiens, il pleut !*); *tiens* or *tenez* also accompanies a gesture of giving (*tenez, voici votre clé*).

**Remarque** or *remarquez* (mind you) adds a point that slightly qualifies what you said. *Cela dit* or *ceci dit* (that said) does the same, and it's the one you can keep in writing. *Pour le coup* (this time, for once) is familiar.

**Tu vois ?** or *vous voyez ?* checks that the listener follows.

<Example fr="Tu aurais pu prévenir, quand même !" en="Honestly, you could have warned me! (spoken)" />
<Example fr="Tiens, je ne savais pas que tu parlais allemand !" en="Oh, I didn't know you spoke German! (spoken)" />
<Example fr="Tenez, voici votre clé." en="Here's your key." />
<Example fr="L'hôtel est loin du centre. Remarque, il est très calme." en="The hotel is far from the centre. Mind you, it's very quiet. (spoken)" />
<Example fr="Le projet coûte cher. Cela dit, il répond à un vrai besoin." en="The project is expensive. That said, it meets a real need." />
<Example fr="D'habitude il est en retard, mais pour le coup, il est arrivé le premier." en="He's usually late, but this time he arrived first. (familiar)" />
<Example fr="Il faut d'abord choisir le thème, tu vois ?" en="You have to choose the theme first, you see? (spoken)" />

## Speech and writing

As a rule of thumb, the markers in this lesson belong to speech. *En fait* and *cela dit* are exceptions: both are common in standard writing too. In formal writing, again as a rule of thumb, keep *cela dit* and replace the others with written connectors: *en réalité* for *en fait*, *cependant* or *néanmoins* for *remarque*, *en somme* for *voilà*. When you rewrite spoken French for a report, the markers simply go; only the information stays.

<Example fr="En réalité, la réunion est reportée." en="In fact, the meeting has been postponed. (written)" />

## En français

Inès and Paul plan a weekend away.

- *Bon, alors, on part samedi ?* (Right, so, are we leaving on Saturday? (spoken))
- *Eh bien, samedi, je travaille le matin.* (Well, on Saturday, I'm working in the morning. (spoken))
- *Tiens, je croyais que tu ne travaillais plus le samedi.* (Oh, I thought you didn't work on Saturdays any more. (spoken))
- *En fait, c'est seulement ce mois-ci.* (Actually, it's only this month. (spoken))
- *D'accord. On part samedi midi, enfin, je veux dire vers treize heures.* (OK. We leave on Saturday at noon, well, I mean around one. (spoken))
- *Parfait. Et on dort où ? Chez ta sœur ?* (Perfect. And where do we sleep? At your sister's? (spoken))
- *Disons que ce n'est pas l'idéal : elle a trois enfants.* (Let's say it's not ideal: she has three children. (spoken))
- *Un petit hôtel, alors ? Remarque, c'est plus cher.* (A small hotel, then? Mind you, it's more expensive. (spoken))
- *Cela dit, on sera plus tranquilles.* (That said, we'll be quieter. (spoken))
- *À la limite, on peut prendre une seule nuit.* (At a pinch, we can take just one night. (spoken))
- *Oui. Et pour le coup, c'est toi qui réserves !* (Yes. And this time, you're the one who books! (familiar))
- *Moi ? Tu pourrais le faire, quand même !* (Me? Honestly, you could do it! (spoken))
- *Bon, je réserve. On trouve l'hôtel, on prend une nuit, et voilà.* (Right, I'll book. We find the hotel, we take one night, and that's it. (spoken))
- *Voilà. Tu vois, c'était simple.* (There you go. You see, it was simple. (spoken))

<Callout type="warning" title="En fait, not en effet, to correct">
  Learners often confuse the two. *En effet* confirms what was just said (indeed); **en fait** corrects it (actually): *je croyais qu'il était parti ; **en fait**, il était encore là*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Listen for one marker at a time">
  When you watch a French interview or podcast, choose one marker, such as *disons*, and note each time you hear it and what it does there. After three or four markers, start using them yourself, one at a time, in conversation.
</Callout>

## En résumé

Les marqueurs de discours n'ajoutent pas d'information : ils montrent comment comprendre ce qu'on dit. Bon, alors ouvre un sujet, eh bien commence une réponse, et voilà ferme une explication. Enfin, je veux dire permet de revenir sur ce qu'on vient de dire, en fait change une idée fausse, et disons que donne une idée moins précise ou rend un jugement plus doux. Si tu veux, ou si vous voulez, montre qu'un mot ne convient pas tout à fait, et à la limite présente une dernière solution. Quand même, à la fin d'une phrase, exprime un reproche. Tiens marque la surprise, et tenez accompagne un objet qu'on donne. Remarque et cela dit ajoutent une nuance, pour le coup est familier, et tu vois vérifie que la personne suit. Ces mots appartiennent à l'oral : à l'écrit soutenu, on garde surtout cela dit, et on remplace en général les autres par des mots de l'écrit, comme en réalité ou cependant.
