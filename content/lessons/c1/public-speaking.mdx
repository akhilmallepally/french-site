## Addressing the audience

<VocabTable group="address" />

A French speech starts by addressing the audience. *Mesdames et messieurs* is the standard formal opening; for a closer group, *chers collègues* or *chers amis*. If there are official guests, name them first by title: *Madame la Maire, mesdames et messieurs*. *Une allocution* is a short formal speech, for example at an inauguration or a ceremony.

After the address comes *l'accroche* (the hook): a question, a short story, a striking fact. Its job is to *interpeller* the audience, to speak to them directly from the first sentence.

<Example fr="Mesdames et messieurs, combien d'entre vous ont lu un livre ce mois-ci ?" en="Ladies and gentlemen, how many of you have read a book this month?" />
<Example fr="L'intervenante a commencé par une accroche qui a interpellé toute la salle." en="The speaker began with a hook that spoke directly to the whole room." />

## Signposting

<VocabTable group="signpost" />

A listener can't go back a page. Signposts tell them where they are. Announce the plan at the start (*je commencerai par…, puis…, enfin…*), mark each move (*en premier lieu*, *j'en viens maintenant à*), and announce the end (*avant de conclure*, *permettez-moi de conclure*).

<Example fr="Je commencerai par l'histoire du projet, puis j'en viendrai à ses objectifs." en="I'll begin with the history of the project, then I'll come to its aims." />
<Example fr="J'en viens maintenant à la question du financement." en="I now come to the question of funding." />
<Example fr="Permettez-moi de conclure par une remarque personnelle." en="Allow me to conclude with a personal remark." />

As a rule of thumb, a speech needs more signposts than an essay: what a reader sees in the paragraphs, a listener needs to hear.

## Devices in a speech

The devices from the last lessons work best when they're heard. An anaphora in three sentences gives rhythm; a rhetorical question wakes the audience up; a gradation builds to the key point. Keep them for the moments that matter: the hook, the turning point and the end. A speech with a device in every sentence sounds like a parody.

## Delivery

<VocabTable group="deliver" />

How you say it matters as much as what you say. Speak more slowly than in conversation. *Marquer une pause* before an important sentence, and after it, so that it lands. Let *la voix* rise and fall: a flat voice loses the audience. Look at the room, not at your notes. If you use *le micro*, hold it at the same distance throughout.

Almost every speaker has *le trac* (stage fright). A practical answer is to learn the first two sentences by heart: once they're out, the rest follows.

<Example fr="Avant son allocution, elle avait le trac, mais sa voix est restée calme." en="Before her speech, she had stage fright, but her voice stayed calm." />

## Presentations at work

A presentation at work follows the same rules, with slides. Address the room with *chers collègues*, announce the plan, and signpost each slide. Don't read the slides aloud: the audience reads faster than you speak. One idea per slide, a short title, few words. Check *le micro* and the screen before you start.

<Example fr="Ne lisez pas vos diapositives : le public les lit plus vite que vous." en="Don't read your slides out: the audience reads them faster than you." />

<Example fr="Chers collègues, je commencerai par les chiffres de l'année, puis j'en viendrai aux projets." en="Dear colleagues, I'll begin with the year's figures, then I'll come to the projects." />
<Example fr="Avant de commencer, vérifiez que le micro fonctionne." en="Before you start, check that the microphone works." />

As a rule of thumb, plan one minute per slide, and keep the last slide for your message, not for "questions?".

## Ending well

The last sentence is the one the audience remembers. Don't end with *voilà* or *c'est tout*. Come back to your hook, sum up your message in one sentence, then thank the audience: *je vous remercie de votre attention*.

## An inauguration speech

The speech below is an original text written for this lesson. The town, the library and the speaker are invented.

*Allocution de la directrice pour l'ouverture de la médiathèque de Saint-Aubin*

*Madame la Maire, mesdames et messieurs, chers amis,*

*Combien d'entre vous ont lu un livre ce mois-ci ? Levez la main… Je vois beaucoup de mains, et c'est une bonne nouvelle, car ce soir, nous ouvrons un lieu fait pour vous.*

*Je commencerai par l'histoire de ce projet, puis j'en viendrai à ce que nous voulons en faire.*

*Ce projet est né il y a cinq ans, quand notre vieille bibliothèque, déjà ouverte le dimanche, est devenue trop petite pour la ville. La nouvelle médiathèque la remplace aujourd'hui.*

*En premier lieu, je voudrais remercier toutes les personnes qui ont rendu ce projet possible : les architectes, les ouvriers, les bibliothécaires, et les habitants qui ont donné leur avis pendant trois ans. Je pense aussi à mon équipe, qui travaille depuis des mois pour ouvrir à temps.*

*Ce bâtiment a été pensé pour tous : il est calme, plein de lumière, et il accueillera aussi des concerts.*

*J'en viens maintenant à notre projet. Nous voulons une médiathèque ouverte. Nous voulons une médiathèque vivante. Nous voulons une médiathèque pour tous : pour les enfants qui découvrent leurs premiers livres, pour les étudiants qui préparent leurs examens, pour les personnes âgées qui cherchent un lieu de rencontre. La lecture publique n'est pas un luxe : c'est un droit.*

*Avant de conclure, un mot sur les horaires. La médiathèque sera ouverte du mardi au dimanche, le dimanche l'après-midi seulement, comme notre vieille bibliothèque.*

*Permettez-moi de conclure par une question. Le mois prochain, combien d'entre vous auront lu un livre de plus ? J'espère que la réponse sera : tous.*

*Je vous remercie de votre attention, et à très bientôt à la médiathèque !*

<VocabTable group="glossary" />

(Speech by the director at the opening of the Saint-Aubin library. Madam Mayor, ladies and gentlemen, dear friends, How many of you have read a book this month? Raise your hand… I can see a lot of hands, and that's good news, because tonight we are opening a place made for you. I'll begin with the history of this project, then I'll come to what we want to make of it. This project was born five years ago, when our old library, already open on Sundays, became too small for the town. The new library replaces it today. First of all, I would like to thank everyone who made this project possible: the architects, the builders, the librarians, and the residents who gave their views for three years. I'm also thinking of my team, who have been working for months to open on time. This building was designed for everyone: it is quiet, full of light, and it will also host concerts. I now come to our project. We want an open library. We want a lively library. We want a library for everyone: for children discovering their first books, for students preparing their exams, for older people looking for a meeting place. Public libraries aren't a luxury: they're a right. Before I conclude, a word about opening hours. The library will be open from Tuesday to Sunday, on Sunday in the afternoon only, like our old library. Allow me to conclude with a question. Next month, how many of you will have read one more book? I hope the answer will be: all of you. Thank you for your attention, and see you very soon at the library!)

## How the speech works

- **Address**: *Madame la Maire* first, then *mesdames et messieurs, chers amis*.
- **Hook**: a question that speaks directly to the audience, with a gesture (*levez la main*).
- **History**: two sentences on how the project began (*ce projet est né il y a cinq ans…*), as the plan announced.
- **Plan**: announced in one sentence (*je commencerai par…, puis j'en viendrai à…*).
- **Signposts**: *en premier lieu*, *j'en viens maintenant à*, *avant de conclure*, *permettez-moi de conclure*.
- **Devices**: an anaphora (*nous voulons…*, three times), a list in three, and an antithesis (*n'est pas un luxe : c'est un droit*).
- **Ending**: the hook comes back as a question, then the thanks.

## En français

The director, Madame Morel, rehearses the speech with her colleague Julien.

- *Alors, tu peux m'écouter ? J'ai un peu le trac.* (So, can you listen to me? I'm a bit nervous.)
- *Bien sûr. Commence quand tu veux.* (Of course. Start whenever you like.)
- *« Madame la Maire, mesdames et messieurs, chers amis… »* ("Madam Mayor, ladies and gentlemen, dear friends…")
- *Très bien. Et ton accroche ?* (Very good. And your hook?)
- *Une question au public : « Combien d'entre vous ont lu un livre ce mois-ci ? »* (A question to the audience: "How many of you have read a book this month?")
- *Parfait. Marque une pause après, laisse-les répondre.* (Perfect. Pause afterwards, let them answer.)
- *Ensuite, j'annonce le plan, puis je remercie tout le monde.* (Then I announce the plan, then I thank everyone.)
- *Ta voix descend à la fin des phrases. Parle un peu plus fort.* (Your voice drops at the end of the sentences. Speak a bit louder.)
- *D'accord. Et l'anaphore, « nous voulons », ce n'est pas trop ?* (OK. And the anaphora, "nous voulons", isn't it too much?)
- *Non, trois fois, c'est juste. Mais regarde la salle, pas tes notes.* (No, three times is right. But look at the room, not at your notes.)
- *Et pour finir, je reviens à ma question.* (And to finish, I come back to my question.)
- *C'est la meilleure idée du discours. Ils vont s'en souvenir.* (That's the best idea in the speech. They'll remember it.)

<Callout type="warning" title="J'en viens à la question, not j'en viens de la question">
  Learners often use *de* after *venir*, as in *je viens de Lyon*. To move to a new point in a speech, the construction is **en venir à**: **j'en viens maintenant à la question du budget**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Learn the first two sentences by heart">
  Before any speech, learn your address and your hook by heart, word for word. Nerves are strongest in the first thirty seconds; once those sentences are out, the rest of the speech, read from short notes, follows much more easily.
</Callout>
