By the end of this lesson you'll be able to tell a short, lively story about something that happened to you, and react like a French speaker when someone tells you one.

## How an anecdote works

*Une anecdote* is a short, true story, usually funny or surprising. In <LessonLink id="a2-telling-a-story" text="the lesson on telling a simple story" />, you learned to set the scene with the imparfait and give the events with the passé composé. An anecdote adds two things: suspense, and a listener who reacts.

A good anecdote has four steps:

1. **The setting:** where you were, and what was going on (imparfait).
2. **The event:** what suddenly happened (passé composé).
3. **The reaction:** how you, or other people, responded.
4. **The ending:** how it turned out, in one or two sentences.

## Telling it

<VocabTable group="telling" />

*Figure-toi que* is informal, for someone you call *tu*. With *vous* or with several friends, it's *figurez-vous que*. *Et là* is the moment just before the surprise, and it often comes with a little pause. *Bref* and *au final* bring you to the ending.

<Example fr="Figure-toi que j'ai vu Paul au supermarché, à six heures du matin !" en="Guess what, I saw Paul at the supermarket at six in the morning!" />
<Example fr="On mangeait, et là, le chat est monté sur la table." en="We were eating, and then the cat jumped onto the table." />
<Example fr="Bref, au final, on a eu un fou rire pendant dix minutes." en="Anyway, in the end, we got the giggles for ten minutes." />

Use the connectors from <LessonLink id="b1-time-connectors" text="the lesson on time connectors" /> to move the story on: *au moment où*, *dès que*, *entre-temps*.

## About to, had just

Two structures help you set the timing of a story. Both use the imparfait.

<VocabTable group="timing" />

**Aller in the imparfait + infinitive** says you were about to do something, often when something interrupted you. The interruption is in the passé composé.

<Example fr="J'allais partir quand Karim est arrivé." en="I was about to leave when Karim arrived." />
<Example fr="Nous allions manger quand le chat est entré." en="We were going to eat when the cat came in." />

**Venir de in the imparfait + infinitive** says something had just happened. You know *venir de* in the present from <LessonLink id="a2-venir-de-en-train-de" text="the lesson on venir de and en train de" />: *je viens de manger* (I've just eaten).

<Example fr="Je venais de rentrer quand le téléphone a sonné." en="I had just got home when the phone rang." />
<Example fr="Elle venait d'arriver, et elle était déjà fatiguée." en="She had just arrived, and she was already tired." />

## Reacting as a listener

When someone tells you a story, French speakers react all the way through. Silence can sound as if you're not interested.

<VocabTable group="reacting" />

<Example fr="Tu as gagné cent euros ? Quelle chance !" en="You won a hundred euros? How lucky!" />
<Example fr="Tu es tombé devant tout le bureau ? La honte !" en="You fell over in front of the whole office? How embarrassing!" />
<Example fr="C'est bizarre, cette histoire. Et alors ?" en="That's a strange story. And then what?" />

*La honte !* is informal: keep it for friends. *Ce n'est pas vrai !* doesn't mean you think the other person is lying. It just means "No way!"

## Direct speech

To report what people said in an anecdote, keep their exact words, in direct speech. It's livelier, and it's simpler too.

<Example fr="Le serveur m'a regardé et il a dit : « Monsieur, vous êtes au mauvais restaurant ! »" en="The waiter looked at me and said: 'Sir, you're in the wrong restaurant!'" />

## En français

Léa tells Karim what happened to her last night.

- *Figure-toi que je suis restée devant ma porte pendant deux heures hier soir !* (Guess what, I was stuck outside my door for two hours last night!)
- *Ce n'est pas vrai ! Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ?* (No way! What happened?)
- *Je venais de rentrer du travail. J'allais ouvrir la porte quand je me suis rendu compte que je n'avais pas mes clés.* (I'd just got back from work. I was about to open the door when I realized I didn't have my keys.)
- *Sans blague ! Et alors ?* (No kidding! And then what?)
- *Et là, j'ai deviné où elles étaient : au bureau !* (And then I guessed where they were: at the office!)
- *La honte ! Tu as appelé quelqu'un ?* (How embarrassing! Did you call anyone?)
- *Oui, j'ai appelé ma sœur, mais elle était au cinéma.* (Yes, I called my sister, but she was at the cinema.)
- *Quelle horreur ! Et tu as attendu ?* (How awful! And did you wait?)
- *Oui, deux heures. Bref, au final, ma sœur est arrivée à minuit avec ses clés.* (Yes, two hours. Anyway, in the end, my sister turned up at midnight with her keys.)
- *C'est incroyable ! La prochaine fois, laisse-moi des clés !* (That's unbelievable! Next time, leave me a set of keys!)

## A group chat

Nadia tells her friends about her brother's wedding.

- **Nadia:** *Je vous raconte le mariage de mon frère ? C'était incroyable.* (Shall I tell you about my brother's wedding? It was unbelievable.)
- **Hugo:** *Oui ! Raconte !* (Yes! Tell us!)
- **Nadia:** *Alors, on était tous dans la salle, et mon frère allait dire « oui »…* (So, we were all in the room, and my brother was about to say "yes"…)
- **Camille:** *Et là ?* (And then?)
- **Nadia:** *Et là, son téléphone a commencé à jouer une chanson !* (And then his phone started playing a song!)
- **Hugo:** *Sans blague ! La honte !* (No kidding! How embarrassing!)
- **Nadia:** *Nous avons eu un fou rire pendant cinq minutes.* (We had the giggles for five minutes.)
- **Camille:** *Quelle horreur ! Et au final ?* (How awful! And in the end?)
- **Nadia:** *Au final, il a dit « oui », et c'était une très belle journée. Bref, je vous montre les photos demain !* (In the end, he said "yes", and it was a lovely day. Anyway, I'll show you the photos tomorrow!)

<Callout type="warning" title="J'allais partir or je suis allé ?">
  *J'allais partir* doesn't mean "I went". It means "I was going to leave", and usually something stopped you. "I went" is **je suis allé**. And *je venais de partir* means "I had just left", not "I came to leave". Check which one you mean before you start your story.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Five sentences, not fifteen">
  The best anecdotes are short. Before you tell one, decide on your five sentences: the setting, the build-up, the *et là*, the reaction and the ending. Anything else is extra. If your listener wants more detail, they'll ask with *Et alors ?*, and that's a good sign.
</Callout>
