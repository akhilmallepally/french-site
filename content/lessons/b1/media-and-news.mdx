By the end of this lesson you'll be able to talk about how you follow the news, understand French headlines, and say whether a source is reliable.

## Newspapers and magazines

You know *le journal* from <LessonLink id="a2-irregular-verbs-present" text="the lesson on common irregular verbs" />. Here are the words for the rest of the press.

<VocabTable group="press" />

<Example fr="Mes parents achètent un quotidien tous les matins." en="My parents buy a daily paper every morning." />
<Example fr="La grève est à la une de tous les journaux." en="The strike is on the front page of all the papers." />
<Example fr="Je lis l'éditorial avant le reste du journal." en="I read the editorial before the rest of the paper." />

*Un titre* is the headline of an article, and also the title of a book or a film.

Many French people now read the press on their phone, but the words stay the same. You still read *un article* in *un quotidien*, even on a screen, and the most important story of the day is still *à la une*, on the front page.

## TV and radio

<VocabTable group="tv" />

<Example fr="Je regarde le JT de 20 heures." en="I watch the 8 o'clock TV news." />
<Example fr="Il y a un bon reportage sur la Bretagne ce soir." en="There's a good report about Brittany tonight." />
<Example fr="Le match est en direct sur quelle chaîne ?" en="Which channel is the match live on?" />

*Le journal* means a newspaper, but also the news bulletin on TV or radio: *le journal de 20 heures*. *Les infos* is the everyday word for "the news", and it's plural.

## What's in the news

<VocabTable group="stories" />

<Example fr="Je ne suis pas l'actualité en ce moment." en="I'm not following the news at the moment." />
<Example fr="Dans le journal, il y a une grande enquête sur les écoles." en="In the paper, there's a big investigation into schools." />
<Example fr="Le ministre donne une interview à la radio." en="The minister is giving an interview on the radio." />

*Une interview* is for the media. For a job, use *un entretien*, from <LessonLink id="b1-cover-letter" text="the lesson on CVs and cover letters" />.

## Reliable or not?

You know how to give your opinion from <LessonLink id="b1-expressing-opinions" text="the lesson on expressing opinions" />. These words help you judge where the news comes from.

Before you believe a story, ask three quick questions. Who wrote it? Is the source a paper or a channel you know? Do other sources say the same thing? If the answer to the last question is no, be careful: it may be *une fausse information*.

<VocabTable group="trust" />

<Example fr="Pour s'informer, il faut des sources fiables." en="To keep informed, you need reliable sources." />
<Example fr="Cette information est fausse : c'est une fausse information." en="This piece of news is false: it's fake news." />

## Headline style

French headlines are short. They often drop the verb, especially *être*, and leave only a noun and a participle or an adjective.

| Headline | Full sentence |
|---|---|
| *Le musée fermé dimanche* | *Le musée sera fermé dimanche.* |
| *Grève des trains lundi* | *Les trains feront grève lundi.* |
| *Nouveau film pour Dany Boon* | *Dany Boon sort un nouveau film.* |

When you read a headline, put the verb back in your head. It's usually *être* or *avoir*, in the present or the futur.

## En français

Two flatmates are reading the news over breakfast.

- *Tu as vu les infos ce matin ?* (Have you seen the news this morning?)
- *Non. Qu'est-ce qu'il y a à la une ?* (No. What's on the front page?)
- *Une grève des trains lundi. Et une enquête sur les prix des appartements.* (A train strike on Monday. And an investigation into flat prices.)
- *Encore une grève ? Tu l'as lu où ?* (Another strike? Where did you read it?)
- *Dans un quotidien. C'est une source fiable.* (In a daily paper. It's a reliable source.)
- *Moi, je regarde le JT le soir. Je n'ai pas le temps de lire.* (I watch the TV news in the evening. I don't have time to read.)
- *Il y a un reportage sur la grève ce soir, en direct.* (There's a report on the strike tonight, live.)
- *Sur quelle chaîne ?* (On which channel?)
- *Sur France 2, à 20 heures.* (On France 2, at 8 o'clock.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Les infos is plural">
  English "news" is singular and uncountable: "the news is bad". French *les infos* and *les informations* are plural: **les infos sont mauvaises**. To talk about one piece of news, French can count it: **une info**, **une information** (a piece of news). And *une nouvelle* is a piece of personal news, as in *J'ai une bonne nouvelle !*
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Five minutes of slow news">
  Several French broadcasters make short news bulletins in simple French for learners, read slowly and with a transcript. Listen to one each morning, with the transcript open. In a few weeks, the political and news vocabulary that comes back every day will feel familiar.
</Callout>
