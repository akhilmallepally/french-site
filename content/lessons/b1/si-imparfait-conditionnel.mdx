By the end of this lesson you'll be able to imagine situations that aren't true, or aren't likely, and say what you would do.

## Imagining: si + imparfait, conditionnel

In <LessonLink id="b1-si-present-futur" text="the lesson on si with the present and future" />, you talked about things that could really happen. To imagine something that isn't true, or is very unlikely, French changes both verbs. The verb after *si* goes into the imparfait, from <LessonLink id="a2-imparfait-formation" text="the lesson on the imparfait" />. The other verb goes into the conditionnel, from <LessonLink id="b1-conditionnel-present" text="the lesson on the conditionnel présent" />.

<Example fr="Si j'avais le temps, je voyagerais." en="If I had time, I would travel." />
<Example fr="Si nous étions riches, nous habiterions au bord de la mer." en="If we were rich, we would live by the sea." />
<Example fr="S'il faisait beau, on irait à la plage." en="If the weather were nice, we'd go to the beach." />

As in English, the imparfait here doesn't talk about the past. *Si j'avais le temps* means "if I had time" now, and the speaker doesn't have it.

The halves can swap places, just as with real conditions: *Je voyagerais si j'avais le temps.*

## Real or imagined?

Compare the two patterns. The choice depends on how likely you think the situation is.

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *si* + present, futur | *S'il fait beau demain, on ira à la plage.* | It may well be nice tomorrow. |
| *si* + imparfait, conditionnel | *S'il faisait beau, on irait à la plage.* | It isn't nice, but imagine it were. |

The same situation can go either way, depending on the speaker. Someone who has bought a ticket and feels lucky might say *si je gagne*. Someone who never plays says *si je gagnais*. *Si je gagne au loto* sounds as if you expect to win. *Si je gagnais au loto* is a daydream, which is why most people say it that way.

## If I were you

*Si j'étais toi* (if I were you) uses the same pattern. It's one of the most common ways to give advice, and you'll use it again in <LessonLink id="b1-giving-advice" text="the lesson on giving advice" />. With *vous*, it's *si j'étais vous*.

<Example fr="Si j'étais toi, je partirais demain." en="If I were you, I'd leave tomorrow." />
<Example fr="Si j'étais vous, je réserverais maintenant." en="If I were you, I'd book now." />

## En français

Two colleagues are chatting on a coffee break.

- *Qu'est-ce que tu ferais si tu gagnais au loto ?* (What would you do if you won the lottery?)
- *Si je gagnais au loto, j'arrêterais de travailler !* (If I won the lottery, I'd stop working!)
- *Moi, je continuerais à travailler, mais je déménagerais.* (I'd keep working, but I'd move house.)
- *Tu irais où ?* (Where would you go?)
- *J'achèterais une maison au bord de la mer.* (I'd buy a house by the sea.)
- *Et la célébrité, ça te plairait ?* (And fame, would you like that?)
- *Pas du tout ! Je préférerais être invisible.* (Not at all! I'd rather be invisible.)
- *Invisible ? Pourquoi ?* (Invisible? Why?)
- *Si j'étais invisible, je pourrais partir du bureau à trois heures !* (If I were invisible, I could leave the office at three!)
- *Si j'étais toi, je ne dirais pas ça devant la directrice.* (If I were you, I wouldn't say that in front of the boss.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Never the conditionnel after si">
  The *-rais* form goes in the result, never straight after *si*. It's **Si j'avais le temps**, not *si j'aurais le temps*. You may hear the second one in casual speech, but it's considered a mistake. A simple test: *si* and *-rais* never sit side by side.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Answer one question a day">
  Imagined situations are a game, so play it. Each morning, pick one question, such as *Si tu pouvais habiter dans un autre pays…* or *Si tu avais un superpouvoir…*, and answer it in two sentences. The silly questions work best, because you remember the answers, and the grammar comes with them.
</Callout>
