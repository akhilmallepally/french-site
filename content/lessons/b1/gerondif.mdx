By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say what you do while doing something else, and how you do things: *en marchant*, *en écoutant des podcasts*.

## How to form it

The **gérondif** is *en* + a verb form ending in *-ant*. To build it, take the *nous* form of the present, remove *-ons*, and add *-ant*.

| Infinitive | *nous* form | Gérondif |
|---|---|---|
| *parler* | *nous parlons* | *en parlant* |
| *finir* | *nous finissons* | *en finissant* |
| *prendre* | *nous prenons* | *en prenant* |
| *faire* | *nous faisons* | *en faisant* |
| *lire* | *nous lisons* | *en lisant* |
| *manger* | *nous mangeons* | *en mangeant* |

<VocabTable group="forms" />

The form never changes: it's the same for every person, masculine or feminine, singular or plural. You know the *nous* forms from <LessonLink id="a2-irregular-verbs-present" text="the lesson on common irregular verbs" />, so most gérondifs are easy.

Three verbs are irregular.

<VocabTable group="irregular" />

<Example fr="En étant à l'heure, tu montres que tu es sérieux." en="By being on time, you show that you're serious." />
<Example fr="En sachant ça, je comprends mieux." en="Knowing that, I understand better." />

## At the same time

The main use of the gérondif is to say that two things happen at the same time. In <LessonLink id="b1-time-connectors" text="the lesson on time connectors" />, you used *pendant que* for this. The gérondif is shorter, but it only works when both actions have the same subject.

<Example fr="J'écoute des podcasts en marchant." en="I listen to podcasts while walking." />
<Example fr="Il mange en regardant son téléphone." en="He eats while looking at his phone." />
<Example fr="Elle chante en faisant la cuisine." en="She sings while she cooks." />

The gérondif usually comes after the main verb. You can also put it first, followed by a comma, when you want to stress the situation: *En marchant, j'écoute des podcasts.* The meaning stays the same.

## How you do it

The gérondif also says how, or by what means, you do something. In English, this is usually "by …ing".

<Example fr="J'apprends le français en écoutant des podcasts." en="I learn French by listening to podcasts." />
<Example fr="On voyage moins cher en prenant le train." en="You travel more cheaply by taking the train." />
<Example fr="En partant tôt, tu arriveras à l'heure." en="If you leave early, you'll arrive on time." />

In the last example, the gérondif works like a condition: *en partant tôt* means *si tu pars tôt*. It's shorter and sounds natural when the link between the action and the result is clear. For longer or more complex conditions, keep *si*.

## Tout en and en attendant

<VocabTable group="uses" />

*Tout en* stresses that the two things happen together, even if they don't usually go together. *En attendant* is a fixed phrase meaning "in the meantime".

<Example fr="Il travaille tout en écoutant de la musique." en="He works while listening to music at the same time." />
<Example fr="Le film commence à neuf heures. En attendant, on mange ?" en="The film starts at nine. In the meantime, shall we eat?" />

## En français

Two friends are comparing their phone habits.

- *Qu'est-ce que tu écoutes en marchant ?* (What do you listen to when you walk?)
- *Des podcasts, toujours. J'apprends beaucoup en marchant.* (Podcasts, always. I learn a lot while walking.)
- *Moi, j'écoute de la musique en faisant la cuisine.* (I listen to music while cooking.)
- *Et en prenant le métro ?* (And on the metro?)
- *En prenant le métro, je lis les infos sur mon téléphone.* (On the metro, I read the news on my phone.)
- *Tu ne lis jamais de livres ?* (Don't you ever read books?)
- *Si, mais pas en marchant ! Je serais trop distraite.* (Yes, but not while walking! I'd be too distracted.)
- *C'est vrai. Mon frère est tombé en regardant son portable.* (That's true. My brother fell over while looking at his phone.)
- *Exactement. Moi, je ne regarde jamais mon téléphone dans la rue.* (Exactly. I never look at my phone in the street.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Same subject only">
  The gérondif always refers to the subject of the main verb. **J'ai vu Paul en sortant du cinéma** means *I* was leaving the cinema, not Paul. If the two actions have different subjects, use *pendant que* or *quand*: **J'ai vu Paul pendant qu'il sortait du cinéma.**
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Describe your commute">
  Each day this week, write one sentence about what you do while getting somewhere: *En prenant le bus, je lis. En marchant, j'écoute un podcast.* Then add one sentence about how you learn: *J'apprends des mots en lisant les infos.* Real daily habits give you a new verb every day.
</Callout>
