By the end of this lesson you'll be able to explain why something happens and what it leads to, in speech and in writing.

## Giving a reason: parce que and car

You know *parce que* from <LessonLink id="a1-question-words" text="the lesson on question words" />, and you used it to justify your opinions in <LessonLink id="b1-expressing-opinions" text="the lesson on expressing opinions" />. It answers *pourquoi ?* and works everywhere, in speech and in writing.

<VocabTable group="cause" />

*Car* means the same, but it belongs to writing and careful speech. It always comes in the middle of a sentence, after the main idea, and it can never start a sentence or answer a question on its own.

<Example fr="Je prends le vélo parce que c'est plus pratique." en="I take the bike because it's more practical." />
<Example fr="Le magasin est fermé, car le patron est malade." en="The shop is closed, as the owner is ill." />

## Puisque and comme

*Puisque* gives a reason that the other person already knows, or can see. It often sounds like "since you know that already…".

<Example fr="Puisque tu es là, tu peux m'aider ?" en="Since you're here, can you help me?" />
<Example fr="On ne sort pas, puisqu'il pleut." en="We're not going out, since it's raining." />

*Comme* (as, since) goes at the start of the sentence, before the main idea. With a past situation, it's often followed by the imparfait, which you know from <LessonLink id="a2-passe-compose-vs-imparfait" text="the lesson on the passé composé or imparfait" />.

<Example fr="Comme il fait chaud, on va à la plage." en="As it's hot, we're going to the beach." />
<Example fr="Comme il pleuvait, nous sommes restés à la maison." en="As it was raining, we stayed at home." />

## With a noun: à cause de and grâce à

*Parce que*, *car*, *puisque* and *comme* are followed by a full sentence, with a verb. When the cause is just a noun, use *à cause de* or *grâce à*.

<VocabTable group="noun" />

*À cause de* is for a cause with a bad result. *Grâce à* is for a cause with a good result. Both join with *le* and *les*: *à cause du bruit*, *grâce aux éoliennes*.

<Example fr="Le train est en retard à cause de la grève." en="The train is late because of the strike." />
<Example fr="J'ai réussi grâce à mon professeur." en="I passed thanks to my teacher." />
<Example fr="Le musée est fermé en raison de la canicule." en="The museum is closed due to the heatwave." />

*En raison de* is neutral and formal. You'll see it on notices, in news reports and in official letters, rarely in conversation.

## Giving a consequence

<VocabTable group="result" />

*Donc* and *alors* both mean "so". *Alors*, from <LessonLink id="a2-writing-an-informal-email" text="the lesson on writing an informal email" />, is more spoken. *Donc* works in speech and writing, and it's the one to choose in an essay. *C'est pourquoi* starts a new sentence in writing, and *c'est pour ça que* is its everyday spoken form.

<Example fr="Il pleut, donc je prends mon parapluie." en="It's raining, so I'm taking my umbrella." />
<Example fr="Les voitures polluent. C'est pourquoi la ville veut moins de voitures dans le centre." en="Cars pollute. That's why the city wants fewer cars in the centre." />
<Example fr="J'ai raté le bus, c'est pour ça que je suis en retard." en="I missed the bus, that's why I'm late." />

## Talking about causes and effects

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="Quelles sont les causes de la pollution ?" en="What are the causes of pollution?" />
<Example fr="La canicule provoque des sécheresses." en="The heatwave is causing droughts." />
<Example fr="Le réchauffement climatique a des conséquences graves." en="Global warming has serious consequences." />

You know the environment words from <LessonLink id="b1-environment" text="the lesson on the environment" />. They're the perfect topic for cause and consequence, because every problem has both.

## En français

Two neighbours meet on the stairs during a heatwave.

- *Bonjour, Madame Leroy. Quelle chaleur, aujourd'hui !* (Hello, Mrs Leroy. What heat today!)
- *Bonjour ! Oui, c'est la canicule. Je ne sors plus l'après-midi, car il fait trop chaud pour moi.* (Hello! Yes, it's the heatwave. I don't go out in the afternoon any more, as it's too hot for me.)
- *Vous avez raison. Et vous avez assez d'eau ?* (You're right. And have you got enough water?)
- *Oui, grâce à mon fils. Il m'a apporté des bouteilles hier.* (Yes, thanks to my son. He brought me some bottles yesterday.)
- *Tant mieux. Chez nous, le jardin est tout sec, à cause de la sécheresse.* (Good. At our place, the garden is completely dry, because of the drought.)
- *Comme il ne pleut pas, il faut économiser l'eau.* (As it isn't raining, we have to save water.)
- *Oui, c'est pour ça que je n'arrose plus les fleurs.* (Yes, that's why I don't water the flowers any more.)
- *Puisque vous êtes là, vous voulez un café ?* (Since you're here, would you like a coffee?)
- *Avec plaisir, mais un café froid, alors !* (With pleasure, but an iced coffee, then!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Parce que + verb, à cause de + noun">
  *Parce que* needs a full sentence with a verb, and *à cause de* needs a noun. It's **parce qu'il pleut** or **à cause de la pluie**, never *à cause qu'il pleut* or *parce que la pluie*. If you've only got a noun, reach for *à cause de* or *grâce à*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Build a chain">
  Take one fact and build a chain of three links: a cause, the fact, then a consequence. *Comme il fait très chaud, les rivières sont sèches. C'est pourquoi il faut économiser l'eau.* Practising with chains trains you to connect ideas, which is exactly what you'll need to present an argument.
</Callout>
