By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say "mine", "yours", "ours" and "theirs" in French, and avoid repeating a noun.

## From mon to le mien

You know the possessive adjectives from <LessonLink id="a1-possessive-adjectives" text="the lesson on possessive adjectives" />: *mon livre*, *ta voiture*, *leurs enfants*. A **possessive pronoun** replaces the adjective and the noun together, the way the pronouns in <LessonLink id="a2-direct-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on direct object pronouns" /> replace a noun.

<Example fr="Ce n'est pas mon stylo, c'est le tien." en="It's not my pen, it's yours." />
<Example fr="Ta voiture est rouge. La mienne est noire." en="Your car is red. Mine is black." />

## One owner

<VocabTable group="singular-owner" />

| Owner | Masc. singular | Fem. singular | Masc. plural | Fem. plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *je* | le mien | la mienne | les miens | les miennes |
| *tu* | le tien | la tienne | les tiens | les tiennes |
| *il*, *elle* | le sien | la sienne | les siens | les siennes |

## Several owners

<VocabTable group="plural-owner" />

| Owner | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| *nous* | le nôtre / la nôtre | les nôtres |
| *vous* | le vôtre / la vôtre | les vôtres |
| *ils*, *elles* | le leur / la leur | les leurs |

The pronouns *nôtre* and *vôtre* have a circumflex, and the *o* is closed, as in *eau*. The adjectives *notre* and *votre* have no accent, and the *o* is more open. *Le leur* has no circumflex, and it takes an *s* only in the plural: *les leurs*. Don't confuse it with *leur* the indirect pronoun, which never takes an *s*: *je leur parle*.

## Agreement: the thing, not the owner

The pronoun agrees with the thing owned, never with the person who owns it. So *le sien* and *la sienne* can both mean "his" or "hers": the gender comes from the noun.

<Example fr="La voiture de Paul ? C'est la sienne." en="Paul's car? It's his. (voiture is feminine)" />
<Example fr="Le parapluie de Marie ? C'est le sien." en="Marie's umbrella? It's hers. (parapluie is masculine)" />
<Example fr="Nos enfants sont à l'école. Les leurs aussi." en="Our children are at school. Theirs are too." />

## With à and de

The article *le* or *les* in the pronoun joins with *à* and *de*, as in <LessonLink id="a1-contractions-a-de" text="the lesson on contractions with à and de" />.

<Example fr="Je parle à mon frère, et tu parles au tien." en="I'm talking to my brother, and you're talking to yours." />
<Example fr="J'ai besoin de mon chargeur. Tu as besoin du tien ?" en="I need my charger. Do you need yours?" />
<Example fr="Nous pensons à nos enfants, et ils pensent aux leurs." en="We think about our children, and they think about theirs." />

## C'est à moi or c'est le mien?

Both answer the question *C'est à qui ?* (Whose is it?). *C'est à moi* (or *à toi*, *à lui*, *à elle*, *à nous*…) just says who owns it. *C'est le mien* is used when you're comparing, or choosing between several similar things.

<VocabTable group="work" />

<Example fr="C'est à qui, ce badge ? C'est à moi." en="Whose is this badge? It's mine." />
<Example fr="Ce n'est pas ton casier, c'est le mien." en="That's not your locker, it's mine." />

## En français

Two colleagues are tidying the office after a meeting.

- *C'est à qui, cette tasse ? C'est la tienne ?* (Whose is this mug? Is it yours?)
- *Non, la mienne est bleue. C'est sûrement la tasse de Marc.* (No, mine is blue. It's probably Marc's mug.)
- *Et ce chargeur ? C'est à toi ?* (And this charger? Is it yours?)
- *Oui, c'est le mien. Merci !* (Yes, it's mine. Thanks!)
- *Il y a aussi deux parapluies. Le noir est à Léa.* (There are two umbrellas too. The black one is Léa's.)
- *Et le rouge, c'est le sien aussi ?* (And the red one, is that hers too?)
- *Non, c'est le parapluie de la directrice.* (No, it's the director's umbrella.)
- *Bon. Et nos badges ? Les nôtres sont où ?* (Right. And our badges? Where are ours?)
- *Dans nos casiers, comme d'habitude !* (In our lockers, as usual!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Agree with the thing, not the owner">
  English chooses "his" or "hers" from the owner. French chooses *le sien* or *la sienne* from the thing. **Paul et sa voiture : c'est la sienne** (feminine, because of *voiture*). **Marie et son livre : c'est le sien** (masculine, because of *livre*). If you're not sure, say the noun with its article first: *la voiture* → *la sienne*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Play 'C'est à qui ?'">
  Pick up five objects around you, real or imagined, and for each one ask and answer: *C'est à qui, ce stylo ? C'est le mien. Et cette tasse ? C'est la sienne.* Change the owner each time. The pronoun has to match a different noun every time, so it's quick, focused practice.
</Callout>
