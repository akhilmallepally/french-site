By the end of this lesson you'll be able to write a short review of a film or a book, and recommend it or warn people off.

## How a review works

A short review, *une critique*, has four parts. The vocabulary for the first two is in <LessonLink id="b1-cinema-and-books" text="the lesson on cinema and books" />.

1. **The presentation:** the title, the genre, who made it, and the year. *C'est un film policier de Claire Dubois, sorti en 2024.*
2. **The summary:** two or three sentences about the story, in the present. Say how it starts, not how it ends.
3. **Your opinion:** what worked and what didn't, with an example.
4. **Your recommendation:** should people see it or read it?

<VocabTable group="review" />

## The parts of a film

<VocabTable group="parts" />

<Example fr="Le scénario est original, et le jeu des acteurs est très bon." en="The screenplay is original, and the acting is very good." />
<Example fr="Les effets spéciaux sont réussis, mais l'histoire est ennuyeuse." en="The special effects are well done, but the story is boring." />
<Example fr="La bande originale est géniale : je l'écoute tous les jours." en="The soundtrack is brilliant: I listen to it every day." />

For a book, talk about *l'intrigue*, *les personnages* and *la fin* instead.

<Example fr="L'intrigue est passionnante, mais la fin est décevante." en="The plot is gripping, but the ending is disappointing." />
<Example fr="Les personnages sont très réussis." en="The characters are very well drawn." />
<Example fr="C'est un roman à lire absolument." en="It's a must-read novel." />

*À lire absolument* works like *à voir absolument*, with the verb that fits: you see a film and read a book.

## Your opinion

The pattern from <LessonLink id="b1-ce-qui-ce-que" text="the lesson on ce qui and ce que" /> is perfect for reviews, because it puts the important part last.

<Example fr="Ce qui m'a plu, c'est la fin." en="What I liked was the ending." />
<Example fr="Ce que j'ai moins aimé, c'est la musique." en="What I liked less was the music." />
<Example fr="Ce qui m'a surpris, c'est le personnage principal." en="What surprised me was the main character." />

<VocabTable group="verdict" />

*Réussi* and *raté* work like adjectives and agree: *une scène réussie*, *une fin ratée*. *Un film raté* is a failure, not a film you missed.

## Recommending or warning off

<VocabTable group="advice" />

<Example fr="C'est un film à voir absolument." en="It's a must-see film." />
<Example fr="C'est un peu long, mais ça vaut le coup." en="It's a bit long, but it's worth it." />
<Example fr="Franchement, c'est un roman à éviter." en="Honestly, it's a novel best avoided." />
<Example fr="C'est mon film préféré. Je vous le conseille !" en="It's my favourite film. I recommend it!" />

*Je vous le conseille* has two pronouns before the verb: *vous* (to you) and *le* (it). Learn it as a fixed phrase for now. You'll see how these pronouns combine in <LessonLink id="b1-double-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on double object pronouns" />. *Ça vaut le coup* is informal but very common in reviews.

## Stars

Online, reviews often end with a rating out of five: *une étoile*, *deux étoiles*… *Cinq étoiles* is the top mark. You can write it as *4/5* (*quatre sur cinq*).

## En français

Two friends are leaving the cinema.

- *Alors, qu'est-ce que tu en penses ?* (So, what do you think?)
- *J'ai adoré ! Ce qui m'a plu, c'est le scénario. Il est très original.* (I loved it! What I liked was the screenplay. It's very original.)
- *C'est vrai. Mais je trouve que le jeu des acteurs est un peu décevant.* (That's true. But I think the acting is a bit disappointing.)
- *Tu trouves ? Pas pour moi. Et la bande originale ?* (Do you think so? Not for me. And the soundtrack?)
- *Géniale ! Mais la fin est ratée, à mon avis.* (Brilliant! But the ending is botched, in my opinion.)
- *Je ne suis pas d'accord. La fin, c'est la meilleure scène !* (I don't agree. The ending is the best scene!)
- *Bon, tu lui donnes combien d'étoiles ?* (Well, how many stars do you give it?)
- *Quatre sur cinq. Ça vaut le coup.* (Four out of five. It's worth it.)
- *Moi, trois. Mais je le recommande à Hugo.* (Three for me. But I'd recommend it to Hugo.)

## An online review

*Le Dernier Été* ★★★★☆

*C'est un drame de Claire Dubois, sorti en 2024. Ça se passe en Bretagne, dans les années 1980. Ça parle de Julie, une jeune fille qui passe ses vacances chez sa grand-mère. Le premier jour, elle trouve une vieille lettre dans un livre. Ce qui m'a plu, c'est le jeu des acteurs : la grand-mère est géniale. Le scénario est un peu long, mais l'histoire devient passionnante. La bande originale est très réussie. Ce que j'ai moins aimé, c'est la fin, un peu trop courte. Bref, c'est un film émouvant, à voir absolument. Je vous le conseille !*

(*The Last Summer*, four stars out of five. It's a drama by Claire Dubois, released in 2024. It's set in Brittany, in the 1980s. It's about Julie, a young girl who spends her holidays at her grandmother's. On the first day, she finds an old letter in a book. What I liked was the acting: the grandmother is brilliant. The screenplay is a bit long, but the story becomes gripping. The soundtrack is very well done. What I liked less was the ending, which is a bit too short. Anyway, it's a moving film, a must-see. I recommend it!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Tell the story in the present">
  When you summarise a film or a book, it's tempting to use the past, because you've already seen it. French reviews, like English ones, tell the story in the present: **Julie passe ses vacances chez sa grand-mère**, not *Julie a passé ses vacances*. Keep the past for your own experience: *J'ai vu ce film samedi.*
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Borrow the opening line">
  Read two or three short reviews on a French cinema or book website, and copy the first sentence of each into your notes. Opening lines follow a small number of patterns, such as *C'est le nouveau film de…* or *Dans son dernier roman, …*. Borrow one, and your own review will start like a real one.
</Callout>
