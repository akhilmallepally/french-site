By the end of this lesson you'll be able to ask "which one?" and "which ones?" when you're choosing between things.

## Quel or lequel?

You know *quel*, *quelle*, *quels* and *quelles* from <LessonLink id="a1-question-words" text="the lesson on question words" />. They go with a noun: *Quel téléphone préfères-tu ?* When the noun is already clear, French replaces *quel* + the noun with one word: *lequel*.

Use *lequel* when the person you're talking to already knows what kind of thing you mean, usually because you've just mentioned it or you're pointing at it. If the noun hasn't come up yet, use *quel* + the noun.

<Example fr="Quel téléphone préfères-tu ? → Lequel préfères-tu ?" en="Which phone do you prefer? → Which one do you prefer?" />
<Example fr="Quelle marque est la meilleure ? → Laquelle est la meilleure ?" en="Which brand is the best? → Which one is the best?" />

## The four forms

<VocabTable group="forms" />

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | *lequel* | *laquelle* |
| Plural | *lesquels* | *lesquelles* |

The form agrees with the noun it replaces. It's made of *le*, *la* or *les* joined to *quel*, *quelle*, *quels* or *quelles*. All four plurals sound the same.

To say "which one of", add *de* and a plural noun. This is common when the things are in front of you.

<Example fr="Lequel de ces téléphones est le moins cher ?" en="Which of these phones is the cheapest?" />

<Example fr="J'ai deux ordinateurs. Lequel veux-tu ?" en="I've got two computers. Which one do you want?" />
<Example fr="Il y a plusieurs offres. Lesquelles sont intéressantes ?" en="There are several deals. Which ones are interesting?" />

You can ask with inversion, with *est-ce que*, or with intonation, as with any question: *Lequel préfères-tu ?*, *Lequel est-ce que tu préfères ?*, *Lequel tu préfères ?*

## With à and de

When the verb takes *à* or *de*, the preposition goes in front, and it joins with *le* and *les*, as in <LessonLink id="a1-contractions-a-de" text="the lesson on contractions with à and de" />.

| | à | de |
|---|---|---|
| *lequel* | *auquel* | *duquel* |
| *laquelle* | *à laquelle* | *de laquelle* |
| *lesquels* | *auxquels* | *desquels* |
| *lesquelles* | *auxquelles* | *desquelles* |

<Example fr="Tu penses à un appareil ? Auquel ?" en="You're thinking of a device? Which one?" />
<Example fr="Tu parles de quelle offre ? De laquelle ?" en="Which deal are you talking about? Which one?" />

These forms are used much less in conversation than *lequel* and *laquelle*, but you'll see them in writing. In speech, people often avoid them: instead of *Auquel ?*, you'll often hear *À quel appareil ?*, or simply *Lequel ?*

## Answering

The natural answer to *lequel ?* uses the demonstrative pronouns from <LessonLink id="b1-demonstrative-pronouns" text="the lesson on demonstrative pronouns" />.

<Example fr="Lequel tu préfères ? Celui-ci, il est plus léger." en="Which one do you prefer? This one, it's lighter." />
<Example fr="Laquelle tu prends ? Celle qui a le plus de mémoire." en="Which one are you taking? The one with the most storage." />

You can also answer with *le* or *la* and an adjective, without repeating the noun. The adjective agrees with the noun you're not saying: *Laquelle tu prends ? La noire.*

## En français

A customer is choosing a phone and a plan in a shop.

- *Bonjour. Je cherche un nouveau téléphone.* (Hello. I'm looking for a new phone.)
- *Nous en avons plusieurs. Lequel vous intéresse ?* (We have several. Which one interests you?)
- *J'hésite entre ces deux-là. Lequel a le plus de mémoire ?* (I'm torn between those two. Which one has the most storage?)
- *Celui-ci. Et il a une garantie de deux ans.* (This one. And it has a two-year warranty.)
- *Très bien. Et pour le forfait ? Il y a plusieurs offres, non ?* (Very good. And for the plan? There are several deals, aren't there?)
- *Oui, trois. Laquelle vous intéresse ?* (Yes, three. Which one interests you?)
- *Celle à 15 euros par mois. Il y a une réduction ?* (The 15-euros-a-month one. Is there a discount?)
- *Oui, 20 % les six premiers mois.* (Yes, 20% for the first six months.)
- *Parfait, je prends ce téléphone et ce forfait.* (Perfect, I'll take this phone and this plan.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Lequel replaces the noun">
  *Lequel* already contains the noun, so don't add it again. It's **Quel téléphone ?** or **Lequel ?**, never *lequel téléphone*. And choose the form from the noun, not from the person asking: *une marque* gives **laquelle**, even if a man is asking.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Narrow it down">
  When you're choosing between things, use one *lequel* question to narrow it down, then one *celui* answer to decide: *Lequel est le plus léger ? Celui-ci. Et lequel est le moins cher ? Celui-là.* Two questions, two answers, and the decision is made. It's the pattern you'll hear in every French shop.
</Callout>
