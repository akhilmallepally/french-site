## A model news item

This is a short *fait divers* from a local news site.

*Lyon : Rex retrouve sa famille*

*Jeudi dernier, Rex, un petit chien noir, a disparu dans un parc de Lyon. Sa famille l'a cherché toute la nuit. Le soir, le fils a publié une photo de Rex sur les réseaux sociaux. La publication est devenue virale : en deux jours, plus de 10 000 internautes l'ont partagée. Dimanche matin, une femme a vu Rex en rentrant chez elle. Elle a appelé la famille tout de suite. « Chaque message nous a aidés », explique la mère.*

(Lyon: Rex finds his family. Last Thursday, Rex, a little black dog, disappeared in a park in Lyon. His family looked for him all night. In the evening, the son posted a photo of Rex on social media. The post went viral: in two days, more than 10,000 internet users shared it. On Sunday morning, a woman saw Rex on her way home. She called the family straight away. "Every message helped us," the mother explains.)

## Reporting it

Here's how you could pass on the story to a friend, with reported speech in the present.

*Le journal dit qu'un chien a disparu à Lyon. Il explique que plus de 10 000 internautes ont partagé sa photo. La mère dit que chaque message les a aidés.*

(The paper says a dog went missing in Lyon. It explains that more than 10,000 internet users shared his photo. The mother says every message helped them.)

## A reaction

And here's a comment a reader left under the article.

*Belle histoire ! Mais attention : certains internautes partagent n'importe quoi. Avant de partager une publication, vérifiez-la. Et si vous voyez une fausse information, signalez-la.*

(Lovely story! But be careful: some internet users share any old thing. Before you share a post, check it. And if you see false information, report it.)

Notice how the style changes across the three texts. The news item is written and neutral, with dates, places and full sentences. The report is what you'd say to a friend over coffee. The comment is short and direct, like speech, but it uses *vous*, because the writer is talking to every reader at once.

## How the texts use the grammar

- In *en rentrant chez elle*, the gérondif says what the woman was doing when she saw Rex. It refers to the subject, *une femme*.
- In *il explique que plus de 10 000 internautes ont partagé*, reported speech passes on the facts. The verb after *que* stays in the passé composé, because the reporting verb is in the present.
- In *chaque message* and *certains internautes*, indefinite words talk about people in general.
- In *vérifiez-la* and *signalez-la*, the pronoun *la* replaces *la publication*.

If a friend asks you about the story, you can use *lequel* too: *Tu as vu l'article sur le chien ? Lequel ? Celui de Lyon.*

## Your turn

Choose a short, real news item in French, from a news site or the radio, and write three pieces, on paper or in a notes app.

1. **A summary** of three or four sentences, as if you're telling a friend. Start with *Le journal dit que…* or *L'article explique que…*
2. **A question someone might ask**, reported: *Mon frère demande si…*
3. **A reaction** of two or three sentences. Use one indefinite word (*chaque*, *certains*, *aucun*), and one gérondif if you can.

## Check your work

- After *dire que* and *expliquer que*, did you keep *que*, and write *qu'* before a vowel?
- In reported questions, did you drop *est-ce que* and inversion?
- Does every gérondif refer to the subject of its sentence?
- Is the verb after *chaque*, *chacun*, *aucun* and *tout le monde* singular?

<Callout type="warning" title="Vérifier, not contrôler">
  To check a fact or a piece of information, use **vérifier**: *Vérifiez l'information*. *Contrôler* is for official checks, like tickets on a train or passports at a border. *Je contrôle l'article* sounds as if you're the police. For news, a link or a password, it's **vérifier**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="One story, three ways">
  When you read a news item in French, retell it three ways: first in one sentence, as a headline; then in three sentences, as reported speech; then as your own reaction. The same words come back each time, so they stick, and you practise three different kinds of writing with one short text.
</Callout>
