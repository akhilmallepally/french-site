## Which si?

The most common mistakes in this unit come from mixing the two patterns. Check every *si* sentence against this table.

| You think it… | After si | In the other half | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| may well happen | present | futur or imperative | *S'il fait beau, on ira au parc.* |
| isn't true, or is unlikely | imparfait | conditionnel | *S'il faisait beau, on irait au parc.* |

The verb after *si* is never in the futur and never in the conditionnel. If you see *-rai* or *-rais* straight after *si*, change it.

## A forum post

*Que feriez-vous si vous pouviez changer de métier ?*

*Bonjour à tous ! Moi, si je pouvais, je deviendrais cuisinier. J'aimerais ouvrir un petit restaurant au bord de la mer. Et vous ? Lucas*

(What would you do if you could change jobs? Hello everyone! If I could, I'd become a cook. I'd like to open a little restaurant by the sea. What about you? Lucas)

*Si je pouvais changer de métier, je deviendrais professeur de musique. Je travaille dans une banque, et je trouve ça ennuyeux. Si j'avais plus de temps, j'apprendrais le piano, et dans l'idéal, je donnerais des cours aux enfants. Emma*

(If I could change jobs, I'd become a music teacher. I work in a bank, and I find it boring. If I had more time, I'd learn the piano, and ideally, I'd give lessons to children. Emma)

## A choice to make

*J'hésite entre deux choix : rester dans mon entreprise à Paris, ou partir à Lyon pour un nouveau travail. Je ne sais pas quelle décision prendre ! Qu'est-ce que vous feriez à ma place ? Nina*

(I'm torn between two choices: staying with my company in Paris, or moving to Lyon for a new job. I don't know what decision to make! What would you do in my place? Nina)

*À votre place, je pèserais le pour et le contre. Si vous partez à Lyon, vous aurez un nouveau travail, mais vous serez loin de vos amis. Si vous restez à Paris, rien ne changera. Je vous conseille de réfléchir deux ou trois jours. Ensuite, prenez une décision. Et si vous changez d'avis plus tard, vous pourrez toujours revenir. Marc*

(If I were you, I'd weigh up the pros and cons. If you move to Lyon, you'll have a new job, but you'll be far from your friends. If you stay in Paris, nothing will change. I'd advise you to think it over for two or three days. Then make a decision. And if you change your mind later, you can always come back. Marc)

Notice how Marc mixes the patterns on purpose. *À votre place, je pèserais* is advice, with the conditionnel. *Si vous partez, vous aurez* is a real possibility, because Nina may well go.

## Linking your ideas

Both pieces read better with a few linking words, which you already know.

- Use *d'abord* and *ensuite* to put your ideas in order.
- Use *de plus* to add a second reason, as in <LessonLink id="b1-review-opinions" text="the review of opinions and feelings" />.
- Use *mais* to introduce the other side of a choice.
- Use *sinon* to give the alternative, without repeating the whole condition.

Start the advice reply by showing you've understood the problem, in one sentence, before you give any advice.

## Your turn

Write two short pieces, on paper or in a notes app.

1. **A forum answer**, six to eight sentences: *Que feriez-vous si vous pouviez changer de vie ?* Use *si* + imparfait at least three times, and *dans l'idéal* or *j'aimerais* once.
2. **An advice reply** to Nina, or to a friend with a real choice to make: four to six sentences, with *à votre place* or *si j'étais vous*, *je vous conseille de*, and one real condition with *si* + present.

## Check your work

- Is every verb after *si* in the present or the imparfait?
- Does each *si* + imparfait have a conditionnel in the other half?
- Did you use *ne pas* together before an infinitive?
- Did you describe both options in the advice reply, with one *si* sentence each?
- Did you use at least one linking word, such as *d'abord*, *de plus* or *sinon*?
- In your advice reply, did you keep *tu* or *vous* all the way through?

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't forget whose choice it is">
  In an advice reply, it's easy to slip from *vous* to *je* halfway through: *Si je pars à Lyon…*. Your reader then can't tell whose life you're talking about. Keep the condition about the other person, **Si vous partez à Lyon**, and save *je* for *à votre place, je…*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Give two paths, not one answer">
  Good advice often describes both options instead of choosing for the other person. Write one *si* sentence for each path, *Si vous restez…* and *Si vous partez…*, then add your own view at the end. The reader can make their own decision, and your advice still comes through.
</Callout>
