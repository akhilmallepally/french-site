By the end of this lesson you'll be able to read a flat ad, visit a flat, ask the right questions and talk about renting and moving in.

## The people

You know the rooms and the furniture from <LessonLink id="a2-home-and-chores" text="the lesson on home and chores" />. Now you need the words for renting.

<VocabTable group="people" />

*Louer* works in both directions: the owner *loue* the flat to you, and you *louez* it from the owner. *Un appartement à louer* is a flat to let. *Locataire* and *propriétaire* are the same for a man or a woman; only the article changes.

<Example fr="Je loue un appartement dans le centre." en="I rent a flat in the centre." />
<Example fr="La propriétaire habite au premier étage." en="The landlady lives on the first floor." />
<Example fr="J'ai trouvé mon studio grâce à une agence immobilière." en="I found my studio flat through an estate agent's." />

## Describing a flat

<VocabTable group="flat" />

<Example fr="C'est un studio meublé de 25 m²." en="It's a 25 m² furnished studio." />
<Example fr="Le salon est très lumineux." en="The living room is very bright." />
<Example fr="Le chauffage marche bien ?" en="Does the heating work well?" />

*La surface* is given in *mètres carrés*, written m². French ads also count rooms differently from English ones: *un T2* or *un F2* is a flat with two main rooms, usually a living room and a bedroom, without counting the kitchen and the bathroom. *Un studio* has one main room.

*Une visite* is the word for a viewing: *Je peux faire une visite samedi ?* You'll also hear *visiter un appartement*.

## Money and paperwork

<VocabTable group="money" />

The rent is often given in two parts. *Le loyer* is the rent itself, and *les charges* cover shared costs, such as water or cleaning the building. *Charges comprises* (often *CC* in ads) means the price includes both.

<Example fr="Le loyer est de 700 euros, charges comprises." en="The rent is 700 euros, charges included." />
<Example fr="Il faut payer une caution et avoir un garant." en="You have to pay a deposit and have a guarantor." />
<Example fr="Avant d'emménager, il faut une assurance habitation." en="Before moving in, you need home insurance." />

In France, landlords often ask for *un garant*, a person who will pay the rent if you can't. For students, it's often a parent. Before you sign *le bail*, you'll usually need to show *une assurance habitation*.

To give the price, use *être de* + the amount: *le loyer est de 700 euros*. This is how prices are given in ads and in conversation.

## Sharing and moving

<VocabTable group="moving" />

<Example fr="Je cherche une chambre en colocation." en="I'm looking for a room in a flat-share." />
<Example fr="Le déménagement est prévu pour samedi." en="The move is planned for Saturday." />
<Example fr="Nous avons emménagé en septembre." en="We moved in in September." />

*La colocation* is very common among students and young workers in big French cities. *Un état des lieux* is done twice: when you move in, and when you move out. The landlord and the tenant check every room together and write down any problems, so the tenant isn't blamed later for damage that was already there.

*Emménager* is to move in, and its opposite *déménager* is to move out, or to move house in general.

## En français

Clara is visiting a flat with the landlord, Monsieur Petit.

- *Bonjour, monsieur. Je viens pour la visite de l'appartement.* (Hello. I've come for the viewing of the flat.)
- *Bonjour, madame. Entrez. Voici le salon. Il est très lumineux, vous voyez.* (Hello. Come in. Here's the living room. It's very bright, as you can see.)
- *C'est vrai. L'appartement est meublé ?* (That's true. Is the flat furnished?)
- *Oui, il y a un lit, une table et une grande armoire dans la chambre.* (Yes, there's a bed, a table and a big wardrobe in the bedroom.)
- *Très bien. Et quel est le loyer ?* (Very good. And what's the rent?)
- *Le loyer est de 700 euros, charges comprises. Le chauffage est dans les charges.* (The rent is 700 euros, charges included. The heating is in the charges.)
- *Et pour la caution ? Il faut un garant ?* (And the deposit? Do I need a guarantor?)
- *Oui, la caution est de 700 euros, et j'ai besoin d'un garant.* (Yes, the deposit is 700 euros, and I need a guarantor.)
- *Mes parents peuvent être garants. Je peux emménager quand ?* (My parents can be guarantors. When can I move in?)
- *Le premier du mois. On fera l'état des lieux ensemble ce jour-là.* (On the first of the month. We'll do the inventory check together that day.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Le loyer, not la rente">
  The rent you pay each month is **le loyer**: *Je paie mon loyer le 5 du mois*. *Une rente* exists in French, but it's a regular income you receive, from investments for example. If you tell a landlord you've paid *la rente*, they won't understand. It's always **payer le loyer**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Learn the ad codes">
  French flat ads are full of short codes. Learn four before you start looking: **T2** (two main rooms), **CC** (*charges comprises*), **HC** (*hors charges*, charges not included) and **m²** (the floor area). With these, you can compare ten ads in a few minutes and know exactly what each flat costs.
</Callout>
