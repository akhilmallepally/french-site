By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say what kind of film or book something is, who made it, what it's about and how it made you feel.

## Films

You know *un film* and *le cinéma*. Here are the main kinds of film.

<VocabTable group="films" />

<Example fr="J'adore les comédies, mais je déteste les films d'horreur." en="I love comedies, but I hate horror films." />
<Example fr="Ce soir, il y a un documentaire sur les animaux." en="Tonight, there's a documentary about animals." />

*Policier* is an adjective here, so it agrees: *un film policier*, *des films policiers*. On its own, *un policier* is a police officer, as in <LessonLink id="a2-jobs-and-workplace" text="the lesson on jobs and the workplace" />, and *un polar* is an informal word for a crime film or novel.

## Books

<VocabTable group="books" />

<Example fr="Je lis un roman policier de Simenon." en="I'm reading a crime novel by Simenon." />
<Example fr="En France, les bandes dessinées sont pour tous les âges." en="In France, comics are for all ages." />

To say who made something, use *de*: *un film de Truffaut*, *un roman de Simenon*. It's the simplest way, and you'll hear it all the time.

## The people

<VocabTable group="people" />

<Example fr="La réalisatrice est très jeune." en="The director is very young." />
<Example fr="La distribution est très bonne." en="The cast is very good." />
<Example fr="Le personnage principal est une femme de quarante ans." en="The main character is a forty-year-old woman." />

*Un personnage* is always masculine, even when the character is a woman. *Autrice* is the usual feminine of *auteur* today, though you'll also see *auteure*.

## The story

<VocabTable group="story" />

Two phrases do most of the work when you describe a story. **Ça se passe** gives the setting, with *à* for a town, *en* for a country or a year. **Ça parle de** gives the subject.

<Example fr="Ça se passe à Marseille, en 1990." en="It's set in Marseille, in 1990." />
<Example fr="Ça parle d'une famille qui déménage au Québec." en="It's about a family who move to Québec." />
<Example fr="La dernière scène est incroyable." en="The last scene is amazing." />

## Describing it

Add these to the words from <LessonLink id="b1-expressing-opinions" text="the lesson on expressing opinions" />: *génial*, *ennuyeux*, *original*.

<VocabTable group="describing" />

<Example fr="C'est une histoire très émouvante." en="It's a very moving story." />
<Example fr="Le roman est passionnant, mais un peu long." en="The novel is gripping, but a bit long." />

## Watching

<VocabTable group="watching" />

In French cinemas, foreign films are shown either in *VO*, with French subtitles, or in *VF*, *version française*, dubbed into French.

<Example fr="Je préfère voir les films en VO, avec des sous-titres." en="I prefer to see films in the original language, with subtitles." />

## Talking about what you're watching

These everyday sentences come up whenever people talk about films and books. They use structures you already know.

<Example fr="Je suis en train de lire un roman de Simenon." en="I'm reading a novel by Simenon at the moment." />
<Example fr="Tu as vu le dernier film de Claire Dubois ?" en="Have you seen Claire Dubois's latest film?" />
<Example fr="J'ai lu cette BD trois fois : je l'adore." en="I've read this comic book three times: I love it." />
<Example fr="On regarde un film ce soir ? Tu choisis !" en="Shall we watch a film tonight? You choose!" />

*Le dernier film* here means "the latest film", the most recent one, not the last one the director will ever make.

## En français

Two friends are choosing a film at the cinema.

- *Qu'est-ce qu'on voit ce soir ? Il y a une comédie et un film policier.* (What shall we see tonight? There's a comedy and a crime film.)
- *Et la comédie, qu'est-ce que c'est ?* (And what's the comedy?)
- *Ça parle d'un couple qui gagne au loto. Ça se passe à Nice.* (It's about a couple who win the lottery. It's set in Nice.)
- *Et le film policier ?* (And the crime film?)
- *C'est un film de Claire Dubois. La distribution est géniale.* (It's a film by Claire Dubois. The cast is brilliant.)
- *Il est en VO ?* (Is it in the original language?)
- *Oui, il est en anglais, avec des sous-titres.* (Yes, it's in English, with subtitles.)
- *Je préfère la comédie. Je n'ai pas envie de lire les sous-titres ce soir.* (I prefer the comedy. I don't feel like reading subtitles tonight.)
- *D'accord. Mais si elle est ennuyeuse, tu paies le dîner !* (OK. But if it's boring, you're paying for dinner!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Un roman isn't a romance">
  **Un roman** is any novel: a crime novel, a science-fiction novel, a family saga. It has nothing to do with love. A love story is **une histoire d'amour**, and a romantic film is **un film romantique**. In the same way, *une BD* is a comic book on paper, while *un dessin animé* is a cartoon on screen.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="The one-breath description">
  Learn one sentence pattern that describes any film or book: *C'est un [genre] de [name]. Ça se passe à [place]. Ça parle de [subject].* Use it for the last three films you saw. Once you can say it without stopping, you have the start of every conversation about a film.
</Callout>
