By the end of this lesson you'll be able to read a French job ad, talk about contracts and pay, and say what happens when people get, change or lose a job.

## Job ads

In <LessonLink id="a2-jobs-and-workplace" text="the lesson on jobs and the workplace" />, you learned the names of jobs and places of work. Now it's time to find one.

<VocabTable group="ads" />

<Example fr="J'ai vu une offre d'emploi intéressante dans le journal." en="I saw an interesting job ad in the paper." />
<Example fr="Elle a postulé à trois postes cette semaine." en="She applied for three jobs this week." />
<Example fr="Il y a vingt candidats pour ce poste." en="There are twenty candidates for this job." />

*Postuler* is followed by *à*: *postuler à un poste*. You'll also hear *postuler pour*. *Un CV* is pronounced like the two letters: *say-vay*.

## Contracts and hours

French job ads always say what kind of contract is on offer.

<VocabTable group="contracts" />

<Example fr="C'est un CDI à temps plein." en="It's a permanent, full-time contract." />
<Example fr="J'ai un CDD de six mois." en="I've got a six-month contract." />
<Example fr="Le salaire est de 2 000 euros brut par mois." en="The salary is 2,000 euros gross a month." />

*Un CDI* (*contrat à durée indéterminée*) has no end date, and it's the contract most people hope for. *Un CDD* (*contrat à durée déterminée*) ends on a fixed date. You know *un stage* from <LessonLink id="a2-review-work" text="the review of work and studies" />: the person who does it is *un* or *une stagiaire*.

Salaries in France are usually given *brut*, before social contributions are taken off. The *net* figure, what you actually receive, is noticeably lower.

## Skills and training

<VocabTable group="skills" />

<Example fr="Vous avez de l'expérience dans la vente ?" en="Do you have any experience in sales?" />
<Example fr="Mes compétences : l'anglais, l'espagnol et le travail en équipe." en="My skills: English, Spanish and teamwork." />
<Example fr="J'aime travailler avec les clients." en="I enjoy working with customers." />
<Example fr="L'entreprise propose une formation de deux semaines." en="The company offers two weeks of training." />

## Getting, leaving and losing a job

<VocabTable group="hiring" />

<Example fr="Mon employeur m'a embauché en CDI." en="My employer hired me on a permanent contract." />
<Example fr="Après dix ans, elle a démissionné pour monter sa propre entreprise." en="After ten years, she resigned to set up her own business." />
<Example fr="L'usine a licencié cent personnes." en="The factory laid off a hundred people." />
<Example fr="Il est au chômage depuis trois mois." en="He's been unemployed for three months." />

*Démissionner* is your decision. *Être licencié* is your employer's decision.

## Talking about your job search

These sentences come up again and again when people talk about looking for work.

<Example fr="Je cherche du travail depuis deux mois." en="I've been looking for work for two months." />
<Example fr="J'ai envoyé ma candidature lundi." en="I sent my application on Monday." />
<Example fr="Une entreprise m'a appelé hier !" en="A company called me yesterday!" />
<Example fr="Ils m'ont embauché en CDD, mais j'espère avoir un CDI." en="They hired me on a fixed-term contract, but I hope to get a permanent one." />

*Chercher du travail* is the everyday phrase for looking for a job. If a company calls you, the next step is the interview, which you'll prepare for in <LessonLink id="b1-job-interview" text="the lesson on the job interview" />.

## A job ad

Here's a typical short job ad. Notice how it drops articles and verbs.

*Restaurant à Lyon cherche serveur ou serveuse. CDI à temps plein. Expérience demandée : deux ans. Compétences : anglais, travail en équipe. Salaire : 1 900 euros brut. Envoyez votre CV et votre candidature à Madame Dubois.*

(Restaurant in Lyon is looking for a waiter or waitress. Permanent, full-time contract. Experience required: two years. Skills: English, teamwork. Salary: 1,900 euros gross. Send your CV and your application to Madame Dubois.)

## En français

Two friends are reading job ads on a website.

- *Regarde cette annonce : « Hôtel à Nice cherche serveur ».* (Look at this ad: "Hotel in Nice seeks waiter".)
- *C'est un CDI ?* (Is it a permanent contract?)
- *Non, c'est un CDD de six mois, à temps plein.* (No, it's a six-month contract, full-time.)
- *Et le salaire ?* (And the salary?)
- *1 800 euros brut. Ce n'est pas beaucoup.* (1,800 euros gross. That's not much.)
- *Ils demandent de l'expérience ?* (Do they ask for experience?)
- *Oui, un an. Et il faut parler anglais.* (Yes, a year. And you have to speak English.)
- *Tu as de l'expérience, et ton anglais est très bon. Tu devrais postuler !* (You have experience, and your English is very good. You should apply!)
- *D'accord, je vais envoyer mon CV ce soir.* (OK, I'll send my CV tonight.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Le poste or la poste">
  The gender changes the meaning. **Un poste** (masculine) is a job: *J'ai un nouveau poste.* **La poste** (feminine) is the post office: *Je vais à la poste.* When you read a job ad or talk about work, check the article, because *le poste* is almost always the one you want.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Read real job ads">
  Spend ten minutes on a French job website, even if you aren't looking for work. Ads are short, they repeat the same words (*CDI*, *temps plein*, *expérience demandée*), and they show you which skills employers ask for in your own field. Note down five words you see more than once.
</Callout>
