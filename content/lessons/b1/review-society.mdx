## A model argued text

Pupils at a lycée have written this text for the school website, to present their petition.

*Non au plastique à la cantine !*

*Dans notre lycée, plus de 500 repas sont servis chaque jour à la cantine. Avec chaque repas, il y a une bouteille d'eau en plastique à usage unique. Ces bouteilles jetables sont mises à la poubelle après dix minutes. C'est pourquoi nous sommes pour l'interdiction du plastique à usage unique à la cantine.*

*Premièrement, le plastique pollue la planète : il y en a de plus en plus dans la mer. Deuxièmement, c'est du gaspillage, car une bouteille en verre peut servir cent fois.*

*Il est vrai que le plastique est pratique et pas cher, mais il coûte cher à la planète.*

*Nous voulons agir, et aussi sensibiliser les autres élèves. Notre pétition a déjà été signée par 300 élèves et professeurs. En résumé : moins de plastique, c'est mieux pour tout le monde. Signez, vous aussi !*

(No to plastic in the canteen! In our school, more than 500 meals are served every day in the canteen. With each meal, there's a single-use plastic bottle of water. These disposable bottles are put in the bin after ten minutes. That's why we're in favour of banning single-use plastic in the canteen. Firstly, plastic pollutes the planet: there's more and more of it in the sea. Secondly, it's wasteful, because a glass bottle can be used a hundred times. It's true that plastic is practical and cheap, but it costs the planet dearly. We want to act, and also to raise awareness among the other pupils. Our petition has already been signed by 300 pupils and teachers. In summary: less plastic is better for everyone. Sign it too!)

## How the text uses the grammar

- In *500 repas sont servis* and *notre pétition a été signée par 300 élèves*, the passive puts the result first. *Signée* agrees with *la pétition*.
- In *de plus en plus dans la mer*, a trend phrase shows that the problem is growing.
- In *c'est pourquoi* and *car*, cause and consequence link the facts to the demand.
- In *il est vrai que…, mais…*, the pupils accept the other side's best point and then answer it.
- The text follows the plan of an argument: the situation, two ordered arguments, the other side, and *en résumé* for the conclusion.

Writing a petition is also part of being a *citoyen*: it's a way for citizens to ask *le maire* or a school to act.

## Your turn

Choose an environmental issue in your town, your school or your workplace, and write two pieces, on paper or in a notes app.

1. **An argued text** of eight to ten sentences, with a title, the situation, two arguments with *premièrement* and *deuxièmement*, one *il est vrai que…, mais…* and a conclusion with *en résumé*.
2. **One sentence for your petition**, in the passive: *Notre pétition a été signée par…*

Try to use one trend phrase (*de plus en plus de*), one cause (*car*, *grâce à* or *à cause de*) and one consequence (*donc* or *c'est pourquoi*). Keep each argument to one or two sentences: a short, clear text convinces more readers than a long one.

## Check your work

- After *de plus en plus* and *de moins en moins*, did you write *de* on its own before a noun?
- Did you use *à cause de* and *grâce à* only before a noun, and *parce que* or *car* before a sentence?
- In each passive, does the participle agree with the subject, and is it *a été* in the passé composé?
- After *il est vrai que*, is the verb in the indicative?

<Callout type="warning" title="Agir is like finir">
  *Agir* is a regular *-ir* verb, conjugated like *finir*. It's **nous agissons** and **ils agissent**, not *nous agons* or *ils agent*. The same pattern gives **il faut que nous agissions** in the subjunctive. A petition that calls people to act should get this verb right.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Write for a real reader">
  When you write an argued text, choose one real reader: your mayor, your head teacher, your manager. Picture that person reading it, and choose *tu* or *vous*, and every argument, for them. A text with a clear reader is easier to write, and it sounds far more convincing than one written for nobody.
</Callout>
