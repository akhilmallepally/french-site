## Which past?

A story in French can use five different ways of talking about the past. Each one has its own job.

| Form | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| imparfait | sets the scene | *Il pleuvait.* |
| passé composé | moves the story on | *Je suis sorti.* |
| plus-que-parfait | goes back further | *J'avais oublié mon parapluie.* |
| *j'allais* + infinitive | was about to | *J'allais rentrer…* |
| *je venais de* + infinitive | had just | *Je venais de partir…* |

A summary of a film or book is different: it's in the present, as you saw in the review lesson.

When you write an anecdote, check each verb against this table. In speech, people mix the forms freely, and their voice makes the order clear. In writing, the reader only has the tenses, so give each one its job. If two events happen one after the other, the passé composé is enough: *Je suis sorti, puis j'ai pris le bus.* Use the plus-que-parfait only when you step back in time.

## A model anecdote

*Un voyage inoubliable*

*L'été dernier, nous allions partir en Italie. Dès le début, tout est allé mal. Le jour du voyage, je me suis rendu compte que j'avais oublié mon billet chez ma mère. Nous sommes retournés chez elle en voiture. Au moment où nous sommes arrivés à l'aéroport, l'avion venait de partir ! Et là, figure-toi qu'il y avait un autre vol pour Rome… à minuit. Bref, nous sommes arrivés à trois heures du matin. Au final, c'est le moment fort de nos vacances : à la fin du voyage, nous avons eu un fou rire dans l'avion.*

(An unforgettable trip. Last summer, we were about to leave for Italy. From the start, everything went wrong. On the day of the trip, I realized I had forgotten my ticket at my mother's. We went back to her place by car. Just as we arrived at the airport, the plane had just left! And then, guess what, there was another flight to Rome… at midnight. Anyway, we arrived at three in the morning. In the end, it was the highlight of our holiday: at the end of the trip, we got the giggles on the plane.)

## A model review

*Les Quatre Cents Coups*, de François Truffaut ★★★★★

*Ça se passe à Paris, à la fin des années 1950. Ça parle d'Antoine, un jeune garçon qui ne s'entend pas bien avec ses parents. Dès le début, l'histoire est émouvante. Il n'y a pas beaucoup de rebondissements, mais ce qui m'a plu, c'est le jeu du jeune acteur. Le moment fort, c'est la fin, au bord de la mer, mais je ne vais pas tout raconter ! C'est un récit simple et inoubliable, à voir absolument.*

(*The 400 Blows*, by François Truffaut, five stars. It's set in Paris, at the end of the 1950s. It's about Antoine, a young boy who doesn't get on with his parents. From the start, the story is moving. There aren't many twists, but what I liked was the young actor's performance. The highlight is the ending, by the sea, but I'm not going to give everything away! It's a simple, unforgettable story, a must-see.)

## Your turn

Write two short pieces, on paper or in a notes app.

1. **An anecdote**, eight to ten sentences, about a day when something went wrong. Use the imparfait for the scene, the passé composé for the events, and at least one plus-que-parfait. Add *j'allais* or *je venais de* once, and finish with *bref* or *au final*.
2. **A review** of a film or book you know, six to eight sentences. Present it, summarise it in the present, give your opinion with *ce qui m'a plu*, and end with a recommendation and a number of stars.

## Check your work

- In the anecdote, does every scene-setting verb use the imparfait?
- Did you use the plus-que-parfait for something that happened before the main events?
- Did you link events with at least two connectors, such as *dès que* or *au moment où*?
- In the review, is the summary in the present?
- Did you keep the ending of the film secret?

<Callout type="warning" title="Two kinds of 'at the end'">
  *À la fin* means "at the end": *À la fin du film, il pleut.* *Au final* (from the anecdote lesson) means "in the end, all things considered": *Au final, c'était une bonne journée.* And *finalement* can mean either "finally, at last" or "in the end". Don't use *à la fin* to sum up how things turned out.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Read it aloud">
  Once you've written your anecdote, read it aloud as if you were telling it to a friend. Wherever you run out of breath, the sentence is too long: split it. Wherever it sounds flat, add *et là* or a reaction. A story that sounds good aloud almost always reads well too.
</Callout>
