By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say "this one", "that one", "the one who…" and "Paul's" without repeating the noun.

## From ce livre to celui-ci

You know *ce*, *cette* and *ces* from <LessonLink id="a1-demonstratives" text="the lesson on demonstratives" />: *ce livre*, *cette robe*. The demonstrative pronouns replace the whole noun. They agree with it in gender and number.

<VocabTable group="forms" />

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | *celui* | *celle* |
| Plural | *ceux* | *celles* |

## This one, that one

Add *-ci* or *-là* to point to things.

<Example fr="Tu préfères ce téléphone ? Non, je préfère celui-là." en="Do you prefer this phone? No, I prefer that one." />
<Example fr="Ces chaussures sont jolies, mais celles-ci sont moins chères." en="These shoes are pretty, but these ones are cheaper." />

In careful French, *-ci* is the nearer one and *-là* the further one. In everyday speech, people often use *celui-là* for both, and point. When you compare two things in the same sentence, though, keep both: *celui-ci est plus cher que celui-là*.

## The one who, the one that

*Celui*, *celle*, *ceux* and *celles* can't stand on their own. If you don't add *-ci* or *-là*, they need *qui*, *que*, *où* or *dont* after them. The choice works exactly as in <LessonLink id="a2-relative-qui-que-ou" text="the lesson on qui, que and où" /> and <LessonLink id="b1-relative-dont" text="the lesson on dont" />.

<Example fr="Celui qui arrive le premier gagne." en="The one who arrives first wins." />
<Example fr="La robe que je préfère, c'est celle que tu portes." en="The dress I prefer is the one you're wearing." />
<Example fr="Le restaurant où on va, c'est celui où on a dîné l'année dernière." en="The restaurant we're going to is the one where we had dinner last year." />
<Example fr="Le livre dont je parle, c'est celui dont tout le bureau parle." en="The book I'm talking about is the one the whole office is talking about." />

## Paul's: celui de

*Celui de* + a person says whose something is, without repeating the noun. It's the French for "Paul's", "my sister's", "the director's".

<Example fr="Ce n'est pas mon livre, c'est celui de Marie." en="It's not my book, it's Marie's." />
<Example fr="Ma voiture est blanche. Celle de mon frère est noire." en="My car is white. My brother's is black." />
<Example fr="Les fraises du marché sont meilleures que celles du supermarché." en="The strawberries from the market are better than the supermarket ones." />

## Ça or celui?

*Ça* refers to an idea or a thing you don't name. *Celui* refers back to a noun you've already said, and agrees with it. If you can point and say the noun, use *celui* or *celle*. If you mean "that" in general, or the whole situation, use *ça*.

<Example fr="Ça, c'est une bonne idée." en="That's a good idea." />
<Example fr="Quelle veste ? Celle de Paul." en="Which jacket? Paul's." />

## Choosing in a shop

<VocabTable group="shop" />

<Example fr="Ce modèle est plus puissant que celui-là." en="This model is more powerful than that one." />
<Example fr="L'abonnement que je préfère, c'est celui à 20 euros par mois." en="The subscription I prefer is the 20-euros-a-month one." />
<Example fr="Cette version est plus légère que l'autre." en="This version is lighter than the other one." />

## En français

Two friends are choosing a new phone in a shop.

- *Tu préfères celui-ci ou celui-là ?* (Do you prefer this one or that one?)
- *Celui-ci est plus léger, mais celui-là est plus puissant.* (This one is lighter, but that one is more powerful.)
- *Et le tarif ?* (And the price?)
- *Celui-ci coûte 300 euros, et celui-là 450.* (This one costs 300 euros, and that one 450.)
- *C'est le même modèle que celui de ta sœur ?* (Is it the same model as your sister's?)
- *Non, le sien est une vieille version.* (No, hers is an old version.)
- *Et pour l'abonnement ?* (And for the subscription?)
- *Je garde celui que j'ai déjà. Il est moins cher.* (I'm keeping the one I've already got. It's cheaper.)
- *Bon. Alors je prends celui-ci, le léger !* (Right. Then I'll take this one, the light one!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Celui needs company">
  *Celui*, *celle*, *ceux* and *celles* never stand alone at the end of a sentence. Something must follow them: *-ci*, *-là*, *de* + a noun, or *qui*, *que*, *où*, *dont*. **Je préfère celui-ci**, **celui de Paul** or **celui que tu as**, but never *je préfère celui*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Shop in French in your head">
  Next time you're choosing between two things in a shop, say the choice to yourself in French: *Celui-ci est plus cher, mais celui-là est plus joli.* Then decide which *celui* you'd buy, and why. It turns any shopping trip into five minutes of practice.
</Callout>
