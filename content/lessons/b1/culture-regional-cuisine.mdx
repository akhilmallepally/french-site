By the end of this lesson you'll be able to talk about some of France's regional dishes, its cheese and wine culture, and why food matters so much in French life.

## Talking about regional food

You know the cooking words from <LessonLink id="b1-food-and-cooking" text="the lesson on food and cooking" />. Here are the words for talking about the food of a place.

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="La choucroute est une spécialité d'Alsace." en="Choucroute is a speciality of Alsace." />
<Example fr="La Bretagne est célèbre pour ses crêpes." en="Brittany is famous for its crêpes." />

France is a country of regions, and each one is proud of its own dishes. With *une spécialité de*, the region usually has no article: *une spécialité de Provence*, *une spécialité d'Alsace*. With a city, there's never an article: *une spécialité de Lyon*.

## Four regions, four tables

<VocabTable group="dishes" />

**L'Alsace**, in the east, near Germany, is known for *la choucroute*: sauerkraut cooked with sausages, pork and potatoes. It's a hearty winter dish, often served with a white wine from Alsace or a beer.

**La Provence**, in the south-east, cooks with olive oil, tomatoes, garlic and herbs. *La ratatouille* is a stew of summer vegetables. Near the coast, in Marseille, the famous dish is *la bouillabaisse*, a rich soup made with several kinds of fish.

**La Bretagne**, in the west, is the land of crêpes. There, *une galette de sarrasin* is the savoury pancake, made with buckwheat flour and filled with cheese, ham or egg. *Une crêpe* is usually the sweet one, made with wheat flour. Both go well with *le cidre*, the local apple drink.

**Lyon** is often called the capital of French gastronomy. Its traditional restaurants are called *les bouchons*. They serve generous, simple dishes, often with a lot of meat, in a friendly, noisy atmosphere.

## Cheese, wine and the terroir

<VocabTable group="land" />

**Le terroir** is a key idea in French food culture. It means the local land, the climate and the traditions together, and the idea that a product tastes of the place it comes from.

**Le fromage**: France has hundreds of cheeses, and many regions have their own. Some, like *le roquefort* or *le comté*, are protected by **l'AOP**, a label which guarantees that the product comes from a specific area and is made in the traditional way.

**Le vin**: France has many famous *vignobles*, such as Bordeaux, Burgundy and Champagne. Wine regions often offer *des dégustations*, where you can taste the local wines.

<Example fr="Ce fromage est un produit local, avec une AOP." en="This cheese is a local product, with an AOP label." />
<Example fr="On a fait une dégustation de vins dans un vignoble." en="We did a wine tasting at a vineyard." />

## The French meal

**Le repas gastronomique des Français**, the French gastronomic meal, was added to UNESCO's list of the world's intangible cultural heritage in 2010. It isn't one dish, but a way of eating together: several courses, good products, wine chosen for the food, and time to talk around the table. You saw how French meals are organized in <LessonLink id="a1-culture-french-meals" text="the lesson on meals in France" />.

## En français

A tourist in Lyon asks a waiter about a *bouchon*.

- *Bonjour. C'est la première fois que je viens dans un bouchon. Qu'est-ce qu'on mange ici ?* (Hello. It's my first time in a bouchon. What do people eat here?)
- *Bonjour, madame. Des spécialités lyonnaises : des plats simples, avec beaucoup de viande.* (Hello. Lyon specialities: simple dishes, with a lot of meat.)
- *Il y a un plat typique que vous me conseillez ?* (Is there a typical dish you'd recommend?)
- *La salade lyonnaise, avec des œufs et du pain. C'est très bon.* (The Lyon salad, with eggs and bread. It's very good.)
- *Parfait. Et comme vin ?* (Perfect. And for wine?)
- *Un vin de la région. Le Beaujolais est un vignoble tout près de Lyon.* (A local wine. Beaujolais is a wine region very close to Lyon.)
- *Très bien. Et pour finir, il y a du fromage ?* (Very good. And to finish, is there any cheese?)
- *Bien sûr. Nous avons des fromages de la région, tous des produits locaux.* (Of course. We have cheeses from the region, all local products.)
- *Super. Je vais goûter un peu de tout !* (Great. I'm going to taste a bit of everything!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Galette or crêpe?">
  In Brittany, the two words aren't the same. For a savoury pancake, with cheese or ham, ask for **une galette** (made with buckwheat). For a sweet one, with sugar or chocolate, ask for **une crêpe**. Outside Brittany, people often say *une crêpe salée* for the savoury kind, but in a Breton *crêperie*, **une galette** is the right word.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Read the menu outside">
  French restaurants usually post their menu outside, by the door. Before you go in, stop and read it: find *la spécialité de la maison*, the regional dishes, and one word you don't know. You'll order more confidently, and every walk through a French town becomes a short reading lesson.
</Callout>
