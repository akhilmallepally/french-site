By the end of this lesson you'll be able to replace two nouns at once, as in *je te l'envoie* and *je le lui ai donné*, with the pronouns in the right order.

## Two pronouns at once

You know each pronoun on its own: *le*, *la*, *les* for a direct object, from <LessonLink id="a2-direct-object-pronouns" text="the lesson on direct object pronouns" />, *lui* and *leur* for an indirect one, and *y* and *en*. When a sentence has both a direct object and an indirect object, you can replace both. The two pronouns go before the verb, in a fixed order.

<Example fr="Je donne le dossier à Marie. → Je le lui donne." en="I give the file to Marie. → I give it to her." />
<Example fr="Tu m'envoies le document ? → Tu me l'envoies ?" en="Will you send me the document? → Will you send it to me?" />

## The order

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *me, te, se, nous, vous* | *le, la, les* | *lui, leur* | *y* | *en* |

Take one pronoun from any two columns, keeping the left one first. *Me*, *te*, *nous* and *vous* come before *le*, but *le* comes before *lui* and *leur*.

<VocabTable group="pairs" />

<Example fr="Il me la prête." en="He lends it to me." />
<Example fr="Je vous les envoie demain." en="I'll send them to you tomorrow." />
<Example fr="Elle le lui montre." en="She shows it to him." />
<Example fr="Nous la leur montrons." en="We show it to them." />

You met one of these in <LessonLink id="b1-reviewing-a-film-or-book" text="the lesson on reviewing a film or book" />: *Je vous le conseille* (I recommend it to you) is *vous* (column 1) + *le* (column 2).

## With en

*En* always comes last. It's common with *lui*, *leur*, *m'* and *t'*, and in the fixed *il y en a*.

<VocabTable group="en" />

<Example fr="Tu as des stylos ? Tu peux m'en prêter un ?" en="Have you got any pens? Can you lend me one?" />
<Example fr="Il donne du chocolat aux enfants. Il leur en donne." en="He gives the children chocolate. He gives them some." />
<Example fr="Il y a du café ? Oui, il y en a." en="Is there any coffee? Yes, there is some." />

## In the negative, the past and with an infinitive

The pair moves as a block. In the negative, *ne* comes before both pronouns. In the passé composé, both go before the auxiliary, and the participle agrees with a direct object pronoun placed before it, as in <LessonLink id="a2-pronouns-in-passe-compose" text="the lesson on pronouns in the passé composé" />.

<Example fr="Je ne te le dis pas." en="I'm not telling you (it)." />
<Example fr="La lettre ? Je la lui ai envoyée hier." en="The letter? I sent it to him yesterday." />
<Example fr="Je vais te le prêter." en="I'm going to lend it to you." />

## In commands

In <LessonLink id="a2-imperative-with-pronouns" text="the lesson on the imperative with pronouns" />, you learned positive commands, where the pronouns follow the verb and the order changes: *Donne-le-moi !* In negative commands, the normal order comes back: *Ne me le donne pas !*

## En français

Two colleagues are sorting out some documents.

- *Tu as le dossier de Marc ?* (Have you got Marc's file?)
- *Oui. Tu veux que je te l'envoie ?* (Yes. Do you want me to send it to you?)
- *Oui, s'il te plaît. Tu me l'envoies en pièce jointe ?* (Yes, please. Will you send it to me as an attachment?)
- *Je te l'ai déjà envoyé ce matin !* (I already sent it to you this morning!)
- *Ah, pardon. Et les photos pour la directrice ?* (Oh, sorry. And the photos for the director?)
- *Je les lui ai données hier.* (I gave them to her yesterday.)
- *Super. Et tu as des stylos ? Je n'en ai pas.* (Great. And have you got any pens? I haven't got any.)
- *Oui, il y en a sur mon bureau. Je t'en prête un ?* (Yes, there are some on my desk. Shall I lend you one?)
- *Merci. Je te le rends demain !* (Thanks. I'll give it back tomorrow!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Le before lui, but me before le">
  The order isn't always "direct, then indirect". With *lui* and *leur*, the direct pronoun comes first: **je le lui donne**. With *me*, *te*, *nous* and *vous*, it comes second: **je te le donne**. *Je lui le donne* and *je le te donne* are both wrong. If in doubt, check the table above.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Learn three sample sentences">
  Instead of memorizing five columns, learn three sentences that cover them: *Je te le donne. Je le lui donne. Je lui en donne.* When you need a new pair, compare it with the closest of the three. Most combinations you'll need in conversation follow one of these patterns.
</Callout>
