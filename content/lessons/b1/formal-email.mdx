By the end of this lesson you'll be able to write a clear, polite formal email in French, to a school, a company or an office, and follow it up if nobody answers.

## Formal or informal?

You know how to write to friends from <LessonLink id="a2-writing-an-informal-email" text="the lesson on writing an informal email" />, and you know the formal register from <LessonLink id="b1-cover-letter" text="the lesson on CVs and cover letters" />. A formal email sits between the two. It uses *vous* all the way through, full sentences and no abbreviations, but it's shorter and a little less ceremonial than a letter.

Use the formal register for anyone you don't know personally, and for anyone in an official role: a school, a bank, a landlord, a company's customer service.

## The parts of a formal email

<VocabTable group="parts" />

A formal email has five parts, always in the same order.

1. **L'objet.** Short and precise: *Demande d'informations sur les cours du soir*.
2. **The greeting.** *Madame, Monsieur,* when you don't know who'll read it. If you know the name, *Bonjour Madame Martin,* is fine in most emails, and *Madame,* or *Monsieur,* alone is more formal.
3. **The purpose.** One sentence saying why you're writing: *Je souhaiterais avoir des informations sur…*
4. **The request.** What you'd like the reader to do, politely.
5. **The closing.** A thank-you and *une formule de politesse*, then your full name.

## Making a request

<VocabTable group="request" />

You know *Pourriez-vous… ?* from <LessonLink id="a2-polite-requests" text="the lesson on polite requests" />. *Je vous serais reconnaissant de…* (I would be grateful if you could…) is a step more formal. It uses the conditional of *être*, then *de* + an infinitive. A woman writes *reconnaissante*.

<Example fr="Pourriez-vous m'envoyer les horaires des cours ?" en="Could you send me the class times?" />
<Example fr="Je vous serais reconnaissante de me répondre avant vendredi." en="I would be grateful if you could reply before Friday." />

*Cordialement* and *Bien cordialement* are the usual closings for emails. *Je vous prie d'agréer…*, which you saw in the cover letter lesson, belongs to letters, and it sounds too heavy in most emails. Put *En vous remerciant par avance,* on its own line just before the closing.

## Following up

<VocabTable group="follow" />

If you don't get an answer, wait about a week, then send *une relance*. Mention your first message and stay polite.

<Example fr="Je me permets de revenir vers vous suite à mon mail du 3 mars." en="I'm writing again following my email of 3 March." />
<Example fr="Suite à notre conversation de ce matin, je vous envoie le document." en="Following our conversation this morning, I'm sending you the document." />

*Revenir vers vous* is everywhere in business emails, though some people find it a little heavy. *Suite à* is very common in emails, and it's always followed by *à*: *suite à votre demande*.

## Attachments and copies

<VocabTable group="attach" />

*Veuillez* is a very formal "please", used in writing, followed by an infinitive. It comes from *vouloir*, but you don't need its grammar: learn it as part of fixed phrases. *Veuillez trouver ci-joint…* (Please find attached…), *Veuillez m'excuser* (Please excuse me) and *Veuillez agréer…*, the letter closing you've already seen.

<Example fr="Vous trouverez ci-joint mon CV." en="Please find attached my CV." />
<Example fr="Veuillez trouver ci-joint le document." en="Please find attached the document." />
<Example fr="Je transfère votre mail à ma collègue, et je vous mets en copie." en="I'm forwarding your email to my colleague, and copying you in." />

## En français

Romain has to write to a new client for the first time, and asks his colleague Sonia for advice.

- *Sonia, j'écris à un nouveau client. Je commence par « Bonjour » ?* (Sonia, I'm writing to a new client. Do I start with "Bonjour"?)
- *Tu connais son nom ?* (Do you know his name?)
- *Oui, c'est Monsieur Garnier.* (Yes, it's Mr Garnier.)
- *Alors « Bonjour Monsieur Garnier », c'est parfait. Et tu lui dis « vous », bien sûr.* (Then "Bonjour Monsieur Garnier" is perfect. And you say vous to him, of course.)
- *Et pour la fin ? « Je vous prie d'agréer… » ?* (And for the end? "Je vous prie d'agréer…"?)
- *Non, c'est pour les lettres. Dans un mail, écris « Bien cordialement ».* (No, that's for letters. In an email, write "Bien cordialement".)
- *D'accord. Je dois mettre notre patronne en copie ?* (OK. Should I copy our boss in?)
- *Oui, mets-la en copie. Et n'oublie pas l'objet !* (Yes, copy her in. And don't forget the subject line!)
- *Merci, Sonia. Je t'envoie le mail avant de l'envoyer au client ?* (Thanks, Sonia. Shall I send you the email before I send it to the client?)
- *Bonne idée. Envoie-le-moi, je le lis tout de suite.* (Good idea. Send it to me, I'll read it straight away.)

## An email and its follow-up

Sophie wants to take Italian classes, and writes to a language school.

*Objet : Demande d'informations sur les cours d'italien*

*Bonjour Madame Martin,*

*Je souhaiterais prendre des cours d'italien le soir dans votre école. Pourriez-vous m'envoyer les horaires et le prix des cours ? Je vous serais aussi reconnaissante de m'indiquer s'il y a un test de niveau.*

*Vous trouverez ci-joint mes horaires de travail.*

*En vous remerciant par avance,*

*Bien cordialement,*

*Sophie Laurent*

A week later, she has no answer, so she sends a follow-up.

*Objet : Relance : demande d'informations sur les cours d'italien*

*Bonjour Madame Martin,*

*Je me permets de revenir vers vous suite à mon mail du 3 mars. Les cours commencent bientôt : pourriez-vous me répondre avant la fin de la semaine ? Veuillez trouver ci-joint mon premier mail.*

*Bien cordialement,*

*Sophie Laurent*

(Subject: Request for information about Italian classes. Dear Ms Martin, I would like to take Italian classes in the evening at your school. Could you send me the times and the price of the classes? I would also be grateful if you could tell me whether there's a level test. Please find attached my working hours. Thanking you in advance, Kind regards, Sophie Laurent. Subject: Reminder: request for information about Italian classes. Dear Ms Martin, I am writing again following my email of 3 March. The classes start soon: could you reply to me before the end of the week? Please find attached my first email. Kind regards, Sophie Laurent.)

<Callout type="warning" title="No s'il vous plaît before ci-joint">
  English says "Please find attached", but French doesn't translate the "please" with *s'il vous plaît*. Write **Vous trouverez ci-joint…** or **Veuillez trouver ci-joint…**, never *S'il vous plaît trouvez ci-joint…*. In formal writing, *veuillez* does the job of "please".
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="The ten-second test">
  Before you press send, read only *l'objet* and your first sentence, and time yourself. In ten seconds, could a stranger say who you are and what you want? If not, move your request higher, or make the subject line more precise: *Demande de remboursement*, not *Question*. Busy offices answer clear emails first.
</Callout>
