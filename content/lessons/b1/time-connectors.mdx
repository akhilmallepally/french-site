By the end of this lesson you'll be able to link the events of a story in time, and say what happened before, during and after something else.

## When: quand, lorsque, au moment où

You know *quand*. **Lorsque** means the same, but it's more formal, and you'll see it more in writing than hear it in conversation. **Au moment où** points to the exact moment.

<VocabTable group="when" />

<Example fr="Lorsque je suis arrivé, tout le bureau était parti." en="When I arrived, the whole office had left." />
<Example fr="Au moment où je suis sorti, il a commencé à pleuvoir." en="Just as I went out, it started to rain." />

## While: pendant que

*Pendant que* links two actions happening at the same time. Often, one is the background, in the imparfait, and the other is an event, in the passé composé.

<Example fr="Pendant que je prenais ma douche, Paul est arrivé." en="While I was having a shower, Paul arrived." />
<Example fr="Je lisais pendant que mon frère regardait la télé." en="I was reading while my brother was watching TV." />

Don't confuse *pendant que* with *pendant* + a noun, from <LessonLink id="a2-depuis-pendant-il-y-a" text="the lesson on depuis, pendant and il y a" />: *pendant deux heures* (for two hours).

## As soon as: dès que, aussitôt que

*Dès que* and *aussitôt que* mean the same thing. About the past, they take the passé composé. About the future, they take the futur, where English uses the present.

<Example fr="Dès que je suis arrivé, j'ai appelé Marie." en="As soon as I arrived, I called Marie." />
<Example fr="Dès que j'arriverai, je t'appellerai." en="As soon as I arrive, I'll call you." />
<Example fr="Aussitôt qu'il aura le temps, il viendra." en="As soon as he has time, he'll come." />

*Quand* follows the same rule: *Quand je serai grand, je serai médecin* (When I grow up, I'll be a doctor). This is the opposite of *si*, which never takes the futur, as you saw in <LessonLink id="b1-si-present-futur" text="the lesson on si with the present and future" />.

## Before and after

<VocabTable group="order" />

**Avant de** is followed by an infinitive. Use it when both actions have the same subject.

<Example fr="Avant de partir, j'ai fermé la porte à clé." en="Before leaving, I locked the door." />
<Example fr="Lave-toi les mains avant de manger." en="Wash your hands before eating." />

**Après** is followed by a noun: *après le dîner*, *après le film*. To say "after doing something", French uses *après avoir* + the past participle. For now, learn it as a fixed phrase with a few verbs that take *avoir*. You'll study the whole structure in <LessonLink id="b2-infinitif-passe" text="the lesson on the past infinitive" />.

<Example fr="Après avoir mangé, nous sommes sortis." en="After eating, we went out." />
<Example fr="Après avoir fini mes devoirs, j'ai regardé un film." en="After finishing my homework, I watched a film." />

**Jusqu'à** (until) and **à partir de** (from) mark the start and end of a period.

<Example fr="Le magasin est ouvert jusqu'à 19 heures." en="The shop is open until 7 pm." />
<Example fr="À partir de lundi, je travaille à Lyon." en="From Monday, I'm working in Lyon." />

## Moving the story on

These three words help you move a story forward, or tell your listener what happened in between.

<VocabTable group="story" />

<Example fr="Il a vu le bus et il est aussitôt sorti." en="He saw the bus and went out straight away." />
<Example fr="Entre-temps, Marie était arrivée." en="In the meantime, Marie had arrived." />
<Example fr="Finalement, on est restés à la maison." en="In the end, we stayed at home." />

## Before, during, after

| Order | Connector | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Before | *avant de* + infinitive | *Avant de sortir, j'ai mangé.* |
| At the same time | *pendant que*, *lorsque*, *au moment où* | *Pendant que je mangeais, il est arrivé.* |
| Straight after | *dès que*, *aussitôt que* | *Dès qu'il est arrivé, on est partis.* |
| After | *après* + noun, *après avoir* + participle | *Après avoir mangé, je suis sorti.* |

## En français

A colleague explains why she was so late this morning.

- *Ça va ? Tu es arrivée très tard au bureau.* (Are you OK? You got to the office very late.)
- *Quelle matinée ! Dès que je me suis levée, j'ai vu qu'il n'y avait plus de café.* (What a morning! As soon as I got up, I saw there was no coffee left.)
- *Oh non ! Et après ?* (Oh no! And then?)
- *Pendant que je prenais ma douche, l'eau est devenue froide.* (While I was having a shower, the water went cold.)
- *Et ensuite ?* (And then?)
- *Avant de partir, j'ai cherché mes clés pendant vingt minutes.* (Before leaving, I looked for my keys for twenty minutes.)
- *Tu les as trouvées ?* (Did you find them?)
- *Oui, finalement. Mais au moment où je suis sortie, il a commencé à pleuvoir !* (Yes, in the end. But just as I went out, it started to rain!)
- *Et entre-temps, ton bus était parti, je suppose ?* (And in the meantime, your bus had left, I suppose?)
- *Exactement. Demain, je reste au lit !* (Exactly. Tomorrow, I'm staying in bed!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Si takes the present, quand takes the futur">
  These two look alike, but they behave differently when you talk about the future. **S'il pleut demain, je resterai à la maison**, with the present after *si*. But **Quand j'arriverai, je t'appellerai**, with the futur after *quand*. The same goes for *dès que* and *aussitôt que*. In casual speech you'll hear *quand j'arrive, je t'appelle*, with the present in both halves, but don't mix a present after *quand* with a futur in the other half.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="A four-connector diary">
  Every evening for a week, write four sentences about your day, each with a different connector: one with *avant de*, one with *pendant que*, one with *dès que* and one with *finalement*. It takes two minutes, and by the end of the week the connectors will come to you without searching.
</Callout>
