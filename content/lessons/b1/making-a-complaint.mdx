By the end of this lesson you'll be able to complain politely but firmly, face to face and in a formal email, and ask for a repair, an exchange or a refund.

## Complaint words

<VocabTable group="complain" />

*Se plaindre* is reflexive and takes *de*: *Je me plains du bruit.* It's irregular: *je me plains*, *nous nous plaignons*. *Une plainte* is a complaint in general, and *porter plainte* means making an official complaint to the police. To a shop or a company, the word is *une réclamation*, and that's what *le service client* calls it.

<Example fr="Les locataires se plaignent du bruit." en="The tenants are complaining about the noise." />
<Example fr="J'ai fait une réclamation au service client." en="I made a complaint to customer service." />
<Example fr="Je suis très insatisfaite de votre service." en="I'm very dissatisfied with your service." />

## Saying what's wrong

<VocabTable group="problem" />

Start politely, with phrases you know from <LessonLink id="a2-polite-requests" text="the lesson on polite requests" />: *Excusez-moi de vous déranger, mais…* Then name the problem with *il y a un problème avec…*

<Example fr="Excusez-moi de vous déranger, mais il y a un problème avec le chauffage." en="Sorry to bother you, but there's a problem with the heating." />
<Example fr="L'ascenseur est en panne depuis lundi." en="The lift has been out of order since Monday." />
<Example fr="Il y a une fuite dans la salle de bains." en="There's a leak in the bathroom." />

*Ce n'est pas normal* is the standard French way to say that something isn't acceptable. It's firm without being rude. *Être en panne* is for machines and systems: the heating, a lift, a car, a cash machine.

## Asking for a solution

<VocabTable group="solution" />

Say clearly what you want. A polite but firm question uses the conditional, from <LessonLink id="b1-conditionnel-present" text="the lesson on the conditionnel présent" />: *Est-ce qu'il serait possible de…?* A stronger request uses *je voudrais que* + the subjunctive, from <LessonLink id="b1-subjonctif-wishes-emotions" text="the lesson on the subjunctive after wishes and emotions" />.

<Example fr="Est-ce qu'il serait possible de réparer le chauffage cette semaine ?" en="Would it be possible to repair the heating this week?" />
<Example fr="Je voudrais être remboursé." en="I'd like to get my money back." />
<Example fr="Je voudrais que vous répariez le chauffage rapidement." en="I'd like you to repair the heating quickly." />

In a shop, you usually choose between *un échange* (another item) and *un remboursement* (your money back). *Être remboursé* is the simple way to say you get your money back. *Rembourser* also means to pay back a loan, as you saw in <LessonLink id="b1-money-and-banking" text="the lesson on money and banking" />.

## Being firm

<VocabTable group="firm" />

If nothing happens, say what you'll do next, with *sinon* and *être obligé de*. Keep the tone calm: in French, a formal, polite complaint is usually more effective than an angry one.

<Example fr="Sinon, je serai obligé de contacter l'agence immobilière." en="Otherwise, I'll have to contact the letting agency." />
<Example fr="J'exige une réponse dans les plus brefs délais." en="I demand an answer as soon as possible." />

*Exiger* is strong, so keep it for the second complaint. *Dans les plus brefs délais* is formal written French; in speech, say *rapidement* or *le plus vite possible*.

## In writing

A written complaint is a formal email, with the format from <LessonLink id="b1-formal-email" text="the lesson on writing a formal email" />: *l'objet*, a greeting, the facts with dates, your request, the next step, and the closing. Give dates and facts, not feelings, and attach a photo if you can.

## En français

Clara phones her landlord, Monsieur Petit, about the heating.

- *Allô, Monsieur Petit ? C'est Clara Moreau, votre locataire. Excusez-moi de vous déranger, mais il y a un problème avec le chauffage.* (Hello, Mr Petit? It's Clara Moreau, your tenant. Sorry to bother you, but there's a problem with the heating.)
- *Bonjour, Madame Moreau. Qu'est-ce qui se passe ?* (Hello, Ms Moreau. What's happening?)
- *Il est en panne depuis hier soir. Il fait très froid dans l'appartement.* (It's been broken since last night. It's very cold in the flat.)
- *Je suis désolé. Il ne marche pas du tout ?* (I'm sorry. It doesn't work at all?)
- *Non, pas du tout. Et il y a aussi une petite fuite dans la salle de bains.* (No, not at all. And there's also a small leak in the bathroom.)
- *D'accord. Je peux envoyer quelqu'un lundi.* (OK. I can send someone on Monday.)
- *Lundi ? Ce n'est pas normal, il fait 15 degrés ! Est-ce qu'il serait possible de venir demain ?* (Monday? That's not acceptable, it's 15 degrees! Would it be possible to come tomorrow?)
- *Je vais essayer. Je vous téléphone ce soir.* (I'll try. I'll phone you this evening.)
- *Merci. Je voudrais vraiment que vous trouviez une solution rapidement.* (Thank you. I'd really like you to find a solution quickly.)
- *Bien sûr, madame. À ce soir.* (Of course. Speak to you this evening.)

## A complaint email

Nobody came, so Clara writes to the landlord.

*Objet : Chauffage en panne dans l'appartement*

*Bonjour Monsieur Petit,*

*Je vous écris parce que le chauffage de l'appartement est en panne depuis le 2 décembre, c'est-à-dire depuis une semaine. Je vous ai téléphoné le 3 décembre, mais personne n'est venu. Il fait 15 degrés dans l'appartement : ce n'est pas normal.*

*Il y a aussi une petite fuite dans la salle de bains. Vous trouverez ci-joint une photo.*

*Je vous serais reconnaissante d'envoyer quelqu'un pour la réparation dans les plus brefs délais. Sinon, je serai obligée de contacter l'agence immobilière.*

*En vous remerciant par avance,*

*Bien cordialement,*

*Clara Moreau*

(Subject: Heating out of order in the flat. Dear Mr Petit, I am writing because the heating in the flat has been out of order since 2 December, that is, for a week. I phoned you on 3 December, but nobody came. It is 15 degrees in the flat: this is not acceptable. There is also a small leak in the bathroom. Please find attached a photo. I would be grateful if you could send someone to do the repair as soon as possible. Otherwise, I will have to contact the letting agency. Thanking you in advance, Kind regards, Clara Moreau.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Se plaindre, not plaindre">
  To complain, you need the reflexive verb and *de*: **je me plains du bruit**, **elle s'est plainte du service**. Without the pronoun, *plaindre* means to feel sorry for someone: *je te plains* (I feel sorry for you). So *je plains le bruit* means you pity the noise.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Facts first">
  Before you complain, write down three things: the date the problem started, what you've already done, and what you want now. Put them in that order, in three short sentences. With those facts ready, you'll sound calm and precise, on the phone or in an email, and it's much harder for anyone to say no.
</Callout>
