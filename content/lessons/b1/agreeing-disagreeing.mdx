By the end of this lesson you'll be able to agree, disagree and half agree with someone, and choose how firm to sound.

## Agreeing

In <LessonLink id="b1-expressing-opinions" text="the lesson on expressing opinions" />, you learned to say what you think. Now it's time to answer someone else.

<VocabTable group="agree" />

<Example fr="Je suis d'accord avec toi." en="I agree with you." />
<Example fr="Tout à fait ! La ville est plus calme le dimanche." en="Absolutely! The town is quieter on Sundays." />
<Example fr="Je suis du même avis : le train est plus pratique." en="I see it the same way: the train is more practical." />

*Être d'accord* uses *être*, and *avec* introduces the person: *avec toi*, *avec vous*, *avec Paul*. To agree strongly, add *tout à fait* in front: *Je suis tout à fait d'accord.*

## Disagreeing

<VocabTable group="disagree" />

The negative is regular: *Je ne suis pas d'accord.* To make it firmer, add *du tout* after *pas*: *Je ne suis pas du tout d'accord* (I don't agree at all). *Pas du tout* also works on its own as a reply. *Au contraire* introduces the opposite opinion.

<Example fr="Je ne suis pas d'accord avec vous." en="I don't agree with you." />
<Example fr="Le musée est ennuyeux ? Au contraire, il est génial !" en="The museum is boring? On the contrary, it's brilliant!" />

## Right and wrong

<VocabTable group="right" />

French uses *avoir*: you "have reason" and "have wrong". *Tu as raison* is friendly and common. *Tu as tort* is direct. With someone you don't know well, it can sound rude, so a gentler reply such as *je ne suis pas sûr* or *ce n'est pas faux, mais…* is often better.

<Example fr="Vous avez raison, c'est vrai." en="You're right, it's true." />

## Half agreeing

These phrases accept part of an opinion and leave room for your own.

<VocabTable group="partly" />

<Example fr="Ce n'est pas faux, mais le train est cher." en="That's a fair point, but the train is expensive." />
<Example fr="Je suis en partie d'accord." en="I partly agree." />
<Example fr="D'un côté, c'est pratique. De l'autre, c'est cher." en="On the one hand, it's practical. On the other, it's expensive." />

*Je ne suis pas sûr* (a woman writes *sûre*) works on its own. Followed by *que*, it needs a different verb form, which comes in <LessonLink id="b1-expressing-doubt-certainty" text="the lesson on doubt and certainty" />.

You know *moi aussi* and *moi non plus* from <LessonLink id="a2-stressed-pronouns" text="the lesson on stressed pronouns" />. *Moi si* contradicts a negative statement: *Je n'aime pas le café. Moi si !* (I don't like coffee. I do!)

## From yes to no

Here are the main replies in order, from full agreement to firm disagreement.

| Reply | Meaning | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| *Tout à fait d'accord !* | I completely agree! | Warm and enthusiastic |
| *C'est vrai.* | That's true. | Calm agreement |
| *Ce n'est pas faux, mais…* | That's a fair point, but… | Half agreeing |
| *Je ne suis pas sûr.* | I'm not sure. | Polite doubt |
| *Je ne suis pas d'accord.* | I don't agree. | Clear, and still polite |
| *Pas du tout !* | Not at all! | Firm |
| *Tu as tort.* | You're wrong. | Direct, and it can sound rude |

In a meeting, or with someone you call *vous*, stay near the middle of the table. With close friends, you can use the whole range.

## En français

Two colleagues are talking about working from home.

- *Je trouve que travailler à la maison, c'est plus pratique.* (I think working from home is more practical.)
- *Je ne suis pas d'accord avec vous. On se parle moins.* (I don't agree with you. We talk to each other less.)
- *Ce n'est pas faux. Mais on perd moins de temps dans le train.* (That's a fair point. But we waste less time on the train.)
- *C'est vrai, vous avez raison.* (That's true, you're right.)
- *Et à la maison, c'est plus calme. Je travaille mieux.* (And at home, it's quieter. I work better.)
- *Moi, je préfère le bureau. J'aime travailler avec mes collègues.* (I prefer the office. I like working with my colleagues.)
- *D'un côté, je vous comprends. De l'autre, deux jours par semaine à la maison, c'est bien.* (On the one hand, I understand you. On the other, two days a week at home is good.)
- *Deux jours ? Tout à fait d'accord !* (Two days? I completely agree!)

## A forum thread

A town is going to open its shops on Sundays. Here's the discussion on a local forum. Two words help you follow it.

<VocabTable group="forum" />

- **Lucie:** *Dans ma ville, les magasins vont ouvrir le dimanche. Qu'est-ce que vous en pensez ?* (In my town, the shops are going to open on Sundays. What do you think?)
- **Marc:** *Je suis tout à fait d'accord. Je travaille toute la semaine, alors le dimanche, c'est le seul jour pour faire les courses.* (I completely agree. I work all week, so Sunday is the only day to do the shopping.)
- **Nadia:** *Pas du tout d'accord ! Le dimanche, c'est pour la famille. Et les vendeurs ont besoin de se reposer aussi.* (I don't agree at all! Sunday is for the family. And shop assistants need to rest too.)
- **Paul:** *D'un côté, c'est pratique. De l'autre, je pense aux vendeurs. Pour moi, le dimanche matin, d'accord, mais pas l'après-midi.* (On the one hand, it's practical. On the other, I'm thinking of the shop assistants. For me, Sunday morning is fine, but not the afternoon.)
- **Lucie:** *Ce n'est pas faux, Paul. Je suis du même avis.* (That's a fair point, Paul. I see it the same way.)

<Callout type="warning" title="D'accord on its own means OK">
  *D'accord* by itself accepts a plan: *On se retrouve à 8 heures ? D'accord.* To agree with an opinion, you need the full verb: **Je suis d'accord** or **Je suis d'accord avec toi**. And remember that right and wrong use *avoir*: **tu as raison**, never *tu es raison*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Agree before you disagree">
  When you want to disagree, start by accepting the part you can: *Ce n'est pas faux*, *C'est vrai, mais…* or *Je suis en partie d'accord*. Then give your own view. The other person feels heard, and the conversation stays friendly, even when you end up on opposite sides.
</Callout>
