By the end of this lesson you'll be able to read and write a simple French recipe, name the main utensils, and describe how food tastes.

## Recipes and ingredients

You know how to give amounts, such as *un kilo de* and *un peu de*, from <LessonLink id="a2-quantities" text="the lesson on quantities" />. Recipes are full of them.

<VocabTable group="recipe" />

<Example fr="Pour cette recette, il faut quatre ingrédients." en="For this recipe, you need four ingredients." />
<Example fr="Ajoutez une cuillère à soupe d'huile." en="Add a tablespoon of oil." />
<Example fr="Du sel et du poivre." en="Salt and pepper." />

After an amount, *de* stands alone, with no article: *une cuillère à soupe de farine*, *un peu d'huile*. In the list of ingredients, the partitive is common: *de la farine*, *du beurre*.

## Recipe style

French recipes give instructions in one of two ways. Most use the *vous* imperative, which you know from <LessonLink id="a1-imperative" text="the lesson on the imperative" />: *Mélangez*, *Ajoutez*. Many recipe books and websites use the infinitive instead: *Mélanger*, *Ajouter*. Both mean the same, and a recipe usually keeps one style from start to finish.

<Example fr="Mélangez la farine et les œufs." en="Mix the flour and the eggs." />
<Example fr="Éplucher les pommes de terre." en="Peel the potatoes." />

To a friend, in the kitchen, you'll use the *tu* imperative: *Mélange la farine*, *Ajoute le lait*.

## Cooking verbs

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

*Faire cuire*, *faire bouillir* and *faire fondre* look like *faire* + an infinitive, and they are, but you don't need the grammar yet. Learn them as fixed cooking phrases. *Faire* changes as usual, and the second verb stays as it is: *Faites cuire*, *je fais fondre*.

<Example fr="Faites fondre le beurre dans une casserole." en="Melt the butter in a saucepan." />
<Example fr="Faites bouillir l'eau, puis ajoutez les pâtes." en="Boil the water, then add the pasta." />
<Example fr="Faites cuire le gâteau au four pendant trente minutes." en="Bake the cake in the oven for thirty minutes." />

*Cuire au four* means to bake. In recipes, you'll usually see *faire cuire au four*, or simply *enfourner* in more advanced books. *Mélanger* adds an *e* in the *nous* form, like *manger*: *nous mélangeons*.

## In the kitchen

<VocabTable group="kitchen" />

<Example fr="Mettez la farine dans un saladier." en="Put the flour in a mixing bowl." />
<Example fr="Mettez un peu d'huile dans la poêle." en="Put a little oil in the frying pan." />

*Une poêle* is a frying pan, with low sides. *Une casserole* is a saucepan, with high sides and usually a handle. The *oê* in *poêle* sounds like *wa*: it rhymes with *étoile*.

*Le four* is the oven, and *au four* means "in the oven": *des pommes de terre au four* are baked potatoes. French recipes give the oven temperature in degrees Celsius, and sometimes as a number on an old scale, *thermostat 6*, which you can look up once and remember.

## Describing taste

<VocabTable group="taste" />

<Example fr="Cette sauce est trop salée." en="This sauce is too salty." />
<Example fr="Je préfère les crêpes sucrées." en="I prefer sweet crêpes." />
<Example fr="Goûte ! C'est un peu amer, non ?" en="Taste it! It's a bit bitter, isn't it?" />

*Salé* means salty, but also savoury, as opposed to sweet: *une crêpe salée* is a savoury crêpe, with cheese or ham. *Le goûter*, from <LessonLink id="a1-culture-french-meals" text="the lesson on meals in France" />, is the afternoon snack; *goûter* is the verb "to taste".

## En français

Léa and Sam are making crêpes, with a recipe on Léa's phone.

- *Bon, qu'est-ce qu'il faut comme ingrédients ?* (Right, what ingredients do we need?)
- *De la farine, des œufs, du lait, du sucre et un peu de beurre.* (Flour, eggs, milk, sugar and a little butter.)
- *D'accord. Je mets la farine dans le saladier ?* (OK. Shall I put the flour in the mixing bowl?)
- *Oui. Ajoute les œufs, puis mélange doucement.* (Yes. Add the eggs, then mix gently.)
- *Et le beurre ? Je l'ajoute comme ça ?* (And the butter? Do I add it like that?)
- *Non, d'abord, il faut faire fondre le beurre dans une casserole.* (No, first you have to melt the butter in a saucepan.)
- *Voilà. Et maintenant, je fais cuire la première crêpe ?* (There. And now, shall I cook the first crêpe?)
- *Oui, dans la poêle, avec un peu d'huile. Goûte-la !* (Yes, in the frying pan, with a little oil. Taste it!)
- *Mmm, elle est très bonne. Pas trop sucrée.* (Mmm, it's really good. Not too sweet.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Faire cuire, not cuisiner">
  *Cuisiner* means to cook as an activity: *J'adore cuisiner*, *Il cuisine bien*. For cooking a particular ingredient or dish, French uses **faire cuire**: **Faites cuire les pâtes dix minutes**, not *cuisinez les pâtes*. And *la cuisine* is both the kitchen and the food you make.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Cook with the recipe out loud">
  Choose a simple French recipe, and read each step aloud as you do it: *Je fais fondre le beurre. J'ajoute la farine.* Turning the recipe's *vous* forms into *je* forms while your hands are busy links the words to real actions, and you'll remember them the next time you cook.
</Callout>
