By the end of this lesson you'll be able to form the conditionnel, and use it to be polite, to say what you'd like, and to pass on news that isn't confirmed.

## You've already used it

In <LessonLink id="a2-polite-requests" text="the lesson on polite requests" />, you learned *je voudrais*, *tu pourrais* and *pourriez-vous* as fixed phrases. They belong to a verb form called the **conditionnel**, which usually translates as "would". Now you can build it for any verb.

## How it's formed

Take the stem of the futur, from <LessonLink id="a2-futur-simple" text="the lesson on the futur simple" />, and add the endings of the imparfait, from <LessonLink id="a2-imparfait-formation" text="the lesson on the imparfait" />: *-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient*.

<VocabTable group="parler" />

For regular verbs, the stem is the infinitive: *finir* → *je finirais*, *partir* → *je partirais*. Verbs ending in *-re* drop the final *e*: *prendre* → *je prendrais*.

The irregular verbs have the same stems as in the futur, so if you know *je serai*, you already know *je serais*. You know the stems of *être*, *avoir*, *aller* and *faire* from A2, and *vouloir* → *voudr-* from *je voudrais*. The other stems are new here, and they work the same way in the futur: *pouvoir* → *pourr-*, *devoir* → *devr-*, *savoir* → *saur-*, *venir* → *viendr-*, *voir* → *verr-*, *falloir* → *faudr-*.

<VocabTable group="irregular" />

<Example fr="Avec plus de temps, je ferais du sport." en="With more time, I would do some sport." />
<Example fr="Il y aurait un train à midi." en="There would be a train at noon." />

*Il y a* becomes *il y aurait*, and *il faut* becomes *il faudrait*.

## Being polite

The conditionnel softens a request or a question. It's the difference between "I want" and "I'd like".

<Example fr="Je voudrais une chambre pour deux nuits." en="I'd like a room for two nights." />
<Example fr="Vous pourriez m'aider ?" en="Could you help me?" />
<Example fr="Vous auriez une chambre pour ce soir ?" en="Would you have a room for tonight?" />

The more steps you add, the softer you sound. *Je veux* is direct. *Je voudrais* is polite. *J'aimerais bien* is softer still, and works well when you're asking for a favour rather than buying something: *J'aimerais bien partir plus tôt ce soir* (I'd quite like to leave earlier tonight).

## Wishes

<VocabTable group="wishes" />

<Example fr="J'aimerais partir en mai." en="I'd like to leave in May." />
<Example fr="Je préférerais rester ici." en="I'd rather stay here." />
<Example fr="Ce serait génial !" en="That would be brilliant!" />

*J'aimerais* and *je préférerais* are followed by an infinitive. *Tu devrais* (you should) gives advice, which you'll practise in <LessonLink id="b1-giving-advice" text="the lesson on giving advice" />.

## News that isn't confirmed

News reports often use the conditionnel for something they've heard but can't confirm. In English, you'd add "reportedly" or "apparently".

<VocabTable group="news" />

<Example fr="Selon les journaux, le ministre serait malade." en="According to the newspapers, the minister is reportedly ill." />
<Example fr="Selon Paul, le restaurant serait fermé le lundi." en="According to Paul, the restaurant is apparently closed on Mondays." />

*Il paraît que* and *apparemment* say the same thing, but with an ordinary verb: *Il paraît que le restaurant est fermé* (Apparently, the restaurant is closed).

## Futur or conditionnel?

The two forms look and sound alike, so watch the ending. With *je*, the futur ends in *-ai* and the conditionnel in *-ais*.

| Futur (will) | Conditionnel (would) |
|---|---|
| *je parlerai* | *je parlerais* |
| *tu seras* | *tu serais* |
| *nous irons* | *nous irions* |

In careful speech, *-ai* sounds like the *é* of *été*, and *-ais* like the *è* of *mère*. Many speakers don't make the difference, so the context usually decides.

## En français

A customer is booking a holiday at a travel agency.

- *Bonjour madame. Je voudrais partir en vacances en mai.* (Hello. I'd like to go on holiday in May.)
- *Bien sûr. Vous aimeriez aller où ?* (Of course. Where would you like to go?)
- *J'aimerais aller au soleil. Le Portugal, ce serait possible ?* (I'd like to go somewhere sunny. Would Portugal be possible?)
- *Oui. Il y aurait un vol le 12 mai, à 200 euros.* (Yes. There would be a flight on 12 May, for 200 euros.)
- *Je préférerais partir le 15. Vous auriez quelque chose ?* (I'd rather leave on the 15th. Would you have anything?)
- *Le 15, il faudrait partir de Lyon, pas de Paris.* (On the 15th, you'd need to leave from Lyon, not Paris.)
- *Ce serait très bien. Je pourrais réserver aujourd'hui ?* (That would be great. Could I book today?)
- *Bien sûr. Et il paraît que le temps est magnifique en mai !* (Of course. And apparently the weather is wonderful in May!)
- *Génial ! Selon mon amie, Lisbonne serait très belle au printemps.* (Brilliant! According to my friend, Lisbon is supposed to be very beautiful in spring.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Two r's in pourrais">
  *Pouvoir* has a double *r* in the conditionnel, like *mourir* and *voir*: **je pourrais**, **je verrais**. With one *r*, *je pourais* is a spelling mistake. *Devoir*, *vouloir* and *savoir* don't double: **je devrais**, **je voudrais**, **je saurais**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Turn your wants into woulds">
  For a day, every time you think *je veux* in French, change it to *je voudrais* or *j'aimerais*, and every *tu peux* to *tu pourrais*. It's the quickest way to sound polite, and you practise the new endings dozens of times without opening a book.
</Callout>
