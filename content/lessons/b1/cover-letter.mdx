By the end of this lesson you'll be able to lay out a French CV and write a short cover letter, *une lettre de motivation*, with the phrases French employers expect.

## The French CV

A French CV is usually one page long, two at most for long careers. It starts with your name and contact details, sometimes with a photo, though more and more people leave it out. Then come these sections, each with a heading:

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| *Expérience professionnelle* | your jobs, most recent first |
| *Formation* | your studies and diplomas |
| *Compétences* | skills, such as computing |
| *Langues* | the languages you speak, and how well |
| *Centres d'intérêt* | two or three hobbies |

<VocabTable group="cv" />

<Example fr="Je maîtrise l'anglais et l'espagnol." en="I have a good command of English and Spanish." />
<Example fr="Centres d'intérêt : la photo, le tennis, les voyages." en="Interests: photography, tennis, travel." />

You know *une formation* and *une compétence* from <LessonLink id="b1-careers-and-job-hunting" text="the lesson on careers and job hunting" />.

## The cover letter: vous, moi, nous

A French cover letter usually has three short paragraphs:

1. **Vous:** the company, and why you're writing to it.
2. **Moi:** your experience and your qualities.
3. **Nous:** what you could do together, and a request for an interview.

It always uses *vous*, and it's quite formal: no abbreviations, no *bises*, no first names.

## Opening

<VocabTable group="letter" />

<Example fr="Madame, Monsieur," en="Dear Sir or Madam," />
<Example fr="Je me permets de vous adresser ma candidature au poste de serveur." en="I am writing to apply for the position of waiter." />

If you know the person's name, write *Madame Dubois,* or *Monsieur Martin,*, never *Cher Monsieur* in a first letter.

## The middle

The conditionnel, from <LessonLink id="b1-conditionnel-present" text="the lesson on the conditionnel présent" />, makes your wishes polite: *je serais ravi de…*, *je pourrais commencer en juin*.

## Closing

<VocabTable group="closing" />

The last two phrases are long fixed formulas. Learn them as they are, and copy them exactly. *Veuillez* is a very formal form of *vouloir*, which you'll meet again in <LessonLink id="b1-formal-email" text="the lesson on writing a formal email" />. In an email, *Cordialement* is usually enough, but in a letter, the full formula is still expected.

## Formal or friendly?

A cover letter is the opposite of the informal email from A2. Compare:

| | Email to a friend | Cover letter |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | *Salut Léa !* | *Madame, Monsieur,* |
| "You" | *tu*, *ton*, *ta* | *vous*, *votre* |
| A wish | *Je veux…* | *Je souhaite…*, *Je serais ravi de…* |
| Closing | *Bises* | *Je vous prie d'agréer…* |

If a word from the left-hand column slips into your letter, change it. A single *tu* or *bises* is enough to make an employer stop reading.

## En français

A student asks a careers adviser to check her cover letter.

- *Bonjour. Vous pourriez lire ma lettre de motivation ?* (Hello. Could you read my cover letter?)
- *Bien sûr. Vous postulez à quel poste ?* (Of course. Which position are you applying for?)
- *Un poste de serveuse dans un hôtel, pour l'été.* (A waitress job in a hotel, for the summer.)
- *D'accord. Vous commencez par « Salut » ? Ce n'est pas possible !* (OK. You start with "Salut"? You can't do that!)
- *Ah. Qu'est-ce que je dois écrire ?* (Oh. What should I write?)
- *« Madame, Monsieur, ». Et à la fin, il faut écrire « Je vous prie d'agréer… ».* ("Madame, Monsieur,". And at the end, you have to write "Je vous prie d'agréer…".)
- *Toute la phrase ? C'est très long !* (The whole sentence? That's very long!)
- *Oui, mais c'est comme ça en France. Et le reste est très bien : on voit que vous êtes dynamique.* (Yes, but that's how it is in France. And the rest is very good: you can see you're energetic.)
- *Merci beaucoup ! Je la change ce soir.* (Thank you very much! I'll change it tonight.)

## A model cover letter

*Madame, Monsieur,*

*Votre hôtel est l'un des plus beaux de Nice, et je me permets de vous adresser ma candidature au poste de serveuse pour l'été.*

*Actuellement, je suis étudiante en langues à Lyon. Je maîtrise l'anglais et l'espagnol, et j'ai travaillé deux mois dans un restaurant l'année dernière. Je suis dynamique, rigoureuse et j'aime parler avec les clients.*

*Je serais ravie de travailler dans votre équipe. Je me tiens à votre disposition pour un entretien. Je vous remercie de votre attention.*

*Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.*

*Julie Martin*

(Dear Sir or Madam, Your hotel is one of the most beautiful in Nice, and I am writing to apply for the position of waitress for the summer. I am currently a languages student in Lyon. I speak English and Spanish well, and I worked for two months in a restaurant last year. I am energetic and thorough, and I enjoy talking to customers. I would be delighted to work in your team. I am available for an interview. Thank you for your time. Yours faithfully, Julie Martin)

<Callout type="warning" title="Actuellement means currently">
  *Actuellement* looks like "actually", but it means "currently", "at the moment": **Actuellement, je travaille à Lyon** (I currently work in Lyon). To say "actually" in the sense of "in fact", French uses *en fait*. In a cover letter, this mistake changes the meaning of your first sentence about yourself, so check it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Build your own template">
  Once you've written one good cover letter, save it as a template with the fixed phrases in bold: the opening, *je me permets de…*, *je me tiens à votre disposition* and the closing formula. For each new job, you only rewrite the first two paragraphs. The fixed phrases stay correct, and you avoid the most common formal mistakes.
</Callout>
