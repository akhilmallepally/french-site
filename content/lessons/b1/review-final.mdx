## What you can do now

This is the end of B1. Use this checklist as *un bilan*: tick what you can do, and go back to the unit review for anything that still feels shaky.

- **Opinions and feelings.** You can give and justify your opinion, agree and disagree, and use *ce qui*, *ce que* and *dont*. See <LessonLink id="b1-review-opinions" text="the review of opinions and feelings" />.
- **Hypotheses and wishes.** You can say what you would do, give advice and talk about dreams with the conditionnel and *si*. See <LessonLink id="b1-review-hypotheses" text="the review of hypotheses and wishes" />.
- **Telling stories.** You can tell a story in the past, with the plus-que-parfait and time connectors. See <LessonLink id="b1-review-stories" text="the review of telling stories" />.
- **The subjunctive.** You can express necessity, wishes, emotions and doubt with the subjunctive. See <LessonLink id="b1-review-subjunctive" text="the review of the subjunctive" />.
- **Work.** You can write a cover letter and answer interview questions, with double and possessive pronouns. See <LessonLink id="b1-review-work" text="the review of work and careers" />.
- **Media and technology.** You can follow the news, report what people say, and use the gérondif. See <LessonLink id="b1-review-media" text="the review of media and technology" />.
- **Society and environment.** You can explain causes and consequences, use the passive, and present an argument. See <LessonLink id="b1-review-society" text="the review of society and environment" />.
- **Home, money and problems.** You can rent a flat, manage money, write a formal email and complain politely. See <LessonLink id="b1-review-home-money-problems" text="the review of home, money and problems" />.
- **Food and culture.** You can follow a recipe, talk about regional food and French in Québec, and make suggestions. See <LessonLink id="b1-making-suggestions" text="the lesson on making suggestions" /> and <LessonLink id="b1-culture-quebec" text="the lesson on French in Québec" />.

## One sentence from each unit

Here's one model sentence from each of the first eight units. Read each one aloud, and check that you understand why every form is there.

*À mon avis, ce qui compte le plus, c'est la famille.*

*Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais au Québec.*

*Quand je suis arrivé à la gare, le train était déjà parti.*

*Il faut que tu viennes à ma fête samedi.*

*Ça fait trois ans que je travaille dans une banque.*

*Mon ami dit qu'il apprend le français en écoutant des podcasts.*

*Il y a de plus en plus de plastique dans la mer, c'est pourquoi il faut agir.*

*Je n'ai que dix euros, et le loyer est de 700 euros !*

(In my opinion, what matters most is family. If I had more time, I would travel to Québec. When I got to the station, the train had already left. You must come to my party on Saturday. I've been working in a bank for three years. My friend says he's learning French by listening to podcasts. There's more and more plastic in the sea, which is why we must act. I've only got ten euros, and the rent is 700 euros!)

## How each sentence works

- In sentence 1, *ce qui* puts the focus on what matters, and *à mon avis* gives an opinion.
- In sentence 2, *si* + imparfait goes with the conditionnel for something imagined.
- In sentence 3, the plus-que-parfait *était parti* shows what had happened before you arrived.
- In sentence 4, *il faut que* is followed by the subjunctive *viennes*.
- In sentence 5, *ça fait… que* + the present says how long something has been going on.
- In sentence 6, reported speech passes on what someone says, and the gérondif says how.
- In sentence 7, a trend phrase and *c'est pourquoi* link a problem to its consequence.
- In sentence 8, *ne… que* says "only", and *être de* gives an amount.

## Your turn

Write your own *bilan* of B1, in ten to twelve sentences, on paper or in a notes app. Write about yourself and your French.

1. Give your opinion on your progress, with *à mon avis* or *je trouve que*.
2. Say how long you've been learning, with *ça fait… que*.
3. Tell a short memory of a moment in French, with the passé composé, the imparfait and one plus-que-parfait.
4. Say what you'd do if you spoke French *couramment*, with *si* + imparfait.
5. Say what you need to do next, with *il faut que* + subjunctive.
6. End with a suggestion to yourself or a friend: *Et si on regardait un film en français ?*

Try to use *s'exprimer* once: *Je peux m'exprimer au travail.*

## Check your work

- After *si* + imparfait, is the second verb in the conditionnel?
- After *il faut que*, *je veux que* and *je suis content que*, is the verb in the subjunctive?
- Does every plus-que-parfait describe something that happened before another past event?
- Is each new word you used defined in a lesson you've done, or checked in a dictionary?

<Callout type="note" title="The French instruction words">
  French textbooks and exams give their *consignes* in French, usually with the *vous* imperative. Learn these four, which match the exercises on this site: **Complétez** (complete: fill in the gaps), **Choisissez** (choose: pick the right option), **Mettez dans l'ordre** (put in order: reorder the words) and **Traduisez** (translate). You'll see them in French exams such as the DELF, so it pays to recognize them at a glance.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="S'exprimer is reflexive">
  To say you can express yourself, you need the pronoun: **je peux m'exprimer**, **elle s'exprime bien**. Without it, *exprimer* needs an object: *exprimer une idée*. So *je peux exprimer en français* is incomplete. Write **je peux m'exprimer en français**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Write a bilan every three months">
  Keep today's text, and write a new *bilan* every three months, without looking at the old one first. Then compare them. You'll see which structures have become natural and which you still avoid, and that's the best guide to what to practise next.
</Callout>
