By the end of this lesson you'll know what the subjunctive is, and you'll be able to form it for regular verbs and for verbs with two stems.

## What is a mood?

Every French verb has a tense (present, past, future) and a **mood**. So far, almost everything you've said has been in the **indicative**: the mood for facts, things you present as real. The **subjunctive** is a second mood. It appears after certain words, usually *que*, when the sentence is about something wanted, necessary, felt or doubted rather than stated as a fact.

<VocabTable group="terms" />

<Example fr="Tu ranges ta chambre." en="You tidy your room. (a fact: indicative)" />
<Example fr="Il faut que tu ranges ta chambre." en="You have to tidy your room. (something necessary: subjunctive)" />

The subjunctive isn't a tense. It has a present form, which you're learning now, and it covers both present and future: *Il faut que tu partes* can mean now or tomorrow.

## Il faut que: the frame

In <LessonLink id="a2-il-faut-and-advice" text="the lesson on il faut and simple advice" />, you used *il faut* + infinitive for what everyone must do: *Il faut manger des légumes.* To say who has to do it, French uses *il faut que* + a subject + the subjunctive. In this lesson and the next, *il faut que* is just a frame for practising the forms. <LessonLink id="b1-subjonctif-necessity" text="The lesson on il faut que" /> looks at how it's used.

<Example fr="Il faut partir." en="We have to leave. (in general)" />
<Example fr="Il faut que je parte." en="I have to leave." />

## How to form it

For regular verbs, three steps:

1. Take the *ils* form of the present: *ils parlent*, *ils finissent*, *ils attendent*.
2. Remove *-ent*: *parl-*, *finiss-*, *attend-*.
3. Add the endings *-e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent*.

<VocabTable group="parler" />

<VocabTable group="others" />

| | parler | finir | attendre |
|---|---|---|---|
| *que je* | parle | finisse | attende |
| *que tu* | parles | finisses | attendes |
| *qu'il* / *qu'elle* | parle | finisse | attende |
| *que nous* | parlions | finissions | attendions |
| *que vous* | parliez | finissiez | attendiez |
| *qu'ils* / *qu'elles* | parlent | finissent | attendent |

Notice two things. First, *nous* and *vous* look exactly like the imparfait, from <LessonLink id="a2-imparfait-formation" text="the lesson on the imparfait" />: *nous parlions*, *vous finissiez*. Second, for most *-er* verbs, *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils* sound exactly like the present. The difference is often only in writing, or with *nous* and *vous*.

## Verbs with two stems

Some verbs have one stem for *nous* and *vous* and another for the other persons, as they do in the present. The rule still works: take *je*, *tu*, *il* and *ils* from the *ils* form, and *nous* and *vous* from the *nous* form of the present.

<VocabTable group="two" />

<Example fr="Il faut que je prenne le bus." en="I have to take the bus." />
<Example fr="Il faut que nous prenions le bus." en="We have to take the bus." />
<Example fr="Il faut qu'elle vienne samedi." en="She has to come on Saturday." />
<Example fr="Il faut que vous buviez beaucoup d'eau." en="You have to drink a lot of water." />

*Acheter* and *appeler* change their spelling in the same way as in the present: *que j'achète* but *que nous achetions*, *que j'appelle* but *que nous appelions*.

## En français

A father and his teenage daughter, Emma, on a Saturday morning.

- *Emma, il faut que tu ranges ta chambre ce matin.* (Emma, you have to tidy your room this morning.)
- *Maintenant ? Je regarde une série !* (Now? I'm watching a series!)
- *Oui, maintenant. Et il faut que tu finisses tes devoirs avant le déjeuner.* (Yes, now. And you have to finish your homework before lunch.)
- *Mais mes amis m'attendent au parc à deux heures.* (But my friends are waiting for me at the park at two.)
- *D'accord, mais il faut que tu prennes ton téléphone.* (OK, but you have to take your phone.)
- *Bien sûr. Et il faut que je rentre à quelle heure ?* (Of course. And what time do I have to come home?)
- *Il faut que tu rentres à sept heures. Ta grand-mère vient dîner.* (You have to be home at seven. Your grandmother is coming to dinner.)
- *Ah, c'est vrai. Il faut que je lui achète des fleurs ?* (Oh, that's right. Do I have to buy her some flowers?)
- *Bonne idée ! Tu es gentille.* (Good idea! You're very sweet.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Start from ils, not the infinitive">
  It's tempting to build the subjunctive from the infinitive or from *je*: *que je finis*, *que je prends*. Both are wrong. Start from *ils*: *ils finissent* gives **que je finisse**, and *ils prennent* gives **que je prenne**. For *-er* verbs, you rarely notice the difference, but for other verbs it changes the sound.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Learn the ils form with every new verb">
  From now on, when you meet a new verb, learn three forms together: the infinitive, the *ils* form of the present and the *que je* form of the subjunctive. *Partir, ils partent, que je parte.* The third one takes one extra second, and you'll never have to look it up again.
</Callout>
