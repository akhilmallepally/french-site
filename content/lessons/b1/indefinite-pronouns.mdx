By the end of this lesson you'll be able to talk about people and things in general: each one, none, several, a few, anyone and anything.

## Each and everyone

<VocabTable group="each" />

*Chaque* goes before a noun, and it's always singular. *Chacun* (or *chacune*) stands on its own: it replaces *chaque* + the noun.

<Example fr="Chaque utilisateur a un mot de passe." en="Each user has a password." />
<Example fr="Il y a trois livres : chacun coûte dix euros." en="There are three books: each one costs ten euros." />
<Example fr="Tout le monde est sur les réseaux sociaux." en="Everyone is on social media." />

*Tout le monde* means "everyone", but grammatically it's singular, like *chacun*: *tout le monde est*, *tout le monde a*.

## None at all: aucun

<VocabTable group="none" />

*Aucun* (masculine) or *aucune* (feminine) means "not one", and it works with *ne*, like the negatives in <LessonLink id="a2-negative-expressions" text="the lesson on other negative expressions" />. It replaces *pas de* and makes the negative stronger.

<Example fr="Je n'ai aucun message." en="I haven't got a single message." />
<Example fr="Elle n'a aucune idée." en="She has no idea." />
<Example fr="Aucun commentaire n'est gentil." en="Not one comment is kind." />

*Aucun* stays singular, because it means "not one". When it's the subject, *ne* still goes before the verb.

In conversation, *aucun* often stands alone as a short answer: *Tu as des messages ? Aucun.* With *en*, it replaces a noun you've already mentioned: *Des messages ? Je n'en ai aucun.*

## Several, a few, some, others

<VocabTable group="some" />

<Example fr="J'ai plusieurs amis à Lyon." en="I have several friends in Lyon." />
<Example fr="J'ai lu quelques commentaires." en="I read a few comments." />
<Example fr="Les commentaires ? J'en ai lu quelques-uns." en="The comments? I read a few of them." />
<Example fr="Certains aiment le film, d'autres le trouvent ennuyeux." en="Some people like the film, others find it boring." />

*Plusieurs* and *quelques* go straight before the noun, with no article. *Quelques-uns* and *certains* can stand on their own. *Plusieurs* is the same for masculine and feminine.

*Quelques* and *plusieurs* are close in meaning. *Quelques* suggests a small number, like "a few", while *plusieurs* suggests "more than two", and often more than you expected. *Certains* is very common for people, as in *certains pensent que…* (some people think that…).

## Anyone, anything, anywhere

<VocabTable group="any" />

*N'importe* + a question word means "any … at all", with the idea that it doesn't matter which.

<Example fr="N'importe qui peut publier sur internet." en="Anyone at all can post on the internet." />
<Example fr="Tu peux m'appeler n'importe quand." en="You can call me any time." />
<Example fr="Il dit n'importe quoi !" en="He's talking nonsense!" />

*N'importe quoi* on its own is also a reaction: *N'importe quoi !* (What nonsense!)

To say "something" with an adjective, use *quelque chose de*: *quelque chose de nouveau* (something new), *quelque chose d'intéressant* (something interesting). The adjective stays masculine. *Rien de* and *personne de* work the same way: *rien de grave* (nothing serious).

## Online

<VocabTable group="online" />

<Example fr="Chaque utilisateur peut laisser un commentaire." en="Each user can leave a comment." />
<Example fr="Certains utilisateurs écrivent n'importe quoi." en="Some users write any old nonsense." />

You'll often see these words in short notices on websites: *Aucun commentaire* when nobody has written anything yet, or *Chaque utilisateur peut…* in the rules. Read them as signs that tell you what you can do.

## En français

Two friends are reading the comments under an article about a new film.

- *Tu as lu les commentaires sous l'article ?* (Have you read the comments under the article?)
- *Oui, quelques-uns. Il y en a plus de deux cents !* (Yes, a few. There are more than two hundred!)
- *Chacun donne son avis, et certains disent n'importe quoi.* (Everyone gives their opinion, and some people talk nonsense.)
- *C'est vrai. Un utilisateur dit que le film est nul, mais il ne l'a pas vu !* (That's true. One user says the film is rubbish, but he hasn't seen it!)
- *Et d'autres ? Il y a quelque chose d'intéressant ?* (And others? Is there anything interesting?)
- *Oui, plusieurs critiques sont très bonnes.* (Yes, several reviews are very good.)
- *Moi, je n'ai lu aucune critique. Je préfère voir le film.* (I haven't read a single review. I'd rather see the film.)
- *Bonne idée. On y va quand ?* (Good idea. When shall we go?)
- *N'importe quand ! Samedi, si tu veux.* (Any time! Saturday, if you like.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Singular words">
  Several of these words are always singular, even though they're about many people. **Chaque utilisateur**, not *chaque utilisateurs*. **Aucun commentaire**, not *aucuns commentaires*. **Tout le monde est content**, not *tout le monde sont*. Only *plusieurs*, *quelques*, *certains* and *d'autres* go with a plural.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="A reaction for every occasion">
  Some of these words make useful reactions on their own. *N'importe quoi !* (What nonsense!), *Aucune idée !* (No idea!) and *Chacun son goût !* (Each to their own!) are all common in conversation. Learn them as whole phrases, and you'll have three quick replies ready.
</Callout>
