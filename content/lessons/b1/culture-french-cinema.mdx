By the end of this lesson you'll know the names and the films that French people mention when they talk about cinema, and you'll be able to say a few things about them yourself.

## La Nouvelle Vague

<VocabTable group="history" />

**La Nouvelle Vague** (the New Wave) began at the end of the 1950s. A group of young directors, many of them film critics, wanted to make films differently. They shot in the street rather than in studios, with small budgets and often with unknown actors.

**François Truffaut** made *Les Quatre Cents Coups* in 1959. It's about a boy in Paris who doesn't get on with his parents or his teachers. **Jean-Luc Godard** made *À bout de souffle* in 1960, a crime film shot in the streets of Paris.

<Example fr="Les Quatre Cents Coups est un film de François Truffaut." en="Les Quatre Cents Coups is a film by François Truffaut." />
<Example fr="Godard a tourné À bout de souffle dans les rues de Paris." en="Godard shot À bout de souffle in the streets of Paris." />

These films are still shown in French schools and in film clubs, and their style influenced directors all over the world.

## Popular comedies

<VocabTable group="success" />

French people go to the cinema a lot, and comedies are often the biggest hits. Success is counted in *entrées*: the number of tickets sold.

**Bienvenue chez les Ch'tis** (2008), by Dany Boon, is about a post office manager sent from the south of France to the north. It plays with the clichés about the north, and it became one of the biggest French hits ever.

**Intouchables** (2011), by Olivier Nakache and Éric Toledano, is about a rich man in a wheelchair and the young man from the suburbs who looks after him. It was a huge success in France and abroad.

<Example fr="Le film est sorti en 2011, et ça a été un très grand succès." en="The film came out in 2011, and it was a huge hit." />
<Example fr="Ce film a eu plus de vingt millions d'entrées." en="This film had more than twenty million admissions." />

## Cannes and the César

<VocabTable group="awards" />

**Le Festival de Cannes** takes place every May on the Côte d'Azur. Stars climb the famous red-carpeted steps, and a jury awards **la Palme d'or** to the best film.

**Les César** are the French equivalent of the Oscars. The ceremony has taken place every year since 1976, and the awards are named after the sculptor César, who designed the statuettes.

<Example fr="Ce film a gagné la Palme d'or." en="This film won the Palme d'or." />
<Example fr="Le jury récompense le meilleur film et le meilleur acteur." en="The jury awards the best film and the best actor." />

## Going to the cinema

<VocabTable group="going" />

In France, new films come out on **Wednesdays**, not Fridays. Many cinemas show foreign films both *en VO*, with subtitles, and *en VF*, dubbed into French. Big cities also have small independent cinemas that show classics and films *d'art et d'essai* (art-house films).

<Example fr="Le nouveau film de Dany Boon sort mercredi." en="Dany Boon's new film comes out on Wednesday." />
<Example fr="Il y a trois salles de cinéma dans ma ville." en="There are three cinemas in my town." />

## En français

Two friends are choosing a film for the evening.

- *Ce soir, on regarde un vieux film de Truffaut ?* (Shall we watch an old Truffaut film tonight?)
- *Un film de la Nouvelle Vague ? Pourquoi pas.* (A Nouvelle Vague film? Why not.)
- *Je pensais aux Quatre Cents Coups. C'est un des films les plus connus de la Nouvelle Vague.* (I was thinking of Les Quatre Cents Coups. It's one of the best-known Nouvelle Vague films.)
- *C'est en noir et blanc, non ?* (It's in black and white, isn't it?)
- *Oui, il est sorti en 1959. Mais l'histoire est très émouvante.* (Yes, it came out in 1959. But the story is very moving.)
- *Moi, je préférerais une comédie plus moderne. Intouchables, par exemple ?* (I'd rather have a more modern comedy. Intouchables, for example?)
- *Je l'ai déjà vu deux fois !* (I've already seen it twice!)
- *Alors, on regarde Truffaut ce soir, et une comédie samedi.* (So, we'll watch Truffaut tonight, and a comedy on Saturday.)
- *D'accord. Et si c'est ennuyeux, on change !* (OK. And if it's boring, we'll switch!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Les César, with no s">
  The awards are named after a person, so the word doesn't change in the plural: **les César**, **trois César**, not *les Césars*. The same goes for **la Palme d'or**, with a small *o* for *or*. And remember that *une entrée* in the box office isn't the entrance to the cinema, but a ticket sold.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="French subtitles on French films">
  Watch a French film with French subtitles rather than English ones. You hear the words and see them at the same time, so you can follow fast speech without losing the meaning. Start with a comedy you already know in English, then move on to something new.
</Callout>
