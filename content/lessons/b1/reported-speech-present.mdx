By the end of this lesson you'll be able to pass on what someone says or asks, in the present: *Il dit qu'il arrive*, *Elle demande si tu viens*.

## Reporting what someone says

To pass on a statement, use *dire que*, *répondre que* or *expliquer que*, then an ordinary sentence. *Que* is never left out.

<VocabTable group="say" />

<Example fr="Paul : « Je suis fatigué. » → Paul dit qu'il est fatigué." en="Paul: 'I'm tired.' → Paul says he's tired." />
<Example fr="Anne : « Mon train est en retard. » → Anne dit que son train est en retard." en="Anne: 'My train is late.' → Anne says her train is late." />
<Example fr="Le porte-parole explique que le ministre est malade." en="The spokesperson explains that the minister is ill." />

The pronouns and possessives change to fit the new speaker, just as in English. *Je* becomes *il* or *elle*, *mon* becomes *son*, *nous* becomes *ils*. *Que* becomes *qu'* before a vowel: *il dit qu'il vient*.

## Reporting a yes/no question

For a question with the answer yes or no, use *demander si*. *Si* becomes *s'* before *il* and *ils*, as in <LessonLink id="b1-si-present-futur" text="the lesson on si with the present and future" />.

<VocabTable group="ask" />

<Example fr="Marie : « Tu viens ? » → Marie demande si tu viens." en="Marie: 'Are you coming?' → Marie's asking if you're coming." />
<Example fr="Léo : « Il arrive ? » → Léo demande s'il arrive." en="Léo: 'Is he arriving?' → Léo's asking if he's arriving." />
<Example fr="Je me demande si c'est vrai." en="I wonder if it's true." />

## Reporting other questions

With *où*, *quand*, *comment*, *pourquoi* or *qui*, keep the question word, then use ordinary word order: subject, then verb. *Est-ce que* and inversion both disappear.

| Direct question | Reported question |
|---|---|
| *Où est-ce que tu habites ?* | *Il demande où tu habites.* |
| *Quand arrivez-vous ?* | *Il demande quand vous arrivez.* |
| *Pourquoi tu pars ?* | *Il demande pourquoi tu pars.* |
| *Qu'est-ce que tu veux ?* | *Il demande ce que tu veux.* |
| *Qu'est-ce qui se passe ?* | *Il demande ce qui se passe.* |

*Qu'est-ce que* and *qu'est-ce qui* become *ce que* and *ce qui*, which you know from <LessonLink id="b1-ce-qui-ce-que" text="the lesson on ce qui and ce que" />.

## In the news

Reported speech is everywhere in the news, because journalists pass on what other people say.

<VocabTable group="news" />

<Example fr="Un témoin dit qu'il a vu la voiture." en="A witness says he saw the car." />
<Example fr="Dans une déclaration, la ministre précise que l'école reste fermée lundi." en="In a statement, the minister adds that the school is staying closed on Monday." />

In this lesson, the reporting verb (*dit*, *demande*) is always in the present. When it's in the past (*il a dit que…*), the tenses in the second half often change. You'll learn that in <LessonLink id="b2-reported-speech-past" text="the lesson on reported speech in the past" />.

## En français

Inès is passing on a voice message to her flatmate Hugo.

- *Hugo, tu as un message de Karim.* (Hugo, you've got a message from Karim.)
- *Ah bon ? Qu'est-ce qu'il dit ?* (Oh? What does he say?)
- *Il dit qu'il arrive à huit heures, et il demande si tu peux l'attendre à la gare.* (He says he's arriving at eight, and he's asking if you can wait for him at the station.)
- *À huit heures ? Il sait que je travaille jusqu'à sept heures ?* (At eight? Does he know I work until seven?)
- *Oui. Il explique que son train est en retard.* (Yes. He explains that his train is late.)
- *D'accord. Il dit où il veut dîner ?* (OK. Does he say where he wants to have dinner?)
- *Il veut savoir ce que tu préfères : une pizza à la maison ou le restaurant.* (He wants to know what you'd prefer: a pizza at home or a restaurant.)
- *Réponds-lui que je préfère la pizza !* (Tell him I'd prefer the pizza!)
- *Je lui réponds tout de suite.* (I'll reply to him straight away.)

<Callout type="warning" title="No inversion, no est-ce que">
  In a reported question, the word order goes back to normal: subject, then verb. It's **Il demande où tu habites**, not *il demande où habites-tu* or *il demande où est-ce que tu habites*. The same goes for yes/no questions: **Elle demande si tu viens**, never *si est-ce que tu viens*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Be the messenger">
  Next time someone sends you a message in English, imagine passing it on in French to a third person: *Elle dit qu'elle est en retard, et elle demande si on peut commencer sans elle.* Real messages give you real sentences to practise with, and the pronoun changes come naturally.
</Callout>
