By the end of this lesson you'll be able to say what someone has to do, mustn't do or had better do, with *il faut que* and similar expressions.

## Il faut que

In the last two lessons, *il faut que* was a frame for practising the forms. Here's how it really works. *Il faut* + infinitive is general: it applies to everyone, or the person is obvious. *Il faut que* + a subject + the subjunctive says exactly who has to do something.

<Example fr="Il faut partir maintenant." en="We have to leave now. (it's time, for everyone)" />
<Example fr="Il faut que tu partes maintenant." en="You have to leave now." />
<Example fr="Il faut que je fasse les courses ce soir." en="I have to do the shopping tonight." />

Now that you know the irregular forms from <LessonLink id="b1-subjonctif-irregular" text="the lesson on irregular subjunctives" />, you can use any verb after *il faut que*.

## Il ne faut pas que

The negative means "must not", exactly like *il ne faut pas* + infinitive in A2.

<Example fr="Il ne faut pas que tu sois en retard." en="You mustn't be late." />
<Example fr="Il ne faut pas qu'ils sachent." en="They mustn't know." />

To say "you don't have to", use *ce n'est pas nécessaire*: *Ce n'est pas nécessaire de venir* (You don't have to come).

## Other expressions of necessity

<VocabTable group="triggers" />

All of these take the subjunctive when they're followed by *que* and a subject.

<Example fr="Il est important que vous soyez à l'heure." en="It's important that you're on time." />
<Example fr="Il est essentiel que vous écoutiez le médecin." en="It's essential that you listen to the doctor." />
<Example fr="Il vaut mieux que tu prennes le train." en="It's better if you take the train." />
<Example fr="Il suffit que tu m'appelles." en="All you need to do is call me." />

*Il vaut mieux que* is the present of *il vaudrait mieux*, from <LessonLink id="b1-giving-advice" text="the lesson on giving advice" />. *Il vaudrait mieux* + infinitive is softer, but *il vaut mieux que* lets you name the person.

## General or personal?

Each of these expressions also works with *de* + infinitive, for a general statement. With *il faut* and *il vaut mieux*, there's no *de*.

| General | Personal |
|---|---|
| *Il faut partir.* | *Il faut que tu partes.* |
| *Il vaut mieux partir.* | *Il vaut mieux que tu partes.* |
| *Il est important de partir.* | *Il est important que tu partes.* |
| *Il suffit de partir.* | *Il suffit que tu partes.* |

## Household chores

You know the rooms and many chores from <LessonLink id="a2-home-and-chores" text="the lesson on home and household chores" />. Here are some more, for sharing a flat.

## En français

Three flatmates are drawing up a chore rota.

- *Bon, il faut qu'on fasse un planning pour les tâches.* (Right, we need to make a rota for the chores.)
- *D'accord. Il faut que quelqu'un sorte les poubelles le lundi.* (OK. Someone has to take the bins out on Mondays.)
- *Je peux le faire. Mais il faut que tu fasses les courses, Léa.* (I can do it. But you have to do the shopping, Léa.)
- *Pas de problème. Et le ménage ?* (No problem. And the cleaning?)
- *Il vaut mieux qu'on le fasse tous les trois, le samedi matin.* (It's better if the three of us do it, on Saturday morning.)
- *Bonne idée. Et le chat ? Il est important que quelqu'un le nourrisse tous les jours.* (Good idea. And the cat? It's important that someone feeds him every day.)
- *Moi. Mais il ne faut pas que vous lui donniez du chocolat !* (Me. But you mustn't give him chocolate!)
- *D'accord. Et il suffit que nous rangions notre vaisselle tous les soirs, non ?* (OK. And all we need to do is put our dishes away every evening, isn't it?)
- *Exactement. Voilà, le planning est prêt !* (Exactly. There you go, the rota's ready!)

<Callout type="warning" title="De or que?">
  After *il est important*, *il est nécessaire* and *il est essentiel*, choose between two patterns, and don't mix them. For everyone: **Il est important de dormir**, with *de* + infinitive. For a named person: **Il est important que tu dormes**, with *que* + the subjunctive. *Il est important que dormir* and *il est important de tu dormes* are both wrong.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Write the house rules">
  Write five rules for where you live, real or imagined, each with a different expression: *il faut que*, *il ne faut pas que*, *il est important que*, *il vaut mieux que* and *il suffit que*. Name a person in each one. Five sentences give you five triggers, and a different verb in each one.
</Callout>
