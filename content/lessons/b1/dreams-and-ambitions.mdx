By the end of this lesson you'll be able to talk about your plans, your hopes and your dreams, and show how sure you are about each one.

## Plans

When you're fairly sure something will happen, use *aller* + infinitive, from <LessonLink id="a1-near-future" text="the lesson on the near future" />, or the futur, from <LessonLink id="a2-futur-simple" text="the lesson on the futur simple" />. These words help you say it's a plan.

<VocabTable group="plans" />

<Example fr="J'ai l'intention de changer de travail." en="I intend to change jobs." />
<Example fr="Je compte ouvrir un café l'année prochaine." en="I'm planning to open a café next year." />
<Example fr="Mon but est d'apprendre l'italien." en="My aim is to learn Italian." />

*Compter* is followed straight by an infinitive, with no preposition. *Avoir l'intention* takes *de*.

## Hopes

*Espérer* is followed by an infinitive, or by *que* and an ordinary sentence, often in the futur.

<Example fr="J'espère trouver un travail à Rome." en="I hope to find a job in Rome." />
<Example fr="J'espère que tu viendras." en="I hope you'll come." />
<Example fr="On espère que le projet réussira." en="We hope the project will succeed." />

## Dreams

For things that may never happen, use the conditionnel, from <LessonLink id="b1-conditionnel-present" text="the lesson on the conditionnel présent" />, or *si* + imparfait, from <LessonLink id="b1-si-imparfait-conditionnel" text="the lesson on si with the imparfait" />. *Rêver de* is in <LessonLink id="b1-relative-dont" text="the lesson on dont" />.

<VocabTable group="dreams" />

<Example fr="Dans l'idéal, je travaillerais quatre jours par semaine." en="Ideally, I'd work four days a week." />
<Example fr="Si je pouvais, je voyagerais pendant un an." en="If I could, I'd travel for a year." />
<Example fr="Pour réaliser mon rêve, je vais apprendre le piano." en="To make my dream come true, I'm going to learn the piano." />

## Work and life

## Asking about plans

To ask someone about their future, you can use the same verbs in a question, or the word *un projet* (a plan), from <LessonLink id="b1-relative-dont" text="the lesson on dont" />.

<Example fr="Qu'est-ce que tu comptes faire après tes études ?" en="What are you planning to do after your studies?" />
<Example fr="Tu as des projets pour l'été ?" en="Have you got any plans for the summer?" />
<Example fr="Quel est ton rêve ?" en="What's your dream?" />

*Un projet* is an everyday plan as well as a big one: *J'ai des projets pour le week-end* (I've got plans for the weekend).

## From sure to dreaming

The verb tells your listener how sure you are. Here are the same plans, from the most certain to a daydream.

| How sure | Example |
|---|---|
| Decided | *Je vais partir en Italie.* (I'm going to go to Italy.) |
| Planned | *Je compte partir en Italie.* (I'm planning to go to Italy.) |
| Hoped for | *J'espère partir en Italie.* (I hope to go to Italy.) |
| Wished for | *J'aimerais partir en Italie.* (I'd like to go to Italy.) |
| Dreamed of | *Si je pouvais, je partirais en Italie.* (If I could, I'd go to Italy.) |

## En français

Two friends are turning thirty, and they're talking about the next ten years.

- *Dans dix ans, qu'est-ce que tu aimerais faire ?* (In ten years, what would you like to be doing?)
- *J'ai l'intention de monter ma propre entreprise.* (I intend to set up my own business.)
- *C'est une bonne idée ! Et quel est ton but ?* (That's a good idea! And what's your aim?)
- *Je compte ouvrir un petit café à Lyon. Et toi ?* (I'm planning to open a little café in Lyon. And you?)
- *Moi, j'espère que j'aurai des enfants. Et dans l'idéal, je travaillerais moins.* (I hope I'll have children. And ideally, I'd work less.)
- *Et ta carrière ?* (And your career?)
- *Si je pouvais, je ferais du bénévolat avec des enfants.* (If I could, I'd do voluntary work with children.)
- *C'est génial. Moi, je rêve de prendre ma retraite à cinquante ans !* (That's brilliant. I dream of retiring at fifty!)
- *À cinquante ans ? Tu es très motivé !* (At fifty? You're very motivated!)
- *Oui, motivé pour arrêter de travailler !* (Yes, motivated to stop working!)

## A blog post

*Ma vie idéale*

*L'année prochaine, je compte changer de travail. J'ai l'intention d'apprendre l'italien, et j'espère que je trouverai un travail à Rome. Dans l'idéal, j'habiterais dans un petit appartement avec un balcon, au centre-ville. Le week-end, je ferais du bénévolat avec des personnes âgées. Si je pouvais, je créerais aussi un petit restaurant. À l'avenir, je ne veux pas une grande carrière : mon but, c'est d'être heureuse. Emma*

(My ideal life. Next year, I'm planning to change jobs. I intend to learn Italian, and I hope I'll find a job in Rome. Ideally, I'd live in a little flat with a balcony, in the town centre. At the weekend, I'd do voluntary work with elderly people. If I could, I'd also set up a little restaurant. In future, I don't want a big career: my aim is to be happy. Emma)

<Callout type="warning" title="Espérer que takes an ordinary verb">
  If you speak Spanish or Italian, you may expect a special verb form after "hope". French doesn't use one here: **J'espère que tu viendras**, with the futur. For something happening now, use the present: *J'espère que tu vas bien.* Keep the conditionnel for dreams, and don't use it after *espérer que*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Climb the ladder">
  Take one plan, such as moving to a new town, and say it five ways, from *je vais* down to *si je pouvais*. Then ask yourself honestly where your real plans sit on that ladder. It's good grammar practice, and it often tells you something about your plans, too.
</Callout>
