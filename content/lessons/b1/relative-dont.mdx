By the end of this lesson you'll be able to join two sentences with *dont* when the verb takes *de*, and use it to say "whose".

## Why you need dont

In <LessonLink id="a2-relative-qui-que-ou" text="the lesson on qui, que and où" />, *que* stood for a direct object: *le film que je regarde* (the film I'm watching). But many verbs take *de*, like *parler de* and *avoir besoin de*, which you met in <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the lesson on en" />. With these verbs, *que* doesn't work. You need **dont**, which stands for *de* + the noun.

<VocabTable group="core" />

<Example fr="J'ai besoin de ce livre. C'est le livre dont j'ai besoin." en="I need this book. It's the book I need." />
<Example fr="Le film dont tu parles est génial." en="The film you're talking about is brilliant." />

*Dont* never changes. It's the same for masculine and feminine, singular and plural, people and things. English often puts the little word at the end ("the film you're talking *about*"), but French keeps it inside *dont*, at the start.

## Verbs with de

Here are some common verbs and expressions that take *de*. Each of them can be followed by *dont*.

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

<Example fr="C'est la ville dont je rêve." en="It's the town I dream of." />
<Example fr="Voici l'outil dont je me sers." en="Here's the tool I use." />
<Example fr="La personne dont je m'occupe a quatre-vingts ans." en="The person I look after is eighty." />
<Example fr="C'est un projet dont il est responsable." en="It's a project he's in charge of." />
<Example fr="C'est une chose dont j'ai honte." en="It's something I'm ashamed of." />

*Se souvenir* works like *venir*: *je me souviens*, *nous nous souvenons*. *Se servir* works like *partir*: *je me sers*, *nous nous servons*. Both use *être* in the passé composé, like every reflexive verb: *je me suis souvenu*.

## Whose: dont with a noun

*Dont* also means "whose". Put the article and the noun straight after it, then the verb.

<Example fr="J'ai un ami dont le père est médecin." en="I have a friend whose father is a doctor." />
<Example fr="C'est la fille dont la sœur habite à Montréal." en="That's the girl whose sister lives in Montréal." />

When the noun is the object of the verb, it moves after the verb: *C'est la femme dont je connais les enfants* (That's the woman whose children I know). Don't add a possessive: it's *dont le père*, not *dont son père*.

## Que or dont?

Look at the verb in the second half. Does it take a direct object, or *de*?

| Verb | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *regarder* (something) | *que* | *le film que je regarde* (the film I'm watching) |
| *parler de* (something) | *dont* | *le film dont je parle* (the film I'm talking about) |
| *aimer* (something) | *que* | *la ville que j'aime* (the town I love) |
| *rêver de* (something) | *dont* | *la ville dont je rêve* (the town I dream of) |

After other prepositions, such as *avec* or *pour*, French uses different words, which you'll meet in <LessonLink id="b2-relative-prepositions" text="the lesson on relative pronouns after prepositions" />.

## En français

A grandmother is showing her grandson some old photos.

- *Grand-mère, c'est qui sur cette photo ?* (Grandma, who's that in this photo?)
- *C'est mon amie Jeanne. C'est l'amie dont je te parle souvent.* (That's my friend Jeanne. She's the friend I often tell you about.)
- *Et la maison derrière vous ?* (And the house behind you?)
- *C'est la maison dont je rêvais quand j'étais petite.* (That's the house I dreamed of when I was little.)
- *Tu te souviens bien de cette époque ?* (Do you remember that time well?)
- *Oui, c'est une époque dont je me souviens très bien. On était jeunes !* (Yes, it's a time I remember very well. We were young!)
- *Et ce monsieur, avec le chapeau ?* (And that man, with the hat?)
- *C'est Henri, un collègue de ton grand-père. C'est l'homme dont je t'ai parlé hier.* (That's Henri, a colleague of your grandfather's. He's the man I told you about yesterday.)
- *L'homme dont le fils est médecin ?* (The man whose son is a doctor?)
- *Exactement !* (Exactly!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Dont already contains de">
  Because *dont* stands for *de* + the noun, don't add *de* again at the end: **le livre dont j'ai besoin**, not *le livre dont j'ai besoin de*. And don't use *que* with a verb that takes *de*: *le film que je parle* is wrong. It's **le film dont je parle**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Say the second sentence on its own">
  If you're not sure which pronoun you need, say the second half as a full sentence first: *Je parle de ce film.* Can you see *de* + the noun? Then it's *dont*. *Je regarde ce film* has no *de*, so it's *que*. It takes two seconds, and it works every time.
</Callout>
