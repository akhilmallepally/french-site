## Which mood?

Most of the time, the word before *que* decides. Use this table to check.

| After… | Mood | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *il faut que*, *il est important que*, *il vaut mieux que* | subjunctive | *Il faut que tu viennes.* |
| *vouloir que*, *aimer que*, *préférer que*, *souhaiter que* | subjunctive | *Je veux que tu viennes.* |
| *être content / triste / surpris que*, *avoir peur que*, *c'est dommage que* | subjunctive | *Je suis content que tu viennes.* |
| *douter que*, *je ne pense pas que*, *je ne suis pas sûr que* | subjunctive | *Je doute qu'il vienne.* |
| *je pense que*, *je crois que*, *je suis sûr que*, *il est clair que* | indicative | *Je pense qu'il viendra.* |
| *espérer que* | indicative | *J'espère qu'il viendra.* |
| the same subject in both halves | infinitive | *Je veux venir.* |

## A model message

Inès is organizing a surprise party for her friend Léa.

*Salut à tous !*

*Samedi, c'est l'anniversaire de Léa, et je voudrais organiser une fête surprise chez moi. Il faut que vous gardiez le secret ! Il faut aussi que vous soyez chez moi à 19 heures, avant Léa. Il y aura vingt invités.*

*Marc, j'aimerais que tu te charges du gâteau. Nadia, est-ce que tu peux te charger de la musique ? Je suis sûre que Léa sera très surprise. Je doute qu'elle devine quelque chose : elle pense qu'on va juste dîner au restaurant.*

*À la fin du repas, elle soufflera les bougies, et je suis contente que nous puissions fêter ça ensemble. Mais il ne faut pas qu'elle sache !*

*Bises, Inès*

(Hi everyone! On Saturday it's Léa's birthday, and I'd like to organize a surprise party at my place. You have to keep the secret! You also have to be at my place at 7 pm, before Léa. There'll be twenty guests. Marc, I'd like you to take care of the cake. Nadia, can you take care of the music? I'm sure Léa will be very surprised. I doubt she'll guess anything: she thinks we're just going to have dinner at a restaurant. At the end of the meal, she'll blow out the candles, and I'm happy that we can celebrate together. But she mustn't find out! Love, Inès)

## How the message works

Look at how Inès chooses each mood:

- In *je voudrais organiser*, she organizes it herself, so it's the infinitive.
- In *il faut que vous gardiez* and *j'aimerais que tu te charges*, she's asking other people, so it's the subjunctive.
- In *je suis sûre que Léa sera*, she's certain, so it's the indicative, in the futur.
- In *je doute qu'elle devine*, she expresses doubt, so it's the subjunctive.
- In *elle pense qu'on va dîner*, what Léa thinks is true for her, so it's the indicative.
- In *il ne faut pas qu'elle sache*, it's necessity, with the irregular subjunctive of *savoir*.

The same message uses all three choices. That's normal: real French moves between moods from one sentence to the next.

## Your turn

Write a message of eight to ten sentences, on paper or in a notes app, organizing an event: a party, a trip or a surprise for someone. Include:

1. two sentences with *il faut que* or *il est important que*
2. one request with *j'aimerais que* or *je voudrais que*
3. one feeling with *je suis content que* or *c'est dommage que*
4. one sentence with *je suis sûr que* (indicative) and one with *je doute que* (subjunctive)
5. one sentence with the infinitive, because you do the action yourself

## Check your work

- After every *que*, did you choose the mood from the table?
- With the same subject in both halves, did you use the infinitive instead of *que*?
- Did you use the irregular forms correctly: *soyez*, *ayez*, *fassiez*, *puissiez*?
- After *espérer que* and *je pense que*, is the verb in the indicative?

<Callout type="warning" title="Que doesn't always mean subjunctive">
  After a unit about the subjunctive, it's easy to put it after every *que*. But many verbs take the indicative: **je pense qu'elle viendra**, **j'espère que tu vas bien**, **je suis sûr qu'il a raison**, **il est clair que c'est vrai**. The subjunctive comes after necessity, wishes, feelings and doubt, not after *que* by itself.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Underline the trigger">
  When you check your message, underline the words just before each *que*: *il faut*, *je veux*, *je pense*, *je doute*. Then look each one up in the table above. The decision about the mood is made there, before you even reach the verb, so checking the trigger is faster than checking the verb.
</Callout>
