By the end of this lesson you'll be able to give your opinion in French, back it up with a comparison, and ask other people what they think.

## Announcing an opinion

In <LessonLink id="a1-likes-and-dislikes" text="the lesson on likes and dislikes" /> you said what you liked with *j'aime* and *j'adore*. An opinion goes one step further. It says what you think is true, good or bad. The easiest way in is a short phrase that announces it.

<VocabTable group="markers" />

<Example fr="À mon avis, ce café est trop cher." en="In my opinion, this café is too expensive." />
<Example fr="Franchement, la série est géniale !" en="Honestly, the series is brilliant!" />

*Pour moi*, which you used to order in <LessonLink id="a1-ordering-at-a-cafe" text="the lesson on ordering at a café" />, can introduce an opinion too: *Pour moi, c'est le meilleur restaurant de la ville* (For me, it's the best restaurant in town). *À vrai dire* (to tell the truth) often comes before an opinion the other person may not expect.

## Penser, croire and trouver

Three verbs introduce a whole opinion with *que*. After *que*, you use an ordinary sentence in the present, the passé composé or the futur.

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

<Example fr="Je pense que Lyon est plus calme que Paris." en="I think Lyon is quieter than Paris." />
<Example fr="Je crois que le magasin ferme à 19 heures." en="I think the shop closes at 7 pm." />
<Example fr="Je trouve que tu travailles trop." en="I think you work too much." />

Each verb has its own shade of meaning. *Je pense que* is the neutral choice. *Je crois que* can sound a little less certain, close to "I believe". *Je trouve que* suggests an opinion based on your own experience: you've been to the café, seen the film or met the person.

*Trouver* also works without *que*, with a noun and then an adjective. The adjective agrees with the noun: *Je trouve ce film ennuyeux* (I find this film boring), *Je trouve cette série géniale* (I think this series is brilliant).

## Asking for an opinion

<VocabTable group="asking" />

*Qu'est-ce que tu en penses ?* uses *en*, because you think *of* something: *penser de*. *En* replaces *de* + the thing, as in <LessonLink id="a2-pronoun-en" text="the lesson on en" />. *Tu trouves ?* gently questions what someone has just said. *Ça dépend* lets you avoid a clear answer, and you can add what it depends on: *Ça dépend du prix* (It depends on the price).

## Describing what you think

<VocabTable group="words" />

An opinion is stronger with a comparison, from <LessonLink id="a2-comparatives" text="the lesson on comparatives" />:

<Example fr="Je trouve que le train est plus pratique que la voiture." en="I think the train is more practical than the car." />
<Example fr="À mon avis, la série est moins originale que le livre." en="In my opinion, the series is less original than the book." />

For a negative opinion, keep the opinion verb positive and put the negative after *que*: *Je trouve que ce n'est pas très original* (I don't think it's very original). *Je ne pense pas que* needs a different verb form, which you'll learn in <LessonLink id="b1-expressing-doubt-certainty" text="the lesson on doubt and certainty" />.

## En français

- *Tu es allé au nouveau café, à côté de la gare ?* (Have you been to the new café next to the station?)
- *Oui, hier. Franchement, je le trouve génial.* (Yes, yesterday. Honestly, I think it's brilliant.)
- *Tu trouves ? À mon avis, c'est un peu cher.* (Do you think so? In my opinion, it's a bit expensive.)
- *Ça dépend. Le café n'est pas cher, mais les gâteaux sont chers.* (It depends. The coffee isn't expensive, but the cakes are.)
- *Et la série que tu regardes, qu'est-ce que tu en penses ?* (And the series you're watching, what do you think of it?)
- *Je pense que c'est la meilleure série de l'année.* (I think it's the best series of the year.)
- *Pour moi, elle est un peu longue.* (For me, it's a bit long.)
- *Longue ? Selon moi, l'histoire est très originale.* (Long? In my view, the story is very original.)
- *Alors, on la regarde ce soir, et tu me donnes ton avis ?* (So shall we watch it tonight, and you give me your opinion?)

## A group chat

Four friends are deciding what to do on Saturday.

- **Inès:** *Salut ! Qu'est-ce qu'on fait samedi ? J'ai deux idées : le musée ou le cinéma.* (Hi! What shall we do on Saturday? I've got two ideas: the museum or the cinema.)
- **Hugo:** *À mon avis, le musée est plus intéressant.* (In my opinion, the museum is more interesting.)
- **Camille:** *Franchement, je trouve les musées un peu ennuyeux. Je pense qu'un film, c'est plus sympa.* (Honestly, I find museums a bit boring. I think a film is more fun.)
- **Malik:** *Pour moi, ça dépend du temps. Il va pleuvoir samedi, alors je crois que le cinéma est une bonne idée.* (For me, it depends on the weather. It's going to rain on Saturday, so I think the cinema is a good idea.)
- **Inès:** *D'après moi, on peut faire les deux : le musée le matin et le cinéma le soir. Qu'est-ce que vous en pensez ?* (As I see it, we can do both: the museum in the morning and the cinema in the evening. What do you think?)
- **Hugo:** *Génial !* (Brilliant!)
- **Camille:** *D'accord, mais je choisis le film !* (OK, but I'm choosing the film!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't drop que">
  English often leaves out "that": "I think it's good". French can't leave out *que*: **Je pense que c'est bien**, not *je pense c'est bien*. The same goes for *croire* and *trouver*. For a short answer, *que* stays too: **Je pense que oui** (I think so) and **Je crois que non** (I don't think so).
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Rotate your openers">
  If every sentence starts with *je pense que*, you'll sound like you're reading a list. Choose three openers you like, such as *à mon avis*, *je trouve que* and *selon moi*, and take turns with them. Then add *franchement* or *à vrai dire* when you want an opinion to stand out.
</Callout>
