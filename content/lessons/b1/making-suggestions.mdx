By the end of this lesson you'll be able to suggest a plan in several ways, and accept or turn down other people's ideas politely.

## Suggesting with si on

You know *si* + imparfait for things that aren't true, from <LessonLink id="b1-si-imparfait-conditionnel" text="the lesson on si with the imparfait and the conditionnel" />: *Si j'avais le temps, je voyagerais*. On its own, as a question, *Si on* + imparfait is something else: a friendly suggestion, with no second half.

<VocabTable group="suggest" />

<Example fr="Si on allait au cinéma ce soir ?" en="How about going to the cinema tonight?" />
<Example fr="Et si on mangeait au restaurant ?" en="What if we ate at a restaurant?" />
<Example fr="Si on invitait tes parents samedi ?" en="Shall we invite your parents on Saturday?" />

The imparfait here doesn't talk about the past. It makes the idea sound soft and open, as if you're only imagining it. *Et si on… ?* works the same way, and it's often used when you've just had a new idea.

## Ça te dirait de… ? and on pourrait…

You know *Ça te dit de… ?* from <LessonLink id="a2-invitations" text="the lesson on invitations" />. With the conditionnel, *Ça te dirait de… ?*, it becomes a little softer and more polite. To a group, or to someone you call *vous*, it's *Ça vous dirait de… ?*

*On pourrait* + an infinitive (we could…) is the simplest way to put an idea on the table, and it fits any situation, from friends to colleagues.

<Example fr="Ça te dirait de faire un pique-nique dimanche ?" en="Would you fancy having a picnic on Sunday?" />
<Example fr="Ça vous dirait de venir dîner chez nous ?" en="Would you like to come to dinner at our place?" />
<Example fr="On pourrait faire une balade au bord de la mer." en="We could go for a walk by the sea." />

*J'ai une idée !* is a natural way to start: *J'ai une idée ! Si on allait à la plage ?*

## Saying yes

<VocabTable group="answer" />

You know *Bonne idée !* and *Avec plaisir !* from the A2 lesson. *Ça marche !* and *Je suis partant* are more informal, and very common between friends. *C'est parti !* is what you say when the decision is made and you're starting: *Allez, c'est parti !* *Pourquoi pas ?* shows you're open to the idea, without being very excited.

<Example fr="Un pique-nique ? Ça marche !" en="A picnic? Deal!" />
<Example fr="Une balade à vélo ? Je suis partante !" en="A bike ride? I'm up for it!" />

## Saying no politely

To turn down an idea, *Ça ne me dit rien* (I don't fancy it) is informal but not rude. Softer still is *Je préférerais…*, the conditionnel of *préférer*, followed by your own idea. Remember too *Je ne suis pas libre* and *Peut-être une autre fois* from A2.

<Example fr="Le cinéma ? Ça ne me dit rien. Je préférerais aller au restaurant." en="The cinema? I don't fancy it. I'd rather go to a restaurant." />

*Ça ne me dit rien* has a second meaning: "it doesn't ring a bell". *Le nom Garnier ? Ça ne me dit rien.* The context makes it clear.

## Plans

<VocabTable group="plans" />

*Un plan* is an informal word for something to do: *J'ai un bon plan pour samedi* (I've got a good idea for Saturday). *Une balade* can be on foot, by bike or by car: *une balade à vélo*, *une balade en voiture*.

## En français

Chloé and Max are planning their Saturday.

- *Max, tu fais quoi samedi ?* (Max, what are you doing on Saturday?)
- *Rien de spécial. Tu as un plan ?* (Nothing special. Have you got a plan?)
- *J'ai une idée ! Si on allait à la plage ?* (I've got an idea! How about going to the beach?)
- *Bof, il va pleuvoir. Ça ne me dit rien.* (Meh, it's going to rain. I don't fancy it.)
- *Alors on pourrait aller au musée, puis au restaurant ?* (Then we could go to the museum, and then to a restaurant?)
- *Le musée, pourquoi pas. Ça te dirait de manger dans un bouchon ?* (The museum, why not. Would you fancy eating in a bouchon?)
- *Oui, je suis partante ! On invite Inès ?* (Yes, I'm up for it! Shall we invite Inès?)
- *Ça marche. Je lui envoie un message.* (Deal. I'll send her a message.)
- *Super. Samedi, musée et bouchon, c'est parti !* (Great. Saturday, museum and bouchon, here we go!)

## A group chat

Inès organizes the dinner in the group chat.

- **Inès :** *Salut ! Ça vous dirait de dîner ensemble samedi soir ?*
- **Max :** *Je suis partant ! Où ?*
- **Inès :** *J'ai une idée : si on allait dans un bouchon lyonnais ? Il y en a un très bon près de la gare.*
- **Chloé :** *Super idée ! Mais on pourrait réserver ? Le samedi, c'est toujours plein.*
- **Inès :** *Ça marche, je réserve pour trois à 20 heures.*
- **Max :** *Et après, si on faisait une balade au bord du Rhône ?*
- **Chloé :** *Pourquoi pas ! C'est parti pour samedi !*

(Inès: Hi! Would you fancy having dinner together on Saturday evening? Max: I'm up for it! Where? Inès: I've got an idea: how about going to a Lyon bouchon? There's a really good one near the station. Chloé: Great idea! But could we book? On Saturdays it's always full. Inès: Deal, I'll book for three at 8 pm. Max: And afterwards, what if we went for a walk along the Rhône? Chloé: Why not! Saturday it is!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Si on allait, with the imparfait">
  A suggestion with *si* uses the imparfait: **Si on allait au cinéma ?**, **Et si on mangeait ici ?** With the present, *si on va au cinéma…* sounds like the start of a condition, and your friend will wait for the second half. Keep the present for real conditions: *Si on va au cinéma, je t'appelle.*
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Turn down, then suggest">
  Whenever you say no to an idea, follow it at once with one of your own: *Ça ne me dit rien, mais on pourrait…* or *Je préférerais…* It keeps the conversation going, it sounds friendly, and it gives you twice the practice: one refusal and one suggestion every time.
</Callout>
