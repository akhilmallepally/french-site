By the end of this lesson you'll be able to form the subjunctive of the most common irregular verbs, the ones you'll need most often.

## Being and having

In <LessonLink id="b1-subjonctif-formation" text="the lesson on regular subjunctive forms" />, you built the subjunctive from the *ils* form. A few very common verbs don't follow that rule. The two most important are *être* and *avoir*, and they have their own endings too.

<VocabTable group="etre-avoir" />

| | être | avoir |
|---|---|---|
| *que je* / *que j'* | sois | aie |
| *que tu* | sois | aies |
| *qu'il* / *qu'elle* | soit | ait |
| *que nous* | soyons | ayons |
| *que vous* | soyez | ayez |
| *qu'ils* / *qu'elles* | soient | aient |

<Example fr="Il faut que tu sois prêt à huit heures." en="You have to be ready at eight." />
<Example fr="Il faut qu'elle ait sa carte d'identité." en="She has to have her identity card." />
<Example fr="Il faut que nous soyons à la gare à midi." en="We have to be at the station at noon." />

Most of the *avoir* forms sound like *ai*: *que j'aie*, *qu'il ait* and *qu'ils aient* all sound the same. The *être* forms *sois*, *soit* and *soient* sound the same too.

You've already met some of these forms. *Sois* and *ayez* look exactly like the imperative in <LessonLink id="a1-imperative" text="the lesson on the imperative" />: *Sois sage !* (Be good!) Imperative and subjunctive share these forms.

## Five more irregular verbs

These verbs take the normal endings, but with a stem you have to learn. *Aller* and *vouloir* have two stems, like the verbs at the end of the last lesson. *Faire*, *pouvoir* and *savoir* keep one stem all the way through.

<VocabTable group="others" />

| | aller | faire | pouvoir | savoir | vouloir |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *que je* | aille | fasse | puisse | sache | veuille |
| *que nous* | allions | fassions | puissions | sachions | voulions |

<Example fr="Il faut que j'aille à la banque." en="I have to go to the bank." />
<Example fr="Il faut que vous fassiez vos valises ce soir." en="You have to pack tonight." />
<Example fr="Il faut qu'il puisse venir." en="He has to be able to come." />
<Example fr="Il faut que tu saches la vérité." en="You have to know the truth." />

*Pleuvoir* only has one form, *qu'il pleuve*, because only *il* can rain. *Falloir* has one too, *qu'il faille*, but you'll rarely need it. You'll use it with the reactions in <LessonLink id="b1-subjonctif-wishes-emotions" text="the lesson on wishes and emotions" />.

## Present or subjunctive?

For these verbs, the subjunctive doesn't sound like the present, so it's easy to hear when someone uses it. Compare:

| Present | Subjunctive |
|---|---|
| *tu es* | *que tu sois* |
| *il a* | *qu'il ait* |
| *je vais* | *que j'aille* |
| *je fais* | *que je fasse* |
| *je peux* | *que je puisse* |
| *je sais* | *que je sache* |

Because the forms are so different, these verbs are the best way to practise noticing the subjunctive. Every time you hear *sois*, *aille* or *fasse*, look back at the beginning of the sentence and find the word that triggered it.

## En français

Two friends are getting ready for a weekend in Bordeaux.

- *Tu es prête pour demain ?* (Are you ready for tomorrow?)
- *Pas encore. Il faut que je fasse ma valise ce soir.* (Not yet. I have to pack tonight.)
- *Et n'oublie pas : il faut que tu sois à la gare à huit heures.* (And don't forget: you have to be at the station at eight.)
- *À huit heures ? C'est tôt !* (At eight? That's early!)
- *Oui, et il faut que tu aies ta carte d'identité.* (Yes, and you have to have your identity card.)
- *Pourquoi ? On reste en France !* (Why? We're staying in France!)
- *Pour l'hôtel. Ils demandent une carte d'identité.* (For the hotel. They ask for an identity card.)
- *D'accord. Il faut que j'aille à la banque, aussi ?* (OK. Do I have to go to the bank too?)
- *Non, on peut payer par carte. Il faut juste que tu puisses te lever tôt !* (No, we can pay by card. You just have to be able to get up early!)

<Callout type="warning" title="No i after the y">
  The *nous* and *vous* forms of *être* and *avoir* are the exception to the "looks like the imparfait" rule from the last lesson. It's **que nous soyons** and **que vous ayez**, with no *i* after the *y*: not *soyions* or *ayiez*. The same goes for *que nous ayons*.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Hang them on the imperative">
  *Sois*, *soyons*, *soyez*, *aie* and *ayez* are also the imperative forms of *être* and *avoir*, as in *Soyez à l'heure !* (Be on time!). If you remember the commands, you already know half of these subjunctives. Learn the remaining forms (*soit*, *soient*, *ait*, *aient*) as a small extra list.
</Callout>
