By the end of this lesson you'll be able to give advice to a friend or to someone you call *vous*, and accept or politely decline someone else's advice.

## Friendly advice

In <LessonLink id="a2-il-faut-and-advice" text="the lesson on il faut and simple advice" />, you used *il faut* and *tu dois*. They're quite strong: they say what's necessary. The conditionnel, from <LessonLink id="b1-conditionnel-present" text="the lesson on the conditionnel présent" />, makes advice softer.

<VocabTable group="friendly" />

<Example fr="Tu devrais dormir plus." en="You should sleep more." />
<Example fr="À ta place, je parlerais à mon directeur." en="If I were you, I'd talk to my manager." />
<Example fr="Ce serait une bonne idée de partir tôt." en="It would be a good idea to leave early." />

*À ta place* (in your place) and *si j'étais toi*, from <LessonLink id="b1-si-imparfait-conditionnel" text="the lesson on si with the imparfait" />, mean the same thing. Both are followed by *je* + the conditionnel. *Tu pourrais* gives an idea rather than advice: *Tu pourrais demander à Paul* (You could ask Paul).

## Polite advice

With someone you call *vous*, or to sound a little more careful, use these.

<VocabTable group="polite" />

<Example fr="Je vous conseille de réserver à l'avance." en="I advise you to book in advance." />
<Example fr="Il vaudrait mieux prendre le train." en="It would be better to take the train." />
<Example fr="N'hésitez pas à m'appeler." en="Feel free to call me." />

## Advice not to do something

To advise someone *not* to do something, keep both parts of the negative together, in front of the infinitive: *ne pas partir*.

<Example fr="Je te conseille de ne pas partir ce soir." en="I advise you not to leave tonight." />
<Example fr="Essaie de ne pas oublier tes clés." en="Try not to forget your keys." />
<Example fr="Il vaudrait mieux ne pas boire de café le soir." en="It would be better not to drink coffee in the evening." />

This is different from an ordinary negative, where *ne* and *pas* go around the verb: *je ne pars pas*. With an infinitive, they don't split.

## How strong is it?

From the strongest to the gentlest, here's what each form tells the other person.

| Form | How it sounds |
|---|---|
| *Il faut partir.* / *Tu dois partir.* | Necessary: there's no real choice. |
| *Tu devrais partir.* / *Je te conseille de partir.* | Advice: the decision is still theirs. |
| *Tu pourrais partir.* / *Ce serait une bonne idée de partir.* | A suggestion, among other possible ones. |
| *N'hésite pas à partir.* | Permission: go ahead if you want to. |

With friends, the middle two rows are the most natural. *Il faut* can sound bossy unless the situation really is urgent.

## Asking for advice, and reacting

<VocabTable group="words" />

<Example fr="Je peux te demander conseil ?" en="Can I ask you for some advice?" />
<Example fr="Qu'est-ce que tu ferais à ma place ?" en="What would you do in my place?" />
<Example fr="Merci pour le conseil. Je vais essayer." en="Thanks for the advice. I'll try." />

*Un conseil* is one piece of advice, and you can count it: *deux conseils*. To turn advice down politely, give a reason: *C'est une bonne idée, mais je n'ai pas le temps.*

## En français

Malik is a student, and he's worried about his exams.

- *Ça ne va pas ? Qu'est-ce que tu as ?* (Are you OK? What's wrong?)
- *Je suis stressé. J'ai trois examens la semaine prochaine.* (I'm stressed. I've got three exams next week.)
- *À ta place, je travaillerais un peu tous les jours.* (If I were you, I'd work a little every day.)
- *Oui, mais je n'ai pas le temps !* (Yes, but I don't have time!)
- *Tu devrais dormir plus. Le sommeil, c'est important.* (You should sleep more. Sleep is important.)
- *Je sais, mais je ne dors pas bien.* (I know, but I don't sleep well.)
- *Je te conseille de ne pas boire de café le soir.* (I'd advise you not to drink coffee in the evening.)
- *C'est vrai, j'en bois beaucoup.* (That's true, I drink a lot of it.)
- *Et si j'étais toi, je ferais du sport. Ça aide à se détendre.* (And if I were you, I'd do some sport. It helps you relax.)
- *Bonne idée. Je vais essayer !* (Good idea. I'll try!)

## A letter to an advice column

*Bonjour, je travaille dans une grande entreprise depuis deux ans. J'ai beaucoup de travail et je suis très stressée. Le soir, je ne dors pas bien. Qu'est-ce que je devrais faire ? Camille, 28 ans*

(Hello, I've been working for a big company for two years. I have a lot of work and I'm very stressed. In the evening, I don't sleep well. What should I do? Camille, 28)

*Chère Camille, d'abord, vous devriez parler à votre directrice : il y a peut-être une solution. À votre place, je ne travaillerais pas le soir. Il vaudrait mieux ne pas lire vos messages après 20 heures. De plus, je vous conseille de faire du sport ou de la musique pour vous détendre. Et n'hésitez pas à demander conseil à un médecin si le stress continue. Cordialement, Sophie*

(Dear Camille, first, you should talk to your manager: there may be a solution. If I were you, I wouldn't work in the evening. It would be better not to read your messages after 8 pm. What's more, I'd advise you to do some sport or music to relax. And feel free to ask a doctor for advice if the stress continues. Kind regards, Sophie)

<Callout type="warning" title="Ne pas stays together">
  With an infinitive, the negative doesn't wrap around the verb. It's **de ne pas partir**, not *de ne partir pas*, and it's **essaie de ne pas oublier**, not *essaie de pas oublier*. Dropping the *ne* is common in casual speech with a conjugated verb, but keep it in front of an infinitive.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask before you advise">
  Advice lands better when the other person feels you've listened. Before *tu devrais…*, ask one question: *Tu as essayé de dormir plus tôt ?* (Have you tried going to bed earlier?) If they say yes, you've avoided giving advice they've already tried, and your next suggestion sounds more thoughtful.
</Callout>
