By the end of this lesson you'll be able to choose between the infinitive and *que* + the subjunctive, which is one of the most common decisions in French.

## One subject or two?

In the last lesson, you used *vouloir que*, *être content que* and *avoir peur que* with a second person: *Je veux que tu partes.* But when both verbs are about the same person, French doesn't use *que* at all. It uses the infinitive.

| One subject: infinitive | Two subjects: que + subjunctive |
|---|---|
| *Je veux partir.* (I want to leave.) | *Je veux que tu partes.* (I want you to leave.) |
| *Elle préfère rester.* (She prefers to stay.) | *Elle préfère que nous restions.* (She'd rather we stayed.) |
| *J'aimerais venir.* (I'd like to come.) | *J'aimerais que vous veniez.* (I'd like you to come.) |

The question is always: who does the second action? If it's the same person as the first verb, use the infinitive.

<Example fr="Je veux vivre à Lyon." en="I want to live in Lyon." />
<Example fr="Je veux que nous vivions à Lyon." en="I want us to live in Lyon." />

## Feelings: de or que

After *être* + an adjective of feeling, and after *avoir peur*, the infinitive needs *de*. The subjunctive needs *que*.

| One subject | Two subjects |
|---|---|
| *Je suis content de partir.* | *Je suis content que tu partes.* |
| *Elle est triste de quitter Paris.* | *Elle est triste que tu quittes Paris.* |
| *J'ai peur d'être en retard.* | *J'ai peur que tu sois en retard.* |
| *Je regrette de ne pas venir.* | *Je regrette que tu ne viennes pas.* |

With the infinitive, the negative stays together: *de ne pas venir*, as in <LessonLink id="b1-giving-advice" text="the lesson on giving advice" />.

## Il faut: general or personal

You've already seen this choice in <LessonLink id="b1-subjonctif-necessity" text="the lesson on il faut que" />. *Il faut* + infinitive is general, or the person is obvious. *Il faut que* names the person.

<Example fr="Il faut se décider." en="We have to make up our minds." />
<Example fr="Il faut que tu te décides." en="You have to make up your mind." />

## Verbs that only take the infinitive

Some verbs are always followed by an infinitive, so the choice doesn't arise. Many take *de* or *à*, like the verbs in <LessonLink id="a2-verbs-with-a-and-de" text="the lesson on verbs + à / de + infinitive" />.

<VocabTable group="verbs" />

<Example fr="Il a accepté de venir." en="He agreed to come." />
<Example fr="Elle m'a proposé de partir ensemble." en="She suggested we leave together." />
<Example fr="Je n'ose pas demander." en="I don't dare ask." />
<Example fr="Préviens-moi si tu es en retard." en="Let me know if you're late." />
<Example fr="Ne t'inquiète pas, je vais faire un effort." en="Don't worry, I'll make an effort." />

## En français

Inès and Hugo are deciding whether to move to another city.

- *J'ai trouvé un travail à Lyon. Je veux partir.* (I've found a job in Lyon. I want to go.)
- *Tu veux partir seule, ou tu veux qu'on parte ensemble ?* (Do you want to go alone, or do you want us to go together?)
- *Ensemble, bien sûr ! Je veux que tu viennes avec moi.* (Together, of course! I want you to come with me.)
- *Je suis content que tu me demandes ça, mais j'ai peur de ne pas trouver de travail.* (I'm happy you're asking me that, but I'm afraid I won't find work.)
- *Ne t'inquiète pas. Tu peux chercher avant de partir.* (Don't worry. You can look before we leave.)
- *D'accord, mais j'ai une condition : je veux qu'on revienne voir mes parents tous les mois.* (OK, but I've got one condition: I want us to come back and see my parents every month.)
- *Tous les mois ? Ça fait beaucoup. Je propose de venir tous les deux mois.* (Every month? That's a lot. I suggest coming every two months.)
- *Bon, c'est un compromis. J'accepte de partir !* (Fine, it's a compromise. I agree to go!)
- *Génial ! Il faut maintenant qu'on prévienne nos amis.* (Brilliant! Now we have to let our friends know.)

<Callout type="warning" title="No que with the same subject">
  English speakers sometimes build a sentence like *je veux que je parte*, because it follows the pattern of *je veux que tu partes*. French never uses *que* when the two verbs share a subject. It's **je veux partir**, **je suis content d'être là** and **j'ai peur d'être en retard**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask 'who?' twice">
  Before you write the second half of the sentence, ask two questions: who does the first verb, and who does the second one? If the answer is the same person, use the infinitive. If it's two different people, use *que* and the subjunctive. It takes two seconds, and it settles almost every case.
</Callout>
