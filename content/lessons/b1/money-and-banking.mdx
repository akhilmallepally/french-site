By the end of this lesson you'll be able to open a bank account, pay in different ways, and talk about saving, spending and borrowing.

## Your bank account

You know how to ask about prices and pay in a shop from <LessonLink id="a1-shopping-for-clothes" text="the lesson on shopping for clothes" />. Now you need the words for what happens behind the payment.

<VocabTable group="account" />

<Example fr="Je voudrais ouvrir un compte en banque." en="I'd like to open a bank account." />
<Example fr="Ma conseillère m'a expliqué les frais." en="My adviser explained the fees to me." />
<Example fr="J'ai fait un virement à mon propriétaire pour le loyer." en="I made a transfer to my landlord for the rent." />

*Un compte* on its own can be any account, including an online account. Add *en banque* when you need to be clear. In France, many people pay their rent by *virement*, and some bills are paid automatically from the account each month. You can check everything on *ton relevé de compte*, on paper or in your bank's app.

## Paying

<VocabTable group="paying" />

The prepositions are different: you pay *par carte*, but *en espèces*. In French shops, card payment is very common, even for small amounts, and *sans contact* is everywhere.

<Example fr="Vous payez par carte ou en espèces ?" en="Are you paying by card or cash?" />
<Example fr="Je peux payer sans contact ?" en="Can I pay contactless?" />
<Example fr="Tapez votre code, s'il vous plaît." en="Enter your PIN, please." />
<Example fr="Je vais retirer de l'argent au distributeur." en="I'm going to take some money out at the cash machine." />

In everyday speech, you'll often hear *la carte bleue*, or *la CB*, for any bank card, whatever its colour. *Le code* is short for *le code secret*, and that's the word cashiers use.

## Your budget

<VocabTable group="budget" />

*Dépenser* is for money, and *passer* is for time: *j'ai dépensé 50 euros*, but *j'ai passé deux heures*. *Une dépense* is one expense; *les dépenses* are your spending in general.

<Example fr="Je dépense trop d'argent au restaurant." en="I spend too much money in restaurants." />
<Example fr="Le loyer, c'est ma plus grande dépense." en="Rent is my biggest expense." />
<Example fr="Je fais des économies pour mes vacances." en="I'm saving up for my holiday." />
<Example fr="J'ai seulement 20 euros sur mon compte." en="I've only got 20 euros in my account." />

You know *économiser* from <LessonLink id="b1-environment" text="the lesson on the environment" />, for saving energy and water. *Faire des économies* is the most common way to say you're saving money. *Seulement* means "only", and it usually goes after the verb or before the amount.

*Être à découvert* means there's less than nothing in your account. The bank usually charges *des frais* for it, so it's best avoided.

## Borrowing

<VocabTable group="borrow" />

<Example fr="Pour acheter un appartement, il faut souvent emprunter." en="To buy a flat, you often have to borrow money." />
<Example fr="La banque m'a fait un prêt de 10 000 euros." en="The bank gave me a loan of 10,000 euros." />
<Example fr="Je rembourse 200 euros par mois." en="I pay back 200 euros a month." />

*Emprunter* is to borrow, and you borrow *à* someone: *J'emprunte de l'argent à ma sœur*. *Rembourser* means to pay someone back, and also to give a customer their money back, as you'll see in <LessonLink id="b1-making-a-complaint" text="the lesson on making a complaint" />.

## En français

Lucas, a student, is opening an account at a bank.

- *Bonjour, madame. Je voudrais ouvrir un compte en banque.* (Hello. I'd like to open a bank account.)
- *Bonjour. Bien sûr. Vous êtes étudiant ?* (Hello. Of course. Are you a student?)
- *Oui, j'étudie à Lyon depuis septembre.* (Yes, I've been studying in Lyon since September.)
- *Très bien. Pour les étudiants, il n'y a pas de frais la première année.* (Very good. For students, there are no fees in the first year.)
- *Parfait. Et je vais avoir une carte bancaire ?* (Perfect. And will I get a bank card?)
- *Oui, une carte sans contact. Votre code arrive dans une semaine.* (Yes, a contactless card. Your PIN will arrive in a week.)
- *Et si je suis à découvert ?* (And what if I'm overdrawn?)
- *Vous pouvez être à découvert jusqu'à 200 euros, mais il y a des frais.* (You can be overdrawn up to 200 euros, but there are fees.)
- *D'accord. Je vais faire attention à mon budget !* (OK. I'll be careful with my budget!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Emprunter is borrow, prêter is lend">
  English speakers often mix these up. **Emprunter** is to borrow, to take: *J'emprunte 10 euros à Paul.* **Prêter** is to lend, to give for a while: *Paul me prête 10 euros.* To ask a friend, it's **Tu peux me prêter 10 euros ?**, never *tu peux m'emprunter 10 euros ?*, which would mean you're lending to them.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep your receipts in French">
  For one week, write down each thing you buy in French, with the price and how you paid: *Lundi : un café, 2 euros, sans contact.* At the end of the week, add it up and write one sentence about your budget. Small, real expenses give you the money words every day without extra study time.
</Callout>
