By the end of this lesson you'll be able to talk about your devices, your accounts and what you do online, and help someone set up an app in French.

## Devices

<VocabTable group="devices" />

<Example fr="J'ai un nouveau smartphone, mais l'écran est trop petit." en="I've got a new smartphone, but the screen is too small." />
<Example fr="Je travaille sur mon ordinateur portable." en="I work on my laptop." />
<Example fr="Tu as une appli pour les trains ?" en="Have you got an app for trains?" />

In everyday French, *un portable* on its own is a mobile phone. *Un smartphone* is used too. For a laptop, say *un ordinateur portable* in full, or just *un ordinateur*.

*Un écran* is any screen: on a phone, a tablet, a computer or a TV. *Une appli* is the short, everyday form of *une application*, and you'll hear it far more often in speech.

## Online

<VocabTable group="online" />

<Example fr="Je cherche les horaires sur internet." en="I'm looking up the times on the internet." />
<Example fr="Tu peux réserver en ligne, sur le site de l'hôtel." en="You can book online, on the hotel's website." />
<Example fr="Je t'envoie le lien par mail." en="I'll send you the link by email." />
<Example fr="J'ai téléchargé une appli pour le voyage." en="I've downloaded an app for the journey." />

*Internet* usually has no article: *sur internet*, *j'ai trouvé ça sur internet*. *Un mail* (also written *un e-mail*) is the everyday word in France. The official French word, *un courriel*, is much more common in Québec.

## Accounts

<VocabTable group="accounts" />

<Example fr="Je n'arrive pas à me connecter : j'ai oublié mon mot de passe." en="I can't log in: I've forgotten my password." />
<Example fr="Ton identifiant, c'est ton adresse mail." en="Your username is your email address." />
<Example fr="N'oublie pas de te déconnecter." en="Don't forget to log out." />

When a site asks you to create an account, the form usually wants the same two things: *un identifiant*, which is often your email address, and *un mot de passe*. *Se connecter* and *se déconnecter* are reflexive verbs, so the pronoun changes with the person: *je me connecte*, *tu te déconnectes*, *nous nous connectons*.

## Social media

<VocabTable group="social" />

<Example fr="Elle publie une photo tous les jours." en="She posts a photo every day." />
<Example fr="Il a 5 000 abonnés sur les réseaux sociaux." en="He has 5,000 followers on social media." />

*Publier* is the general verb for posting. You'll also hear *poster*, borrowed from English.

Notice two pairs that learners often mix up. *Recharger* is for a battery, and *télécharger* is for an app or a file. *Enregistrer* keeps a document, and *supprimer* removes it for good.

## Looking after your devices

<VocabTable group="care" />

<Example fr="Je n'ai plus de batterie. Je peux recharger mon téléphone ?" en="My battery's dead. Can I charge my phone?" />
<Example fr="Il faut mettre à jour l'application." en="You need to update the app." />
<Example fr="Oh non, j'ai supprimé la photo !" en="Oh no, I've deleted the photo!" />
<Example fr="N'oublie pas d'enregistrer ton document." en="Don't forget to save your document." />

Many of these verbs follow *de* in instructions, as in <LessonLink id="a2-verbs-with-a-and-de" text="the lesson on verbs + à / de + infinitive" />: *n'oublie pas de recharger*, *essaie de te connecter*.

## En français

A grandmother asks her grandson for help with a video call.

- *Tu peux m'aider ? Je veux appeler ta tante en vidéo.* (Can you help me? I want to video-call your aunt.)
- *Bien sûr. Tu as l'application sur ta tablette ?* (Of course. Have you got the app on your tablet?)
- *Non, je ne crois pas.* (No, I don't think so.)
- *D'accord. Il faut d'abord la télécharger. Voilà.* (OK. First you have to download it. There you go.)
- *Et maintenant ?* (And now?)
- *Maintenant, il faut créer un compte avec ton adresse mail et un mot de passe.* (Now you need to create an account with your email address and a password.)
- *Un mot de passe ? Je vais l'oublier !* (A password? I'll forget it!)
- *Je vais te l'écrire. Voilà, tu es connectée. Tu peux appeler !* (I'll write it down for you. There, you're logged in. You can call!)
- *Merci, mon chéri. Tu es génial !* (Thank you, darling. You're brilliant!)

<Callout type="warning" title="Portable means phone">
  In France, **un portable** on its own almost always means a mobile phone: *J'ai oublié mon portable* (I forgot my phone). If you mean a laptop, say **un ordinateur portable** or **un ordinateur**. And *un mobile* is also used for a mobile phone, especially in writing.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Switch your phone to French">
  Change the language of your phone, or of one app you use every day, to French. You already know where everything is, so the menus teach you the words without effort: *Paramètres*, *Enregistrer*, *Supprimer*, *Mettre à jour*. After a week, you'll recognize them everywhere.
</Callout>
