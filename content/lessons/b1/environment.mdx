By the end of this lesson you'll be able to talk about pollution, climate, energy and recycling, and describe how things are changing with *de plus en plus* and *de moins en moins*.

## The problems

The environment is in the news almost every day, as you saw in <LessonLink id="b1-media-and-news" text="the lesson on media and news" />. Here are the words for the main problems.

<VocabTable group="problems" />

<Example fr="La pollution est un grand problème dans les villes." en="Pollution is a big problem in cities." />
<Example fr="Le réchauffement climatique change le climat de la planète." en="Global warming is changing the planet's climate." />
<Example fr="Cet été, il y a eu une sécheresse en Espagne." en="This summer, there was a drought in Spain." />

*L'environnement* is masculine and *la planète* is feminine. *Le climat* is the general pattern of weather over many years, while *le temps* is the weather today. *Polluer* is a regular -er verb: *les voitures polluent*.

## Energy

<VocabTable group="energy" />

<Example fr="Il y a de plus en plus d'éoliennes en France." en="There are more and more wind turbines in France." />
<Example fr="Nous avons l'énergie solaire à la maison." en="We have solar power at home." />
<Example fr="On économise l'énergie en prenant le vélo." en="You save energy by taking the bike." />

*Économiser* works for energy, water and money: *économiser l'eau*, *économiser de l'argent*. It's a regular -er verb, so it's easy to use straight away. *Les énergies renouvelables* is usually plural, because it covers the sun, the wind and water.

## Waste and recycling

<VocabTable group="waste" />

<Example fr="Chez nous, on trie nos déchets : le plastique, le verre et le reste." en="At home, we sort our rubbish: plastic, glass and everything else." />
<Example fr="Il faut recycler les bouteilles en plastique." en="You must recycle plastic bottles." />
<Example fr="Le gaspillage de pain, c'est un vrai problème." en="Wasting bread is a real problem." />

English "waste" has two meanings, and French has two words. *Les déchets* are the things you throw away. *Le gaspillage* is the act of wasting something you could have used. *Gaspiller* is the verb: *Ne gaspille pas l'eau !*

In France, rubbish is sorted into different bins, and the colours and rules aren't the same in every town. When you move, check the rules in your new town, often on the town hall's website.

## Being green

<VocabTable group="green" />

<Example fr="Le vélo, c'est écologique." en="Cycling is environmentally friendly." />
<Example fr="Elle achète toujours du bio." en="She always buys organic food." />
<Example fr="Il faut protéger les espèces menacées." en="We must protect endangered species." />

*Bio* is short for *biologique*. As a noun, *le bio* means organic food in general: *manger bio*, *acheter du bio*. As an adjective, it never changes: *des légumes bio*, *une pomme bio*. *Protéger* changes its accent like *préférer*: *je protège*, but *nous protégeons*.

## More and more, less and less

<VocabTable group="trends" />

To describe a trend, use *de plus en plus* or *de moins en moins*. Before an adjective or after a verb, there's nothing more to add. Before a noun, add *de*, with no article, as with *beaucoup de*.

<Example fr="Il fait de plus en plus chaud." en="It's getting hotter and hotter." />
<Example fr="Les gens recyclent de plus en plus." en="People are recycling more and more." />
<Example fr="Il y a de plus en plus de plastique dans la mer." en="There's more and more plastic in the sea." />
<Example fr="Il y a de moins en moins de poissons dans la mer." en="There are fewer and fewer fish in the sea." />

*De moins en moins de* covers both "less and less" and "fewer and fewer", so you don't need to think about whether the noun can be counted.

## En français

Two flatmates, Sarah and Théo, are in the kitchen.

- *Théo, tu as mis la bouteille dans la mauvaise poubelle !* (Théo, you've put the bottle in the wrong bin!)
- *Et alors ? C'est juste une bouteille.* (So what? It's just one bottle.)
- *C'est du plastique. Il faut la recycler, pas la mettre avec le reste.* (It's plastic. You have to recycle it, not put it in with everything else.)
- *Tu sais, je trie déjà mes déchets, en général.* (You know, I already sort my rubbish, usually.)
- *En général, oui. Mais il y a de plus en plus de plastique dans la mer.* (Usually, yes. But there's more and more plastic in the sea.)
- *D'accord, d'accord. Et toi, tu prends toujours des douches très longues !* (OK, OK. And you always take really long showers!)
- *Ce n'est pas faux. Je vais faire attention, et économiser l'eau.* (That's a fair point. I'll be careful, and save water.)
- *Alors on fait un effort tous les deux ?* (So we both make an effort?)
- *Oui. Pour la planète !* (Yes. For the planet!)

<Callout type="warning" title="De plus en plus de, with no article">
  Before a noun, *de plus en plus* and *de moins en moins* take **de** on its own, like *beaucoup de*. It's **de plus en plus de plastique**, not *de plus en plus du plastique*, and **de moins en moins de poissons**, not *de moins en moins des poissons*. Before an adjective, there's no *de* at all: **de plus en plus chaud**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Turn every chart into a sentence">
  When you see a chart or a figure in the news, say what it shows in one French sentence: *Il y a de plus en plus d'éoliennes*, *On recycle de plus en plus*. Charts are everywhere in news about the environment, and each one is a free chance to practise the trend phrases.
</Callout>
