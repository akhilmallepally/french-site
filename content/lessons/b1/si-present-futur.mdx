By the end of this lesson you'll be able to talk about things that will probably happen, or could easily happen, and say what you'll do then.

## If it rains…

When something is a real possibility, French uses **si** with the present, and the futur in the other half of the sentence. You know the futur from <LessonLink id="a2-futur-simple" text="the lesson on the futur simple" />.

<VocabTable group="core" />

<Example fr="S'il pleut, on restera à la maison." en="If it rains, we'll stay at home." />
<Example fr="Si tu viens, je serai content." en="If you come, I'll be happy." />
<Example fr="Si nous partons à huit heures, nous arriverons à l'heure." en="If we leave at eight, we'll arrive on time." />

The two halves can swap places. When *si* comes second, you usually don't need a comma: *On restera à la maison s'il pleut.*

English works the same way here: "if it rains" is in the present too. The difference is that French never puts the futur straight after *si*, even when the sentence is about tomorrow.

## Si and il

*Si* becomes *s'* before *il* and *ils*, and only there. Before *elle*, *on* or any other word, it stays *si*.

<Example fr="S'ils viennent, nous ferons un gâteau." en="If they come, we'll make a cake." />
<Example fr="Si elle arrive en retard, on partira sans elle." en="If she's late, we'll leave without her." />

## If…, do this

The second half can also be a command, as in <LessonLink id="a1-imperative" text="the lesson on the imperative" />. This is useful for instructions and friendly advice.

<Example fr="Si vous êtes en retard, appelez-moi." en="If you're late, call me." />
<Example fr="Si tu es fatigué, repose-toi." en="If you're tired, rest." />

## If…, always

For a habit or something that's always true, both halves are in the present. *Si* then means something close to "whenever".

<Example fr="Si je bois du café le soir, je ne dors pas." en="If I drink coffee in the evening, I don't sleep." />
<Example fr="S'il fait beau, je vais au travail à vélo." en="If the weather's nice, I cycle to work." />

## Otherwise: sinon

*Sinon* gives the other possibility, without repeating the whole condition.

<Example fr="Pars maintenant, sinon tu vas rater le train." en="Leave now, otherwise you'll miss the train." />
<Example fr="On ira à la plage s'il fait beau. Sinon, on ira au cinéma." en="We'll go to the beach if the weather's nice. Otherwise, we'll go to the cinema." />

## Words for making plans

<VocabTable group="plans" />

<Example fr="S'il y a des bouchons, nous serons en retard." en="If there are traffic jams, we'll be late." />
<Example fr="S'il y a un imprévu, on reportera le dîner." en="If something comes up, we'll postpone the dinner." />
<Example fr="Le train est en retard ? Tant pis, on prendra le bus." en="The train is late? Never mind, we'll take the bus." />

*Rater* is the everyday word for missing a train, a bus or a plane. To say how you arrive, choose between three phrases: *en avance* (early), *à l'heure* (on time) and *en retard* (late). *Prévoir* works like *voir*, so its participle is *prévu*: *On a prévu un dîner samedi* (We've planned a dinner on Saturday). An *imprévu* is literally something "not foreseen".

*Tant pis* accepts something you can't change. *Tant mieux* welcomes good news: *Il fera beau demain ? Tant mieux !* (It'll be nice tomorrow? Good!)

## En français

Two friends are planning a Saturday out.

- *On va au parc samedi, avec Léa et Hugo ?* (Shall we go to the park on Saturday, with Léa and Hugo?)
- *Bonne idée ! Mais s'il pleut, qu'est-ce qu'on fera ?* (Good idea! But if it rains, what will we do?)
- *S'il pleut, on ira au musée.* (If it rains, we'll go to the museum.)
- *D'accord. Si tu veux, je prendrai la voiture.* (OK. If you like, I'll take the car.)
- *Non, avec la voiture, il y a toujours des bouchons. On prendra le train.* (No, with the car there are always traffic jams. We'll take the train.)
- *Si on part à neuf heures, on sera en avance ?* (If we leave at nine, will we be early?)
- *Oui, mais si tu es en retard, on ratera le train !* (Yes, but if you're late, we'll miss the train!)
- *Je serai à l'heure, c'est sûr. Et si Hugo ne vient pas ?* (I'll be on time, for sure. And what if Hugo doesn't come?)
- *Tant pis ! On ira sans lui.* (Too bad! We'll go without him.)

<Callout type="warning" title="No futur after si">
  It's tempting to say *si il pleuvra*, because the rain is tomorrow. But after *si* meaning "if", French uses the present: **S'il pleut demain, on restera à la maison.** Check the spelling too: *si il* always becomes **s'il**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Always have a plan B">
  Each time you make a plan, add a back-up in French: *Samedi, on ira au parc. S'il pleut, on ira au cinéma. Sinon, on restera à la maison.* Three sentences, three structures, and it takes ten seconds. Do it with your real plans for the week, and the pattern soon becomes automatic.
</Callout>
